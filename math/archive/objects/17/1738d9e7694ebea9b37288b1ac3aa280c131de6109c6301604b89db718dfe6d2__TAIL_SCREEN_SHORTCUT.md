# Exact boundary reduction of the two-sided tail screen

9 October 2026. Independent corollary of the uploaded bilateral tail-rank theorem. This is a necessary-condition screening improvement, not a Fourier algorithm or a general circuit lower bound.

## One boundary test per orientation

Let pi be a permutation of 0,...,N-1 and let s be a nonnegative integer rank-sum bound that applies uniformly to every cut. The whole family

    min(t, N-1-max(pi[-t:])) <= s,  1 <= t < N,

is automatic if s >= N-1. Otherwise it is equivalent to

    max(pi[-(s+1):]) >= N-1-s.

Proof: necessity is the t=s+1 inequality. For t<=s the original minimum is already <=s. For t>=s+1, the suffix contains the passing boundary suffix, so its maximum cannot decrease and N-1-max remains <=s. The mirror family similarly reduces to

    min(pi[:s+1]) <= s.

Different prefix and suffix rank sums use their respective boundary sizes. Thus checking every cut gives no stronger filter than these two boundary checks when the rank bounds are constant. If the rank budget changes with the cut, this shortcut is not asserted.

For two rank-one stages, the relevant sum is s=2. The first three entries must meet {0,1,2}, and the last three entries must meet {N-3,N-2,N-1}. The test costs O(s+1) accesses per orientation after the permutation is available. It does not certify any passing permutation or preserve validity under arbitrary endpoint reorderings.

## Closed-form count

For equal rank sum s, put k=s+1 and assume N>=2k so that the two boundary position sets and the two boundary value sets are disjoint. Define

    R_k(a) = binom(k,a)^2 a!,
    A = (N-k)!^2 / (N-2k)!,
    B = sum_{a=0}^k sum_{b=0}^k (-1)^(a+b) R_k(a) R_k(b) (N-a-b)!.

Exactly N! - 2A + B permutations pass both boundary tests. Indeed A counts permutations missing the first boundary match, or, by symmetry, the last boundary match. B is the inclusion-exclusion count missing both; its two forbidden k-by-k blocks have disjoint rows and columns, so their rook counts multiply.

For N=8 and s=2 this gives 28,584 passing permutations out of 40,320, exactly reproducing the supplied exhaustive count. The other 11,736 fail this necessary condition.

A simple probability bound, for uniform random permutations, is

    P(pass both) <= k^4 / (N(N-1)).

There are k^2 possible allowed first-boundary matches and k^2 possible last-boundary matches. Every selected pair concerns distinct positions and distinct values and occurs with probability 1/(N(N-1)); the union bound proves the formula. For fixed k the exact probability is k^4/N^2 + O_k(N^-3), as intersections of distinct two-match events prescribe at least three distinct assignments. Thus constant-rank two-stage candidates in this specific fixed-order architecture have a small proportion of boundary-compatible uniformly random internal permutations at large N. This says nothing about the complexity or existence of deliberately chosen passing permutations.

## Independent verification

Run `python3 independent_checks.py` with the standard library. It imports no uploaded code. It exhaustively compares every-cut and boundary predicates for N=2,...,8, both orientations and all s=0,...,N-1. It independently compares the closed form with exhaustive counts for every N>=2k in that range, and records larger exact counts for s=2. The source theorem is proved algebraically in the bilateral review; finite tests here corroborate the new screening/count formulas.
