# Sparse-surrogate Fourier research v2: vanishing error, head/tail acceleration, and a near-tight support barrier

**Date:** 2026-10-09  
**Status:** theorem/prototype branch for a randomized **approximate** full DFT on one fresh arbitrary input. The asymptotic speedup comes from this project's time-domain sparsification + input-pruned full-output DFT, not from OpenAI #130 or the CrocSwap/Swapnil finite-network machinery. **This does not solve the exact FFT problem.**

## 1. Base setting

Let

\[
U_N=F_N/\sqrt N
\]

be the unitary length-\(N\) DFT, with \(N\) a power of two in the current implementation.

For a fresh arbitrary \(x\in\mathbb C^N\), put

\[
p_j=\frac{|x_j|}{\|x\|_1}.
\]

Draw \(J_1,\ldots,J_k\) independently from \(p\) and define

\[
y=\frac{\|x\|_1}{k}\sum_{s=1}^k \operatorname{phase}(x_{J_s})e_{J_s}.
\]

Then \(\mathbb E y=x\), and after repeated locations are coalesced, \(y\) has at most \(k\) nonzero coordinates.

The v1 theorem was

\[
\Pr\!\left[
\|U_Ny-U_Nx\|_\infty>
\varepsilon\frac{\|x\|_1}{\sqrt N}
\right]
\le \delta
\]

provided

\[
k\ge \frac{4}{\varepsilon^2}\log\frac{4N}{\delta}.
\]

The classical sparse-input full-output decomposition in `hybrid_sparse.py` then computes all \(N\) outputs of \(y\) in

\[
O(N\log(k+2))
\]

arithmetic.

---

## 2. New theorem: vanishing error while remaining below \(N\log N\)

The sample count is

\[
k=O\!\left(\varepsilon^{-2}\log\frac{N}{\delta}\right).
\]

Therefore

\[
\log(k+2)
=
O\!\left(
\log\frac1\varepsilon+
\log\log\frac{N}{\delta}
\right).
\]

Hence the full fresh-input/all-output algorithm satisfies

\[
\boxed{
T(N,\varepsilon,\delta)
=
O\!\left(
N\left[
\log\frac1\varepsilon+
\log\log\frac{N}{\delta}
\right]
\right).
}
\]

This is the useful accuracy/runtime form of the theorem.

### Polynomially high confidence and polylogarithmically vanishing error

Take any constants \(a,b>0\),

\[
\varepsilon_N=(\log N)^{-a},
\qquad
\delta_N=N^{-b}.
\]

Then

\[
k=O((\log N)^{2a+1})
\]

and

\[
\boxed{T(N)=O(N\log\log N)}.
\]

Meanwhile, with probability at least \(1-N^{-b}\),

\[
\boxed{
\|U_Ny-U_Nx\|_\infty
\le
\frac{\|x\|_1}{\sqrt N(\log N)^a}.
}
\]

Thus the additive error can genuinely tend to zero; fixed \(\varepsilon\) is not required.

### Stretched-exponential accuracy

More generally, for any fixed \(0<\gamma<1\), choose

\[
\varepsilon_N=\exp(- (\log N)^\gamma),
\qquad
\delta_N=N^{-b}.
\]

Then

\[
k
=\exp(2(\log N)^\gamma)\,\operatorname{polylog}N
=N^{o(1)},
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
T(N)=O(N(\log N)^\gamma)=o(N\log N).
}
\]

So any error schedule satisfying

\[
\log(1/\varepsilon_N)=o(\log N)
\]

is compatible with an \(o(N\log N)\) transform by this route (for polynomially small failure probability).

The crossover is asymptotic. At practical sizes, very small \(\varepsilon\) makes the sample budget large and native FFTs remain preferable.

## 3. New head/tail theorem: preserve large coordinates exactly

Let \(H_s\) be the indices of the \(s\) largest values \(|x_j|\), and write

\[
x=h_s+r_s,
\]

where \(h_s\) is supported on \(H_s\).

Keep \(h_s\) exactly and apply the same importance sampler only to \(r_s\).

We keep the **same global target**

\[
\|U_Ny-U_Nx\|_\infty
\le
\varepsilon\frac{\|x\|_1}{\sqrt N}.
\]

One residual sample has magnitude \(\|r_s\|_1/\sqrt N\) in every Fourier coordinate. Therefore it is enough to use

\[
\boxed{
k_s
\ge
\frac{4}{\varepsilon^2}
\left(\frac{\|r_s\|_1}{\|x\|_1}\right)^2
\log\frac{4N}{\delta}.
}
\]

The final surrogate has support at most

\[
s+k_s.
\]

The tail ratio enters **quadratically**.

### Deterministic head-only case

If

\[
\|r_s\|_1\le\varepsilon\|x\|_1,
\]

then no random samples are needed at all, since directly

\[
\|U_Nr_s\|_\infty
\le\frac{\|r_s\|_1}{\sqrt N}
\le\varepsilon\frac{\|x\|_1}{\sqrt N}.
\]

The approximate transform is simply the exact transform of the \(s\)-sparse head, costing

\[
O(N\log(s+2)).
\]

### Planner

`plan_head_tail` tests a logarithmic family of head sizes `0,1,2,4,...` up to a configurable polylogarithmic limit and minimizes the certified support upper bound \(s+k_s\).

Each candidate uses `argpartition`, so with the default \(O(\log\log N)\) candidate count the planner itself is \(O(N\log\log N)\), within the worst-case target budget.

## 4. Compressible-input consequences

The worst-case dense guarantee remains \(O(N\log\log N)\) for fixed accuracy.

But fresh compressible inputs can be faster without any cached relation.

### Exponentially decaying sorted magnitudes

Suppose

\[
|x|_{(j)}\le C e^{-cj}.
\]

For fixed \(\varepsilon\), a constant-size head makes

\[
\|r_s\|_1\le\varepsilon\|x\|_1,
\]

so the deterministic head-only path gives

\[
\boxed{T(N)=O(N)}
\]

for that fixed approximation target.

If instead

\[
\varepsilon_N=(\log N)^{-a},
\]

then \(s=O(\log\log N)\), and therefore

\[
\boxed{T(N)=O(N\log\log\log N)}.
\]

This is a true data-dependent hierarchy inside our own sparse mechanism:

- worst dense: `O(N log log N)` for polylogarithmically vanishing error;
- rapidly compressible dense: as low as `O(N log log log N)` for the same error schedule;
- fixed-accuracy exponentially compressible: `O(N)`;
- exactly sparse: exact `O(N log(s+2))`.

## 5. New universal ceiling: natural per-bin accuracy forces nearly dense support

This is independent of the random sampler.

For any \(k\)-sparse surrogate \(y\), because \(U_N\) is unitary,

\[
\|U_N(y-x)\|_\infty
\ge
\frac{\|U_N(y-x)\|_2}{\sqrt N}
=
\frac{\|y-x\|_2}{\sqrt N}.
\]

Let

\[
\sigma_k(x)_2
=
\min_{\|z\|_0\le k}\|x-z\|_2
\]

be the best \(k\)-term time-domain approximation error. Then every time-domain \(k\)-sparse surrogate, with arbitrary signed or complex weights, obeys

\[
\boxed{
\|U_N(y-x)\|_\infty
\ge
\frac{\sigma_k(x)_2}{\sqrt N}.
}
\]

For constant-modulus inputs,

\[
\sigma_k(x)_2
=
\|x\|_2\sqrt{1-k/N}.
\]

Therefore achieving the natural per-bin scale

\[
\|U_N(y-x)\|_\infty
\le
\eta\frac{\|x\|_2}{\sqrt N}
\]

requires

\[
\boxed{k\ge N(1-\eta^2).}
\]

For example:

- \(\eta=1/2\): at least \(0.75N\) nonzeros;
- \(\eta=0.1\): at least \(0.99N\) nonzeros;
- \(\eta=0.01\): at least \(0.9999N\) nonzeros.

So no time-domain sparse-surrogate algorithm can simultaneously retain sublinear support and approximate every Fourier coefficient at a small constant fraction of the natural RMS coefficient scale on worst-case flat inputs.

## 6. Stronger support barrier: even complex/signed weights need logarithmic support for constant `l_inf / ||x||2` error

The previous lower bound addresses the much stronger natural scale \(\|x\|_2/\sqrt N\). There is also a distinct logarithmic lower bound at the coarser \(\varepsilon\|x\|_2\) scale relevant to the Monte-Carlo theorem.

Take the flat input

\[
x=(1,\ldots,1),
\qquad
U_Nx=\sqrt N e_0.
\]

Suppose there exists a \(k\)-sparse **complex-weighted** surrogate \(y\) with

\[
\|U_Ny-\sqrt N e_0\|_\infty\le\varepsilon\sqrt N,
\qquad 0<\varepsilon<1/4.
\]

Put

\[
z=U_Ny/\sqrt N.
\]

Then \(z\) entrywise \(\varepsilon\)-approximates \(e_0\).

For each frequency shift \(r\), modulate the same time-domain surrogate by a phase ramp. The support remains the same \(k\)-set, while its Fourier transform is the corresponding cyclic shift of \(z\). Put those \(N\) shifted vectors into the columns of an \(N\times N\) matrix \(M\).

Then:

1. all columns of \(M\) lie in the Fourier image of the same \(k\)-dimensional coordinate subspace, so
   \[
   \operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb C}M\le k;
   \]
2. \(M\) entrywise \(\varepsilon\)-approximates the identity:
   \[
   \|M-I\|_{\max}\le\varepsilon.
   \]

Realify the complex matrix:

\[
\mathcal R(M)=
\begin{pmatrix}
\Re M&-\Im M\\
\Im M& \Re M
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Then

\[
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb R}\mathcal R(M)
=2\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb C}M
\le2k,
\]

and \(\mathcal R(M)\) is a real \(2N\times2N\) perturbed identity with diagonal bounded away from zero and every off-diagonal entry bounded by \(\varepsilon\).

Noga Alon's perturbed-identity rank theorem gives, in the range \(\Theta(N^{-1/2})\le\varepsilon<1/4\),

\[
\operatorname{rank}\mathcal R(M)
=
\Omega\!\left(
\frac{\log N}{\varepsilon^2\log(1/\varepsilon)}
\right).
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
k
=
\Omega\!\left(
\frac{\log N}{\varepsilon^2\log(1/\varepsilon)}
\right).
}
\]

This lower bound allows arbitrary signed/complex surrogate coefficients. It kills the main escape hatch left open in v1.

### Comparison with our upper bound

Our randomized importance sampler uses

\[
k=O(\varepsilon^{-2}\log(N/\delta)).
\]

Thus:

- for fixed \(\varepsilon\) and fixed/polynomial failure probability,
  \[
  \boxed{k=\Theta(\log N)}
  \]
  up to constants;
- for \(\varepsilon=(\log N)^{-a}\), our upper support is
  \[
  O((\log N)^{2a+1}),
  \]
  while Alon's bound gives
  \[
  \Omega\!\left(\frac{(\log N)^{2a+1}}{\log\log N}\right).
  \]
  The gap is only a \(\log\log N\) factor.

So the support scale of this project is not merely an artifact of iid sampling. For the flat worst case it is close to optimal among **all** time-domain sparse complex surrogates.

Primary source for the rank bound: Noga Alon, *Perturbed Identity Matrices Have High Rank: Proof and Applications*, Combinatorics, Probability and Computing 18 (2009), 3--15, DOI 10.1017/S0963548307008917.

## 7. Experiments

The complete regression suite now has **47 tests passing**.

`benchmark_sparse_surrogate_v2.py` records three families of checks.

### Accuracy/runtime schedules

With failure target \(\delta=N^{-2}\), theoretical inner radix depths behave as predicted. For example at \(N=2^{64}\):

- fixed \(\varepsilon=0.5\): inner depth 12 versus full depth 64 (`0.1875` ratio);
- \(\varepsilon=1/\sqrt{\log N}\): 15 versus 64 (`0.2344`);
- \(\varepsilon=1/\log N\): 21 versus 64 (`0.3281`);
- \(\varepsilon=e^{-\sqrt{\log N}}\): 29 versus 64 (`0.4531`).

All still remain asymptotically below full \(N\log N\).

### Head/tail planner at `N=65536`, `epsilon=0.18`, `delta=0.02`

| input | plain budget | best head | tail samples | total support bound | reduction |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| flat | 2024 | 0 | 2024 | 2024 | 1.00x |
| Gaussian | 2024 | 0 | 2024 | 2024 | 1.00x |
| power `j^-0.8` | 2024 | 256 | 1113 | 1369 | 1.48x |
| power `j^-1.2` | 2024 | 128 | 145 | 273 | 7.41x |
| exponential | 2024 | 32 | 0 | 32 | **63.25x** |
| 32 spikes + noise | 2024 | 32 | 325 | 357 | 5.67x |

Every reported one-shot numerical trial stayed below its theorem target; these timings/errors are only sanity checks, not proof.

### Natural-scale ceiling at `N=2^20`

The rigorous flat-input lower bound requires:

| target `eta * ||x||2/sqrt(N)` | minimum support fraction |
|---:|---:|
| `eta=0.5` | 75% |
| `0.25` | 93.75% |
| `0.1` | 99% |
| `0.01` | 99.99% |

This is why the sparse-surrogate path should be viewed as a high-throughput additive approximation scheme, not a route to uniformly high relative precision on every Fourier bin.

## 8. Current research verdict

The strongest defensible statement of this branch is now:

> For one fresh arbitrary dense power-of-two input, all Fourier outputs can be approximated with polynomially high confidence in
> \[
> O\!\left(N[\log(1/\varepsilon)+\log\log N]\right)
> \]
> arithmetic, with simultaneous error
> \[
> \|U_Ny-U_Nx\|_\infty\le\varepsilon\|x\|_1/\sqrt N.
> \]
> In particular, polylogarithmically vanishing error still costs only \(O(N\log\log N)\), and rapidly compressible inputs admit still smaller sparse supports. For flat worst-case inputs, the required support is \(\Theta(\log N)\) for fixed accuracy up to the known approximate-rank constants, even if arbitrary complex surrogate weights are allowed.

This remains approximate and does not beat the exact FFT barrier. But unlike the discarded branches, both the speedup and the limitations here arise from **our sparse-surrogate mechanism itself**.
