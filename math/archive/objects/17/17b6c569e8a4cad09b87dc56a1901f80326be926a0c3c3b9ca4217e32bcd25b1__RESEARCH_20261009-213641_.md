# Permutation-displacement and adaptive transcript gates for exact FFT research — v16

**Checkpoint:** 10 October 2026  
**Status:** Exact, reproducible obstruction theorems for several proposed one-cycle / overlapping-measurement continuations of the v15 Green-frame work. **No exact sub-`N log N` FFT is constructed.** The all-length randomized approximate `O(N log log N)` branch from v15 remains unchanged.

## 1. Starting point

The v15 complete local Green frame resolved the identity exactly, but its joint decoder algebraically collapsed to mixed-radix Cooley–Tukey and retained `Theta(N log N)` work. Fewer than a complete local frame had a common kernel.

The unresolved exact idea was to replace the local frame by either:

1. a globally overlapping/sliding measurement system;
2. a one-cycle Fourier recurrence driven by a clever input permutation;
3. several incomplete projected rows with adaptive or nonlinear postprocessing.

This checkpoint closes those three versions in precise restricted models.

## 2. Exact all-permutation displacement theorem

Let

\[
F_N[k,j]=\omega^{kj},\qquad \omega^N=1
\]

with `omega` primitive. Let `Z` be the cyclic output shift. With the convention used by the verifier,

\[
Z F_N=F_ND,
\]

where `D` is diagonal with the `N` distinct roots of unity (possibly in inverse order; this does not affect the theorem).

For any permutation matrix `P`,

\[
\operatorname{rank}(ZF_N-F_NP)
=
\operatorname{rank}(D-P).
\]

### Theorem 2.1 — exact cycle formula

Decompose the permutation into disjoint cycles. Let `z(P)` be the number of cycles `C` whose index sum satisfies

\[
\sum_{j\in C}j\equiv0\pmod N.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}(D-P)=N-z(P).}
\]

### Proof

After reordering coordinates by permutation cycles, `D-P` is block diagonal. On a cycle

\[
C=(i_1,\ldots,i_\ell),
\]

the homogeneous equations propagate one coordinate around the cycle. Returning to the start gives the consistency condition

\[
\omega^{i_1+\cdots+i_\ell}=1.
\]

If the cycle sum is zero modulo `N`, the block has a one-dimensional kernel. Otherwise it is nonsingular. Summing the nullities proves the formula.

### Theorem 2.2 — sharp minimum over every permutation

\[
\boxed{
\min_P\operatorname{rank}(ZF_N-F_NP)
=
\left\lfloor\frac N2\right\rfloor.
}
\]

The unique minimizing permutation is negation:

\[
P(j)=-j\pmod N.
\]

For odd `N`, its zero-sum cycles are `{0}` and all pairs `{j,-j}`. For even `N`, the same cycles occur and `N/2` remains a nonzero-sum singleton. Hence the nullity is `ceil(N/2)` and the rank is `floor(N/2)`.

For the matching upper bound, note that only `{0}` can be a zero-sum singleton. Every other zero-sum cycle consumes at least two indices. Therefore

\[
z(P)\le
1+\left\lfloor\frac{N-1}{2}\right\rfloor
=
\left\lceil\frac N2\right\rceil.
\]

Equality forces every nonzero zero-sum cycle to be a pair `{j,-j}`, proving uniqueness.

### Consequence

Any exact one-permutation displacement recurrence for the full Fourier matrix needs a correction/forcing space of dimension at least `floor(N/2)`. A constant-rank or polylog-rank one-cycle correction is impossible in this model.

This is a **displacement-rank obstruction only**. It is not a lower bound on unrestricted arithmetic circuits.

## 3. Why the minimizing permutation still does not produce a cheap half-transform

For the negation permutation, the kernel of `D-P` has dimension `ceil(N/2)`. On every non-self-inverse pair, a kernel basis vector can be written

\[
u_j=e_j+\omega^{-j}e_{-j},
\]

while a complementary vector is

\[
v_j=e_j-\omega^{-j}e_{-j}.
\]

Their Fourier images are

\[
F_Nu_j[k]
=
2\omega^{-j/2}
\cos\!\left(\frac{2\pi(k+1/2)j}{N}\right),
\]

\[
F_Nv_j[k]
=
2i\omega^{-j/2}
\sin\!\left(\frac{2\pi(k+1/2)j}{N}\right).
\]

Thus the rank-minimizing displacement splits the original transform into two independent dense trigonometric transforms:

- a cosine-like half of dimension `ceil(N/2)`;
- a sine-like half of dimension `floor(N/2)`.

Both have full column rank, and together they have rank `N`. The `floor(N/2)` residual is not merely a few missing seeds; it is an entire second dense half-transform. This is another form of the standard even/odd or cosine/sine decomposition, not a linear-time shortcut.

## 4. Minimal-state moment theorem

For arbitrary input `x`, define the output sequence

\[
y_k=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}x_j\omega^{kj}.
\]

### Theorem 4.1

Any scalar linear recurrence with input-independent coefficients that generates `y_k` for **every** input must have order at least `N`.

### Proof

An order-`r` recurrence gives a nonzero polynomial `q` of degree at most `r` such that

\[
q(\omega^j)=0
\]

for every basis input `x=e_j`. The `N` roots are distinct. Therefore `r>=N`.

By Cayley–Hamilton, every linear time-invariant state-space realization with `r` states induces a recurrence of order at most `r`; hence it also needs at least `N` states.

For generic nonzero modal weights, the `N x N` Hankel moment matrix factors as

\[
V\,\operatorname{diag}(x)\,V^T
\]

with `V` Vandermonde and therefore has rank `N`. The finite-field verifier checks this exactly.

This closes the idea that diagonal modulation of the input might permit an `O(1)`-state exact output scan. The modulation update is simple, but the output moment ladder has `N` independent states.

## 5. Adaptive block-measurement transcript barrier

Consider any collection of projected rows. Row `r` produces `K_r` scalar linear summaries of the input—one per block or group. The blocks may:

- overlap across rows;
- have unequal sizes;
- use arbitrary weights;
- use different partitions or permutations.

After those summaries are formed, allow **arbitrary** downstream processing: Green scans, FFTs, resampling, nonlinear decoding, or unlimited input-independent precomputation.

Let `A` be the stacked measurement matrix. Then

\[
\operatorname{rank}(A)
\le
\sum_rK_r.
\]

### Theorem 5.1 — deterministic transcript necessity

If

\[
\sum_rK_r<N,
\]

then no decoder from that transcript can return the exact DFT for every input.

### Proof

There is a nonzero `v` in `ker A`. Inputs `0` and `v` produce the same transcript. Any decoder therefore returns the same output for both, but `F_Nv` is nonzero because `F_N` is invertible.

### Theorem 5.2 — adaptive zero-branch version

Suppose a deterministic algorithm chooses later linear measurements as a function of previous measurement values. Follow the branch generated by input `0`. If that branch obtains fewer than `N` scalar measurements, choose a nonzero vector in their common kernel. It generates the same all-zero responses, follows the identical branch, and remains indistinguishable from zero. Exact recovery fails on one of the two inputs.

This theorem is stronger than the former same-partition local-kernel argument: changing or overlapping partitions does not evade the dimension gate.

It does **not** rule out approximation, algorithms that continue rereading the raw input during decoding, or unrestricted arithmetic circuits that never pass through such a compressed transcript.

## 6. Separate-row rank/work conservation

Now impose the actual separately executed projected-row schedule used by the earlier prototypes. Row `r` pays:

1. at least `N` charged work for a full fresh-input scan / row construction;
2. a length-`K_r` transform cost represented by `K_r log_2 K_r`.

Exact rank coverage requires

\[
\sum_rK_r\ge N.
\]

For `R` rows, convexity gives

\[
\sum_rK_r\log_2K_r
\ge
N\log_2(N/R).
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
C_R
\ge
RN+N\log_2(N/R).
}
\]

Over integer `1<=R<=N`, the continuous/equalized lower proxy is minimized at `R=1` or `R=2`, where

\[
C_R\ge N\log_2N+N.
\]

So varying block sizes, using Egyptian-fraction rank coverage, or overlapping independently executed rows does not beat `N log N` in this **specific charged schedule**.

Joint batching can evade the separate-row accounting. v15 tested the complete fixed-partition frame, and its joint evaluator collapsed exactly to Cooley–Tukey. The present theorem does not prove that every conceivable joint evaluator has the same fate.

## 7. Sliding/overlapping shift filters collapse to output scaling

Let `Z` be the cyclic input shift and let

\[
p(Z)=\sum_{s=0}^d c_sZ^s
\]

be any cyclic sliding-window filter. Since the Fourier matrix diagonalizes `Z`,

\[
\boxed{
F_Np(Z)=p(D)F_N.
}
\]

Consequently an invertible geometric, FIR, or polynomial shift preconditioner only diagonally rescales Fourier coefficients. Applying or inverting it in linear time does not remove the need to evaluate the Fourier transform itself.

This closes the simplest globally overlapping continuation of the local projector: a filter living entirely in the cyclic-shift algebra is a preconditioner, not a new transform evaluator.

A viable exact continuation must use a genuinely noncommuting global operator, not only a polynomial/rational function of one shift.

- The minimum displacement ranks are exactly

- In every tested size the negation permutation is the unique minimizer.

  1. the zero-sum-cycle formula;

- Exact Hankel moment ranks equal `N` for `N=5,7,8,9,16,25,32,49`.
- Exact identities `F p(Z)=p(D)F` pass for several composite and prime-power lengths.

- `N=32`, seven overlapping rows with four summaries each: transcript rank `28`, explicit nonzero common-kernel witness, and nonzero DFT of that witness.
- Eight such rows reach rank `32` in the sampled certificate.
- `N=60`, block counts `(30,20,10)` reach the information threshold `60` and sampled full rank.
- Adaptive zero-path certificate at `N=64` with seven rows of eight measurements produces a nonzero indistinguishable witness.

### Cosine/sine split

For `N=7,8,9,16,25,32,64`, the explicit formulas agree numerically to about `10^-13` or better, and the two subtransforms have dimensions `ceil(N/2)` and `floor(N/2)` with combined rank `N`.

The automated suite reports

\boxed{12/12\text{ tests passing}.}

## 9. Prior-art boundary

Displacement rank is a classical structured-matrix framework, and the Fourier matrix's diagonalization of cyclic shift is standard. Cosine/sine transforms as symmetry-restricted Fourier transforms are also classical. A limited search did not locate the exact sharp statement

\[
\min_P\operatorname{rank}(ZF_N-F_NP)=\lfloor N/2\rfloor
\]

with the unique negation minimizer, but this is **not** a literature-wide novelty proof. The theorem is presented as a project checkpoint requiring independent review.

## 10. Honest conclusion and next gate

### Closed exact continuations

1. **One input permutation + low-rank forcing:** needs rank at least `floor(N/2)`.
2. **Diagonal-modulation moment scan:** needs `N` states.
3. **Fewer than `N` total block summaries:** has a common-kernel ambiguity, even with overlapping/adaptive partitions.
4. **Separately executed projected rows:** rank/work proxy is at least `Theta(N log N)`.
5. **Complete same-partition frame:** v15 shows it is Cooley–Tukey.
6. **Sliding filters in the shift algebra:** only rescale Fourier outputs.

### Still open

An exact improvement must use a full-rank, globally coupled, **noncommuting** measurement/evaluator whose application and inverse-side Fourier decoder are both fully charged below `N log N`. It must not reduce to:

- a compressed block transcript;
- a polynomial of one shift;
- a constant-state moment recurrence;
- an independently executed bank of projected rows;
- the complete local frame already identified with Cooley–Tukey.

The strongest constructive result on the current path remains the randomized all-length approximate `O(N log log N)` theorem candidate from v15. Exact arbitrary-input sub-`N log N` remains open in this project.
