GLOBAL-BOTTLENECK PACKAGE: STATIC AUDIT AND INTEGRATION
9 October 2026

BOTTOM LINE

The package supplies sound, elementary necessary conditions for compressing a
complete fresh dense Fourier input, plus a correct-in-principle finite-field
Bluestein/Kronecker control exposing hidden bigint work. It supplies no new
Fourier speed bound, no universal N log N arithmetic lower bound, and no
constructive ingredient that already improves our O(N log k) sparse expansion.

Its useful addition relative to selected-frequency sampling barriers is that
the binary packing argument permits EVERY bounded encoder, including nonlinear
and discontinuous encoders. The dense complex rank barrier is a width theorem,
not a time theorem. Both barriers must be adjusted before applying them to the
k-dimensional sparse-amplitude family. They do not obstruct the decoded-state
fusion or the conditional thin-matrix route.

1. DENSE COMPLEX WIDTH THEOREM: VALID, CAREFULLY SCOPED

For differentiable E:C^N -> C^w and D:C^w -> C^N with D(E(x))=F_N x on an
open set, differentiation at a compatible point yields F_N=J_D J_E. Thus
N=rank(F_N)<=w. For real differentiability use real dimensions 2N and 2w;
the conclusion is unchanged. For finite piecewise algebraic algorithms use
a regular open branch, with all information selecting the branch accounted for.

This remains true if the maps contain regular nonlinear algebraic operations.
It does not establish that the algorithm needs N log N operations. A putative
O(N) algorithm retaining N scalar coordinates is compatible with the theorem.
Previously emitted outputs, retained original inputs, side memory, and any
other information crossing the claimed cut cannot be left out of its width.

The finite-field rank tests are useful implementation sanity checks. They are
not proofs of the complex differentiability theorem; the chain rule is the
proof. The supplied documentation makes this distinction correctly.

2. BINARY NONLINEAR PACKING THEOREM: VALID

If E:{0,1}^N -> C^w has |E_j(x)|<=M, and a decoder has absolute Fourier-output
error at most epsilon<1/2 and Lipschitz constant L on the encoded image, put
tau=1-2 epsilon. Distinct binary inputs have unnormalized Fourier-output
separation at least 1 in maximum modulus: Parseval gives
||F_N(x-y)||_infinity >= ||x-y||_2 >= 1.

The decoded outputs are consequently separated by tau, and encoded points by
tau/L. A real-coordinate grid of side tau/(2L), with at most
Q=ceil(4ML/tau)+1 cells per coordinate, admits at most Q^(2w) separated points.
Hence 2^N<=Q^(2w). The endpoint slack and complex sqrt(2) factor are adequate.
For exact decoding, Q<4ML+2 gives the stated strict lower bound
L>(2^(N/(2w))-2)/(4M).

For polynomial M and L, w=Omega(N/log N). More generally the approximate
version also needs log(1/tau)=O(log N); allowing epsilon to approach 1/2
arbitrarily closely changes that asymptotic deduction. The upload states its
polynomial-width conclusion after setting epsilon=0, so there is no error.

The theorem does not forbid enormous dynamic range or extreme sensitivity.
It does not give arithmetic gate complexity. Its L is an absolute Lipschitz
constant in specified coordinates, so rescaling changes M and L separately;
the product ML is the relevant scale-invariant quantity. Nor can the same
constant-error statement be copied unchanged to outputs normalized by 1/N.

The explicit block code genuinely has a pair at distance 2^(-b) with Fourier
outputs separated by 1, giving L>=2^b. This is a valid lower witness. No proof
of an upper bound L=O(2^b) for the entire decoder is provided or needed. The
word "sharp" should mean the same exponential scale, not exact constants or
a proved matching decoder upper bound.

3. ESSENTIAL INTEGRATION: USE THE SPARSE INPUT DIMENSION

3a. Fixed support and arbitrary complex amplitudes

Our actual sparse family is
  f_m=sum_{s=1}^k a_s z_s^m, 0<=m<N,
for distinct supplied roots z_s. The first k rows of its N-by-k Vandermonde
matrix form an invertible square Vandermonde, so the full output map has
rank k. A smooth exact factorization through C^w therefore needs w>=k,
NOT w>=N. Keeping the k amplitudes, or a length-k decoded recurrence state,
meets that requirement exactly. An N-component output is not the same thing
as N independent input dimensions.

For unknown support the domain is a finite union of k-dimensional subspaces;
it still contains no open subset of C^N when k<N. Discrete support information
must be counted if it is hidden across a cut, but the dense rank theorem cannot
be applied unchanged to that union.

3b. Unknown sparse Boolean support

For exactly k ones there are S=binomial(N,k) legal inputs. Repeating the same
packing proof gives
  binomial(N,k) <= Q^(2w),
  w >= log_2(binomial(N,k))/(2 log_2 Q).
For at most k ones, replace S by sum_{j=0}^k binomial(N,j). The separation
argument still applies. For polynomial M,L and k<=N/2 this implies
  w=Omega(k log(N/k)/log N).
At k=Theta(log N), this is only Omega(log N), compatible with k states.
For one FIXED support and Boolean amplitudes there are only 2^k inputs, so
the corresponding counting lower bound is weaker still.

This corollary is an elementary application of the supplied packing argument,
not a literature-wide novelty claim. It prevents an incorrect synthesis in
which a dense-input barrier is used to reject our sparse recurrence or modal
state construction.

3c. Selected Fourier measurements of k-hot signals

A useful specialization connects this audit to the Galois/sampling package.
Suppose the encoder is r fixed unnormalized Fourier bins of a binary signal
with exactly k ones. There are B=binomial(N,k) inputs, and every measured
coordinate has modulus at most k. Two distinct such inputs differ in at
least two coordinates, so Parseval gives full-output maximum separation at
least sqrt(2).

Let a=floor(log_2(B)/(2r)) and M_grid=2^a-1, when a>=1. Partition each real
and imaginary component of the r bins into M_grid intervals across [-k,k].
There are M_grid^(2r)<B cells, so two inputs have measurement max-distance
at most 2 sqrt(2) k/M_grid. Every measurement-only exact full-output decoder
therefore has
  L >= M_grid/(2k).
If the measurements collide exactly, no such exact decoder exists and the
conditioning obstruction is stronger. This covers any choice of r fixed bins;
it does not assume that the bins are a Galois generating set or independent.

For k=Theta(log N), log B=Theta(log^2 N). A polynomial-in-N decoder L requires
r=Omega(log N). A constant-bin sketch may still be injective in exact
arithmetic, but injectivity does not supply a stable low-precision decoder.

If every real/imaginary measurement is stored on a step-2^(-b) fixed-point
grid over [-k,k], there are at most ((2k+2)2^b)^(2r) distinct stored states
for b>=0. Exact reconstruction requires
  b >= log_2(B)/(2r) - log_2(2k+2).
Here b denotes fractional grid bits, not an unspecified floating-point
mantissa. With arbitrary storage of at most b_total bits per real component,
the simpler counting necessity is 2r*b_total>=log_2(B). Thus one complex bin
needs Omega(log^2 N) total distinguishable-value bits in this regime.

These are measurement-only reconstruction requirements, not runtime lower
bounds for a full sparse expansion that still retains its original support
and amplitudes. Its support already contains Theta(k log(N/k)) information.

3d. Decoded fusion and conditional thin multiplication

J_Q=-T_(1/Q) U_Q remains a valid length-k recurrence extension. The new rank
and packing conditions do not add a terminal decoder, disprove the fusion,
or raise its existing O(N log k) bound. The unresolved step remains a cheaper
TOTAL-cost application to the jointly constrained family of states.

The baby-step/giant-step product U[r,s]=z_s^r and
W[s,b]=a_s(z_s^n)^b with n=ceil(sqrt N) remains the strongest concrete
conditional shortcut. A uniform total-O(n^2) n-by-log n times log n-by-n
algorithm would give O(N) sparse expansion after O(k sqrt N+k log N) setup.
All output entries depend on only k amplitudes; their count is not a rank-N
obstruction. The supplied package neither verifies nor refutes the missing
total-operation construction in the Brockett--Dobkin/Chan source audit.

Even a proved O(N) k=Theta(log N) sparse expansion does not automatically give
a dense O(N) FFT. Splitting a dense input into N/k sparse groups and fully
expanding each by that black box costs O(N^2/k), plus aggregation. A new
composition identity or cheaper shared batching is needed to bridge that gap.

4. PACKED DFT: ALGEBRA AND IMPLEMENTATION

For even N and primitive 2N-th root zeta, d_j=zeta^(j^2) and h_j=d_j^(-1)
satisfy h_(j+N)=h_j. Consequently
  d_k sum_j h_(k-j) d_j x_j = sum_j x_j zeta^(2kj).
The implemented positive-exponent DFT convention matches its two references.
N=1, accepted by the code, is a separate trivial identity case.

Canonical lifts 0<=a_j,h_j<=p-1 have integer convolution coefficients at most
N(p-1)^2. Choosing a byte-aligned base B=2^s strictly larger than that bound
means integer multiplication contains the convolution coefficients without
interdigit carries. The extraction length 2N cells is ample; only 2N-1
coefficients are nonzero. Folding c_j+c_(j+N) produces the cyclic convolution,
then multiplication by d_j produces the DFT. The phase recurrence correctly
generates consecutive square exponents by successive odd-exponent increments.

The code supports power-of-two lengths only. In a prime field the tested
conditions zeta^N=-1 and zeta^(2N)=1 suffice to establish exact order 2N for
these lengths. They would not suffice for arbitrary N without extra order
checks. The routines assume p is prime; they do not prove primality. The
general even-N identity and all-length rank/packing theorems should not be
mistaken for an all-N tested implementation.

PackedCost.operand_bits=N*cell_bits is an allocated/worst-case width, not the
actual bit length of both operands on every input. The structure separately
records actual bit lengths, correctly allowing the all-zero input operand.

The large integer multiplication costs M_int(Theta(N(log N+log p))) bit work,
with additional packing, extraction, and modular field arithmetic. For a
prime field supporting a primitive 2N-th root, p>=2N+1; explicit N fixed-width
field outputs require Omega(N log N) bits. This is an output-format bit lower
bound, not a complex-arithmetic or word-RAM N log N operation lower bound.

In particular, this does NOT imply an Omega(n log n) lower bound for multiplying
two n-bit integers. In a transform reduction with N=Theta(n/log n) digits and
Theta(log n)-bit field entries, the explicit transform output size is
Theta(N log N)=Theta(n). Even computing that full transform does not exceed
linear-in-n output size. A general integer-multiplication algorithm need not
materialize that particular transform at all. The package cannot rule out
O(n) integer multiplication or convert a transform-length bound into an
input-bit-length bound without this parameter translation.

"One multiplication" refers only to one GIANT convolution multiplication;
phase generation, input/output scaling, and modular arithmetic contain many
other multiplications. This deliberate control does not establish a one-gate
DFT. Initial modular exponentiation/inversion also adds O(log p) modular
multiplications in a straightforward implementation, so uniform variable-p
phase setup is more precisely O(N+log p), not unconditionally O(N) when p may
grow arbitrarily fast. Prime/root search and certification are separate input
assumptions. These refinements strengthen the package's own cost warning.

5. TEST COVERAGE AND MINOR HARDENING POINTS

The static test inventory includes 126 basis-vector inputs for N=2..64,
random signed field vectors through N=2048, direct-reference random cases,
three additional primes plus the default prime, zero/constant vectors,
exact block encoding enumeration, and finite-field rank sanity checks.
The captured benchmark has five samples at each N=64,256,1024,4096,16384.
It is internally consistent with the byte-cell thresholds 72 and 80 bits.
The captured timings are educational Python comparisons and provide no
asymptotic superiority, native-performance, or stability evidence.

No correctness defect was found within the documented valid-prime,
power-of-two, integer/Boolean input contract. Optional hardening:
- Explicit tests for N=1, invalid root, unsupported root order and field
  assumptions would improve boundary coverage.
- block_digits accepts an empty sequence despite its error text saying
  "nonzero signal length" and converts entries through int(), so floats
  such as 0.5 are silently truncated. Validate the exact Boolean contract
  if this becomes a user-facing API.
- The final information-count test checks (w-1)*b<n+b; the sharper intended
  minimal-capacity assertion is (w-1)*b<n. The current weaker assertion does
  not invalidate the theorem but tests less than the accompanying comment.
- audit.py's displayed sensitivity bounds use floating-point exponentiation.
  They are illustrative numerical values; the exact symbolic inequality and
  integer grid_count routine carry the rigorous statement.

6. RECOMMENDED MERGE INTO THE RESEARCH PROGRAM

Keep this package as a candidate-screening and cost-accounting appendix.
Do not count it as an independent fast-FFT engine or combine its benchmark
with sparse recurrence timings to claim a speedup.

For every candidate first specify the input family and its actual dimension
or entropy; then specify the exact arithmetic/bit/word/accuracy model. Apply
the relevant width barrier only to a claimed information cut. Charge every
remaining decoding, convolution, coefficient setup and output materialization
operation. No rank or descriptor-length statement supplies that cost.

The highest-value constructive next step remains the existing total-addition
and construction audit for thin matrix multiplication. In parallel, the
explicit fused primitive/transposed remainder is a legitimate target for a
new jointly batched circuit. For a truly dense fresh complex FFT, demand an
actual wide evaluator and a complete scalable operation recurrence. None of
the newly uploaded global-bottleneck material supplies that evaluator yet.
