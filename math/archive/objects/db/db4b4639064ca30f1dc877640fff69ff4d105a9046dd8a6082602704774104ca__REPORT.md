All 24 are new full-static reads relative to the frozen inventory, the additive registry and the other coverage packages checked at completion. Name matches and role-only coverage do not count. ## Most consequential findings

1. **Direct test entrypoints omit substantial coverage.** `test_phase15.py` (a97cdc4f… line 79) calls `unittest.main()` before the final three methods are defined: direct invocation collects seven, discovery can collect ten. `test_phase13.py` (1df4056a… line 81) likewise collects seven directly versus twelve under discovery, omitting the modular Haar, covariance, phase/permutation, and strong-coupling controls. Phase15 `validate.py` explicitly uses discovery, so it avoids these entrypoint omissions.
2. **Test count is not pass count.** `validate.py` (904a6846… lines 8–12) accepts zero exit status plus `Ran N tests`, then labels all N passed. Unittest skipped/expected-failure outcomes require separate accounting. Two phase12 methods explicitly skip without NumPy. If NumPy is absent everywhere, the later required `unitarity_probe.py` fails, so missing NumPy alone does not yield a completed successful validation; this does not repair the suite parser’s insufficient acceptance rule. Probe success labels and many invariants also depend on assertions not being optimized away.
3. **A callable helper can fail to terminate.** `random_row_sparsity_audit(N,s,t)` (53fca373… lines 21–35), for positive N and s>N, requests more distinct labels than exist. Its sampling loop never terminates. This is a direct static argument; the failure was not executed.
4. **Folding does not reduce FFT work.** The phase15 executor and complexity calculator explicitly sum the base, fold and orbit work to N log₂N / 2 butterflies. The R frequency mixer is linear-time, but the full pipeline includes the B-FFT bank and orbit FFTs. Dense verification routines and reference oracles have additional quadratic/cubic costs. This implementation supplies no asymptotic FFT speedup.
5. **Certificate generators are not automatically scalable.** The Green-sum determinant expansion enumerates (q+1)! permutations. Dense rank certificates cost cubic work in minor dimension. Product escape explicitly builds dense matrices despite returning a separate O(N) factored-application description.
6. **Preserve the exact mathematical quantifiers.** Additive separated-path Green cut barriers do not prohibit products of Green sums. The covariance rank/2 bound is for an additive correction to one fixed stage. The diagonal-multiset two-stage veto quantifies over any intermediate invertible diagonal and permutation, with endpoint stages fixed. Matching diagonal multisets are only a necessary condition. None is a general FFT lower bound.
7. **Guard and parameter problems remain.** Several public helpers lack positive-size, shape, prime, root-order, or canonical-cycle validation. `audit.tree_exact_root_leaf(depth,alpha)` uses a comparator derived for alpha=2 regardless of its argument. `phase_permutation_gate.diag_modulation_conjugate` does not check n divides MOD−1. `lp_gate` uses ordinary floating log/ceil plus a fudge for integer stage lower bounds, not certified interval rounding.
8. **Packaging logic is not a preservation receipt.** The phase15/17 packagers hash files before later reads, allowing a stale-manifest race. Phase17 only verifies CRC and member names; phase15 compares archived bytes to current files but not to the archived manifest. Historical archive identities are not pinned. This review therefore provides its own source snapshot hashes and a post-review preservation receipt.

## Mathematical conclusions supported by static review

- Chirp Hankel factorization into diagonal factors and a Vandermonde matrix is sound within the stated even-size/root setting. A rank lower bound on sums of first-order cyclic resolvents additionally requires the specified no-wrap exponential-sequence model; the source does not implement a general resolvent classifier.
- A separated cross-cut of an inverse path-tridiagonal has rank at most one; q additive terms give rank at most q. Iterative two-color median separation retains at least floor(N/2^q) labels of each color in arbitrary q path orders. The normalized Fourier submatrix has Frobenius norm squared |R||C|/N and operator norm at most one, hence rank at least |R||C|/N over the complex numbers. This supports the additive obstruction when m²>qN. Numerical random coefficients are regression controls, not the source of universal quantification.
- Exact Fourier-minor polynomials reduced modulo X^(N/2)+1 are appropriate for power-of-two N. Prime-length nonvanishing uses the cyclotomic relation together with the determinant coefficient-sum condition. Composite Fourier minors can vanish, which the tests explicitly acknowledge. A nonzero modular minor is useful algebraic evidence only with the stated compatible root/reduction; unlucky reductions can lower modular rank.
- The dyadic fold/inverse butterfly formulas are algebraically consistent. Canonical orbit/groups and valid dyadic parameters remain part of the contract. Small direct DFT references are stronger algorithmically independent controls than larger comparisons that reuse `fft_radix2` and its phase tables.
- Rank(SZ−DS)≤2 rank(E) whenever S+E has Fourier covariance, yielding the stated finite-field correction bound. For a complex interpretation, consistent algebraic reduction and nonzero denominators must be established. Finite-field transpose/inverse identities do not by themselves certify complex unitarity.
- The diagonal-multiset invariant follows by conjugating the covariance equation through S2 Q P S1. It avoids factorial intermediate-permutation search, but it does not test arbitrary endpoints or more stages.
- The sparse-inverse support lower bound is valid for the explicitly stated product-of-sparse-inverses/monomial architecture. Sums and nonsparse inverses fall outside it. Strong Haar coupling similarly falls outside the fixed phase12 norm/perturbation hypotheses.
