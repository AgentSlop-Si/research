# Proposed universal two inverse branch obstruction

Status: root analytical draft, requires independent proof review and prior-art assessment. No FFT speedup or novelty claim.

Let N>=34, let pi,sigma be arbitrary permutations, and let Q0=D0+E0 P_pi and Q1=D1+E1 P_sigma be invertible over a field. Allow arbitrary diagonal multipliers on both sides of each inverse. The union of the two permutation digraphs has indegree and outdegree two (loops and parallel arcs counted). Its underlying undirected multigraph is four-regular.

Claim: there exists nonempty S with |S|<=N/2 and at most |S|-1 union arcs leaving S. Consequently any sum of these two diagonal-scaled inverses has rank at most |S|-1 in block [S complement,S]. Any target with full column rank for every such block is excluded.

Graph argument: if a component has size<=N/2 take it. Otherwise work in a component of size M>N/2. If an induced radius-r ball has at most one non-tree edge, four-regular breadth-first growth gives at least 3^r vertices. Worst case puts the sole cycle as a root loop, reducing root children from four to two; every remaining non-root tree vertex has three children before the boundary. More generally the sole surplus edge removes at most two child incidences, and moving these removals toward the root minimizes the count. Take r=floor(log_3 N)+1 so 3^r>N. Therefore a ball contains two non-tree edges. Their endpoints' four root paths plus those edges form a connected bicyclic subgraph with at most 4r+1 vertices. Its induced vertex set S has at least |S|+1 internal arcs, so at most 2|S|-(|S|+1)=|S|-1 outgoing arcs. For N>=34 the bound 4(floor(log_3 N)+1)+1<=N/2 must be checked carefully: it holds N34..80; at powers81,243,... radius jumps but inequality remains. Thus S is legal.

For any invertible Q, rank Q^{-1}[Sc,S]=rank Q[Sc,S], by the kernel bijection v maps to Qv on vectors v and Qv both supported on S. The latter rank is at most the number of permutation arcs leaving S. Rank subadditivity and diagonal scaling finish the obstruction.

For a prime N>=37 in characteristic zero, the Fourier matrix has full-rank such blocks by prime-order Fourier full-spark/uncertainty theorem. Thus no sum of two inverse diagonal-plus-permutation branches, even with arbitrary diagonal endpoint multipliers, equals that Fourier matrix. No first factor mixing the cut is allowed. This does not exclude three branches or multiple non-diagonal layers.

Potential risks requiring review: multigraph loops/parallel edge conventions; BFS expansion inequality with one surplus edge; exact radius/size threshold; incomplete components; orientation of crossing arcs; required Fourier full-spark characteristic assumptions; diagonal endpoint grammar; literature priority of the combined graph/rank application.

## Proposed strengthening, 04:33 UTC, awaiting independent verification

The component split is unnecessary: the same radius-r ball argument works inside every component, including exhausted components. Thus always choose |S|<=B(N)=4(floor(log_3 N)+1)+1.

For any complex unitary matrix U with max entry modulus mu, if |S|mu<1 then U[Sc,S] has full column rank. Otherwise nonzero x supported on S has Ux supported on S. But ||U[S,S]||_2 <= ||U[S,S]||_F <= |S|mu<1, contradicting unitary norm preservation. In particular every normalized Fourier matrix of every length has mu=N^(-1/2). Thus B(N)^2<N suffices for exclusion, without primality.

Claim B(N)^2<N for all N>=842. On 842<=N<2187, r=7 and B=29, so B^2=841<N. At subsequent powers N=3^k (k>=7), r=k+1 and B=4k+5. The inequality (4k+5)^2<3^k holds for k=7 and propagates because (4k+9)^2<3(4k+5)^2. Between powers B stays constant. Prime N>=37 remains a stronger small-length consequence using prime full spark.

This broadens only the excluded two-inverse-branch grammar. It does not lower-bound arbitrary Fourier arithmetic circuits or exclude more inverse branches or non-diagonal layers. The norm fact is classical coherence/uncertainty; combined-application novelty remains unresolved.
