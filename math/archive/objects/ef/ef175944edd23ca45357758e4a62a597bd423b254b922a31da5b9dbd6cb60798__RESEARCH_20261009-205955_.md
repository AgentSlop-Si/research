## 2. All-length coprime-padded approximate route

For any original length `N`, choose a prime block width

\[
M=\Theta(\log N),
\]

then choose

\[
Q=MK\ge 4N
\]

with no prime below `M` dividing `K`. Consequently,

\[
\gcd(Q,d)=1\qquad (1\le d<M).
\]

The search for such `K` has an elementary inclusion–exclusion upper bound of `N^{o(1)}` trials, so its charged setup is asymptotically below the input scan.

The centered input is Gaussian-preweighted and embedded into length `Q`. A random unit-step chirp projector is evaluated by one length-`K` FFT and one length-`Q` Green scan. Short Gaussian interpolation maps the projected `Q`-grid spectrum back to all `N` target frequencies.

### Sharpened one-row covariance

For every target frequency `xi` and every fixed arbitrary input,

\[
\mathbb E|\Delta_\xi|^2
\le
\frac{Q}{N}\frac{R_G^2(M-1)}{\varphi(Q)}\,\|x\|_2^2,
\]

where `R_G=1+o(1)` bounds the local Gaussian preweight ratio. The improvement comes from:

1. random `beta` killing mixed local displacements;
2. every nonzero displacement below `M` being a unit modulo `Q`;
3. Parseval over the full `Q` character family;
4. each occupied coordinate having only `M-1` in-block partners.

This is a factor `M` smaller than the previous same-padding proof bound. It is an upper bound, not a measured speed ratio.

### Conditional all-output theorem candidate

Fix `0<alpha<1/2`. With a constant odd number of independently sampled rows, coordinatewise real/imaginary medians, and the inherited finite Gaussian interpolation estimate, the branch derives

\[
\Pr\!\left[
 \|\widehat X-U_Nx\|_\infty
 > C_\alpha N^{-\alpha}\|x\|_2
\right]
\le
N^{1-h(1-2\alpha)+o(1)},
\]

where the row count is `2h-1`. For example:

- `alpha=1/3`, seven rows: failure `N^{-1/3+o(1)}`;
- `alpha=1/3`, nine rows: failure `N^{-2/3+o(1)}`.

The charged arithmetic is

\[
\boxed{O_\alpha(N\log\log N)}
\]

and storage is `O_\alpha(N)`, for **every integer length**, including primes.

This statement is conditional on the inherited Gaussian interpolation theorem and its idealized coefficient-access/exact-complex model. It is not a binary64, bit-complexity, or machine-precision FFT theorem.

## 3. Exact complete Green frame

Fix `Q=KM`, a unit `a mod Q`, and

\[
\beta_r=\beta_0+rK,\qquad r=0,\ldots,M-1.
\]

Inside block `b` and local coordinate `t`, let

\[
S_r[b,t]
=
S_0[b,t]\,e^{2\pi i rt/M}.
\]

The associated block projectors satisfy the exact resolution of identity

\[
\frac1M\sum_{r=0}^{M-1}P_r=I_Q.
\]

Thus averaging all `M` projected spectra recovers the exact length-`Q` DFT of every input.

The inherited implementation batched all block summaries in `O(Q log M)`, but then executed `M` separate length-`K` FFTs and `M` separate length-`Q` Green scans. The explicit question was whether those scans could be jointly evaluated below `QM` work.

## 4. New exact joint-decoder identity

Write

\[
x_{b,t}=x[bM+t]
\]

and factor the base phase as

\[
S_0[b,t]=c_t e^{2\pi i abt/K}.
\]

The batched summary is

\[
D_r[b]
=
\sum_{t=0}^{M-1}c_t x_{b,t}
 e^{2\pi i abt/K}
 e^{2\pi i rt/M}.
\]

Take a length-`K` DFT in `b`:

\[
F_r[p]
=
\sum_{t=0}^{M-1}
 c_t e^{2\pi i rt/M}
 H_t[p-at],
\]

where

\[
H_t[q]=\sum_{b=0}^{K-1}x_{b,t}e^{-2\pi i qb/K}
\]

is the length-`K` DFT of polyphase column `t`.

Now take a length-`M` DFT across `r`:

\[
\frac1M\sum_{r=0}^{M-1}F_r[p]e^{-2\pi i rt/M}
=c_t H_t[p-at].
\]

After a diagonal dechirp and cyclic reindexing, all `H_t[q]` are recovered. The final exact transform follows from

\[
X[q+Ks]
=
\sum_{t=0}^{M-1}
H_t[q]e^{-2\pi i qt/Q}e^{-2\pi i st/M}.
\]

Therefore the complete-frame joint evaluator costs

\[
\boxed{\Theta(Q\log K+Q\log M)=\Theta(Q\log Q)}.
\]

## 5. Stronger closure: the Green frame cancels to Cooley–Tukey

The input batching applies an `M`-point inverse DFT in every block. The channel demixing applies the inverse operation after length-`K` transforms. Because transforms acting on the two tensor coordinates commute, those two `M` transforms cancel algebraically.

After cancellation, the program is exactly:

1. `M` length-`K` DFTs of the polyphase columns;
2. diagonal twiddle multiplication;
3. `K` length-`M` DFTs.

That is the ordinary mixed-radix Cooley–Tukey factorization.

Hence the exact complete-frame gate has a definitive answer:

> The `M` Green scans can be jointly eliminated, but the surviving joint program is exactly a standard `Theta(Q log Q)` FFT, not an exact sub-`Q log Q` algorithm.

This is not a lower bound against every possible Green/nonunitary construction. It closes this particular complete-beta-frame family.

## 7. Numerical checks

The complex implementation verifies:

- batched summaries versus individually formed Green summaries;
- joint decoder versus a reference unitary FFT;
- joint decoder versus the algebraically collapsed mixed-radix program;
- joint decoder versus literal averaging of all Green scans at small sizes.

Across the saved benchmark cases through `Q=2057`, maximum joint/reference discrepancy was below `2.5e-15`.

The joint Python code is much slower than NumPy's native FFT and the collapsed implementation remains slower because it is written as Python loops. These timings demonstrate correctness and the expected removal of literal Green scans, not practical superiority.

## 9. Honest boundary and next gate

### Established within the stated model

1. A cold-reproduced, conditional all-length approximate route with `O(N log log N)` ideal arithmetic and `N^{-alpha}||x||_2` all-bin additive error for any fixed `alpha<1/2`.
2. An exact joint evaluation of the complete Green frame in `Theta(Q log Q)` work.
3. An algebraic proof that this exact joint frame is ordinary mixed-radix Cooley–Tukey in disguise.

### Not established

- exact `o(N log N)` or exact `O(N)` FFT;
- fixed-precision or bit-complexity guarantee;
- coefficientwise relative accuracy;
- wall-clock advantage over FFTW/NumPy;
- scholarly novelty or independent peer review.

### Next non-repetitive research gate

The complete rank-`M` local frame reconstructs every input but collapses to the ordinary FFT. Fewer than `M` fixed measurements per full block have a nonzero common kernel and cannot be exact for every input. A real exact advance therefore needs a different source of cross-block information or a globally coupled evaluator that crosses this local-rank boundary without reproducing the standard tensor FFT.

For the approximate route, the next practical/theoretical work is a certified finite-precision implementation and a native fused kernel for the Green row plus Gaussian resampler.
