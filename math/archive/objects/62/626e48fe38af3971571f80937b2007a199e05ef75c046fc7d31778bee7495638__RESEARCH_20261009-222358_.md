# FFT: three-stage double-bit-reversal obstruction and next research gate

**10 October 2026 — reproducible research checkpoint**

## Executive result

A three-stage Fourier factorization

\[
F_{2^k}=S_3P_{\rm bitrev}S_2P_{\rm bitrev}S_1
\]

is **impossible for every `k>=6`** when each stage has upper and lower quasiseparable order at most one, with *completely arbitrary* complex generators, transitions, and diagonals. This includes all zero-generator cases and singular stages. The Fourier endpoints are fixed in natural index order; both internal permutations are the same exact bit reversal.

The contradiction is an explicit scalar communication cut; see `PROOF_CERTIFICATE.md` for the all-length proof. The new proof **strictly strengthens** the previous checkpoint's three-stage window bound for this particular architecture: repeated bit reversal had survived every tested contiguous-window gate, but is ruled out by a full-network cut that tracks joint channel reuse.

This is an architectural impossibility theorem, **not** a general `Omega(N log N)` bound for Fourier transforms and **not** a new fast FFT construction.

## What changed since the previous checkpoint

The old three-tap certificate checked individual source/output windows and measured one middle-stage quasiseparable cut. It was too weak for the repeated-bit-reversal layout because bit reversal makes the support sets strongly interleaved. The new method does not demand one contiguous middle cut; it removes **multiple scalar channels at all three stages jointly**, preserving the exact arbitrary endpoint ordering and the individual transition recurrences.

The key insight was a finite 32-position binary pattern, not a numerical optimization:

- Split `N=32m` into 32 blocks of `m` positions or `m` blocks of 32 positions as appropriate.
- Bit reversal maps the first 13 `m`-blocks to residues `{0,2,4,6,8,10,12,16,18,20,24,26,28}` in each 32-block.
- Remove only three troublesome input residues per 32-block and one forward and inter-block backward channel.
- On the next bit reversal, all surviving influences occupy 21 `m`-blocks, of which exactly seven are at or beyond the target boundary.
- Cut those seven groups and the final forward channel.

This costs only `12m+1` scalar wires while the selected Fourier minor has rank `13m`. For `m>=2` the inequality is strict.

## What did NOT work (don't repeat it)

1. The previous **single-window** three-stage rank gate missed all tested bitrev/bitrev layouts. The all-network cut succeeds.
2. Earlier **random uniform subset** tests at lengths 8, 16, and 32 often showed full min-cut capacity. This was misleading about the larger-length behavior. An adversarial *specific* input-output corner reveals the obstruction at `N=64`.
3. The entire three-stage result is specific to **rank-one** channels and the fixed pair of internal bit reversals. Applying it to rank-two stages or arbitrary shuffles would be an invalid extrapolation.
4. Three layers of global mixing can have high intermediate cut ranks. They do **not** necessarily supply all Fourier-specific information: the parity-block scan example shows rank alone can be maximal even for an `O(N)` operator.
5. Numerical failures, high Jacobian codimension, full min-cut capacity, or large parameter counts do not prove a specific Fourier factorization impossible. The current result has an actual checkable contradiction.

## Four-stage control (survives this test, not an algorithm)

For the next family

\[
F_N\stackrel{?}{=}S_4P S_3P S_2P S_1,
\]

with the same bit reversal repeated three times and rank-one QS stages, the `13m`-window full-network min-cut is equal to `13m` at `N=32,64,128,256,512` (no rank contradiction). An additional **8,000** seeded general-subset cut queries at `N=64` and **4,000** at `N=128` also found no rank violation. These are exploratory negative results, not an existence proof.

This is the next research problem: discover a **different adversarial family of cuts**, possibly crossing more stages, or a Fourier-specific nonlinear syzygy that the channel-rank test cannot detect. Always include true operation counts and arbitrary-input exactness checks before claiming a faster FFT.

## Additional exact results this round

### Maximum cut rank from a cheap parity-shuffled scan

The homogeneous stage with below/diagonal/above values `1/3/2` can be evaluated using two scans, yet after any permutation that puts even original indices before odd indices it has maximum **possible** upper-right and lower-left rank at every cut. This holds for every even `N` and comes from explicit nonzero partner minors of determinant `2` and `(-1)^(k-1)`. This settles why merely observing globally full cut ranks is not evidence of Fourier-like arithmetic complexity.

### Exact coefficient-amplitude tradeoff for separated-generator stages

A `3N × 3N` sparse determinant lifting gives `|det S| <= 3^(3N/2) B^(2N)` when the five generator families are bounded in magnitude by `B>=1`. Therefore a `t`-stage exact factorization of **unnormalized** `F_N` using this narrower scan chart and arbitrary permutations requires

\[
\prod_{i=1}^t B_i\ge3^{-3t/4}N^{1/4}.
\]

This is a determinant/scale restriction, not a robust lower bound for exact unrestricted-coefficient FFTs. It is related to Morgenstern's classical bounded-coefficient determinant method.

### Fourier compound minors for the next nonlinear branch

The number of vanishing `2×2` minors of a `2^k`-length Fourier matrix is exactly

\[
Z_N=N^3(k-2)/8+N^2/4.
\]

The verifier checks the identity at sizes 4, 8, 16, 32. A separate exact test produces the Cauchy–Binet expression for the exterior-square constraints of a **branch-complete** three-stage algebraic feasibility system. The inverse-stage formulation preserves every zero-generator branch, though the uniform rank-cut theorem makes solving this system unnecessary for the now-excluded bitrev/bitrev, three-stage architecture.

## Scientific limits

No `O(N)` FFT, no stable floating-point speedup, and no new unrestricted integer-multiplication exponent has been established in this project. The OpenAI math repository currently reports a separate, much more general exact-complex-arithmetic power-saving DFT result, but **our** contribution here is an explicit obstruction for a particular scan architecture; we did not reimplement or improve that published algorithm.

## Next steps

1. **Four-stage repeated bit reversal:** search for a parametric min-cut or Fourier-specific polynomial compatibility identity, with exact positive controls for known circuits.
2. **Alternate permutations:** use the full-network cut search, then derive a short symbolic channel-cut certificate rather than relying on a single finite-size optimizer result.
3. **Higher order QS stages:** adapt the cut bound to a state width `r`; the exhibited 3-stage cut costs `10m+r_1+(2m-1)r_2+r_3`, so a different pattern is necessary at rank 2.
4. **Implementation comparison:** any successful construction must be charged for all additions, multiplications, coefficient generation, and indexing. Caching previously seen inputs, threads, changing operands, and merely relabeling macro-stages do not meet the target.
