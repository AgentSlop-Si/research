# Unit-step binomial-chirp Fourier sketch

**Checkpoint:** 9 October 2026  
**Version:** v9  
**Status:** Reproducible theorem candidate, independent algebraic derivation, exact small-state enumeration, numerical recurrence verification, native C++/FFTW implementation, and automated tests. Not independently peer reviewed.

## 1. Executive result

The v7/v8 global-chirp row used the local quadratic phase `a t^2`. Its first difference is `2at+a`, so the exact output decoder advances by `2a`. For dyadic lengths this forced two output cycles and the sharp one-output covariance norm was `2M/N`.

Replace that phase by the integer-valued binomial polynomial

\[
B(t)=\binom t2=\frac{t(t-1)}2.
\]

Its first difference is

\[
B(t+1)-B(t)=t.
\]

This apparently small change removes the factor two from both the decoder and the covariance arithmetic.

Let

\[
N=p^L=KM,
\]

where `p` is prime, `K` and `M` are powers of `p`, and `K>=M`. Choose `a` uniformly from the units modulo `N` and `beta` uniformly from `Z_N`. One randomized row:

1. is unbiased;
2. produces all `N` Fourier estimates in

   \[
   O(N+K\log K)
   \]

   arithmetic operations;
3. has one recurrence cycle for **every** prime power, including dyadic lengths;
4. needs no separately evaluated recurrence seed—the balanced scan total closes the cycle;
5. has, for every output frequency, the exact covariance identity

   \[
   \boxed{
   Q^{(m)}=\frac{M}{N}\Pi_m,
   }
   \]

   where `Pi_m` is an orthogonal projector of rank `N-K`.

Consequently,

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{\|x\|_2=1}
\mathbb E|\widehat X_m-X_m|^2
=\frac MN.
}
\]

For powers of two this halves the sharp v7/v8 constant from `2M/N` to `M/N`, reduces two scans to one, and relaxes the structural condition from `K>=2M` to `K>=M`.

When `M=Theta(log N)` as a nearby power of `p`, one complete approximate row is `O(N)`.

## 2. Randomized block projection with parity twist

Write the input in contiguous blocks

\[
x_{r,t}=x_{rM+t},
\qquad 0\le r<K,
\quad 0\le t<M,
\]

and let

\[
\zeta=e^{2\pi i/N},
\qquad
\eta=e^{\pi i/N},
\qquad
h=N\bmod 2.
\]

Choose independently

\[
a\sim \operatorname{Unif}(\mathbb Z_N^*),
\qquad
\beta\sim\operatorname{Unif}(\mathbb Z_N).
\]

Define

\[
S_{r,t}
=
\zeta^{aB(t)+(aMr+\beta)t}\eta^{ht},
\tag{2.1}
\]

one summary per block

\[
d_r=\sum_{t=0}^{M-1}S_{r,t}x_{r,t},
\tag{2.2}
\]

and the implicit rank-one reconstruction

\[
y_{r,t}=\overline{S_{r,t}}d_r.
\tag{2.3}
\]

One row estimates the unitary DFT by

\[
\widehat X=U_Ny.
\tag{2.4}
\]

The projected vector `y` need not be materialized.  For even `N`, `h=0` and this is the original unit-step phase.  For odd `N`, the deterministic factor `eta^t` is a half-step linear phase.  It does not affect the covariance spectrum, but it forces the cycle monodromy to be `-1`, eliminating the direct seed for odd lengths as well.

### Unbiasedness

For fixed `r,t,u`,

\[
\overline{S_{r,t}}S_{r,u}
=
\zeta^{a(B(u)-B(t))+aMr(u-t)+\beta(u-t)}
\eta^{h(u-t)}.
\]

Averaging over uniform `beta` kills every term with `u!=t`. Therefore

\[
\mathbb E[y]=x
\]

and

\[
\boxed{\mathbb E[\widehat X_m]=X_m.}
\tag{2.5}
\]

## 3. Exact one-cycle, zero-direct-seed decoder

Define

\[
P_n
=
\sum_{t=0}^{M-1}
\zeta^{-aB(t)-nt}\eta^{-ht}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Using `B(t+1)=B(t)+t`, shift the index by one:

\[
\boxed{
P_n
=1+\zeta^{-n}\eta^{-h}P_{n+a}
-\zeta^{-nM-aB(M)}\eta^{-hM}.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

Let

\[
D_j=\sum_{r=0}^{K-1}d_r e^{-2\pi i jr/K}
\]

be the unnormalized length-`K` DFT of the summaries. The output representation and recurrence give

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\widehat X_m
={}&\frac{D_{m\bmod K}}{\sqrt N}
+\zeta^{-(m+\beta)}\eta^{-h}\widehat X_{m+a}\\
&-\zeta^{-[M(m+\beta)+aB(M)]}\eta^{-hM}
\frac{D_{(m+aM)\bmod K}}{\sqrt N}.
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

Because `a` is a unit modulo `N`, the map

\[
m\mapsto m+a\pmod N
\]

is one cycle of length `N` for every modulus.

### Cycle closure removes the seed

Enumerate the cycle by

\[
m_j=aj\pmod N.
\]

The forward multiplier is

\[
\zeta^{m_j+\beta}\eta^h.
\]

Its cumulative gauge is

\[
A_j
=\zeta^{aB(j)+\beta j}\eta^{hj}.
\tag{3.4}
\]

For even `N`, a unit `a` is odd and `zeta^{aB(N)}=-1`; for odd `N`, `zeta^{aB(N)}=1` and `eta^N=-1`. Hence in both cases

\[
\boxed{A_N=-1.}
\tag{3.5}
\]

Writing the recurrence as

\[
\widehat X_{m_{j+1}}
=\zeta^{m_j+\beta}\eta^h
\left(\widehat X_{m_j}-F_{m_j}\right)
\]

and setting

\[
Z_j=\widehat X_{m_j}/A_j,
\qquad
q_j=F_{m_j}/A_j,
\]

gives

\[
\boxed{Z_{j+1}=Z_j-q_j.}
\tag{3.6}
\]

After one complete cycle,

\[
Z_N=-Z_0=Z_0-\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}q_j,
\]

so

\[
\boxed{
\widehat X_0=Z_0=\frac12\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}q_j.
}
\tag{3.7}
\]

Thus the scan total determines the seed; no separate direct `O(N)` seed evaluation is required.

### Charged row work

- summaries: `O(N)`;
- one `K`-point FFT: `O(K log K)`;
- forcing/gauge construction: `O(N)`;
- one balanced scan, whose total also supplies the seed: `O(N)`;
- explicit output writes: `O(N)`.

Therefore

\[
\boxed{T_{\rm row}(N,M)=O(N+K\log K).}
\tag{3.8}
\]

If `M=Omega(log K)`, the row cost is `O(N)`.

## 4. Balanced gauge scan

Equation (3.6) shows that the whole output is an exclusive prefix sum followed by gauge multiplication:

\[
\widehat X_{m_j}
=A_j\left[
\frac12\sum_{q=0}^{N-1}q_q
-
\sum_{q<j}q_q
\right].
\tag{4.1}
\]

A Blelloch up-sweep/down-sweep evaluates all prefixes using `O(N)` additions and `O(log N)` arithmetic depth. The root of the up-sweep is already the total needed in (3.7), so seed recovery adds no asymptotic work and essentially no extra scan traffic.

This is structurally stronger than v8: one cycle, one scan, and zero direct seeds for every prime-power length.

## 5. Exact covariance: a scaled orthogonal projector

Fix an output frequency `m` and a source coordinate

\[
n=rM+u.
\]

For displacement

\[
d=u-t\ne0,
\]

we use

\[
B(u)-B(u-d)=du-\frac{d(d+1)}2.
\]

The error coefficient multiplying `x_n` is

\[
h_n
=\frac{\zeta^{-mn}}{\sqrt N}
\sum_{d\in D_u}
\zeta^{a[dn-d(d+1)/2]+(\beta+m)d}\eta^{hd},
\tag{5.1}
\]

where

\[
D_u=\{u-t:0\le t<M,\ t\ne u\}.
\]

The parity twist contributes the deterministic factor `eta^(h d)` to each displacement term. Averaging over `beta` forces the same displacement in both copies, so that factor cancels from the covariance. Therefore

\[
Q^{(m)}_{n,n'}
=\frac{\zeta^{m(n-n')}}N
\sum_{d\in D_u\cap D_{u'}}
\mathbb E_{a\in\mathbb Z_N^*}
\zeta^{ad(n'-n)}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

### Prime-power unit-character identity

For `N=p^L`, the normalized Ramanujan sum is

\[
\mathbb E_{a\in\mathbb Z_N^*}\zeta^{ac}
=
\begin{cases}
1,&N\mid c,\\
-1/(p-1),&N/p\mid c\text{ but }N\nmid c,\\
0,&N/p\nmid c.
\end{cases}
\tag{5.3}
\]

Write

\[
d=p^v q,
\qquad p\nmid q,
\qquad 0\le v<\log_p M.
\]

A surviving term forces

\[
n'-n\equiv0\pmod{N/p^{v+1}}.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Because `K>=M`, the modulus in (5.4) is divisible by `M`; hence `u'=u`. The covariance separates into `M` blocks indexed by the within-block position.

### Fourier projector on the block index

For fixed `u,v`, let

\[
c_{u,v}
=
\#\{d\in D_u:\nu_p(|d|)=v\}.
\]

On the block index `r in Z_K`, one displacement contributes the circulant kernel

\[
\frac{p}{p-1}\mathbf 1_{H_v}
-
\frac1{p-1}\mathbf 1_{H_{v+1}},
\tag{5.5}
\]

where `H_j` is the subgroup of multiples of `K/p^j`, of size `p^j`. Its Fourier eigenvalue is

\[
\frac{p^{v+1}}{p-1}
\left[
\mathbf 1_{p^v\mid\ell}
-
\mathbf 1_{p^{v+1}\mid\ell}
\right].
\tag{5.6}
\]

Thus it is a positive multiple of the projector onto modes

\[
\nu_p(\ell)=v.
\]

Projectors from different `v` are orthogonal.

### Exact displacement count

The number of integers in `1,...,L` having valuation `v` is

\[
\left\lfloor\frac{L}{p^v}\right\rfloor
-
\left\lfloor\frac{L}{p^{v+1}}\right\rfloor.
\]

Since the two prefix lengths in `D_u` add to `M-1` and `M` is divisible by `p^{v+1}`, a direct quotient/remainder calculation gives the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
 c_{u,v}=\frac{(p-1)M}{p^{v+1}}
}
\tag{5.7}
\]

for every `u`.

Multiplying (5.6) by (5.7) and the outer factor `1/N` shows that **every nonzero covariance eigenvalue is exactly**

\[
\frac MN.
\]

For each fixed `u`, the active modes are precisely those not divisible by `M`. The zero multiplicity per `u` is `K/M`; over all `M` blocks the full zero multiplicity is `K`.

### Theorem 5.1 — exact projector covariance

For every prime-power length `N=p^L`, every factorization `N=KM` into powers of `p` with `K>=M`, every output `m`, and every input,

\[
\boxed{
Q^{(m)}=\frac MN\Pi_m,
}
\tag{5.8}
\]

where `Pi_m` is an orthogonal projector of rank `N-K`. Consequently,

\[
\boxed{
\|Q^{(m)}\|_{2\to2}=\frac MN,
\qquad
\operatorname{tr}Q^{(m)}=M-1.
}
\tag{5.9}
\]

The constant is sharp. A unit vector supported on local coordinate `u=0` and block Fourier mode `ell=1` attains equality for output zero; modulation gives extremizers for every output.

This completely explains the old total-error identity: the row error has covariance `M/N` on an `(N-K)`-dimensional subspace and zero covariance on a `K`-dimensional subspace.

## 6. Streaming robust aggregation with the improved variance

For one scalar real or imaginary component, set

\[
\sigma^2=\frac MN\|x\|_2^2,
\qquad
\tau=g\sigma,
\qquad g>\sqrt2.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Chebyshev gives a bad-row probability at most `p=1/g^2`.

Use the v8 streaming estimator:

- three scalar grids of width `7 tau`;
- offsets `0,7tau/3,14tau/3`;
- one Boyer--Moore candidate and counter per grid;
- separate states for real and imaginary components.

If a strict majority is within `tau` of the target, the scalar estimate is within `5tau/2`. Therefore the complex estimate is within

\[
\frac{5}{\sqrt2}g\sqrt{\frac MN}\|x\|_2.
\]

Let

\[
D_g=D\!\left(\frac12\middle\|\frac1{g^2}\right).
\]

### Theorem 6.1 — exact-arithmetic streaming result

For `R` independent unit-step chirp rows,

\[
\boxed{
\Pr\left[
\|\widetilde X-U_Nx\|_\infty
>
\frac5{\sqrt2}g\sqrt{\frac MN}\|x\|_2
\right]
\le
2N e^{-R D_g}.
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

The charged resources are

\[
\boxed{
T(N,M,R)=
O\!\left(R\left[N+\frac NM\log\frac NM\right]\right),
\qquad
S(N,M,R)=O(N).
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

Relative to v8, the probability formula is unchanged while the deterministic radius improves by a factor `sqrt(2)`.

### Fixed-row linear-time regime

For `R=2s-1` fixed, `M=Theta(log N)`, and constant failure probability,

\[
\boxed{T(N)=O(N),\qquad S(N)=O(N),}
\]

with

\[
\boxed{
\|\widetilde X-U_Nx\|_\infty
=O\!\left(
\sqrt{\log N}\,N^{-1/2+1/(2s)}\|x\|_2
\right).
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

### Growing-row sub-`N log N` regime

Let

\[
L=\log N,
\qquad
\ell=\log\log N,
\qquad
1\le f(N)=o(\ell),
\]

and choose

\[
M=\Theta(L/f(N)),
\qquad
 g=L^\varepsilon,
\qquad
R=\Theta\!\left(
\frac{(1+a)L}{\varepsilon\ell}
\right).
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
T(N)=O\!\left(
\frac{1+a}{\varepsilon}
\frac{f(N)}{\log\log N}
N\log N
\right)
=o(N\log N),
}
\tag{6.5}
\]

memory is `O(N)`, and with probability at least `1-N^{-a}`,

\[
\boxed{
\|\widetilde X-U_Nx\|_\infty
=O\!\left(
\frac{(\log N)^{1/2+\varepsilon}}{\sqrt{f(N)}}
\frac{\|x\|_2}{\sqrt N}
\right).
}
\tag{6.6}
\]

## 7. End-to-end finite-precision bridge

The v8 checkpoint had only a conditional prefix-scan error. The one-cycle algorithm allows a complete normwise row bound under an explicit arithmetic contract.

### Arithmetic contract

Assume:

1. phase exponents are computed exactly with `O(log N)`-bit modular integers;
2. every tabled root of unity has absolute error `O(u)`;
3. complex additions and multiplications obey the standard unit-roundoff model;
4. block summaries use balanced pairwise reductions; the seed is recovered from the balanced scan total;
5. the unnormalized length-`K` FFT satisfies the normwise forward bound

   \[
   \|\widetilde D-D\|_2
   \le
   C_Fu\log K\,\sqrt K\|d\|_2
   \]

   after including its input perturbation;
6. the gauged recurrence is evaluated by a balanced scan;
7. `u log N` is below a fixed small constant.

The constant below depends only on the primitive complex-operation and FFT constants, not on `N,M,x`.

### Theorem 7.1 — finite-precision row error

There is a universal implementation constant `C_fp` such that the computed row satisfies

\[
\boxed{
\|\operatorname{fl}(\widehat X)-\widehat X\|_\infty
\le
C_{\rm fp}
 u\log N\sqrt{MN}\,\|x\|_2.
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

#### Proof accounting

- Exact summaries satisfy `||d||2 <= sqrt(M)||x||2`.
- Pairwise summary errors satisfy

  \[
  \|\delta d\|_2
  =O(u\log M\sqrt M\|x\|_2).
  \]

- The stable `K`-FFT gives

  \[
  \|\delta D\|_2
  =O(u\log N\sqrt N\|x\|_2).
  \]

- Along the unique output cycle every `D_j` is used exactly `M` times, so

  \[
  \sum_m|F_m|
  \le 2\sqrt{MN}\|x\|_2.
  \]

- Perturbations in `D`, roots, forcing terms, and gauge division have total `l1` effect

  \[
  O(u\log N\sqrt{MN}\|x\|_2).
  \]

- The balanced scan contributes the same order because its arithmetic depth is `O(log N)`; its up-sweep total supplies the seed, so there is no separate seed-evaluation error.

### Robust theorem with numerical error

Let

\[
\eta_{\rm fp}
=C_{\rm fp}u\log N\sqrt{MN}\|x\|_2.
\]

Use the three grids with scalar radius

\[
\tau_{\rm total}
=g\sqrt{\frac MN}\|x\|_2+\eta_{\rm fp}.
\]

The probability bound remains `2N exp(-R D_g)`, while the final complex radius becomes

\[
\boxed{
\frac{5}{\sqrt2}
\left[
 g\sqrt{\frac MN}\|x\|_2+\eta_{\rm fp}
\right].
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

To ensure `eta_fp <= theta g sqrt(M/N)||x||2`, it is sufficient that


\[
\boxed{
 u
\le
\frac{\theta g}{C_{\rm fp}N\log N}.
}
\tag{7.3}
\]

Thus a mantissa length

\[
\boxed{
 b
\ge
\log_2N+\log_2\log N-\log_2g+O(1)
}
\tag{7.4}
\]

is sufficient. This is `Theta(log N)` bits in both the fixed-row and growing-row regimes, so the arithmetic fits the intended logarithmic-word model. It does **not** say ordinary fixed binary64 precision works uniformly as `N` tends to infinity.

## 8. Coprime-cofactor extension

The prime-power theorem extends beyond pure prime powers through the classical Good--Thomas/prime-factor factorization.

Let

\[
N=AB,
\qquad
A=p^L,
\qquad
\gcd(A,B)=1.
\]

Up to input and output permutations,

\[
U_N=U_A\otimes U_B.
\]

Compute the exact `B`-point transforms first, then apply the unit-step chirp estimator independently along every length-`A` column. The exact `B`-stage preserves total `l2` energy.

For `R` robust rows, the charged work is

\[
\boxed{
O\!\left(
N\log B
+R\left[N+\frac NM\log\frac AM\right]
\right),
}
\tag{8.1}
\]

with `O(N)` memory and error radius

\[
\boxed{
\frac5{\sqrt2}g\sqrt{\frac MA}\|x\|_2.
}
\tag{8.2}
\]

If `B=N^{o(1)}` and `M=Theta(log A)`, the fixed-row cost is `o(N log N)`. For polylogarithmic `B`, it is `O(N log log N)` plus the linear row work.

This is not yet an all-length theorem: a length with no dominant prime-power factor can still leave a full `Theta(N log N)` cofactor stage.

### Native C++20/FFTW implementation

`native_benchmark.cpp` contains:

- an `O(N)` root table;
- exact modular phase indices using `unsigned __int128`;
- block compression;
- one FFTW length-`K` transform;
- zero direct seeds (the scan total closes the cycle);
- one Blelloch scan;

- no `R x N` allocation.

On the current machine, the unoptimized research implementation remained slower than FFTW:

- one row: about `3.2x`--`11.6x` the FFTW time over the saved size sweep;
- five rows plus streaming aggregation: about `26.0x`--`72.0x` the FFTW time.

At the largest saved case, `N=262144`, the ratios were about `3.65x` and `30.0x`, respectively.

The finite-size approximation error was around one half of the conservative theorem radius in these deterministic runs. This is a negative practical result, not hidden: the current code establishes the mechanism and memory contract, not a library-speed win.

### Exact combinatorial certificate

`projector_certificate.py` independently checks the shell counts, projector ranks, common rational eigenvalue, total rank, and trace using integer and `Fraction` arithmetic. The saved certificate covers 141 prime-power parameter triples and checks every local coordinate for each retained `M`. See `PROJECTOR_CERTIFICATE.md`.

### Direct composite-modulus extension rejected

The decoder remains one-cycle for any modulus when `a` is a unit, but the covariance is not generally `(M/N)` times a projector outside the prime-power setting. Exact normalized-Ramanujan evaluation gives, for example, operator-norm ratios `||Q||/(M/N)` of about `1.58` at `(N,M)=(12,3)`, `2.00` at `(18,3)`, and `2.61` at `(60,5)`. See `COMPOSITE_MODULUS_AUDIT.md` and `composite_modulus_results.json`.

This is a useful negative result: the all-length gap cannot be closed merely by replacing the prime-power Ramanujan identity with its general-modulus formula.

## 10. Prior-art boundary

Known ingredients include:

- quadratic and polynomial-phase chirps;
- integer-valued polynomial bases and finite differences;
- Ramanujan sums over units modulo prime powers;
- work-efficient prefix scans;
- Boyer--Moore majority cancellation;
- robust medians/majorities from finite-variance estimators;
- Good--Thomas coprime FFT factorization;
- normwise FFT roundoff analysis.

A limited search did not identify the exact combination

- phase `a*binom(t,2)` inside contiguous rank-one block projections;
- one-cycle exact full-output recurrence;
- covariance exactly `(M/N)` times a projector of rank `N-K`;
- streaming full-output robust aggregation;
- logarithmic-word finite-precision closure.

That is not a novelty proof. The theorem should be treated as project-originated and unreviewed until independently checked against the randomized linear algebra, chirp sensing, approximate transform, and numerical FFT literature.

## 11. Honest boundary

This checkpoint does **not** establish:

- an exact sub-`N log N` DFT;
- ordinary machine-precision FFT accuracy;
- coefficientwise relative error;
- a theorem for every integer length;
- fixed binary64 stability for asymptotically unbounded `N`;
- practical superiority over FFTW or cuFFT;
- independent peer review or established novelty.

What it does establish, subject to proof review, is stronger than v8:

> For every prime-power length, a fresh arbitrary dense input admits an unbiased, explicit full-output randomized transform row with one-cycle `O(N+K log K)` decoding and exact one-output covariance `(M/N)` times a projector. With streaming robust aggregation this yields the same `O(N)` and `o(N log N)` approximation regimes with a smaller sharp variance constant, and `Theta(log N)` precision is sufficient under an explicit stable-arithmetic model.
