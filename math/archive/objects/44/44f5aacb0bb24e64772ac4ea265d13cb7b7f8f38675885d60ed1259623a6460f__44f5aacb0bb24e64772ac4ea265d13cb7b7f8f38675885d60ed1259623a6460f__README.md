# Four-character Fourier and Green identities

`FOUR_CHARACTER_OPERATOR_IDENTITIES.md` gives explicit operator identities for the square and rectangular dyadic grid layouts and their four-sheet folding map.

The Fourier identity reuses four ordinary size-N transforms with sparse selectors and paid phases. The bank identity exposes four size-b transforms per active old block. In a common Walsh basis, a covering Green operator consists of signed scans of lengths N, N, and 2N, and arbitrary endpoint masks become four-channel XOR convolution. This gives a closed four-state operator representation. A route-specific C4 basis instead gives four scalar Green channels with paid order-dependent gauges and shifted poles. The fixed-pole scalar eight-order family is not closed unchanged.

The identities are reusable algebraic controls. They do not provide Fourier-compatible Green coefficients, a seed bridge, a speedup, or a constant-state recursive algorithm.

## Reproduction

Run the exact finite-field standard-library checker:

    python check_four_character_identities.py

Its output is `FOUR_CHARACTER_CONTROLS.json`, with a short terminal summary. The portable milestone includes three route helper scripts authored in this same constructor track. Only their definitions are imported; their main routines are guarded. No uploaded program is executed.

The controls check full Fourier block entries and reconstruction at six geometries, active bank entries, 48 hierarchical route instances, signed cycle lengths and holonomy, Green intertwining, all four pole gauges, endpoint-mask XOR convolution, and separate physical nonsingularity guards. Finite controls supplement the all-size proofs rather than establish them alone.

The work ledger counts four ordinary F_N transforms; four F_b transforms per active bank block; and, alternatively, 24 mixed-channel signed scans of total length 32N or 32 scalar size-N Green streams per eight-term size-4N layer. Basis changes, phases, masks, and data routing are all charged O(N) per fixed order. Repeated four-state enlargement needs a separate reduction theorem to prevent state growth.
