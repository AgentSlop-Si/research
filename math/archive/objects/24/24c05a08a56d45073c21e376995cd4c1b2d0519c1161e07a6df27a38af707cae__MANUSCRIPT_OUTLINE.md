# Local ordinary discriminants of Rogers Szegő polynomials

Private manuscript plan • 10 October 2026

This is a theorem-centered outline of the accepted results, not a public manuscript or an originality claim. The intended audience is researchers in special functions and algebraic number theory. The proposed organizing question is how a fixed-root q-Lucas degeneration controls an ordinary discriminant and arithmetic at a related cyclotomic order. The original transform motivation is not needed to state the main results.

## 1 Definitions and conventions

Define

    H_n(z;q) = sum_{k=0}^n [n choose k]_q z^k,
    D_n(q) = Disc_z H_n(z;q) in Z[q],
    P_A = product_{j=1}^A j^j,
    Q_d(q) = (q^d−1)/Φ_d(q).

The Gaussian coefficients are integer polynomials. All discriminants are ordinary derivative discriminants, with Disc(f) = product_{i<j}(r_i−r_j)^2 for monic f. All resultants use Res(f,g) = product_{f(α)=0} g(α) when f is monic. Use Disc(1) = Disc(linear monic polynomial) = Res(f,1) = 1; set D_0 = D_1 = 1. Write (a;q)_m = product_{j=0}^{m−1}(1−a q^j). He_A is the monic probabilists' Hermite polynomial, He_0 = 1, He_1 = y, He_(A+1) = y He_A−A He_(A−1).

Fix odd d ≥ 1, a primitive complex dth root ω, A ≥ 1, 0 ≤ b < d, and n = Ad+b. The established q-Lucas identity gives

    H_n(z;ω) = F(z)^A G(z),  F(z) = z^d+1,  G(z) = H_b(z;ω).

Use q(ε) = ω(1+ε²)^(1/d), with constant term ω, so q(ε)^d−1 = ε² exactly. Local identities are formal power-series statements in characteristic zero, not claims about positive orthogonality measures.

Notation repair for the combined paper: use c_d^sep for the separated content and c_d^pair for the overlap paired content. The source packages use c_d for two different invariants. They must never be substituted for one another. For zero remainders put the associated content equal to zero and declare the corresponding sufficient criterion inapplicable.

## 2 Theorem 1 Separated local model and ordinary discriminant

Local coefficient hypotheses: the setup in Section 1 alone. For every α satisfying α^d = −1,

    H_n(α+εx;q(ε))
      = ε^A (−1)^A d^(A/2) G(α) He_A(√d x/α)
        + O(ε^(A+1)).

The apparent square roots cancel coefficientwise. The local coefficient at x^(A−2j) is

    (−1)^(A+j) A! d^(A−j) G(α) α^(−A+2j)
        / (2^j j! (A−2j)!).

For the discriminant conclusion add the hypotheses

    Δ = Disc(G) ≠ 0,  R = Res(F,G) ≠ 0.

Let K = dA(A−1)/2. Then

    D_n(q(ε)) = ε^(2K) (C_sep(ω)+O(ε²)),
    C_sep(ω) = (−1)^(A(d−1)/2)
      d^(dA(A+1)/2) P_A^d Δ R^(2A).

In particular ord_(Φ_d) D_n = K. The integral quotient E_sep = D_n/Φ_d^K lies in Z[q] and satisfies

    E_sep(ω) = Q_d(ω)^K C_sep(ω).

Required proof: derive the formal generating function; split its logarithm into d-divisible and regular degrees; prove that only the linear and quadratic singular terms attain maximal pole weight. Combine their Hermite generating function with the leading coefficient of (q;q)_n. Prove Disc(He_A)=P_A from the derivative and recurrence. Lift the distinct leading roots formally, count all n branches, and multiply within-cluster, cross-cluster, residual, and mixed squared differences. Retain the sign Disc(z^d+1) = (−1)^((d−1)/2)d^d. Evenness in ε gives the stated remainder. Galois conjugacy and successive monic division give the integral quotient.

The local coefficient identity remains true when G(α)=0, but then its displayed coefficient is zero and cannot be used as that cluster's leading model. The two nonvanishing hypotheses are distinct.

## 3 Theorem 2 A simple shared root

Hypotheses: odd d ≥ 3, odd 1 ≤ b < d, A ≥ 1, and the same ω and q(ε). Reciprocity gives

    G(z) = (z+1)U(z),  g = U(−1) = G′(−1),
    Δ_U = Disc(U) ≠ 0,  R_U = Res(z^d+1,U) ≠ 0.

Thus U is squarefree and disjoint from every cluster; R_U ≠ 0 already implies g ≠ 0. Define

    W = (ω;ω)_b / (ω²;ω²)_((b+d)/2),
    τ = dW/g,
    J_(A+1)(y;τ) = y He_A(y)−Aτ He_(A−1)(y)
                 = He_(A+1)(y)+A(1−τ)He_(A−1)(y).

The product denominator is nonzero because (b+d)/2 < d and d is odd. The local coefficient conclusion, before imposing any condition on Disc(J), is

    H_n(−1+εx;q(ε))
      = ε^(A+1)(−1)^(A+1)d^((A−1)/2)g
        J_(A+1)(−√d x;τ) + O(ε^(A+2)).

Its coefficient of ε^(A+1)x^(A+1) is gd^A, as required by F^A(z+1)U. At the other d−1 clusters use Theorem 1's local coefficient with G(α) ≠ 0.

Add the hypothesis S_A(τ) := Disc_y J_(A+1)(y;τ) ≠ 0 for the discriminant conclusion. Set L = dA(A−1)/2+A. Then

    D_n(q(ε)) = ε^(2L)(C_ov(ω)+O(ε²)),
    C_ov(ω) = (−1)^(A(d−1)/2)
      d^(dA(A+1)/2+A) P_A^(d−1)
      S_A(τ) Δ_U R_U^(2A) g².

Consequently ord_(Φ_d) D_n = L, D_n/Φ_d^L belongs to Z[q], and its value at ω is Q_d(ω)^L C_ov(ω).

Required proof beyond Theorem 1: in the regular factor of the generating function, the t^b coefficient has first term εxg/(ω;ω)_b. The t^(b+d) coefficient at z=−1 is 1/(ω²;ω²)_((b+d)/2). The first uses singular index A and the second index A−1; all higher regular degrees contribute only above ε^(A+1). Derive the second coefficient formally from the product of the two q-exponential series at t and −t. In the discriminant product the enlarged cluster adds a factor d^(2A) across clusters and g² against residual roots. Give the complete branch count and sign, not only the exponent.

Positioning: J itself is an established quasi-orthogonal form. Put the exact quasi-Laguerre identification and existing discriminant formula in a short attribution remark, not in a theorem advertised as a new polynomial family. See PRIORITY_GATE.md.

## 4 Lemma 3 The integer cyclotomic bridge

State separately the common algebraic step. Let p be an odd prime, p ∤ d, and suppose an ordinary discriminant polynomial has an exact factorization

    D(q) = Φ_d(q)^e E(q),  E in Z[q],

such that E has nonzero reduction at one primitive-d residue root over F_p. Then D(ζ_(dp)) ≠ 0 for every primitive ζ_(dp).

Proof: if D vanished, irreducibility would give Φ_(dp) | D in Z[q]. Coprimality with Φ_d gives Φ_(dp) | E. The identity Φ_(dp)Φ_d = Φ_d(q^p), reduced and cancelled in F_p[q], gives Φ_(dp) ≡ Φ_d^(p−1). Then E vanishes in every primitive-d residue component, a contradiction. No inequality e < p−1 is required. Never replace the full integer quotient with an undivided discriminant or a truncated p-adic leading term.

Corollary for separated clusters: for odd d ≥ 1, b=(d−1)/2, define

    B_d^sep = rem_(Φ_d)[D_b(q) Res_z(z^d+1,H_b(z;q))],
    c_d^sep = positive coefficient content of B_d^sep when nonzero.

For odd prime p with c_d^sep ≠ 0 and p ∤ d c_d^sep, H_((dp−1)/2)(z;ζ_(dp)) is squarefree. Indeed A=(p−1)/2<p, so P_A is a p-unit. The canonical remainder being nonzero modulo p is equivalent to the existence of one component with both residual guards nonzero. Theorem 1 and Q_d being a unit supply the bridge. For d ≡ 3 modulo 4 the residual polynomial is odd-degree reciprocal, so B_d^sep = 0 and this corollary does not apply.

## 5 Lemmas 4 and 5 Reciprocal parameters and good pair selection

Lemma 4 hypotheses: d=4r+3, b=2r+1, and ω primitive of order d. For g(ω) and v(ω)=dW(ω), the denominator-safe identity is

    g(ω)/v(ω) + g(ω^−1)/v(ω^−1) = 1.

The denominators v are nonzero and are units upon reduction in any characteristic p ∤ d. If both g values are nonzero, then τ=v/g and τ*=v*/g* satisfy 1/τ+1/τ*=1, or τ*=τ/(τ−1).

Proof to retain: put Q=ω², m=2r+1, a_j=(ω;Q)_j/(Q;Q)_j and M=(ω;Q)_m. Establish g=(Q;Q)_r sum_(j=0)^r a_j and v=(Q;Q)_r M. The finite q-binomial identity sums a_j from 0 to m to M; complementary terms pair with the inverted-root half-sum. State this as a specific application of a classical identity.

Lemma 5 hypotheses: p ≡ 3 modulo 4 prime, A=(p−1)/2, τ and τ* nonzero elements of an algebraic closure of F_p satisfying 1/τ+1/τ*=1. Conclusion: at least one J_(A+1)(y;τ), J_(A+1)(y;τ*) is squarefree.

Proof to retain: A is odd and A<p, so J(0;τ)≠0 and He_A is squarefree. A multiple root y would satisfy He_A′/He_A=y/τ and y²=−τ(1+Aτ)/(1−τ). The reciprocal substitution negates this square. If both parameters were bad, use roots y and iy, and U_0=He_A(y), V_0=i^(−A)He_A(iy). The two Hermite differential equations give

    (U_0′V_0−U_0V_0′−yU_0V_0)′ = −(2A+1)U_0V_0 = 0,
    U_0′V_0−U_0V_0′−yU_0V_0 = −y^p.

The logarithmic derivatives and reciprocal identity would make the left side zero, contrary to y≠0. Credit the exact hook-Wronskian antecedent even when retaining this shorter elementary proof. The p ≡ 3 condition belongs to the nonzero-root step; the Wronskian identity alone holds for every odd prime.

## 6 Theorem 6 Paired residue squarefreeness

Hypotheses: d ≡ 3 modulo 4, b=(d−1)/2, p ≡ 3 modulo 4 prime, p ∤ d. Define the monic polynomial U_b(z;q)=H_b(z;q)/(z+1), and

    Γ_d(q) = Disc_z(U_b) Res_z(z^d+1,U_b),
    B_d^pair = rem_(Φ_d)[Γ_d(q)Γ_d(q^(d−1))],
    c_d^pair = positive coefficient content of B_d^pair when nonzero.

Assume c_d^pair ≠ 0 and p ∤ c_d^pair. Conclusion: H_((dp−1)/2)(z;ζ_(dp)) is squarefree for every primitive complex ζ_(dp).

Proof dependency: the nonzero canonical remainder selects one finite-field component in which both reciprocal residual guards are nonzero. Lemma 4 defines the reciprocal pair; Lemma 5 supplies at least one member with nonzero S_A. Every factor of C_ov is then a unit at that selected member. Theorem 2 and Lemma 3 conclude. This does not assert that every component is good, or that the initially chosen member rather than its inverse is the good one.

## 7 Theorem 7 Conditional rational content valuation

Use only c^sep here. Hypotheses: positive s ≡ 1 modulo 4, prime p ≡ 1 modulo 4, c_s^sep ≠ 0, and p ∤ s c_s^sep; include c_1^sep=1 by the empty-product convention. Set A=(p−1)/2, b=(s−1)/2, d=sp and n=As+b=(sp−1)/2.

Conclusions:

    c_(sp)^sep ≠ 0,
    v_p(c_(sp)^sep) = floor(3s(p−1)/8).

Stronger intermediate statement: for every prime 𝔓 above p in Z[ζ_(sp)], with normalized uniformizer valuation, the element B_(sp)^sep(ζ_(sp)) has valuation at least Λ=3sA²/2; equality holds at at least one such prime.

Required proof in five steps:

1. A good lower residue component gives the hypotheses of Theorem 1 and a unit normalized discriminant coefficient. Put K=sA(A−1)/2.
2. For each 0 ≤ j < d define the integer polynomial V_j(q)=H_n(−q^j;q). Its leading value at q^s−1=0 is

       V_j(q) = (q^s−1)^(A/2) κ H_b(−ω^j;ω)
                + O((q^s−1)^(A/2+1)),
       κ = (−s)^(A/2)(A−1)!!.

   The moving cluster coordinate is O(ε), A is even, and evenness improves the remainder to the displayed integral power. Every V_j has exact Φ_s-order A/2 in characteristic zero.
3. Put T_d(q)=product_(j=0)^(d−1)V_j(q). Divide each factor and D_n by their complete cyclotomic powers in Z[q], obtaining

       D_n(q)T_d(q) = Φ_s(q)^Λ E(q),  E in Z[q],
       Λ = K+dA/2 = 3sA²/2.

   Each lower cluster occurs p times, so the normalized product coefficient contains κ^d R^p; it is a unit at the good lower component.
4. At ζ=ζ_(sp), the values −ζ^j are exactly the roots of z^d+1. Hence B_d^sep(ζ)=Φ_s(ζ)^Λ E(ζ). At every 𝔓|p, v_𝔓(Φ_s(ζ))=1 and E is integral. For equality choose the upper prime explicitly as the kernel of Z[ζ]→k, ζ↦ω̄, where k is the good lower residue field. The cyclotomic congruence makes this map well defined, and E remains nonzero.
5. The canonical power basis of Z[ζ] identifies coefficient content with divisibility by p^k in the full integer ring. Since e(𝔓/p)=p−1,

       v_p(c_d^sep) = floor(min_(𝔓|p) v_𝔓(B_d^sep(ζ))/(p−1))
                     = floor(Λ/(p−1)).

   Both the lower bound at all primes and equality at one prime are needed. A norm, or a valuation at only one prime without the global factorization, does not suffice.

Corollary: for every prime ℓ ≡ 1 modulo 4, v_ℓ(c_ℓ^sep)=floor(3(ℓ−1)/8). This is the s=1 specialization; it is an attractive exact corollary, not evidence of a new general ramification principle.

- Separated bases: c_5^sep=5; c_9^sep=3^8; c_13^sep=13^4; c_17^sep=17^6; c_21^sep=3^11·7^9; c_25^sep=5^17.
- Paired bases: c_3^pair=1; c_7^pair=7²; c_11^pair=11^5; c_15^pair=3^12·5^9; c_19^pair=19^11.

The separated bases give squarefreeness for every odd p not dividing the base. For the p ≡ 1 modulo 4 families at bases 5,9,13, separate certificates at N=25,169 close the relevant exceptions. Do not use this to assert N=27. The new separated bases 17,21,25 leave respectively N=289; N=63,147; and N=125 excluded by these family corollaries.

The five paired bases give squarefreeness for every p ≡ 3 modulo 4 with p ∤ d. Separate existing certificates at N=9,45,49,121,361 close all p|d cases for those five families. State that finite certificate route separately from the uniform proof.

Optional determinant proposition: for d=4r+1≥5, write H_(2r)(z)=z^r P(z+z^−1), and (z^d+1)/(z+1)=z^(2r)C_d(z+z^−1). Over Z[ζ_d] put η_j=−ζ_d^j−ζ_d^(−j) and C_d^*=C_d/[(u−η_1)(u−η_(2r))]. Define M_d=Res_u(P,(u−2)P′C_d^*). For rational primes p ∤ d,

    p | c_d^sep  if and only if  M_d belongs to p Z[ζ_d].

Prove the reciprocal-quadratic identity Disc(H)=P(2)P(−2)(Disc P)^2 and identify the removed endpoint evaluations as units away from d. State explicitly that this is an unramified divisibility equivalence, not equality of contents or ramified exponents. Keep d=1 out of this construction.

The restricted scalar-resolvent corollary is optional. Its hypotheses must include scalar coefficients, one common natural cycle, first-order resolvents with distinct nonzero legal nodes and λ^N≠1, and one free diagonal. The smaller minimum additionally uses N≡1 modulo 4 and the accepted resonance result. No unrestricted inverse-bank or transform-complexity conclusion belongs in the abstract.
