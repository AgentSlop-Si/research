# Cyclic regularization reaches a singular Fourier change of basis

**Status:** exact constructive identities, an explicitly charged evaluator,
and a precise singular-limit obstruction for this proposed rewrite. This is
not a lower bound on arbitrary FFT algorithms and not an independent
sub-N-log-N FFT. No global novelty is claimed.

The previous checkpoint made the Fourier middle into H_q(C), where C is sparse
and nilpotent but a full polynomial action remained expensive. A natural next
attempt is to use a cyclic quantum dilogarithm to diagonalize a nearby operator
and take a limit. This checkpoint makes that attempt legal, counts its cost,
and identifies exactly what survives the limit.

## 1. Set-up and explicit gauge

Let N be an even power of two, q a primitive Nth root, and characteristic not
divide N. Use the same H_q(z)=sum_(j=0..N-1) z^j/f_j and
f_j=product_(r=1..j)(1-q^r) as before. Let

    U = diag(q^(-j)),
    W[i,i+1] = q-q^(-i)                   (i<N-1),
    W[N-1,0] = -q/N,
    C = U+W.

Then UW=qWU, U^N=I, W^N=-I, C^N=0. Set V=-WU^-1. The Weyl relation gives

    V^N=I,    UV=qVU,    C=(I-V)U.

Here V has upper cyclic edge weights

    v_i=q(1-q^(i+1))  (i<N-1),    v_(N-1)=q/N.

Their product is one. If S is the unweighted cyclic upper shift and

    G=diag(g_j),    g_j=q^(-j)/f_j,

then V=G S G^-1. All these diagonals can be prepared using O(N) exact field
operations and O(N) storage, given q. No dense matrix is needed by the evaluator.

## 2. A legal regularization

Choose nonzero t, alpha satisfying alpha^N=1-t^N. Then t^N is not one. Define

    C_t=(I-tV)U=U+tW,
    d_0=1,
    d_k=alpha^(-k) product_(r=1..k)(1-t q^(-r)),  1<=k<N.

Let F be the positive-exponent Fourier matrix. Since F S F^-1=U, define

    D_t = G F^-1 diag(d_k) F G^-1.

This is the finite cyclic function whose values obey

    d_k/d_(k-1) = (1-t q^(-k))/alpha,

including the wrap edge k=0. The wrap is valid because the product of all N
numerators is 1-t^N=alpha^N. It follows directly that

    C_t = alpha D_t U D_t^-1,
    H_q(C_t) = D_t H_q(alpha U) D_t^-1.                 (1)

These formulas are derived here with the even-N signs and the corner retained.
They do not substitute our matrices into a generic pentagon formula without
checking its hypotheses.

The vectors d and 1/d take O(N) scalar preparation. The diagonal entries of
H_q(alpha U) also take O(N), using the finite recurrence

    H_q(qz)=(1-z)H_q(z)+z^N/N.

On z=alpha q^j the affine one-orbit multiplier is 1-alpha^N. Composing the
orbit with initial value zero gives B, and the correct starting value is
B/alpha^N. This scalar-spectrum preparation does not transform arbitrary input.

## 3. Honest action cost

The runnable `apply_regularized` expands (1) into four length-N radix-2 FFTs
and five pointwise diagonal multiplications. With L=log2(N), the exact charged
field-operation count is

    multiplications: 2NL+7N,
    additions:       2NL,
    subtractions:    2NL.

Every butterfly multiplication is counted, even a multiplication by one.
The 7N term includes five explicit diagonals and two inverse-FFT scales.
Twiddles and all diagonal arrays are prepared in O(N) field operations;
the report includes their exact counts, including constant inversions.
Indexing, recursive array slices, reads, writes, and allocations total
O(N log N) work. Simultaneously live vector and coefficient storage is O(N).
The implementation does not construct dense matrices in its evaluator.

The input model supplies q, t, and alpha. If they are not supplied, root and
parameter construction costs must be added. In particular, adjoining an
Nth root alpha need not be free or preserve bit complexity. The test harness
finds finite-field parameters by enumeration solely as oracle setup, and does
not count that search as an O(N) preparation algorithm. The analysis is exact
field arithmetic, not floating-point stability or bit complexity.

Thus regularization is a legal O(N log N) evaluator of a nearby family. It
does not improve even the asymptotic FFT baseline, and the target t=1 is
excluded by its own denominators.

## 4. Exact limit: a full triangular kernel survives

At the desired t=1, alpha=0. Along the complex branch
t=(1-alpha^N)^(1/N) near one,

    d_k = alpha^(-k) e_k (1+O(alpha^N)),
    e_k = product_(r=1..k)(1-q^(-r)) != 0.

The N eigenvalues of D_t therefore have distinct pole orders 0,...,N-1.
No scalar rescaling of D_t can have a finite invertible limit: to eliminate
the largest pole, all but the final eigenvalue vanish. Likewise there is no
simultaneous nonsingular finite limit for D_t and its inverse. Their product
in (1) remains finite by cancellation; the singular factors cannot individually
be replaced by finite diagonal gates at alpha=0.

The cancellation can be resolved exactly without numerical limiting:

    F G^-1 C G F^-1 = E A E^-1,                       (2)
    E=diag(e_k),

where A is the ordinary lower nilpotent shift. To see (2), Fourier transform
(I-V)U after removing G. The result is diag(1-q^(-k)) times the cyclic lower
shift. Its k=0 wrap coefficient is exactly zero; all other edges are e_k/e_(k-1).
Consequently

    H_q(C) = G F^-1 E H_q(A) E^-1 F G^-1.             (3)

Equation (3) is the precise remaining operator, not an omitted error term.
It costs two full FFTs, one full triangular q-factorial convolution H_q(A),
and O(N) diagonal work. A direct convolution is quadratic; using an FFT-based
convolution gives O(N log N) with additional transforms. This limit supplies
no independent improvement for a fresh arbitrary FFT.

The same conclusion appears by expanding H_q(alpha U): every degree
r=0,...,N-1 contributes alpha^r D_t U^r D_t^-1 = C_t^r. Retaining only a
fixed number of Taylor coefficients would remove required powers of C.
This statement concerns this expansion and does not exclude a different
compressed algorithm for the complete polynomial.

## 5. Reproduction and next useful gate

Run `python3 run_audit.py`. The audit uses two prime fields (257 and 65537)
for N=2,4,8,16,32, plus exact rational cyclotomic fields for N=2,4,8.
It checks complete matrices for (2) and (3), totaling 138 basis outputs.
For available nonsingular finite-field parameters it checks (1) and the
fully expanded radix-2 evaluator on 92 additional basis outputs. The source
records parameter availability rather than assuming every finite field
contains the required Fermat-curve point. All cases pass.

`prior_exact_arithmetic.py` is copied unchanged from the earlier q-Weyl
checkpoint. Its dense matrix routines appear only in the independent oracle.

The next useful gate is **fusing the specific two basis changes and triangular
kernel in (3), or their neighboring H_q(A), before evaluating them**. A useful
candidate must retain the wrap cancellation and beat the charged aggregate
FFT/convolution cost on arbitrary vectors. Merely writing a cyclic function
as one operator, or choosing a tiny nonzero alpha, does not pass that gate.
