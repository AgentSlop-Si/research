# Independent review of the linear decoder and rank-budget arguments

9 October 2026. Reviewed `fft_linear_decoder_barrier/README.md` and
`fft_sparse_grading_research/RANK_BUDGET.md`. Both arguments are correct
under their stated models. They are representation constraints, not general
FFT time lower bounds. No experimental tests are needed for these elementary
proofs.

## Pointwise input probability

For a fixed seed w, let P_w project orthogonally onto the range of A_w, and
let I_j(w) indicate success on the fixed input e_j. Success gives

\[
\|A_w e_j-Ue_j\|_2^2
\le N\left(\frac{\epsilon}{\sqrt N}\right)^2=\epsilon^2.
\]

The projection minimizes distance to the range. Since Ue_j has norm one,

\[
I_j(w)(1-\epsilon^2)\le \|P_w Ue_j\|_2^2.
\]

Sum over the orthonormal basis of columns of U. The right side is
trace(P_w)=rank(A_w). Taking expectations gives

\[
\mathbb E[\operatorname{rank}(A_w)]
\ge (1-\epsilon^2)\sum_j\Pr(I_j=1)
\ge N(1-\delta)(1-\epsilon^2).
\]

This uses only the separate probability guarantee for each of N fixed
coordinate inputs. It does not intersect their success events, invoke a
union bound, choose an input depending on the seed, or require independence
between their success events. It remains valid for variable rank. The
standard measurability needed to state these probabilities and expectations
is implicit in the randomized-algorithm model.

## Dense-input extension and the direction of reverse Fatou

For complete precision, fix the countable deterministic sequence
t_m=1/m and x_(j,m)=e_j+t_m*1. Every coordinate of x_(j,m) is nonzero, and
x_(j,m) tends to e_j. For each fixed seed, define the continuous function

\[
f_w(x)=\|A_wx-Ux\|_\infty
       -\epsilon\|x\|_1/\sqrt N.
\]

Success is the closed condition f_w(x)<=0. If infinitely many x_(j,m)
succeed, a convergent subsequence and continuity force success at e_j.
Consequently, with I_(j,m) the dense-input success indicators,

\[
\limsup_m I_{j,m}(w)\le I_j(w).
\]

The indicators lie in [0,1], so reverse Fatou applies with the correct
direction:

\[
\Pr(I_j=1)
\ge\mathbb E[\limsup_m I_{j,m}]
\ge\limsup_m\mathbb E[I_{j,m}]
\ge1-\delta.
\]

Thus a guarantee for every dense input implies exactly the coordinate
guarantees needed by the first proof. No uniform bound on the random matrix
norm, integrability of its entries, or one common success event is needed:
continuity is seedwise, and the bounded indicators provide the dominating
function. The README's notation with t tending to zero is sound when read
along such a sequence; spelling out t_m=1/m would remove any possible
ambiguity about applying a sequential lemma.

The qualifications about a quantitatively separated dense-input promise,
input-dependent adaptation, nonlinear decoding and the weaker l2-scaled
error are appropriate. The numeric value 0.84375N for epsilon=1/4,
delta=1/10 is correct. Fixed weighted sums of R rank-at-most-K linear
estimators have rank at most RK regardless of dependence between seeds.

## Review of the exact correction-graph rank budget

For the separate q-Weyl claim, assume N>=2 and the existing field/root
hypotheses. H_q(0)=1 and the coefficient of z in H_q is
1/(1-q), which is nonzero. Thus H-I=Cg(C) with g(0)!=0. A polynomial
in a nilpotent matrix with nonzero constant term is invertible, so

\[
\operatorname{rank}(H-I)=\operatorname{rank}(C)=N-1.
\]

For H=I+sum_j L_j, rank subadditivity immediately gives
N-1<=sum_j rank(L_j)<=sum_j r_j. For the product convention
P_j=P_(j-1)(I+L_j), P_0=I, telescoping gives

\[
P_s-I=\sum_{j=1}^s P_{j-1}L_j.
\]

Every summand has rank at most rank(L_j), so the same bound follows.
Commutativity and invertibility of the factors are unnecessary. Choosing
the opposite multiplication convention merely moves P_(j-1) to the other
side and leaves the rank argument unchanged.

If each correction has rank at most r and each application separately
materializes all N output coordinates, there must be at least
ceil((N-1)/r) applications and hence at least that many full N-entry
writes. The resulting conditional output-write bound is correct. It does
not apply to an implementation that fuses corrections, represents an
intermediate compactly, changes the starting operator, or exploits a
different structured high-rank operation. The README explicitly preserves
these exceptions, as it should.

**Review outcome:** no mathematical defect found. The only suggested edit
is to express the dense limit along an explicit sequence; it clarifies
presentation without changing the result.
