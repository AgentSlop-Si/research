# Focused prior-art and mathematical-value review

9 October 2026. Scope: two audited Fourier/chirp results and the decision whether a short research page is worthwhile. This is a focused primary-source comparison, not a literature-wide novelty certification.

## Recommendation

**A concise mathematical page is justified by substance. Lead with the sharp Fourier endpoint-rank theorem. Do not claim established novelty or an FFT breakthrough.**

The Fourier theorem solves an exact extremal problem: for every dyadic size N ≥ 8, arbitrary and independent row/column permutations cannot reduce the maximum off-diagonal cut rank below N/4 + 1, and double bit reversal attains that value. The proof has a useful mechanism: the second moment of a Fourier support projection, improved by the fact that an odd-cardinality set in a cyclic 2-group has no nontrivial translational stabilizer. This is enough mathematical content for a short, self-contained note even if its relationship to all earlier work remains unsettled.

The chirp certificate is a good secondary result, or a separate later page once the polynomial-time determinant construction is integrated and checked. Its strongest feature is the small, provably nonzero phase-elimination polynomial. Neither common-rate chirps, dechirping, Hankel/Prony reconstruction, nor quadratic Gauss-sum evaluation should be presented as new ideas in themselves.

## Fourier: what is genuinely useful

Write q(A) for the maximum rank of either off-diagonal block across all cuts. The audited statement is

    min_(rho,sigma) q(P_rho F_N P_sigma^T) = N/4 + 1,
    N = 2^m, m ≥ 3.

This says something stronger than evaluating a proposed ordering: no independent choice of the two endpoint orders can do better. Double bit reversal supplies a matching constructive certificate. The lower bound uses only one critical cut, so it avoids a permutation search.

For an odd cut k, the additional bound

    rank ≥ ceil(k(N-k)(N+1)/N^2)

is more informative than the first-trace bound ceil(k(N-k)/N). Combined with the latter on even cuts, it matches the entire bit-reversal profile at N=8 and N=16. The exact upper-right rank sums are therefore 14 and 48. These are appealing checks and corollaries, but should not displace the all-size minimax theorem as the headline.

The closest prior source found is Steven Delvaux and Marc Van Barel, *Rank-deficient submatrices of Fourier matrices*, Linear Algebra and its Applications 429 (2008), 1587–1605, DOI [10.1016/j.laa.2008.04.043](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.laa.2008.04.043). Its [institutional record](https://lirias.kuleuven.be/1537109) explicitly describes prime-power Fourier matrices, low-rank hierarchical subdivisions, and FFT/uncertainty connections. The earlier [TW470 report record](https://www.cs.kuleuven.be/publicaties/rapporten/tw/TW470.abs.html) was also verified. Indexed excerpts define its extremal rank-deficiency question through Hamming numbers, concerning existence of a nonzero supported null vector. That is closely related but is not by itself the two-order minimax cut-rank question here. The full PDF could not be retrieved in this review, so a theorem-by-theorem implication check remains open.

David Donoho and Philip Stark's *Uncertainty Principles and Signal Recovery*, SIAM Journal on Applied Mathematics 49 (1989), 906–931, provides the classical concentration/uncertainty backdrop, not evidence of priority for the present exact value. [DOI 10.1137/0149053](https://doi.org/10.1137/0149053).

## Chirps: focus on the certificate

The proposed family consists of sequences

    x[j] = omega^(a*j^2) sum_(ell=1)^s c_ell omega^(b_ell*j), s ≤ r.

The meaningful algebraic lemma is that one window with nonzero center yields

    P_s(t) = t^(-E_min) det[x_(s+i+j) t^binom(i+j,2)]_(i,j=0)^r,

with degree **at most** D = binom(r+2,3) and nonzero constant term ±x_(s+r)^(r+1). Every valid common phase supplies a root t = omega^(-2a). The degree can drop for special input data, so “degree D” should only mean a uniform upper bound, or the generic degree. N ≥ 3r guarantees a usable interior witness for every nonzero member of the family. Even-N phase equivalence must remain in the statement.

Three primary comparisons matter:

- Abhinek Shukla, Debasis Kundu, Amit Mitra, and Rhythm Grover, *On estimating parameters of a multi-component Chirp Model with equal chirp rates* (2023). Their model has a common unknown quadratic coefficient and several frequencies; they study noisy real-valued observations, least squares and sequential estimators, with consistency and asymptotic distributions. This establishes the model's prior existence. It does not state the finite-field, all-input, bounded-degree recognition certificate examined here. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.07098), especially Sections I–II.
- Zai Yang, Sikai Ge, and Wenlong Wang, *Pursuing the Limit of Chirp Parameter Identifiability: A Computational Approach* (2025 preprint; a 2026 journal version was found). The inspected preprint allows different continuous chirp rates, encodes identifiability as uniqueness of a rank-constrained structured matrix problem, and develops numerical optimization. Its task is different from exact membership recognition on the finite roots-of-unity grid. In particular, do not claim that N ≥ 3r is a new universal identifiability threshold or that the returned representation is always unique. [Preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.01286), Theorems 2 and 4; [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.01286v1).
- M. Z. Ikram, K. Abed-Meraim, and Y. Hua, *Fast Quadratic Phase Transform for Estimating the Parameters of Multicomponent Chirp Signals*, Digital Signal Processing 7 (1997), 127–135. The publisher's abstract describes a discrete quadratic-phase transform, fast computation, and explicit arithmetic counts. It is important transform/estimation background; the abstract alone does not establish equivalence to the one-window certificate. [DOI 10.1006/dspr.1997.0286](https://doi.org/10.1006/dspr.1997.0286).

For the present raw-input algorithm, the useful complexity assertion is worst-case O_r(N) exact field arithmetic with fixed r, supplied suitable root, all candidates checked, and rejection meaning only nonmembership in this structured family. It is neither a floating-point robustness theorem nor an algorithm for the DFT of every input.

## Suggested claim language

**Title:** Optimal endpoint cut rank of dyadic Fourier matrices

**Opening:** “For N=2^m ≥ 8, we prove that the least possible quasiseparable rank of the complex Fourier matrix, under arbitrary independent row and column permutations, is exactly N/4+1. Double bit reversal attains the bound. A second-moment support-projection argument yields the lower bound and additional odd-cut inequalities.”

**Literature sentence:** “The result belongs to the established study of rank-deficient Fourier submatrices and finite uncertainty principles. We give a self-contained proof of this precise endpoint-ordering statement; literature priority has not been established.”

**Optional secondary sentence:** “A separate algebraic recognition result reduces the unknown common quadratic phase of an r-character signal to at most binom(r+2,3) candidates using a provably nonzero window determinant.”

Avoid: “first,” “novel,” “unrestricted FFT lower bound,” “linear-time FFT,” and “exact total-rank optimum for every dyadic N.” The same-coordinate product consequence is legitimate only when interstage coordinate changes are included or charged.

## Scope and stopping point

The page decision does not require settling priority: the sharp theorem and its short proof meet the mathematical-substance condition. A stronger novelty claim would require the full 2008 Fourier paper and more citation-chain review, plus broader algebraic chirp-identification references. This review does not infer novelty from unsuccessful searches. No page was created or published.
