# Hero #1 — Phase 18: Certified sparse-defect recurrence Green transforms

**Date:** 2026-10-10. **Status:** proved exact algebraic identities and an executable finite-field algorithm with extensive tests. **Not** an exact arbitrary-input `o(N log N)` FFT, not a new FFT lower bound, and not a numerical-stability-certified complex implementation.

## 1. Conventions and inherited factorization

Let `E` be a field with `char(E)` not dividing `N`, containing a primitive `N`-th root `omega`. Write the unnormalized Fourier matrix `F[k,j]=omega^(kj)` and the cyclic shift `(Sr)[j]=r[(j-1) mod N]`. Thus

    F S = D F,  D[k,k] = omega^k.                         (1)

Take the inherited Phase16/17 operators `L=7I+2S+D` and `P=7I+S+2D^-1` (with the appropriate input/output coordinate convention). The exact Weyl identities imply

    L F = F P.                                           (2)

Both `P` and `L` are invertible over C, with `cond_2 <= 2.5`, and admit O(N) cyclic-recurrence solvers. In the finite-field implementation, invertibility is checked by the explicit cycle-closure denominators; the complex norm estimate is not imported into finite fields.

For a fresh input x, compute the **dense** Green residual r=Px. The remaining task is to compute Fr, followed by L^-1.

## 2. Sparse defects of graph shifts (main exact theorem)

Choose an integer graph shift `1 <= delta < N` and a polynomial

    q(z) = q_0 + q_1 z + ... + q_d z^d,  q_0 != 0, 0<=d<N,

and define `h=q(S^delta)r`. Suppose `K=||h||_0`. The operator q(S^delta) is applied by `d+1` scalar taps per vertex; hence `O(N(d+1))` arithmetic, even when `delta = Theta(N)`. From (1),

    F h[k] = q(omega^(k*delta)) * F r[k].                 (3)

For each k with nonzero denominator, recover

    F r[k] = F h[k] / q(omega^(k*delta)).                 (4)

For the remaining frequencies, compute `sum_j r_j omega^(kj)` **directly**, retaining exactness. Since `omega^delta` has order `N/g`, with `g=gcd(N,delta)`, any nonzero polynomial q of degree d vanishes at at most d distinct `N/g`-th roots, each attained by g original Fourier frequencies. The number of direct repairs is therefore at most `d*g` (zero when d=0).

The Phase17 sparse-series evaluator computes Fh, at arbitrary lengths, in

    O(N log(K+1) + K log^2(K+1))                        (5)

field arithmetic when K>=1. For K<=3 the implementation instead uses K ordinary geometric streams for `O(NK)` arithmetic. For K=0 it emits N zeros. The per-frequency polynomial evaluation costs `O(Nd)` field arithmetic (the nodes are obtained by one geometric progression), and all **nonzero denominators** can be inverted with one batch inversion and O(N) multiplications. Direct repairs cost at most `O(N d g)` arithmetic. Consequently, including input-side P, the Green solve, data read/write, annihilator application, root streaming, and inversion,

    T(N,d,K,g) = O(N(d+1) + N log(K+1) + K log^2(K+1) + N*d*g).     (6)

This assumes an available fast exact convolution engine in the relevant field, with its calls and twiddle preprocessing charged. For `g=1` the repair toll is `O(Nd)`. **Constant d and K give exact O(N) arithmetic**, regardless of how far the graph edge delta spans the input. For K=polylog N and d<=O(log log N), this branch is `O(N log log N)` up to the lower-order K-polylog term.

This is a *structured residual* bound. Discovering an arbitrary unknown q or delta may be difficult and cannot be treated as free.

## 3. Projective majority: guaranteed linear-time discovery for a single shift

For a fixed candidate delta and an r with every coordinate nonzero, consider the N projective edge ratios

    rho_j = r_j / r_(j-delta),  j in Z/NZ.              (7)

Do **not** evaluate N inversions. Represent each ratio by the nonzero projective pair `(a_j,b_j)=(r_j,r_(j-delta))`; test equality of pairs by the exact identity `a_j*b_other = a_other*b_j`. Boyer-Moore cancellation finds a majority projective ratio in O(N) multiplications/comparisons and O(1) memory. Let its representative be `(a,b)` with b nonzero, and set

    h_j = b*r_j - a*r_(j-delta).                         (8)

If some alpha=a/b gives exactly K<N/2 defects, at least N-K>N/2 projective edge ratios agree, so majority detection **must** find this alpha. The full O(N) calculation of h independently verifies that there are only K nonzero defects. Candidates with too many defects are rejected and trigger the ordinary FFT. This is adaptive discovery with **no per-vertex inverse**, not an oracle for the annihilator. The outer engine checks two prescribed candidate delays (delta=1 and a far coprime shift) unless the caller supplies a different fixed-size bank.

Special case `q(z)=b-a*z`: if `g=gcd(N,delta)` and `ell=N/g`, there are exactly g singular Fourier frequencies when `(a/b)^ell=1`, and none otherwise. The production fast selector avoids candidates with expensive singular repairs, while direct low-level decoding remains correct even when g is large.

**Graph-cycle cost boundary.** If `(a/b)^ell != 1`, every delta-cycle has an invertible restricted operator `bI-aS^delta`. If r is nonzero at every vertex, no entire cycle can have zero defects. Therefore `K >= g`. Large `g` forces many defects on everywhere-dense r; coprime long shifts (`g=1`) are the attractive regime.

## 4. Degree-d recurrence discovery on a long graph cycle

For a fixed candidate delta, sample the first `2d_max+2` points along the orbit `0,delta,2delta,...`. Run bounded Berlekamp-Massey on these samples; it proposes

    q(z)=1+c_1 z+...+c_d z^d, d<=d_max.

This uses O(d_max^2) field arithmetic, **but does not certify the full sequence**. Apply q(S^delta) to every one of the N coordinates, charging O(N(d+1)), and accept only if the resulting K and singularity gate meet the algorithm's explicit thresholds. For a clean degree-d graph recurrence whose sampled prefix contains a sufficiently informative recurrence window, the proposed q is correct. For inputs with an early defect or ambiguous prefix, the proposal may fail; correctness is preserved by the verified fallback. This is a *sufficient* recovery method, not a proof of discovery for every sparse-defect signal.

For a fixed number of candidate shifts and fixed d_max, failure discovery overhead is O(N). The test implementation charges candidate generation, rejected full scans, exact forcing, divisions, singular repairs and L^-1; arbitrary inputs use Phase17 Bluestein in O(N log N) field arithmetic.

**Example.** For N=384, delta=191 (coprime), write each j=t*delta (mod N) and let

    r_j = 2^t + 3^t (mod p).

Its graph recurrence is `q(z)=1-5z+6z^2` with only two cyclic boundary defects, despite all 384 r coordinates being nonzero in the tested field. The unchanged Green inverse supplies dense x=P^-1r. The exact N-output transform is recovered in O(N) field arithmetic, by a degree-2 graph prefix fit and a full-vector defect certificate. One can also inject a later nonzero defect along the graph cycle: the same recurrence recovers h with K=3.

## 5. A genuine generic-input limitation for adaptive short annihilators

Let C be the complex field, with N fixed, and define the model class

    C(d,K) = { r in C^N : exists q(z)=1+sum_{i=1}^d c_i z^i,
                           ||q(S)r||_0 <= K },    d<N.

**Proposition.** The algebraic dimension of C(d,K) is at most `d+K`. In particular, if `d+K<N`, its Zariski closure is a proper subset of C^N; hence it has Lebesgue measure zero. This allows q to depend adaptively on r: it is **not** restricted to a finite prechosen bank.

**Proof.** Fix a possible defect support T of size at most K, and fix the exact set R of e distinct N-th roots at which q vanishes. Since q has constant term 1 and degree at most d, e<=d. Conditions q(rho)=0 (rho in R) impose e independent linear conditions on the d coefficient parameters, by a Vandermonde minor; the admissible q stratum has dimension at most d-e. For each q, q(S) has kernel dimension exactly e because cyclic S is diagonalizable at distinct N-th roots. A defect vector h supported in T has at most K free parameters; if in the image of q(S), its preimages r form an affine space of dimension e. The incidence family therefore has dimension at most `(d-e)+K+e=d+K`. Projecting to r cannot increase dimension; the finite union over T and R preserves that bound. QED.

Since P is invertible over C, the same measure-zero limitation applies to the original inputs x through r=Px. **This is a restriction on small-degree / sparse-defect compression, not a lower bound for arbitrary FFT algorithms.** High-degree but few-tap graph polynomials require their own singularity and dimension accounting; `q(z)=1-z^N` is the zero operator on length N and yields no useful Fourier recovery because every frequency is singular.

## 6. Precision and arithmetic models

All exact tests use finite fields such as GF(2013265921) and GF(998244353), with supported roots explicitly checked. Formal asymptotic costs use unit-cost scalar arithmetic in a suitable root-rich field and fast polynomial convolution. They do **not** imply O(N) bit operations as N grows; prime construction, representation bit length, root supply, and exact complex arithmetic must be charged in an actual machine model.

Over C, a denominator q(omega^(k delta)) can be arbitrarily small without being zero. The exact finite-field branch is therefore not a floating-point error guarantee. In `PRECISION_GATE.json` a near-resonant 128-point one-mode signal has about 2.76% relative error in a naive double-precision quotient at epsilon=1e-14, despite an exactly valid algebraic identity. A stable complex implementation requires high-precision or cancellation-aware evaluation and a separate forward-error analysis.

## 8. Exact graph-filter unions with independently dense defects (additional theorem)

A second exact branch receives a **supplied** list of m graph factors `(delta_s, alpha_s)` and forms

    Q = product_{s=1}^m (I-alpha_s*S^(delta_s)),
    h = Q r.

Each factor can be applied to its input with only two scalar products and one subtraction per vertex. Hence computing the complete h is `O(mN)` arithmetic, **without expanding Q into up to 2^m monomials**. By Fourier diagonalization,

    Fh[k] = [product_s (1-alpha_s*omega^(k delta_s))] Fr[k].  (9)

Evaluate the denominator using m independently streamed geometric phase vectors, costing `O(mN)` arithmetic. Where it is nonzero, one batch inverse and pointwise products recover Fr. Every resonant frequency is repaired by a direct exact Fourier coordinate. The number of such frequencies is bounded by `sum_s gcd(N,delta_s)`, since each binomial factor contributes either zero or gcd(N,delta_s) resonant values. Thus the total bound is

    O(mN + N log(K+1) + K log²(K+1) + N*Z),           (10)

where `K=||h||_0` and Z is the actual number of distinct resonant frequencies, at most `sum_s gcd(N,delta_s)`. In the **nonresonant** case, constant m and K give O(N) field arithmetic for all outputs. This remains true if the expanded polynomial has degree Θ(N) or more. The computation is charged in `decode_factor_union` and tested by `test_phase18.py` and `union_audit.py`.

**Strict separation:** These graph filters commute with each other because they are polynomials in the same cyclic shift. The surrounding L and P form the noncommuting Green layer. The factor parameters are supplied as part of this *structured-input algorithm*; there is no proof that they can be discovered for arbitrary inputs in o(N log N).

**Concrete witness.** At `N=384`, set `delta1=191`, `delta2=383`, `alpha1=2`, `alpha2=3` in the prime field. Let h have three nonzero entries and solve `Q1 Q2 r=h` using the two inverse cycle recurrences (charged when generating the test signal, but not part of the transform invocation). Then all 384 coordinates of x=P^-1r and of r are nonzero, and

    ||Q1 r||_0 = 384,
    ||Q2 r||_0 = 384,
    ||Q1 Q2 r||_0 = 3.

Neither individual graph filter produces any zero defect. The combined filter, evaluated with *provided parameters*, recovers the exact transform with a complete field-arithmetic proxy of 14,628, versus 56,148 for full Bluestein. At N=4096 the corresponding proxies are 155,696 and 570,070. **Those are same-input arithmetic proxies under a structural promise, not same-information automatic model selection comparisons.** The entire 330,520-entry matrix basis audit for this union construction passes independently.

For supplied constant-size factor descriptions and invertible Q, the set of r for which `||Qr||_0<=K` has dimension at most K. Allowing m unknown scalar parameters (with a finite bank of possible delays) gives dimension at most K+m on nonresonant strata, so generic inputs remain excluded whenever K+m<N. When factors are singular, all lost spectral directions must be repaired and paid for.
