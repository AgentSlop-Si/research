# Hero #17 — Shear-Gauge and Multi-Shear Trace Union

**Checkpoint:** 10 October 2026  
**Goal:** An exact, sequential, fully charged, uniform \(O(N)\) discrete Fourier transform on **every fresh arbitrary vector**.  
**Status:** Goal still **not achieved**. Three exact, fully charged, raw-input **conditional linear-work theorems** have been derived, implemented and audited; a universal orbit representation pinpoints why extending from constant to growing orbit count is the unresolved bottleneck. The component mathematics is classical or adjacent to classical Prony/Hankel and Fourier-shift identities. **No publication-priority claim.**

## 1. Arithmetic contract

Let \(N=QM\) with balanced factors \(Q,M=\Theta(\sqrt N)\), and write the supplied *raw* vector as

\[
y_t[u]=x_{t+Qu},\qquad 0\le t<Q,\quad 0\le u<M.\]

Work in a prime field \(\mathbb F_p\), where \(N\mid(p-1)\), \(p>N\), \(p=N^{O(1)}\), and a primitive \(N\)-th root \(\omega\) is **supplied**. Put \(\eta=\omega^Q\), a primitive \(M\)-th root. The output is the complete unnormalized DFT

\[
\widehat x[k]=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}x_j\omega^{-kj},\quad k=0,\ldots,N-1.
\]

Work counts include scanning the entire raw input; detecting unknown structure, including rejecting unsupported inputs; root table construction and validation; phase inversions; all intermediate transforms; every scalar operation; and explicitly writing all \(N\) outputs. There is **no FFT/NTT call in the online implementations**. The `test_*.py` files and `independent_audit.py` use direct \(O(N^2)\) reference transforms *only for testing*. Word-RAM arithmetic on \(O(\log N)\)-bit field elements has unit cost; a fixed number of words holds modular products. Field inversion can be charged \(O(\log p)\) word operations via Euclid and is absorbed by the explicitly given \(M^2\) work on the nondegenerate balanced regime. No numerical complex-precision or general bit-complexity bound is claimed.

The results are **recognize-or-reject theorems**. When a candidate is accepted, it has been verified against the *entire* raw vector and all outputs are exact. A rejected arbitrary input is not transformed by this special executor.

## 2. Result A — recover an unknown shear and scale from noisy fibers

Assume an unknown shift \(a\in\mathbb Z/M\mathbb Z\), unknown nonzero \(\mu\in\mathbb F_p^\times\), and a *gauged* raw matrix \(v\) such that

\[
 y_t[u]=\mu^t v_t[u-at\bmod M]. \tag{1}
\]

Define the length-\(M\) row spectra

\[
 C_t[h]=\sum_u y_t[u]\eta^{-hu},\qquad D_t[h]=\sum_u v_t[u]\eta^{-hu}.
\]

By a change of variable,

\[
 C_t[h]=\mu^t\eta^{-aht}D_t[h]. \tag{2}
\]

### 2.1 Finding both hidden parameters in deterministic \(O(M^2)\) work

From the first two *raw* rows, compute \(C_0,C_1\) via two direct length-\(M\) DFTs: \(2M^2\) charged arithmetic terms. If their gauged difference has \(e\) Fourier modes, then for all but \(e\) indices

\[
\rho_h=C_1[h]/C_0[h]=\mu\eta^{-ah},
\]

provided \(C_0[h]\ne0\). For any two good indices \(h_i,h_j\) with \(\gcd(h_j-h_i,M)=1\), the ratio \(\rho_{h_j}/\rho_{h_i}=\eta^{-a(h_j-h_i)}\) identifies \(a\), and then \(\mu\).

There are \(U=M\varphi(M)/2\) unordered pairs of spectral indices separated by a unit residue. Each corrupted channel belongs to exactly \(\varphi(M)\) such pairs. If **every** \(C_0[h]\ne0\) and \(e<M/4\), then more than

\[
 U-e\varphi(M)>\tfrac12 U
\]

pairs are good-good pairs, so the true \((a,\mu)\) is a strict majority among all emitted candidate pair-votes. This remains valid for composite \(M\) and adversarial corruption positions.

**Algorithm:** construct at most \(M^2\) quotient queries; join their field values to the \(M\) powers of \(\eta\); convert matched quotient logs to \((a,\mu)\); use Boyer–Moore majority and a second verification pass. The implementation uses a **deterministic stable radix sort** for the quotient/root join: integer keys are bounded by \(p=N^{O(1)}\) and base \(M=\Theta(\sqrt N)\), so only a constant number of radix passes is required. All counters, inversions and memory allocations are charged. A nonmajority returns `None`.

The sufficient condition \(e<M/4\) is for **recovering** the hidden gauge. The later Newton decoder permits further changes. For Newton order \(d>1\), first two gauged rows may be arbitrary in principle, but our recovery guarantee additionally requires an identifiable first-pair pilot (e.g. \(v_1=v_0\)); without it this recognizer may reject a valid gauged Newton signal.

### 2.2 Transform identity after discovering the gauge

For \(k=h+sM\), put

\[
\theta_{k,h}=\mu\omega^{-(k+Qah)}.
\]

Equations (1)–(2) give the **exact** identity

\[
\boxed{\widehat x[k]=\sum_{t=0}^{Q-1}\theta_{k,h}^{t}D_t[h]}. \tag{3}
\]

Suppose, as in inherited `newton_union.py`, the \(d\)-th backward difference of \(v_t\) has a *total* of \(R\) length-\(M\) Fourier modes over \(t\ge d\), while the first \(d\) gauged fibers are arbitrary dense. The existing finite-difference identity decomposes \(D_t[h]\) into \(d\) dense Newton baseline terms and \(R\) spectral events. Each can be summed in constant work per relevant output frequency using

\[
B_{d,L}(z)=\sum_{v=0}^{L-1}\binom{v+d-1}{d-1}z^v.
\]

For \(z\ne1\), the rational closed form is

\[
B_{d,L}(z)=\frac{1-z^L\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\binom{L+j-1}{j}(1-z)^j}{(1-z)^d},
\]

and for \(z=1\) the correct value is \(\binom{L+d-1}{d}\). Global tables of \(\omega^k\), \(\mu^t\) and a batch of inverses of \(1-\mu\omega^{-k}\) remove any hidden \(\log N\) exponentiation inside an output loop. There can be at most one zero denominator among the \(N\) phase positions, and the \(z=1\) limit is handled exactly.

**Conditional complexity theorem.** For constant \(d\), balanced \(Q,M\), and \(R=O(Q)\), recognition and all \(N\) output coefficients cost

\[
O_d\bigl(N+M^2+dM^2+MR+QR\bigr)=\boxed{O(N)}
\]

arithmetic/word operations, with \(O(N)\) memory (and extra \(O(M\log p)\) field-inversion accounting absorbed into \(M^2\)). Incorrect candidates do not lead to incorrect outputs because the raw gauged differences are all independently checked. The theorem is **not** for unrelated arbitrary dense inputs.

### 2.3 Why this is a strict improvement over ungauged spectral differences

Choose \(Q=M\), \(\gcd(a,M)=1\), an arbitrary baseline \(b\) with **all \(M\) Fourier coefficients nonzero**, and \(\mu\notin\langle\eta\rangle\). Let \(v_t=b\) for every \(t\), so the corrected event budget is exactly \(R=0\). The original row spectra are

\[
C_t[h]=\bigl(\mu\eta^{-ah}\bigr)^t B[h].
\]

Every ungauged first-order difference is nonzero at **all \(M\) frequencies** for each \(t=1,\ldots,Q-1\). Hence its event count is exactly \((Q-1)M=\Theta(N)\), while the shear-gauged event count is **zero**. The \(Q\times M\) raw fiber matrix has maximum rank \(M\): it is an invertible diagonal row scaling of a circulant matrix, and all \(B[h]\ne0\) ensure the circulant is nonsingular. Exact \(N=256\) evidence tests this full-rank example and verifies the earlier Newton recognizer rejects at a linear-sized event budget.

## 3. Result B — recover a constant number of *different* dense shears together

The stronger family is a union of \(g\) independently shifted and scaled *arbitrary dense* baseline signals:

\[
\boxed{y_t[u]=\sum_{j=1}^g \mu_j^t b_j[u-a_jt\bmod M].} \tag{4}
\]

The parameters \(a_j,\mu_j\) and *all* \(gM\) raw baseline coefficients are **unknown**. Set

\[
B_j[h]=\sum_u b_j[u]\eta^{-hu},\quad
\lambda_j(h)=\mu_j\eta^{-a_jh}.
\]

For every frequency channel \(h\), its temporal moment sequence satisfies

\[
C_t[h]=\sum_{j=1}^g B_j[h]\lambda_j(h)^t. \tag{5}
\]

This is an ordinary order-\(g\) Prony sequence. Using only the first \(2g\) raw fibers, compute their length-\(M\) DFTs (\(2gM^2\) work). For each \(h\), build the Hankel matrix

\[
H_h[r,s]=C_{r+s}[h],\quad 0\le r,s<g.
\]

It factors as \(H_h=V_h\operatorname{diag}(B_1[h],\ldots,B_g[h])V_h^T\), where \(V_h[r,j]=\lambda_j(h)^r\). Thus

\[
\det H_h=\left(\prod_j B_j[h]\right)\prod_{i<j}(\lambda_j(h)-\lambda_i(h))^2.
\]

For the theorem, require **every** component coefficient \(B_j[h]\ne0\) and all \(\lambda_j(h)\) distinct. This is ensured, for example, by choosing multipliers in distinct multiplicative cosets of the order-\(M\) subgroup, but this choice is **not supplied** to the recognizer. Also require distinct shifts \(a_j\) and \(Q\ge2g\). The algorithm rejects if any required invertibility test fails.

Solve the \(g\times g\) Hankel system in \(O(g^3)\) arithmetic per \(h\). It gives the coefficient of \(z^{g-1}\) in \(\prod_j(z-\lambda_j(h))\), whose negative is

\[
\boxed{s[h]=\sum_j\lambda_j(h)=\sum_{j=1}^g\mu_j\eta^{-a_jh}.} \tag{6}
\]

**Crucial graph-sharing identity:** While the original raw rows and their spectra are dense, \(s[h]\) contains only \(g\) Fourier characters in its index \(h\). One direct \(M\)-point DFT of \(s\) reveals the \(g\) unknown shifts and multipliers:

\[
\operatorname{DFT}_M(s)[\ell]
=\begin{cases}M\mu_j,&\ell\equiv-a_j\pmod M,\\0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}
\]

Recover every \(B_j[h]\) from the first \(g\) row moments using a small transposed Vandermonde solve. Recover \(b_j\) by \(g\) **charged direct inverse** length-\(M\) DFTs. Then reconstruct **every one of the \(N\) raw input samples**, rejecting if anything disagrees. This is a full deterministic certificate, not a basis supplied by a producer.

The exact output is

\[
\boxed{
\widehat x[k]=\sum_{j=1}^g B_j[h]\,
\sum_{t=0}^{Q-1}(\mu_j\omega^{-(k+Qa_jh)})^t,
\quad h=k\bmod M.
} \tag{7}
\]

Each inner sum uses a constant-work geometric kernel per coefficient after \(O(gN)\) phase preparation and batch inversions, including \(z=1\) limits.

**Conditional complexity theorem.** Under the nondegeneracy hypotheses, for *fixed* \(g\), balanced factors, and a supplied \(\omega\), complete discovery, exact raw validation, and all \(N\) outputs use

\[
O\bigl(gN+gM^2+g^3M+g^2M+gM\log p\bigr)
=\boxed{O_g(N)},
\]

and \(O_g(N)\) words. Without the hypotheses, the algorithm may reject. Validation means any input it accepts genuinely has the reconstructed decomposition and exact spectrum.

A verified \(g=2\), \(N=256\) example has **full ordinary matrix rank** and is rejected by the single-gauge Newton recognizer at orders 1, 2, 3 with a \(Q\)-event budget. The two-orbit trace recognizer accepts and reproduces the complete direct DFT. The sufficient reason a single gauge is inadequate is that the *other* orbit can leave all \(M\) frequencies active in every consecutive difference (e.g. distinct multiplier cosets and fully dense \(B_j\)).

## 4. Result C — Multi-Shear Green-Union with sparse innovations

The previous theorem requires a clean union of \(g\) orbit signals **throughout the entire input**. A strictly broader family permits innovations after only the first \(2g\) clean fibers. The unknown orbit parameters and dense baseline vectors remain undisclosed to the algorithm.

Let \(S_a\) denote circular translation on the fiber coordinate, \((S_af)[u]=f[u-a\bmod M]\), and let \(E\) denote advancement of the fiber-time variable. Define the *annihilator graph operator*

\[
\mathcal P(E,S)=\prod_{j=1}^g(E-\mu_jS_{a_j}).
\]

The first \(2g\) raw fibers obey the exact multiple-orbit model (4), are nondegenerate at every spectral channel as specified in Result B, and have **zero** annihilator innovation. For \(t\ge2g\), the raw fibers obey the inhomogeneous recurrence

\[
\boxed{
 r_t[u]=y_t[u]+
 \sum_{\ell=1}^{g}(-1)^\ell\sum_{|J|=\ell}
 \left(\prod_{j\in J}\mu_j\right)
 y_{t-\ell}[u-\sum_{j\in J}a_j],
} \tag{8}
\]

where the *total* number of length-\(M\) Fourier modes in all \(r_t\) (for \(t\ge2g\)) is \(R=O(Q)\). The modes are not supplied: each \(r_t\) is computed from the raw rows using \(2^g-1\) explicit shifted terms and recognized with the prior exact sparse-frequency recognizer.

### 4.1 A finite Green-function identity for every innovation

For fixed spectral channel \(h\), put \(\lambda_j=\mu_j\eta^{-a_jh}\). The row spectrum obeys the scalar recurrence

\[
 \left[\prod_{j=1}^g(E-\lambda_j)\right] C_t[h]=\widehat r_t[h].
\]

Because the \(\lambda_j\) are distinct, the causal impulse response, obtained from partial fractions, is

\[
 H_v(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_g)
 =\sum_{j=1}^g w_j(h)\lambda_j^v,\qquad
 w_j(h)=\frac{\lambda_j^{g-1}}{\prod_{i\ne j}(\lambda_j-\lambda_i)}.
\tag{9}
\]

The identity follows from the generating function

\[
 \sum_{v\ge0} H_v z^v=\prod_{j=1}^g(1-\lambda_j z)^{-1}.
\]

If \(r_t[u]\) contributes the Fourier character \(c\eta^{hu}\) at fiber time \(t=a\), then its complete DFT contribution at \(k=h+sM\), where \(z=\omega^{-k}\), is

\[
 \boxed{M c z^a\sum_{j=1}^g w_j(h)\,G_{Q-a}\!\left(z\lambda_j\right)},
 \qquad G_L(w)=\sum_{v=0}^{L-1}w^v. \tag{10}
\]

Every \(G_L\) is computed in constant work using a charged phase table, \(\mu_j\)-power tables and one batched inverse table per orbit; the case \(w=1\) returns \(L\). Hence **one sparse spectral innovation changes all relevant \(Q\) output bins in \(O(gQ)\)**, with no new FFT or extra dense transform.

### 4.2 Charged raw recognition, certification, and complexity

1. Compute the DFT of the first \(2g\) raw fibers by direct \(M^2\) operations apiece and recover all unknown \((a_j,\mu_j,b_j)\) through the Hankel-trace method of Result B. Independently verify the entire \(2g\)-fiber pilot, not merely its low-order moments.
2. Expand the operator (8) into \(2^g-1\) shifted previous-row terms. For every remaining raw fiber, **compute** its residual \(r_t\) from the raw input. Discover and exactly verify its Fourier support; reject if the total event budget is exceeded.
3. Independently reconstruct **all \(N\) raw samples**, starting from the recovered first \(g\) orbit rows and reapplying the recognized innovations through (8). Reject unless every reconstructed field element equals the original raw input.
4. Evaluate the homogeneous baseline with (7). Add the Green response (10) for every recognized event. Explicitly materialize all \(N\) coefficients.

For fixed \(g\), balanced factors and \(R=O(Q)\), the charged cost is

\[
 O\!\left(gM^2+g^3M+2^gN+MR+gN+gQR+gM\log p\right)
 =\boxed{O_g(N)}, \tag{11}
\]

with \(O_g(N)\) words. The compact bound \(O_g(N+M^2+MR+QR)\) suppresses constants depending on \(g\). **Only inputs satisfying the nondegenerate clean pilot and sparse later innovations are guaranteed accepted.** All accepted inputs have been checked against their exact full raw reconstruction; unsupported inputs may reject rather than returning an incorrect spectrum.

For any fixed \(g\), the family still has low description capacity: at most

\[
 \binom Mg(p-1)^g p^{gM}
 \sum_{e=0}^{R}\binom{(Q-2g)M}{e}(p-1)^e
\]

possible raw vectors. Under balanced factors, \(p\ge N\) and \(R=O(Q)\), this is a negligible fraction of \(p^N\). The method is thus a further **structured-input** exact theorem, not a universal one.

The implementation `multishear_green_union.py` passed an **eight-group** new unit suite, including \(g=1,2,3\), odd/even and non-coprime factor lengths, adversarial edits, and strict extension checks. The separately implemented `green_independent_audit.py` built raw signals *without the package's fixture builder*, verified **162 complete DFTs and 37,728 exact coefficients**, rejected 450 unrelated random dense inputs, and generated every output at \(N=65,536\) with \(g=3,R=250\), checking 36 selected coefficients directly from their full raw \(N\)-term sums. The earlier pure multi-orbit recognizer rejected an innovation-bearing \(N=256,g=2\) fixture, whereas this Green executor accepted it and matched the entire reference DFT.

## 5. Universal *representation* at square lengths, but NOT linear-time computation

A mathematically useful frontier theorem follows by allowing the number of orbits to grow.

For \(N=M^2\), \(Q=M\), select **\(g=M\)** distinct shifts \(a_j\) and nonzero multipliers \(\mu_j\) lying in *different cosets* of \(\langle\eta\rangle\subset\mathbb F_p^\times\). This is possible because \(N\mid(p-1)\) implies there are at least \(M\) such cosets.

For each frequency \(h\), the \(M\times M\) matrix \(V_h[t,j]=(\mu_j\eta^{-a_jh})^t\) is a **square, invertible Vandermonde matrix** (its nodes lie in distinct cosets and therefore cannot collide). Consequently, for **every arbitrary fresh vector** \(x\in\mathbb F_p^N\), the \(M\) raw row spectra \(C_t[h]\) can be solved for unique weights \(B_j[h]\), which invert to the corresponding arbitrary baselines \(b_j\). Equation (4) becomes a representation of **every** raw vector.

**But the currently certified evaluator uses \(gN=M N=N^{3/2}\) geometric contributions even if those \(B_j\) were given.** Recovering all the parameters/weights costs more when \(g\) grows. This is **not** a new all-input fast FFT. The universal basis is a *target for joint evaluation*, not a way of dismissing computational work. `universal_basis_audit.py` reconstructed 24 arbitrary random inputs exactly at \(N=16,64,256\).

### Why constant-orbit coverage cannot solve the original goal

For fixed \(g\), the number of different vectors expressible by (4) is at most

\[
\binom Mg(p-1)^g p^{gM},
\]

from shifts, multipliers, and \(gM\) arbitrary baseline coefficients. Dividing by \(p^N\), their fraction among all raw vectors is \(p^{-N+O_g(\sqrt N)}\). The shear-gauged Newton family has the analogous upper bound

\[
M(p-1)p^{dM}\sum_{e\le R}\binom{(Q-d)M}{e}(p-1)^e,
\]

which is also a negligible fraction for fixed \(d\), \(R=O(Q)\), balanced factors and \(p\ge N\). These counts are *restricted-description-size barriers*, **not** lower bounds against every Fourier algorithm.

## 7. Graph, union, sparse tables and quantum-inspired phase — actual roles

- **Graph:** the temporal-evolution graph has \(g\) unknown shear/scale orbits; each Fourier channel yields a tiny Prony/Hankel recurrence. \(g\) orbit nodes connect to an output through a geometric-series edge.
- **Union:** a **single trace sequence** merges the \(g\) unknown spectral modes and exposes global shift parameters. The same global phase tables are shared by all outputs.
- **Sparse tables:** compact \(O(N)\) root and inverse tables, with the full construction cost charged, not a lookup oracle containing spectra for all input vectors.
- **Quantum-inspired phases:** roots of unity encode constructive/destructive interference; nothing here uses a quantum computer or gets \(N\) classical amplitudes from an \(O(\log N)\)-qubit QFT without their measurement/output cost.

## 8. The next exact research challenge

The fixed-\(g\) theorem and \(g=M\) universal representation create a concrete frontier:

> Given a square length \(N=M^2\), *chosen input-independent* shifts \(a_j\), coset-distinct multipliers \(\mu_j\), and arbitrary dense coefficient arrays \(B_j[h]\), find an exact fully charged evaluator for
>
> \[F[h+sM]=\sum_{j=1}^{M} B_j[h]\;\frac{1-\theta_{j,h,s}^{M}}{1-\theta_{j,h,s}},\quad
> \theta_{j,h,s}=\mu_j\omega^{-(h+sM+Ma_jh)},\]
> including \(\theta=1\) limits, in **\(O(N)\) total work** — not \(O(MN)\) — *and* charge the representation recovery from fresh raw input.

This target is equivalent to finding a genuinely joint, input-independent way to merge the dense orbit channels. Merely reusing a known FFT internally, performing \(M\) independent solves, asserting quantum interference is free, or hiding a polynomial-size input oracle does not meet the target. It also has to be compared against the published exact-complex-arithmetic all-length sub-\(N\log N\) construction in the **same** arithmetic model.

## 9. Literature and priority boundary

- Classical Prony/Hankel exponential reconstruction and matrix-pencil methods underlie equation (5), e.g. [Potts & Tasche, Linear Algebra and its Applications (2013)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.laa.2012.10.036), [Almunif et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1002/2050-7038.12283).
- Fourier shift/modulation identities are classical; finite chirp transforms have longstanding study: [Casazza & Fickus (2006)](https://doi.org/10.1155/ASP/2006/70204).
- Lower bounds of \(\Omega(N\log N)\) apply in **restricted** coefficient/conditioning models, not unconditionally to all exact arithmetic: [Morgenstern (1973)](https://doi.org/10.1145/321752.321761), [Ailon (2014)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1403.1307).
- [OpenAI Math #130](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/lean/docs/130.md) documents a uniform exact DFT/convolution result \(O(N(\log N)^{1-10^{-13}})\) in a permissive exact-complex-arithmetic model. Neither of the present conditional executors makes a stronger *universal* claim.

**Verdict:** We derived and reproducibly tested three additional recognized families with exact linear-time full transforms. Their main mathematical novelty *as a research exploration* is the particular union architecture and trace-recovery mechanism, **not** an established first-publication result. The all-input \(O(N)\) FFT and any resulting arbitrary integer-multiplication breakthrough remain open.
