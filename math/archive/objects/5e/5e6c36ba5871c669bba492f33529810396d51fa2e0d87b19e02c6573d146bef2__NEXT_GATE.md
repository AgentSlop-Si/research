# Phase 23 research gates — return to the generic-input FFT obstacle

1. **Test generic-input cross-block sharing, not just sparse-rational islands.** Formalize the smallest exact shared circuit that jointly evaluates all output frequencies on *arbitrary fresh vectors*. Require an input-independent, invertible full-rank map with an all-cost DAG bound `o(NlogN)`. Audit against Cooley–Tukey/diagonal/permutation disguise.
2. **Reduce the expensive constant-size Hankel-resultant compiler.** The self-reciprocal `s=4` polynomial still needs 31 fixed-size determinant evaluations plus three witnesses; reducing their charge can improve the crossover at N<=2048. Batch pivot inversions across interpolation nodes, or symbolically share Hankel-pencil operations. Count all precomputation and inversions.
3. **General trace-gcd root extraction without half-subgroup scans.** If `deg G>1`, exact finite-field polynomial factorization can recover O(b) trace roots. Do not treat factoring or discrete logarithms as free; prove a total complexity bound with fallback for unsupported fields.
4. **Exception completeness.** Handle vanishing early Fourier probes, identically zero resultants, and singular spectral denominators without masking quadratic work. A full cyclic certificate still must guard short-prefix adversaries.
5. **Resource model and stability.** Convert field-operation counts to actual word/bit costs with verified root and nonresidue setup, quantify RAM for BSGS and root tables, and investigate whether a stable complex analogue exists.
6. **Independent theorem audit.** Re-derive Phase 21's universal self-reciprocal resultant in computer algebra or a proof assistant; current formal status is a handwritten derivation plus exact finite-field tests.
7. **Negative baseline gate.** Explicitly reject claims of a general sub-NlogN transform arising solely from constant-sparsity H/Q fits. Those families have bounded algebraic dimension (Phase 21 theorem); dense inputs alone do not imply generic inputs.

**Acceptance criterion:** an unrestricted, all-input, one-query exact Fourier algorithm with a complete `o(NlogN)` arithmetic account in a clearly specified model. No such result is yet available.
