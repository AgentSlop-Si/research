# Priority assessment: ramified Rogers–Szegő squarefreeness

Date: 2026-10-10. This is a bounded primary-source priority and publication-scope assessment, not a global originality certificate or a replacement correctness audit.

## Decision

**This is a materially stronger short-note candidate than the earlier prime-order theorem alone, but publication novelty remains unresolved.** The reusable contribution to investigate is the leading local root-cluster formula at a fixed odd root of unity, its exact ordinary-discriminant coefficient, and the resulting ramified nonvanishing criterion. The three infinite families are meaningful corollaries of that mechanism. They should not be presented as a new polynomial family, a new q-Lucas theorem, a new Hermite limit in general, or a new Padé/realization theory.

The advance is real **within the project**: it replaces finitely many composite tests by a proof for infinitely many composite orders. That fact is independent of whether an older paper already contains an equivalent theorem. No exact antecedent to the full cluster-plus-ordinary-discriminant statement was established in the inspected sources. This negative search outcome does not establish priority.

Two particularly pertinent older original sources still need legitimate full-text review: Lubinsky–Saff (1987), DOI 10.1007/BF01890574, and Zhedanov (1997), DOI 10.1142/S0217751X97000438. These are genuine outstanding checks, not evidence that the theorem is unpublished. The previously denied Vanderbilt PDF route was not revisited or bypassed.

## What would actually be the paper's main theorem?

Use the general degree n=Ad+b statement, rather than only the three base values, as the mathematical center. For odd d, primitive omega of order d, 0<=b<d, and squarefree G=H_b(z;omega) coprime to z^d+1, the report proposes:

1. After q^d-1=epsilon^2 and z=alpha+epsilon*x, each cluster at alpha^d=-1 has a scaled degree-A probabilists' Hermite polynomial as its first nonzero term.
2. The exact ordinary-discriminant order is K=d*A*(A-1)/2, with leading coefficient

   (-1)^(A(d-1)/2) d^(d*A*(A+1)/2) (product_{j=1}^A j^j)^d Disc(G) Res(z^d+1,G)^(2A).

3. Dividing D_n(q) by Phi_d(q)^K in Z[q] before reducing modulo p gives a valid nonvanishing test at primitive dp-th roots. This avoids an unjustified inference from a p-adic first term beyond its available precision.
4. A finite lower-order content c_d controls the existence of at least one good residue factor. For fixed d with c_d nonzero, all odd primes outside the divisors of d*c_d satisfy the sufficient criterion.

The first two statements are the strongest candidate for reusable original mathematics. The third is an application of elementary cyclotomic divisibility to the new coefficient; it should not be promoted as new ramification theory. The fourth packages the unit conditions efficiently, but content reduction itself is standard algebra.

## What the new literature checks change

An exact q-Hermite antecedent to the degenerate factorization is explicit in Atakishiyeva–Atakishiyev–Koornwinder. The standard change of variables transfers their formula to this project; that comparison is derived here. [Primary paper, §2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0811.4100)

The recent Mao–Wang–Zheng interlacing paper does not state the required complex-root-of-unity theorem: its Rogers–Szegő example uses t in [0,1] and a gamma-sign-change/coefficientwise-reciprocal transform. It therefore supplies neither an immediate proof nor a demonstrated duplication. [Example 4.4 and Remark 4.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.34551)

Cigler's supplied identifier was a handoff-only unverified lead and was wrong. The opened correct paper is arXiv:1602.07850; 1602.07854 is an unrelated cylinder paper. Its inspected limit statements concern q approaching 1 or -1 and special evaluations; they do not by themselves provide the arbitrary-odd-root cluster coefficient. [Cigler](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1602.07850)

## Recommended publication scope

A suitable working title is **“Local discriminants of Rogers–Szegő polynomials at odd roots of unity.”** A focused special-functions/algebraic-combinatorics note is plausible after the two outstanding source comparisons. The independent correctness audit has accepted the stated theorem without a mathematical correction. Lead with the local theorem, give the arithmetic criterion and infinite families next, and place the scalar-resolvent application last or in a short appendix. Include the bad-prime and overlap controls: they explain the hypotheses and are more valuable than an enlarged numerical census.

This strengthens the case for continuing mathematical preparation. It is not yet a recommendation to announce a globally new result, and it is not enough by itself to rank this above other project candidates whose novelty and significance have already been more thoroughly established. No venue-acceptance prediction is warranted.

If an older result already gives the family squarefreeness, the local discriminant formula could still be a distinct contribution; compare it separately. If the formula is also old, the appropriate output is a carefully attributed application or expository note, not a novelty claim.

## Scope and stopping conditions

- Preserve p not dividing d and the two independent lower-order unit conditions.
- Preserve the distinction between existence of one good residue factor and goodness at every prime above p.
- Preserve intrinsic-overlap exclusions and the higher-ramification failure when the Hermite discriminant becomes divisible by p.
- Do not infer a general content theorem from three exact values.
- Obtain legitimate full text of the two priority blockers; then compare exact polynomial normalizations, degree ranges, root orders, multiplicities, and derivative versus q-operator discriminants.
- No public posting, author contact, submission, account access, or external sharing was performed.
