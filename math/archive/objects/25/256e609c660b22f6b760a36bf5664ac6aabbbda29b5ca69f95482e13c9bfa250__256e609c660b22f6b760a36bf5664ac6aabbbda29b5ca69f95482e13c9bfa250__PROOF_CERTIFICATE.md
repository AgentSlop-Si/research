# Exact proof certificate: optimally ordered power-of-two Fourier matrices

**Date:** 2026-10-09  
**Status:** Complete proof *within a single-matrix quasiseparable-rank optimization problem*. Not a new FFT circuit complexity bound, not a new exact DFT algorithm. No literature-priority claim.

Let \(N=2^m\), \(m\ge3\), and \(F_N=(\omega^{ab})_{a,b=0}^{N-1}\), where \(\omega=\exp(2\pi i/N)\). A permutation on the row/output coordinates and a permutation on the column/input coordinates may be chosen independently. Let

\[
q(A)=\max_{1\le k<N}\max\{\operatorname{rank}A[0:k,k:N],\operatorname{rank}A[k:N,0:k]\}.
\]

## Theorem 1. Exact optimal reordered quasiseparable rank

**For every \(m\ge3\),**

\[
\boxed{\min_{\rho,\sigma\in S_N}q(P_\rho F_NP_\sigma^T)=N/4+1.}
\]

One minimizing pair consists of bit-reversal on **both** endpoints.

### Proof of the universal lower bound using a Fourier projection moment

Write \(N=4L\), \(L\ge2\). Let \(H,I\subset\mathbb Z_N\) both have cardinality \(h=t=2L+1\), and \(R=\mathbb Z_N\setminus I\), of size \(2L-1\). Put \(U=F_N/\sqrt N\), unitary, and let \(P_H,P_I\) be coordinate orthogonal projections. On \(\mathbb C^H\), the positive contraction

\[A=P_H U^*P_IUP_H\]

has an eigenvalue 1 exactly when the corresponding \(x\) is supported in \(H\) and \(Ux\) is supported in \(I\). Thus the multiplicity \(d\) of eigenvalue 1 equals

\[d=\dim\ker(F_N[R,H])=h-\operatorname{rank}F_N[R,H].\]

All eigenvalues of \(A\) lie in \([0,1]\), hence \(d\le\operatorname{tr}(A^2)\).

Let \(\widehat{1_I}(s)=\sum_{a\in I}\omega^{as}\), and \(c_H(s)=|\{b\in H:b+s\in H\}|\). Expanding the Hilbert--Schmidt norm,

\[
\operatorname{tr}(A^2)=\frac1{N^2}\sum_{s\in\mathbb Z_N}c_H(s)|\widehat{1_I}(s)|^2.
\]

We have \(c_H(0)=h\). For any nonzero \(s\in\mathbb Z_{2^m}\), addition by \(s\) has even order. Since \(h=2L+1\) is **odd**, \(H\) cannot be invariant under this translation: \(c_H(s)\le h-1\). Fourier Parseval gives \(\sum_s|\widehat{1_I}(s)|^2=Nt\), and \(|\widehat{1_I}(0)|^2=t^2\). Consequently,

\[
\operatorname{tr}(A^2)\le
\frac{h t^2+(h-1)(Nt-t^2)}{N^2}
=\frac{(h-1)Nt+t^2}{N^2}.
\]

With \(N=4L\), \(h=t=2L+1\), this equals

\[
L+\frac34+\frac1{4L}+\frac1{16L^2}<L+1\qquad(L\ge2).
\]

Therefore \(d\le L\), and **every** \((2L-1)\times(2L+1)\) Fourier submatrix has rank \(\ge (2L+1)-L=L+1\). For *any* reordered Fourier matrix, its upper-right cut after the first \(2L-1\) rows is such a submatrix. This proves \(q\ge L+1\), independent of either endpoint permutation. \(\square\)

### Proof of matching bit-reversal upper bound: exact recurrence

Let \(b_m(j)\) denote reversal of the \(m\) binary digits of \(j\), and let \(Q_N[i,j]=\omega^{b_m(i)b_m(j)}\). Set

\[r_N(k)=\operatorname{rank}Q_N[0:k,k:N],\qquad r_N(0)=r_N(N)=0.\]

For \(N=4L\) and \(0\le k\le N/2=2L\), we prove

\[
\boxed{r_N(k)=\lceil k/2\rceil+r_L(\lfloor k/2\rfloor).}\tag{1}
\]

The first \(2L\) bit-reversal positions are exactly the **even** Fourier indices. Write their actual frequency labels as \(2q_i\), where

\[q_{2j}=b_{m-2}(j),\quad q_{2j+1}=L+b_{m-2}(j),\qquad 0\le j<L.\]

Let \(p=\lfloor k/2\rfloor\) and \(d=\lceil k/2\rceil\). The first \(k\) frequencies contain \(p\) full pairs \(\{q,q+L\}\) and, if \(k\) is odd, one unpaired frequency. They contain exactly \(d\) different residues modulo \(L\).

All odd natural-time columns \(2\ell+1\) occur to the right of the cut. Evaluated at the selected even frequencies, these columns give

\[\omega_N^{(2q)(2\ell+1)}=\omega_{2L}^{q(2\ell+1)}.
\]

Their span has dimension exactly \(d\): after removing nonzero diagonal phases, the evaluations on the \(d\) distinct residues \(q\bmod L\) form a complete-row-rank Vandermonde submatrix of \(F_L\), while the two rows \(q,q+L\) always have opposite signs. Quotienting the output space by the odd-column span therefore leaves precisely \(p\) coordinates, one for each complete pair; a quotient coordinate can be taken as the **sum** of the two paired rows.

Each remaining even natural-time column \(2\ell\), after this quotient, evaluates to a nonzero scalar times \(\omega_L^{q\ell}\) on those \(p\) paired rows. Modulo \(L\), their actual \(\ell\) indices are exactly the bit-reversal suffix \(b_{m-2}(p),\ldots,b_{m-2}(L-1)\); paired row residues are the bit-reversal prefix \(b_{m-2}(0),\ldots,b_{m-2}(p-1)\). The quotient-column rank is consequently \(r_L(p)\), proving (1).

For \(k>N/2\), simultaneous index reversal satisfies

\[Q_N[N-1-i,N-1-j]=\omega_N^{1+b_m(i)+b_m(j)}Q_N[i,j].\]

This differs from \(Q_N\) by invertible row/column diagonal scalings. Since \(Q_N\) is symmetric, it follows that \(r_N(k)=r_N(N-k)\). Base cases are \(r_1(0)=r_1(1)=0\) and \(r_2(1)=1\); (1) is valid for all \(N\ge4\).

If \(k=2a\le2L\), (1) yields \(r_N(k)=a+r_L(a)\le L\) since \(r_L(a)\le L-a\). If \(k=2a+1<2L\), then \(r_N(k)=a+1+r_L(a)\le L+1\). In particular \(k=2L-1\) has \(a=L-1\), and \(r_L(L-1)=1\) because every Fourier matrix entry is nonzero. Thus the upper bound \(L+1\) is attained, proving Theorem 1. \(\square\)

## Theorem 2. Asymptotically optimal total cut-rank sum

For any independently reordered Fourier matrix \(M\), define the upper-right total \(S(M)=\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}\operatorname{rank}(M[0:k,k:N])\). Then

\[
\boxed{S(M)\ge (N^2-1)/6.}\tag{2}
\]

Indeed, for each cut take \(H\) to be its \(N-k\) input columns, and \(I\) the \(N-k\) Fourier output frequencies absent from its first \(k\) output rows. The same positive contraction above gives, using only its **first trace**,

\[d=|H|-\operatorname{rank} F_N[I^c,H]\le\operatorname{tr}(A)=|H||I|/N=(N-k)^2/N.\]

Consequently \(r(k)\ge k(N-k)/N\). Summing gives \(\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}k(N-k)/N=(N^2-1)/6\), proving (2).

For bit reversal, let \(S_N=\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}r_N(k)\). The recurrence and the symmetry give

\[S_N=N^2/8+N/4+4S_{N/4},\quad S_1=0,\quad S_2=1.\]

Writing \(N=2^m\), solving separately for the parity of \(m\) yields the **exact integer formula**

\[
\boxed{S_N=\begin{cases}
N^2/6+N(m/8-1/6),&m\text{ even},\\[2pt]
N^2/6+N(m/8+1/24),&m\text{ odd}.
\end{cases}}\tag{3}
\]

Therefore \(S_N=N^2/6+O(N\log N)\), asymptotically matching (2). Because the reordered Fourier matrix is symmetric, the lower-left cut ranks equal the upper-right ranks at each cut. \(\square\)

## Corollary. A one-stage limitation, not an FFT lower bound

Even after arbitrary independent endpoint permutations, one full Fourier operator needs quasiseparable rank at least \(N/4+1\). Therefore it cannot be implemented as **one fixed-rank** quasiseparable scan stage for growing \(N\). Theorems 1--2 are statements about the **matrix representation of one stage**; they do not rule out a product of many cheaper stages, nonlinear precomputation, alternate computation models, or a sub-\(N\log N\) Fourier circuit.

## Supplement: exact dyadic coset rank identity

For \(N=QL\), \(Q=2^a\), select a set \(B\subseteq\{0,\ldots,Q-1\}\) of *full* input cosets \(S=\bigcup_{b\in B}\{b+Qj:0\le j<L\}\). For **any** Fourier output row set \(K\), partition it into \(T_q=\{s:q+Ls\in K\}\), for \(0\le q<L\). Then

\[\boxed{\operatorname{rank} F_N[K,S]=\sum_{q=0}^{L-1}\operatorname{rank}F_Q[T_q,B].}\]

The proof applies an invertible length-\(L\) Fourier transform separately to the \(j\) columns of each input coset. This block-diagonalizes by the output residue \(q=k\bmod L\), leaving the \(Q\)-point Fourier blocks up to invertible row and column scalings. For a single consecutive portion \(b+Qj\), \(j=0,\ldots,s-1\), the restricted matrix is a Vandermonde matrix in the distinct bases indexed by \(k\bmod L\), hence its rank is exactly \(\min(s,|\{k\bmod L:k\in K\}|)\).

This identity explains exact rank deficiencies under non-affine/digit endpoint orderings. It is a specialization of standard Fourier group decomposition; no new priority is asserted.
