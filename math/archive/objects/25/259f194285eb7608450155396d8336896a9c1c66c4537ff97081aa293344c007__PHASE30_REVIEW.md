For fixed source row/column triples r,c and target rows/columns 0,1,2, the matrix E_ij=B_h[r_i,c_j]/T_ij must have rank one under any successful monomial equivalence. The target entries are nonzero, so these divisions are legitimate. Clearing them gives all nine homogeneous 2×2-minor equations of the selected 3×3 matrix.

Multiplying those nine quadrics by all four variables produces 36 homogeneous cubics in a 20-dimensional coefficient space. Rank 20 means every cubic, including each pure variable cube, belongs to the ideal. A common nonzero projective zero would make all four cubes vanish, a contradiction.

This is an exact necessary-condition veto. It even excludes singular/zero-entry middle matrices and ν=0 projective boundary points, so no saturation is needed for this full-span negative certificate. The affine family ν=1 is contained in the excluded projective family. Zero individual term coefficients are allowed. It remains essential that A,C, all cycle orders, term couplings and term gauges are fixed.

Every complete permutation pair restricts to one of the ordered triples, so exhaustive triple enumeration is sufficient. At N=4 a triple determines the remaining permutation coordinate; at larger N many full permutations share the same triple. No random coverage inference is made.

## 4. Why characteristic-zero lifting is legitimate

Every matrix entry is defined over Q(ζ_N) with fixed rational denominators. The chosen finite-field roots have exact order N. Sparse resolvent denominators and the outer inverses are verified nonsingular at the reduction, and all T entries are verified nonzero there. Thus a valid homomorphism exists from the required localized cyclotomic ring to the audit field.

For every checked mapping, a nonzero 20×20 modular minor implies that the corresponding exact cyclotomic minor is nonzero. Consequently the same Macaulay matrix has full rank in characteristic zero and no complex projective solution. This is a nonvanishing-minor lift, not a claim that a failed finite-field point search excludes complex points.

The source's conditional lifting statement and its use here are correct. It would fail if the roots had the wrong order or a needed denominator reduced to zero; those checks are explicitly performed. Agreement at one prime would not suffice for a positive characteristic-zero identity.

## 5. Anchored zeros and the projective dimension threshold

With four homogeneous parameters and a 3-column anchor row, setting those three anchor entries to zero always has a nonzero parameter solution. The anchored equations then vanish automatically, even though the point cannot have the required everywhere-nonzero target support. This is why simply increasing the anchored Macaulay degree cannot repair the problem. Taking all minors removes the privileged anchor; any remaining rank-one point with zeros is still extraneous, but a full-span negative certificate excludes it along with all other points.

For an m×m window, nonzero rank-one matrices form the Segre variety P^(m−1)×P^(m−1), dimension 2m−2 in ambient P^(m²−1). A q-dimensional injective linear coefficient space gives a projective P^(q−1). The standard projective intersection theorem forces intersection when the two dimensions sum to at least the ambient dimension, yielding q≥(m−1)²+1. The primary [Stacks Project, Lemma 33.34.3](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0B2R) states the needed projective nonemptiness theorem.

A useful clarification strengthens the gate limitation: if the restriction to the selected window is not injective, a nonzero coefficient vector already produces the zero matrix, and all its minors vanish. Hence q≥(m−1)²+1 precludes an unsaturated homogeneous emptiness certificate in parameter space whether the window components are independent or not. Global generator independence alone must not be confused with independence after restricting to a window.

For q≤(m−1)², generic avoidance is possible in the ambient linear-space model, but is not guaranteed for every structured Green family. Passing a small-window test does not certify the full matrix, nonzero entries, ν≠0, a valid scaling/permutation reconstruction, or a scalable algorithm.

## 7. Preparation cost and construction implications
