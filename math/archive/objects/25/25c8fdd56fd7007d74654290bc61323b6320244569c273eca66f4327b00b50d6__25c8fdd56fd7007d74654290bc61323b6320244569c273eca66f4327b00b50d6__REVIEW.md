# Independent review: seven-wire state transport

9 October 2026 UTC. No producer program was executed or imported. No sparse Fourier transport witness has been constructed.

## 1. Exact state realization and all branches

Use state v=(x,y11,y12,z1,y21,y22,z2), each wire of length N, and equations

    x=E xin,
    U1 y11-x=0,        V1 y12-x=0,
    z1-alpha y11-beta y12=0,
    U2 y21-M z1=0,    V2 y22-M z1=0,
    z2-gamma y21-delta y22=0.

Here U1,V1,U2,V2 are known invertible routed grid denominators, M is the middle monomial, B injects E into wire 0, and C selects D times wire 6. Then

    T=C K^-1 B = D (gamma U2^-1+delta V2^-1) M
                      (alpha U1^-1+beta V1^-1) E.

K is block lower triangular with diagonal blocks I,U1,V1,I,U2,V2,I. Therefore it is invertible for all scalar couplings, including pure branches and all-zero stage pairs. Singularity of a stage sum does not make the state system singular. Even zero endpoints or middle coefficients leave this state-system conclusion unchanged, although they may prevent an invertible Fourier transfer.

Each row and column of K has at most four nonzeros. For the displayed grid denominators the structural nonzero count is at most 23N-8M_grid, where M_grid is the grid side; zero weights may reduce it. B and C each have at most N nonzeros. Applying K^-1 follows the displayed finite sequence of known routed grid solves and is O(N) given all coefficients and routes. No free dense inverse oracle is used.

## 2. Transport implication and Fourier sign

For a boundary pair X,Y, suppose state matrices R,S satisfy

    K S=R K,    R B=B X,    C S=Y C.

Since K is invertible, K^-1 R=S K^-1. Therefore

    T X=C K^-1 B X=C K^-1 R B=C S K^-1 B=Y T.

Neither R nor S must be invertible.

For F[i,j]=q^(-i j), let Z e_j=e_(j+1 mod N) and W=diag(q^-i). The two pairs are

    (X,Y)=(Z,W),     (X,Y)=(W,Z^-1).

Their transfer consequences are T Z=W T and T W=Z^-1 T. The first gives T[i,j]=a_i q^(-i j); the second gives a_i=a_(i+1). Thus T=c F. Nonzero calibration only establishes c!=0; exact unnormalized F additionally requires c=1 or explicitly applying c^-1. The scalar can be measured by one known entry, e.g. T[0,0], using a charged O(N) application.

## 3. Exact no-go for wire-block-diagonal transports

Assume both R and S are diagonal in the seven N-dimensional wire blocks for each of the two Weyl pairs. Assume endpoints D,E and middle M are invertible, as they must be in any nonzero full Fourier realization of the displayed square product. Write S_j,R_j for wire blocks.

The literal K equations imply

    R0=S0=E X E^-1,
    R1=R2=S0,
    U1 S1=S0 U1,       V1 S2=S0 V1,
    S3=R3,
    alpha!=0 implies S1=S3,
    beta !=0 implies S2=S3,
    R4=R5=M S3 M^-1,
    U2 S4=R4 U2,       V2 S5=R5 V2,
    S6=R6,
    gamma!=0 implies S4=S6,
    delta!=0 implies S5=S6,
    D S6=Y D.

Choose any active first branch Ua in {U1,V1} and any active second branch Ub in {U2,V2}. These equations force the individual invertible path

    P=D Ub^-1 M Ua^-1 E

to satisfy P X=Y P. The active set is determined by the same scalar couplings for both Weyl pairs, so this same path satisfies both identities. Hence P is a nonzero scalar multiple of F.

But

    P^-1=E^-1 Ua M^-1 Ub D^-1

has at most nine nonzeros per row, since each component denominator has at most three and monomial factors preserve support counts. Fourier inversion is dense. This is impossible when N>9.

Pure first or second branches are fully included. If a whole stage pair is zero, then T=0 and nonzero Fourier calibration fails. Singular nonzero stage sums are also incompatible with nonzero T=cF. Zero endpoint diagonal entries cannot be an escape for the target, since they force an entire zero transfer row or column.

The conclusion is precise: a successful transport certificate for N>9 must have an off-diagonal wire block in at least one R or S. It does not identify which pair of wires must mix, exclude more general sparse transports, or exclude a product of sums itself.

## 4. Boundary constraints for a mixed-wire search

With invertible E,D, even unrestricted transports obey

    R[i,0]=0 for i!=0,      R[0,0]=E X E^-1,
    S[6,j]=0 for j!=6,      S[6,6]=D^-1 Y D.

These follow directly from R B=B X and C S=Y C. Off-diagonal candidates must respect them. They are constraints on a future witness, not a witness construction.

## 5. Verification and coefficient ledger

Let each transport have at most d nonzeros per row. Each row of K S or R K generates at most 4d product contributions. Direct-index accumulators with per-row stamps combine repeated positions and compare differences deterministically in O(Nd) arithmetic/index operations, using O(N) temporary storage. The two boundary products cost O(Nd) as well because B,C,X,Y have bounded row support. Thus fixed d gives an O(N) verifier and O(N) witness storage. No sorting or dense N-by-N product is required.

This is conditional on receiving explicit indexed coefficients for R,S and for the grid/middle/endpoint maps. Finding the witness or generating its coefficients is not free. A scalable Fourier claim needs an explicit O(Nd) synthesis procedure, exact identities at every size, nonzero calibration, and the cost of producing q and required powers. Unit-cost algebra does not establish bit complexity or numerical stability.

## 6. Independent controls

At N=4 and N=16, sixteen literal state tests include generic, pure, cancelling and zero scalar pairs. Each verifies K times the constructed state response equals B, the output response equals the direct product, K has the expected nonzero block-product determinant, and maximum row/column degree is at most four. Eight independent component paths verify the displayed inverse formula and support bound nine. Fourier Weyl signs and dense inverse support are independently checked.

Separate abstract transport controls use singular R,S on unused state coordinates and verify all three transport equations and the implied boundary identity. These validate the algebraic lemma only; they are explicitly not seven-wire Green Fourier realizations.

Novelty has not been assessed. The open constructive question remains an explicit bounded-degree mixed-wire witness, not merely another formulation of full Fourier equality.
