## Accepted rank and probability results

For stable binary sorting, let W be the successor in the sorted coordinate order and Z the original cyclic successor. If T counts noncyclic bit transitions, then over every field

rank(W−Z)=2 floor(T/2).

For iid fair bits, rank 2k has probability binom(N,2k+1)/2^(N−1). For N≥3 its mean is N/2−1 and its variance is N/4. The exact zero-rank event has 2N binary words. More generally, a fixed rank cap 2k covers only 2 sum_(j=0)^k binom(N,2j+1) words, polynomially many out of 2^N.

The source's simultaneous all-stride bound also checks out: for all a=1,…,N−1 at once, rank(W−Z^a)≥N/8 with an explicit probability tending to one. At N=8192 the lower bound is approximately 0.995693. The result includes noncoprime strides and does not assume all cyclic powers are Hamiltonian cycles. The all-stride theorem addresses a gap that a standard-shift-only obstruction would leave.

These are restrictions on correction rank, not unrestricted arithmetic lower bounds. A high-rank correction might still have some unrelated fast representation or evaluator. See `rank_audit/RANK_AUDIT.md` for proofs and scope.

## Corrections that should accompany reuse

1. The source says every mixed word produces one nontrivial defect cycle and s=rank(W−Z)+1 changed edges. This fails for a one-transition word such as (0,1): W=Z and s=0. Correctly, s=0 for T≤1 and s=rank+1 for T≥2. The main rank theorem is unaffected.
2. The submitted identity-order Woodbury branch recomputes pow(z,N,p) inside an N-output comprehension. Under ordinary exponentiation this costs Θ(N log N) modular multiplication work; compute it once before the comprehension to recover the intended linear arithmetic count. This is a fixable code defect, not a contradiction in the identity.
3. Phase24 rejects z^N=1. Phase23's unpermuted-character shortcut does not directly evaluate a generally permuted character. The original Phase24 module therefore is not an all-z executor. The independently supplied sparse method handles the one exceptional Fourier bin by a charged direct O(N) sum.
4. Sorting-based permutation validation and repeated scalar inversions must be accounted for. An explicit seen-array validator and batch inversion make the intended costs transparent. A generic sorting bound alone does not prove a superlinear lower bound at fixed edge defect, since such orders have few increasing runs.

## Exact positive construction and independent improvement

The Woodbury representation is valid when z^N≠1. With s changed successor edges, its small correction matrix K is always nonsingular; in fact det K=1 because both conjugate cycle resolvents have determinant 1−z^N. The source's dense formation/evaluation schedule has an O(N(s+1)²+s³) field-operation upper bound when preparation is charged. This is not an intrinsic lower bound on every evaluator for the same structured input.

A simpler route works with x_(L[k])=z^k and the twisted cyclic difference d=(I−zZ)x. Its support is at most 2s+1. Compute the DFT of d by explicitly accumulating its sparse columns, divide each regular output by 1−z omega^k, and evaluate the sole possibly singular bin directly from x. This takes O(N(s+1)) field arithmetic, O(N) memory, one batched field inversion, and charged root/power/route preparation. It works for all z, including zero and z^N=1. Conversion to the real residual costs a further O(N) operations given its normalization constants.

`woodbury_audit/linear_twisted_dft.py` is the standalone independently authored unnormalized-DFT evaluator, with no dense Fourier matrix setup. The audit harness separately constructs dense reference matrices and performs expensive exact checks; those verification costs are not claimed as part of the fast evaluator. See `woodbury_audit/WOODBURY_AUDIT.md` for the derivation and complete cost ledger.

This is a positive near-order geometric-input result. The supplied permutation and geometric node specify a low-dimensional family. For a raw fresh vector, membership in that specified family must be checked, which costs O(N); an arbitrary vector need not belong. For random stable sorting, the defect is usually linear and this schedule becomes quadratic. Finite-field arithmetic is not a free bit-operation model, and a fixed supported prime does not establish an all-size complex FFT theorem.

No claim of external peer review, publication novelty, unrestricted lower bound, or general faster FFT follows from the source or these finite checks.

The meaningful next gate is still a scalable exact identity plus an evaluator for every fresh input, with complete exceptions and charged setup, data routing, constants, arithmetic, and precision. High cut rank, exact small examples, covariance, or fast special inputs alone do not meet that gate.
