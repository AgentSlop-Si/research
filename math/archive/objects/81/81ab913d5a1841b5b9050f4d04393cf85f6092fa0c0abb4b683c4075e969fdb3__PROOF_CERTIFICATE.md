# Phase 9 proof certificate — exact Fourier root exchanges

**Date:** 2026-10-10  
**Status:** Theorems proved algebraically and checked by independent finite-field tests. **No unrestricted exact O(N) FFT.**

## 1. Model and notation

Let `N=2m>=2`, and let `K` be any field containing all `N` distinct roots of `z^N=1`. In particular, the field has characteristic different from 2 and has a primitive `N`th root `omega`.

The roots are `z_j=omega^(-j)`, indexed `j=0,...,N-1`. Define `E={z_0,z_2,...,z_(N-2)}` and `O={z_1,z_3,...,z_(N-1)}`. Consequently `e^m=1` for `e in E`, `o^m=-1` for `o in O`.

Exchange any `t` distinct roots `e_1,...,e_t` from `E` with `t` distinct roots `o_1,...,o_t` from `O`, forming

- `A=(E\{e_i}) ∪ {o_i}` and
- `B=(O\{o_i}) ∪ {e_i}`.

Write an arbitrary degree-`<2m` polynomial as `f(z)=L(z)+z^m H(z)`, where `deg L,deg H<m`. Let `R_A` (respectively `R_B`) denote the `m×m` coefficient matrix taking `H` to `z^m H mod P_A` (respectively `mod P_B`), with `P_A(z)=∏_{a∈A}(z-a)`.

Therefore the two simultaneous remainder coefficient vectors are `a=L+R_A H`, `b=L+R_B H`. All ranks in this certificate are over `K`.

## 2. Involution and exact individual correction ranks

Let `V_A` be the square Vandermonde matrix evaluating a degree-`<m` coefficient vector at all the distinct roots in `A`. The remainder property gives

`V_A R_A = diag(a_j^m : a_j∈A) V_A`.

Since every `a_j^m` equals `+1` or `-1`,

`R_A²=I`, and `rank(R_A-I)=#(A∩O)=t`.

Likewise `R_B²=I` and `rank(R_B+I)=#(B∩E)=t`.

This does *not* imply sparse matrix entries. For `N=16,t=1`, both `R_A` and `R_B` can have all 64 entries nonzero even though their corrections have rank one.

## 3. Root-exchange interpolation identity

The baseline remainders for the even and odd cosets are `L+H` and `L-H`, respectively. For each newcomer `o_i∈A∩O`, let `ell_{A,o_i}` be its unique degree-`<m` Lagrange cardinal polynomial on `A`:

`ell_{A,o_i}(z) = P_A(z) / ((z-o_i) P_A'(o_i))`.

Define `ell_{B,e_j}` analogously. Direct evaluation at the `m` roots of each side gives the *exact* identities

```
a(z) = L(z)+H(z) - 2 ∑_{i=1}^t H(o_i) ell_{A,o_i}(z)
b(z) = L(z)-H(z) + 2 ∑_{j=1}^t H(e_j) ell_{B,e_j}(z)
```

as coefficient polynomials of degree `<m`.

**Proof:** On all retained even roots, the `ell_A` terms vanish and `z^m=1`, so `a=f`. On the incoming odd roots, exactly one cardinal term survives and `L+H-2H=L-H=f`. Uniqueness of interpolation through `m` distinct roots establishes the first identity. The second is symmetric.

For `t=1` and exchanged `e∈E,o∈O`, synthetic division yields an especially simple pair:

```
a(z)=L(z)+H(z)+(o-e) H(o) * (z^m-1)/(z-e)
b(z)=L(z)-H(z)+(e-o) H(e) * (z^m+1)/(z-o)
```

Both quotients are degree `m-1` geometric polynomials computable in O(m) arithmetic.

## 4. Exact stacked correction rank

Let `U_A` collect the t coefficient columns `ell_{A,o_i}`, and `U_B` collect `ell_{B,e_j}`. Let `V_O` and `V_E` evaluate a degree-`<m` polynomial at the incoming odd and removed even roots, respectively. Then

`R_A-I=-2 U_A V_O`, and `R_B+I=+2 U_B V_E`.

The map `(V_O;V_E)` evaluates a polynomial at `2t` distinct roots, so it has rank `min(m,2t)` (rectangular Vandermonde). The stacked map is

```
[ R_A-I ]   [ -2 U_A   0 ] [ V_O ]
[ R_B+I ] = [   0    2 U_B] [ V_E ].
```

Both cardinal-column collections are linearly independent individually; their block-diagonal placement is injective. Therefore

`rank([R_A-I ; R_B+I]) = min(m,2t)`.

This is the *minimum number of rank-one correction channels* in the particular stacked additive-correction grammar, not a lower bound on unrestricted Fourier arithmetic circuits.

## 5. Stronger symmetric, common-basis-invariant reflection theorem

The two remainder transfers are involutions. Moreover, the complete CRT evaluation transform factors through `[[I,R_A],[I,R_B]]`. It is invertible because every input polynomial of degree `<N` is uniquely determined by its values at all N roots. Thus `R_A-R_B` is invertible (the Phase 8 bridge theorem).

We prove the stronger exact identities

```
rank(R_A + R_B)
= rank(R_A R_B + I)
= rank(R_A R_B - R_B R_A)
= 2 min(t,m-t).
```

**Proof for `2t<=m`:** The root-exchange identities show `R_A+R_B=2(U_B V_E-U_A V_O)`. The combined coefficient map `U=[U_A U_B]` has full column rank `2t`: a polynomial in the intersection of their two image spaces vanishes at the retained `m-t` even and `m-t` odd roots. Those `2(m-t)>=m` roots are distinct; a degree-`<m` polynomial vanishing at all of them is zero. The evaluation map `V=[V_O;V_E]` has full row rank `2t` by the Vandermonde property. Therefore `rank(R_A+R_B)=2t`.

**For `2t>m`:** swap the labels `A` and `B`. This replaces `t` with `m-t` and leaves `R_A+R_B` unchanged. Hence its rank is `2(m-t)`.

Finally,

`R_A(R_A+R_B)=I+R_A R_B`, and

`(R_A+R_B)(R_A-R_B)=-(R_A R_B-R_B R_A)`.

Since `R_A` and `R_A-R_B` are invertible, the three ranks coincide. QED.

**Corollary:** `R_A,R_B` commute iff `t=0` or `t=m`. Thus only the two classical parity root splits give commuting remainder transfer involutions (in this exact two-child model).

**Gauge invariance:** Any shared invertible basis conjugation `R_A→S R_A S^{-1}`, `R_B→S R_B S^{-1}` preserves the rank of their commutator and product-plus-identity. This corrects a weakness of Phase 5's basis-sensitive transition-mask rank.

## 6. Cauchy cross systems and inverse CRT bridge

Let `U=[U_A U_B]` and `V=[V_O;V_E]`. Subtracting the two child remainders gives

`y=(a-b)/2=(I-U V)H`.

The 2t×2t matrix `I-VU` is block anti-diagonal:

```
I-VU = [ 0  -C ]
       [ -D  0 ]
```

where

`C_(i,j)=ell_{B,e_j}(o_i)` and `D_(j,i)=ell_{A,o_i}(e_j)`.

Their elements factor as

```
C_(i,j) = P_B(o_i) / [(o_i-e_j) P_B'(e_j)]
D_(j,i) = P_A(e_j) / [(e_j-o_i) P_A'(o_i)].
```

The polynomial evaluations and derivatives are all nonzero. Each is a diagonally scaled Cauchy matrix with pairwise distinct row and column nodes, hence both `C` and `D` are nonsingular.

Let `v_O=V_O y`, `v_E=V_E y`. Then set

```
z_O = -D^(-1) v_E
z_E = -C^(-1) v_O
H   = y + U_A z_O + U_B z_E
L   = a - H + 2 U_A z_O.
```

The expressions recover the *arbitrary original coefficients* exactly from the two remainders. They are an explicit small-system version of the Woodbury inverse; no full m×m inverse is needed.

## 7. Complexity and charged accounting

Assume the primitive Nth root is supplied, field arithmetic is charged at unit cost, and `t` root-exchanges are specified as part of the input-independent plan. The code charges all root-power generation, monic synthetic divisions, polynomial multiplications by linear factors, cardinal coefficients, cross-node evaluations, inverses, and t×t Gauss elimination. It also charges all online Horner evaluations, additions, multiplications and output writes.

- **Preparation:** `O(N+mt+t³)` field additions/multiplications, `O(t²)` inversions, `O(N+mt+t²)` memory or less. In modular arithmetic the inverse implementation has a separate logarithmic-in-p word complexity, still within O(N) for fixed t and p polynomial in N.
- **Forward two-child remaindering:** `O(m(t+1))` operations and 2m output writes.
- **Inverse two-child remaindering:** `O(m(t+1)+t²)` operations and 2m output writes.
- **A complete DFT** must then evaluate both child polynomials at all their assigned roots. The runnable nonperiodic-root hybrid uses two ordinary length-m FFTs plus `2t` direct Horner evaluations, costing `O(N log N+tN+t³)` *including this plan*. It is still **not** o(N log N) for fixed t.

For a random balanced root partition, the exact number of configurations with t incoming odd roots is `binom(m,t)^2`. For fixed t, the fraction of low-exchange partitions decreases exponentially with m; thus this linear-cost bridge is highly special as a root-partition strategy even though input coefficients themselves are arbitrary.

## 8. Prior art and claims boundary

This certificate combines classic Lagrange interpolation, finite Fourier-root properties, polynomial CRT, involutions, and Cauchy matrices. Their foundations and Cauchy inverse formulas long predate this project; historical novelty of the rank-commutator identity has NOT been established. The executable tests are finite-field certificates of the identities for tested instances, not a complete formal proof machine check. The proofs above are algebraic for all valid N and K. No bit-complexity improvement or numerically stable floating-point implementation is claimed.
