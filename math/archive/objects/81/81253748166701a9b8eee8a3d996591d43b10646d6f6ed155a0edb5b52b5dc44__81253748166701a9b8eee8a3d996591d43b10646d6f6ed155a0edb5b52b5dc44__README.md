# H-specific branch fusion: exact relations and a checked obstruction

This checkpoint uses the actual q-factorial coefficients to connect the even
and odd branches of the previous complete decimation. The identities are
stronger than the generic polynomial split, but they do **not** reduce its
three recursive calls. The most direct rewrite needs four calls to one odd
kernel. A precise determinant certificate rules out a restricted two-call
factorization of that kernel. It is not a lower bound against general branch
fusion or FFT algorithms.

The independent exact arbitrary-input/all-output FFT goal remains unresolved.
All finite-boundary terms, initial preparation and output work remain charged.
No external fast FFT theorem is imported and no global novelty is claimed.

## Exact finite H-specific relations

Let N=2^L>=4 and m=N/2, with q a supplied primitive Nth root in an exact field
of characteristic not dividing 2N, and

\[
t_j=\left(\prod_{a=1}^j(1-q^a)\right)^{-1},\quad
E(z)=\sum_{j<m}t_{2j}z^j,\quad
O(z)=\sum_{j<m}t_{2j+1}z^j.
\]

The normalized first split from the previous checkpoint is

\[
H_q\!\begin{pmatrix}0&D\\I&0\end{pmatrix}
=\begin{pmatrix}E(D)&D O(D)\\O(D)&E(D)\end{pmatrix},       \tag{1}
\]

where `D=(I+J)(I+qJ)diag(q^(-2i))`, J is the length-m upper cyclic shift,
`D^m=0`, and `J D=q^(-2)D J`. The original finite corner in C is included in
the similarity that produces (1); it is not discarded here.

With `tau f(z)=f(q^2 z)`, the exact coefficient relations give

\[
E=(1-q\tau)O,\qquad
(1-\tau)E=zO-t_{N-1}z^m.                                 \tag{2}
\]

The second identity has a **nonzero final coefficient**. Evaluating at D
legally removes it because D^m=0. Evaluating at J would leave
`-t_(N-1)I`, since J^m=I. The audit checks both outcomes exactly.

Conjugation by J therefore yields

\[
E(D)=O(D)-qJ^{-1}O(D)J,                                  \tag{3}
\]

\[
D O(D)=O(D)-(1+q)J^{-1}O(D)J+qJ^{-2}O(D)J^2.             \tag{4}
\]

There is also an exact norm identity:

\[
E(D)^2-D O(D)^2=H_{q^2}(D).                              \tag{5}
\]

For completeness, set `H(z)=E(z^2)+zO(z^2)`. The finite q-difference identity
`H(qz)=(1-z)H(z)` holds modulo z^N. Thus `H(z)H(-z)` is even and its
coefficient series R(s) obeys `R(q^2s)=(1-s)R(s)` modulo s^m, with R(0)=1.
The nonzero factors `1-q^(2j)` for j<m uniquely determine R as H_(q^2).
This proves (5), including its truncation. These derivations are finite
algebra, not an unbounded-series substitution at a root of unity.

## Fully evaluated four-call candidate

For an arbitrary pair of independent half-vectors x,y, use four odd-kernel
actions: `Ox`, `Oy`, `O(Jx)`, `O(Jy)`. Then (1) is exactly

\[
\begin{aligned}
u&=Ox-qJ^{-1}O(Jx)+D(Oy),\\
v&=Ox+Oy-qJ^{-1}O(Jy).
\end{aligned}                                             \tag{6}
\]

The phase shifts and D have O(N) action here, so if A_O(m) denotes the actual
cost of the complete odd-kernel action, (6) costs `4 A_O(m)+O(N)`, including
the original gauge and basis maps. Linearity shares `O(x+y)` and `O(J(x+y))`
as sums of these values; it does not determine both shifted outputs from one
independent half-input. Treating (3) as free evaluation of E would hide a call.

`four_odd_kernel_rewrite` implements (6) for the full original H_q(C)x.
The verification backend uses charged Horner for O(D), not a free macro.
Writing m=N/2, its exact online field counts are

- `16m^2+4m-1` multiplications;
- `12m^2-5m` additions;
- `6m-2` subtractions and two negations.

Their sum is `28m^2+5m-1`. This is quadratic and worse than the previous
three-call evaluator and the available O(N log N) full-operator comparator.
The implementation reuses the complete old decimation plan; its O(N log N)
preparation is recorded, including unused deeper levels. A dedicated plan for
only (6) would need O(N) coefficients, which would not change the online
quadratic result or make it an FFT improvement. Every input and output is
materialized; shifts, copies and indexing cost O(N) outside the four actions.

## A restricted two-identical-kernel obstruction

A tempting stronger proposal is to evaluate the complete block (1) as

\[
L(J)\operatorname{diag}(O(D),O(D))R(J),                  \tag{7}
\]

where every entry of the two-by-two block matrices L and R is a function of J.
This would mean two identical O-kernel calls surrounded by phase filters. The
following argument excludes (7) for dyadic N>=8, even allowing dense functions of J.
It does **not** cover different kernels, non-J preprocessing, additive bypasses
or a general two-call algorithm.

In a J eigenbasis, write its eigenvalues as `lambda_i=-q^(-2i)`, i=0,...,m-1.
Here m is even, so these are precisely mth roots of unity, as required by J^m=I.
The operator D sends the i-th eigenvector to a nonzero multiple of the next,
except that the last edge is zero. Consequently O(D) is lower triangular in
that ordering and every entry on or below its diagonal is nonzero: each is
`t_(2(i-j)+1)` times a product of nonzero edge weights.

For such an entry (i,j), put `t=lambda_j/lambda_i`. Dividing the corresponding
two-by-two block of (1) by `O(D)_(i,j)`, (3)-(4) give

\[
M(t)=\begin{pmatrix}
1-qt&(1-t)(1-qt)\\1&1-qt
\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\det M(t)=(1-q)t(1-qt).                                  \tag{8}
\]

If (7) held, then `M(t_(i,j))=L(lambda_i)R(lambda_j)`. Its determinant would
factor into a function of i times a function of j. Take output rows 2,3 and
input columns 0,1, all lying in the nonzero triangular support. The determinant
minor of those four scalar determinants is exactly

\[
(1-q)^2t_{20}t_{31}\,q(\lambda_1-\lambda_0)
(\lambda_2^{-1}-\lambda_3^{-1})\ne0.                      \tag{9}
\]

All factors are nonzero. Equation (9) contradicts the required row/column
separability. It is an explicit failed-identity certificate for (7), not an
asymptotic lower bound. The norm identity (5) remains useful structure, but its
Gaussian block factorization would additionally require ratios such as O/E;
their full actions have not been shown cheap.

Run `python run_audit.py`. Ten exact field/size cases pass: both F_65537 and
F_998244353 at N=4,8,16,32, and cyclotomic fields at N=4,8. The audit checks
66 half-basis vectors for (3)-(5), 132 full-basis vectors for (6), the finite

`results/branch_fusion_audit.json` contains the captured results and preparation.

The starting q-factorial Fourier factorization is established context:
Alexey Kuznetsov, *Using q-calculus to study LDL^T factorization of a certain
Vandermonde matrix*, https://arxiv.org/abs/1507.02959 . The finite identities and
restricted certificate above are derived directly; no literature theorem is
invoked as an uncharged algorithm. `branch_fusion.py` and `run_audit.py` are
new. The exact arithmetic, q-kernel, sparse-operator and decimation files are
unchanged copies of the earlier checkpoint, included for reproducibility.
