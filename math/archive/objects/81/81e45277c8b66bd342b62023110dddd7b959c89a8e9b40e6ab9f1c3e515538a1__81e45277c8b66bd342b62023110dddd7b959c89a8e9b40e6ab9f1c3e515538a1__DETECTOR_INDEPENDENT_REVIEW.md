## Completeness and soundness

Write s for the defect cap and m for the number of nonzero input coordinates.
The implementation's paid bidiagonal action is exactly

    (Ex)_i = -q^i x_i - q^i(q^(i+1)-1)x_(i+1),

with the second term absent on the last row. That omitted coefficient would
be zero because q^N=1.

If m>2s and some admissible lambda has at most s nonzero coordinates in
`(E-lambda I)x`, at least m-s>m/2 of the defined ratios `(Ex)_i/x_i` equal
lambda. Equality of these ratios is tested by cross multiplication; all
denominators are nonzero because only nonzero input coordinates participate.
The Boyer-Moore cancellation invariant therefore returns that strict majority.
It uses two field multiplications per ratio comparison and only one division
after selection. A final positive balance is not itself a majority certificate;
the implementation correctly follows it with independent residual verification.

For m<=2s, the sparse-input path evaluates the complete DFT directly and covers
the cases where a strict ratio majority is not guaranteed. In particular the
boundary m=2s takes the sparse path, while m=2s+1 can take the ratio path.
The zero input returns all N zero outputs even at s=0.

The candidate must be nonzero and satisfy lambda^N!=1. Since E has diagonal
entries -q^i and even N, the roots of its characteristic polynomial are
exactly the Nth roots of unity. Thus the root test excludes every singular
shift. The additional zero exclusion is the existing target-solver contract,
not a claim that E itself is singular at zero.

Every coordinate of `(E-lambda I)x` is computed and tested, including locations
where x_i=0 and a ratio was unavailable. Acceptance therefore cannot result
from a misleading majority alone. Once the verified residual r has support
at most s, its full DFT is explicitly evaluated. The existing identity
`F(E-lambda I)=(J-lambda I)F` then implies that solving
`(J-lambda I)y=Fr` returns exactly Fx. No cached transform of a nonzero input
is assumed. On other inputs the procedure may decline, as its interface states.

## Operation accounting and model

Root powers plus the complete E action cost 6N-4 counted scalar operations.
Ratio cancellation costs two multiplications per comparison; the candidate
division and binary powering for the root test are paid. Full residual
construction costs 2N arithmetic operations plus N zero comparisons. The
existing solver is prepared afresh, and even its duplicated root-power work
is included. Using the already computed power table, a d-term residual costs
`(2N-1)d` arithmetic operations for its full DFT. The target solve costs 9N
operations under the current fixed-width solver. The proof's complete total
`38N+2R+(2N-1)d+log2(N)+6` follows from these stages and the charged
`21N+7` preparation, where R is the number of ratio comparisons. Its
specialization `42N+log2(N)+3` for the dense one-defect fixtures is correct.

The sparse-input branch also pays to compute each phase step q^j; its cost is
O(m log N+Nm). With m<=2s, and with the detector and cold preparation included,
the accepted-family upper bound is O(N(s+1)) field operations/comparisons and
O(N) field-element storage. All accepted paths explicitly write N outputs.
Index operations, allocation, and copies are further linear bookkeeping,
not concealed general transforms. The zero path has no arithmetic gates but
still scans the input and writes its N outputs.

These are exact-field arithmetic counts, not bit costs, numerical stability
guarantees, or timings. In particular, the inherited ledger label
`constant_inverse` includes inversion of an input-dependent candidate
denominator and subsequent coefficients depending on the discovered lambda.
They are paid field inversions in an adaptive rational computation; the label
does not make them fixed input-independent constants or a fixed linear circuit.
Root validation/construction and exact field-representation costs are outside
the supplied-root model. The tested cold detector counts remain larger than
the ordinary FFT counts through N=8192; no measured practical speedup follows.

## Independent adverse controls

`detector_independent_checks.py` passes eight focused cases: four exact full
outputs and four valid declines. They cover the two support boundaries, a
dense one-defect recurrence, zero input, zero/root shifts, no majority, and a
hidden defect at a zero input coordinate. For the last case, at N=8 construct
`x=(E-2I)^-1 e_7` and replace x_0 by zero. The seven available ratios still
select 2, but the full residual has defects at 0 and 7 and correctly declines
at cap one.

An outer field counter independently observes every arithmetic call made by
the detector, including cold solver preparation. All eight observed totals
equal the returned ledger, and each ledger equals the sum of its recorded
stages. Accepted outputs match a separately evaluated full DFT. The JSON
records the reviewed implementation hash. No implementation change was needed.

The author audit additionally records 51 accepted exact outputs, 40 decline
controls, and four invalid-argument controls across ten field/size settings,
including two finite fields and small exact cyclotomic fields. Its larger
same-input comparisons are operation ledgers, not runtime measurements.
