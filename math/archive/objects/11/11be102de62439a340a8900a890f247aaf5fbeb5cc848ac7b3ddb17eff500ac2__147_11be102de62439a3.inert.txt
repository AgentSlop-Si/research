# Next experiment gate — Boolean nonlinearity cannot hide source/target work

The original goal is a **fresh, exact, all-output O(N) complex FFT**. Our latest Boolean-specific branch tests polynomial completion of uncomputed spectrum bins from computed bins. Do not rebrand selective Fourier outputs, memoization or CrocSwap constant tuning as general speedups.

## Known boundaries

- General quadratic non-generation theorem for any proper source-frequency subset.
- Exact characteristic-zero degree<=3 obstruction when an entire conjugate pair `{1,N-1}` is absent, for N=8,16,32. These are finite certificates, not an all-N proof.
- A one-missing-bin cubic identity at N=8 exists, but conjugation already recovers that bin in O(1) on real inputs, and the 33-gate polynomial is slower than direct evaluation.
- All even spectrum bins alone cannot recover odd bins from Boolean input, because `x=(u,1-u)` yields `2^(N/2)` ambiguous inputs.
- The self-convolution equation `X*X=N X` is old. Completing a bin from N-1 precomputed bins costs O(N) and fails at balanced inputs if its denominator is not replaced by a globally valid formula.
- Frobenius/cyclotomic orbit optimizations for finite-field inputs have documented prior art and incur huge base-field representation costs. Do not present them as exact-complex O(N).

## Falsifiable untested question

Can a genuinely *global* degree>=4 Boolean quotient circuit reconstruct a **positive fraction** of missing conjugate pairs from a smaller computed spectrum, at a total amortized cost o(N log N), with neither interpolation tables nor singular/failing input cases?

Before coding such a candidate, (i) average it over cyclic shifts and keep only weight-compatible monomials; (ii) run the input-weight-<=d rank certificate against the source-frequency subset; (iii) inspect whether the missing source data is information-theoretically determined; (iv) charge every source Fourier bin and all polynomial gates; and (v) compare against ordinary real-FFT conjugate symmetry and pruned FFT algorithms.

No new worst-case O(N) algorithm has been demonstrated.
