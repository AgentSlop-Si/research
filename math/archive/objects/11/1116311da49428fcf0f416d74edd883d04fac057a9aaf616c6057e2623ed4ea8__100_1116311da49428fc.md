# Verified gates for the current Green/reflection/forward-cycle family

9 October 2026. The candidate proofs are valid. No counterexample was found; the all-size conclusions follow from the proofs, not finite tests.

## 1. Rank-one wrappers do not remove the bare Green stage barrier

For normalized Fourier F and invertible G with rank(F-G)<=r<N,

    ||G||_1 >= sqrt(N)/(r+1),
    ||G^-1||_1 >= sqrt(N)/(r+1),
    kappa_1(G) >= N/(r+1)^2.

A nonzero vector supported on r+1 chosen coordinates lies in the kernel of F-G. Flatness and unitarity bound its Fourier l_1 spread. Applying the same argument to the inverse gives the second norm bound; the inverse-difference rank is exactly preserved. For a rank budget at least N, the condition-number inequality remains trivially true, but the individual norm claim need not hold.

Deleting B rank-one Householder factors from a Fourier factorization changes its product by rank at most B. If t retained Green factors each satisfy kappa_1<=7 and the remaining factors are unitary monomials/global scalars, the retained product has condition number at most 7^t. Consequently

    7^t(B+1)^2 >= N,
    t+B >= ceil(log_7 N).

With one reflection per Green stage, B=t and t>=log_7 N-O(log log N).

Full proof and model restrictions: `PROOF.md`.

## 2. Forward full-cycle K stages need logarithmically many stages

For K=I+(I-M)^-1 with M a unit-modulus full N-cycle of total weight -1, let a and b be K's largest and smallest singular values and let g=|det K|^(1/N). Every such K has the same a,b,g.

For even N>=4, any scalar-unitary product using t>=1 forward K stages with arbitrary unitary factors and global scalars must obey

    t >= log(a/b)/log(g/b).

Here g/b<4/3 and a/b grows proportionally to N, giving t=Omega(log N). For example, the numerical values of this exact necessary bound imply t>=4 at N=8, t>=6 at N=16, and t>=8 at N=32. These sample evaluations are illustrations of the closed formula, not construction claims.

N=2 has a=b=g and a vacuous bound. A two-stage scalar-unitary realization exists at N=4 in the enlarged arbitrary-unitary-separator model, consistently with the bound t>=2.

## 3. Mixing the two forward families still needs logarithmic depth

Suppose there are s bare Green stages or their inverses, t>=1 forward K stages, and any unitary factors. After determinant normalization, each Green factor has inverse operator norm below 5, while each forward K has inverse norm below 4/3 and norm greater than N/(2pi). Isolating one K gives

    a/g <= 5^s(g/b)^(t-1),
    s+t-1 >= log_5[N/(2pi)].

Thus the specified composite family requires Omega(log N) nonunitary stages whenever it includes a K. If it includes no K, the rank-one-wrapper result above applies, provided its remaining unitary factors are rank-one Householders and unitary monomials.

The arbitrary-unitary enlargement is valid only in the branch containing at least one K. Granting a free arbitrary unitary in the zero-K branch would permit Fourier itself and destroy that branch's conclusion.

Proofs of sections 2 and 3: `FORWARD_K_AND_COMBINED_GATE.md`.

## Cost and research boundary

These results rule out sublogarithmic explicit-stage factorizations within the specified family. If the dense stages are applied in individually charged full-array passes, they force Omega(N log N) work in that execution model.

They do not establish a general FFT lower bound, exclude arbitrary nonunitary coordinate gains, cover inverse-K architectures, or prohibit algebraic fusion that avoids separately evaluating the displayed factors. The user's broader exact FFT target remains open. No literature-priority or novelty claim is made.
