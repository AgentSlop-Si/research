# Hero #17 — Phase 6: four-leg Fourier tensor rank and endpoint-sparsification rigidity

**Date:** 10 October 2026  
**Status:** Proofs, exact finite-field implementation, independently coded audit, complex singular-value tests. **No new uniform sub-FFT arithmetic upper bound. No exact O(N) arbitrary-input Fourier algorithm.**

## Research contract and provenance

Target: **fresh arbitrary length-N input, every explicit Fourier coefficient, sequential fully charged work, no cached related input, no special-input restrictions**. Prior Phase 5 proved that one can vary an orbit basis's channel-transition rank without changing the composed input-to-output Fourier map. This phase avoids that coordinate-dependent proxy. We investigate the entire four-leg Fourier operator, with invariants under changes of basis acting *separately* on its four digit legs.

The structural results are scoped to this four-leg / endpoint-local class. They must **not** be advertised as unconditional FFT circuit lower bounds or first discoveries: Cooley–Tukey, quantum Fourier tensor networks and Fourier matrix rank phenomena have extensive prior art (references at end).

## 1. Fully general mixed-radix identity

Let `N=Q*M`, `Q,M>=2`. Work over a field `K` where `N` is invertible and `omega` is a *supplied primitive Nth root*. Put `xi=omega^M` (order Q), `eta=omega^Q` (order M). Use indices

```
input:    j=t+Q*u,    t in [0,Q), u in [0,M)
frequency: k=h+M*s,  h in [0,M), s in [0,Q)
```

For the unnormalized negative-exponent DFT, the **entire** linear input/output operator is the four-leg tensor

```
T[s,h,t,u] = omega^(-h*t) * xi^(-s*t) * eta^(-h*u).
```

This follows from `k*j = h*t + Q*h*u + M*s*t + N*s*u`; the final term contributes 1 to the phase. **No coprimality assumption on Q and M is needed.** The normalized complex tensor is `T/sqrt(N)`.

Every tensor entry is nonzero, hence its literal dense tensor storage has exactly `N^2` entries.

## 2. Theorem A — exact rank profile of all three balanced tensor cuts

Define flattening rank using the indicated pairs as rows versus the complementary pairs as columns:

| Flattening | Exact rank |
|---|---:|
| `(s,h) | (t,u)` | `N` |
| `(s,t) | (h,u)` | `min(Q,M)` |
| `(s,u) | (h,t)` | `N` |

### Proof

**First cut.** With row `k=h+Ms`, column `j=t+Qu`, the matrix is the usual DFT, which is invertible because its nodes are distinct and the characteristic does not divide N.

**Third cut.** Up to reordering columns `(h,t)`, the matrix is `(F_Q ⊗ F_M) D`, with `D[h,t]=omega^(-h*t)`. The two Fourier matrices and the diagonal matrix D are invertible, so the rank is N.

**Second cut.** Write `T_{(s,t),(h,u)} = A_{(s,t),r} B_{r,(h,u)}`, where the repeated-index sum is over `r in [0,Q)`,

```
A[(s,t),r] = [r=t] * xi^(-s*t)
B[r,(h,u)] = omega^(-h*r) * eta^(-h*u).
```

A has full column rank Q because its columns have disjoint t-support. For a fixed h, all columns of B indexed by u are scalar multiples of the same length-Q vector `(omega^(-h*r))_r`. The M different h-vectors form a rectangular Vandermonde matrix with distinct nodes, of rank `min(Q,M)`. Therefore `rank(AB)=min(Q,M)`.

**Gauge invariance (scope explicit).** If independent invertible matrices act on `s,h,t,u`, each balanced flattening is left- and right-multiplied by invertible Kronecker products (possibly after column permutations). All three ranks are unchanged. This does **not** include arbitrary joint changes of basis on pairs of legs or full `GL_N` input/output transformations. Those can change this cut profile and must have their execution cost charged.

### Consequence for one-cross-cut graph union

Any exact representation `T = sum_{l=1}^r A_l(s,t) B_l(h,u)` needs at least `r=min(Q,M)` terms. Such a decomposition exists by grouping either all t values or all h values, proving **optimality**. In the balanced case, a one-round separable union therefore has width at least `sqrt(N)`. For the other two pairings, it needs at least N terms.

This is a **lower bound on single-cut separable width**, **not** on the number of scalar arithmetic gates in an arbitrary multi-stage Fourier circuit.

### Another structural certificate: exact CP rank N

The full four-leg tensor has canonical polyadic rank **exactly N**. The first flattening gives lower bound N. An explicit N-term decomposition indexed by `(t,u)` is

```
T[s,h,t',u'] = sum_{t,u}
  xi^(-s*t) * [omega^(-h*t) eta^(-h*u)] * [t=t'] * [u=u'].
```

Each term is rank one across **all four** individual legs, so CP rank ≤ N; equality follows. Low tensor rank alone does not deliver linear-time full matrix-vector multiplication.

## 3. Theorem B — unique endpoint-local sparse-core gauges

Allow invertible local basis transforms `A in GL_Q(K)` acting on `s`, and `B in GL_M(K)` acting on `u`, **without** changing `h` and `t`. The transformed kernel is

```
T'[a,h,t,b] = sum_{s,u} A[a,s] * T[s,h,t,u] * B[b,u]
            = (A F_Q)[a,t] * omega^(-h*t) * (F_M B^T)[h,b].
```

Put `P=A F_Q`, `R=F_M B^T`. Since every twiddle `omega^(-h*t)` is nonzero,

```
        nnz(T') = nnz(P) * nnz(R) >= Q*M = N.
```

Every nonsingular d×d matrix has at least d nonzeros. Equality holds if and only if both P and R have exactly one nonzero in every row and every column: that is, they are **monomial** (permutation times nonzero diagonal) matrices.

Hence **all** endpoint-local transforms achieving the minimum N-entry core are

```
A = P * F_Q^(-1)
B^T = F_M^(-1) * R,   P,R monomial.
```

A canonical minimizer, `A=F_Q^(-1)` and `B=F_M^(-T)`, yields

```
T'[a,h,t,b] = omega^(-h*t) * [a=t] * [b=h].
```

This middle tensor has only **N** nonzero entries rather than N². But obtaining these coordinates requires precisely the two local inverse-Fourier basis operations; they are **not free**. The theorem characterizes *all* minimizers in this restricted endpoint-local model, not all possible general tensor decompositions.

### Exact edge-count audit of inverse-Fourier gauges

At square `Q=M=m`, applying inverse DFT bases on the following digit axes yields these **central tensor nonzero counts**:

| Local axes transformed | Exact count |
|---|---:|
| none | `m^4` |
| `s` only / `u` only | `m^3` |
| `s,h` / `t,u` | `m^3-m^2+m` |
| `s,u` | `m^2` |
| `t` only / `h` only | `m^4-m^3+m^2` |

The middle two-axis `s,h` and `t,u` cases reproduce Phase 4's `m^3-m^2+m` geometric-routing count; that is a connection to prior work, **not** a new faster circuit. All 16 inverse-Fourier/identity choices were enumerated over finite fields at m=2,3,4,6,8; only `s,u` attained `m²` within that 16-choice family.

For rigor: `s,h` gives `m+(m-1)m²` entries because one geometric channel has one surviving root and all other channels have m nonzeros. The `t,u` count follows symmetrically. Finite-field tests check these identities exactly.

## 4. Theorem C — exact crossed-cut singular spectrum (complex model)

For the **unitary-normalized** Fourier tensor `T/sqrt(N)` over C, both full-rank balanced cuts `(s,h)|(t,u)` and `(s,u)|(h,t)` are **unitary matrices**, after leg permutations: all N singular values are 1.

For the crossed cut `(s,t)|(h,u)`, the `min(Q,M)` nonzero singular values are **exactly the singular values** of the rectangular partial Fourier/Vandermonde matrix

```
V[t,h] = exp(-2*pi*i*h*t/N),  0<=t<Q, 0<=h<M.
```

Proof: factor crossed T = `A B/sqrt(N)` as in Theorem A. `A^* A=Q I_Q`. The Gram matrix of `B/sqrt(M)` equals `V V^*`; its columns over u contribute a unit-norm Fourier phase for each h. Thus the nonzero singular spectra coincide.

This spectral description is invariant under **unitary** local digit-basis changes (not general poorly conditioned GL changes). It connects Phase 5's clustered-root Vandermonde conditioning to an intrinsic crossed-cut Schmidt spectrum. A small set of dominant Schmidt values can approximate this pairing well, but the full exact rank still grows as `min(Q,M)`; the other two cuts remain full rank N.

**Prior-art warning:** The low-entanglement character of reordered QFT and exponentially decaying Schmidt spectra have been studied and published, e.g. Chen–Stoudenmire–White (PRX Quantum 2023) and Chen–Lindsey's MPO work (ACHA 2026). Neither the reduced numerical rank nor quantum circuit gate counts establish an O(N) arbitrary-dense-input classical FFT.

## 5. Full cost ledger — the work hidden by the N-sparse middle

For powers of two `Q,M`, `phase6.py::charged_two_axis_fft` is a complete, ordinary Cooley–Tukey calculation. Unlike earlier prototype implementations, it prepares **every needed Nth-root power in N−1 multiplication operations**, so there are no uncounted repeated modular exponentiations in butterfly loops.

Let `B=N/2*(log2 Q+log2 M) = (N/2)log2 N`. This specific implementation charges:

```
root-table preparation:            N - 1 field multiplies
radix-2 Fourier butterflies:       B units = B scalar multiplies + 2B additions/subtractions
intermediate cross twiddles:       N scalar multiplies
input read and output write:       2N values
root storage:                     N field values
arithmetic total (this program):   3B + 2N - 1 field additions/multiplies
addressing, branch, temporary I/O: O(N log N) standard sequential work
```

No numerical speedup is implied. The arithmetic ledger is an upper bound for a classical implementation, **not** a lower bound on other algorithms; it counts even trivial twiddle multiplies.

| N | Original dense tensor entries (formula) | Sparse middle nnz | Charged conventional butterflies |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 16 | 256 | 16 | 32 |
| 64 | 4,096 | 64 | 192 |
| 256 | 65,536 | 256 | 1,024 |
| 1,024 | 1,048,576 | 1,024 | 5,120 |
| 65,536 | 4,294,967,296 | 65,536 | 524,288 |

Large dense tensor counts are **formulas**, not claims that the matrices were materialized. At N=65,536 the executor wrote all outputs and 16 independently selected coefficients were checked directly; all-output independent direct DFT comparisons were done only at smaller sizes.

The key caution is mathematical: `nnz(middle)=O(N)` does **not** imply `cost(full map)=O(N)`, because the necessary basis transforms have costs that must be charged or jointly improved.

## 7. What this eliminates, and the next legitimate direction

**Eliminated inside the stated model:** a constant-width exact separation across any balanced two-digit cut; a free N-sparse Fourier middle obtained by local endpoint basis changes; treating Schmidt numerical rank as exact rank; and counting a hidden Fourier basis conversion as zero operations.

**Not eliminated:** multistage circuits with overlapping channels and cancellation; nonseparable and input-independent global basis conversions with their work charged; finite exact tensor networks using large unbounded scalar constants; nonlinear arithmetic circuits; novel joint algorithmic execution of two sets of local transforms; and the specialized model of OpenAI Math #130.

**Next falsifiable target:** design an input-independent **joint basis conversion** for the two endpoint Fourier families that, including root preparation, index work, storage and output materialization, has a fully charged recurrence *strictly better than the known generic FFT benchmark* for all arbitrary inputs. Rank- or sparsity-only objectives do not count. Require a finite exact circuit that can be checked on every basis vector at small N before claiming asymptotic savings.
