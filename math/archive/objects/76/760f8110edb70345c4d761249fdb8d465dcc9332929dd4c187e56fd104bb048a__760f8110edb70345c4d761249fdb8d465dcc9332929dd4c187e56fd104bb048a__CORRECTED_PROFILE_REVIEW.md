## 1. Definitions and the projection

Let `N=km`, with dyadic m and `k=m` or `2m`. Write a physical coordinate as `am+i`, `0<=a<k`, `0<=i<m`, and let `rev_t` reverse the `log2(t)` bits. Work over a field containing a primitive m-th root omega, with m invertible; the analytic obstructions in Section 7 use characteristic zero.

For `s!=0`, `c!=1`, define the natural weighted Green matrix by

`T_N(s,c)[i,j] = s^(i-j)/(1-c)` for `i>=j`,

`T_N(s,c)[i,j] = c*s^(i-j)/(1-c)` for `i<j`.

Its ordinary cycle edges are s and its wrap is `c/s^(N-1)`. Put `J_N(r,h)=R_N T_N(r,h) R_N^-1`, with `r!=0`, `h!=1`. An identity with all edges zero is a separately appended branch; it is not defined by substituting r=0 into expressions that divide by r. Nonzero wrap limits with zero ordinary edges need their own parameter and are not included implicitly.

Let U_s expand a coarse input as `(U_s x)_(am+i)=s^i x_a`, and let V_s contract as `(V_s y)_a=sum_i s^(-i)y_(am+i)`. Given nonzero s0,s2, choose

`L_(am+i)=s0^i omega^(a rev_m(i))`,

`M_(am+i)=s2^(-i) omega^(-a rev_m(i))/m`.

Then `Theta(B)=V_s0 L B M U_s2` can be written directly as

`Theta(B)_ab=sum_(i,j) X_ai B_(am+i,bm+j) Y_bj`,

where `X_ai=omega^(a rev_m(i))` and `Y_bi=omega^(-b rev_m(i))/m`. This direct formula is the reference definition used by the checker.

Orthogonality gives `(X Y^T)_ab=1_(a mod m=b mod m)`. Thus X has rank m and both masks have maximum possible k-by-m reshape rank m. This high reshape rank by itself says nothing about the cost of generating the masks: character powers can be generated by geometric recurrences and scattered into bit-reversed positions with O(N) arithmetic and writes, once the required field elements are available. Root representation, reciprocals, storage, and bit costs are not free.

## 2. Direct correction of the identity branch

For `B=I_N`, a matrix entry is nonzero only when both `a=b` and `i=j`. Hence

`Theta(I_N)_ab=delta_ab sum_i X_ai Y_ai=delta_ab`.

The erroneous replacement `Theta(I_N)=X Y^T` forgets the coarse-coordinate Kronecker delta. For example, with `m=2,k=4`, the correct projection is `I_4`, whereas the repeated Gram matrix has ones at `(0,2),(2,0),(1,3),(3,1)` as well as on the diagonal. The direct identity calculation must be used when appending the r=0, zero-wrap branch.

## 3. General bit-reversal compression, with r nonzero

Let `rho(a)=rev_k(a)`, `v=r^k`, and `E=J_m(v,h)`. The exact bit identity

`rev_N(am+i)=k rev_m(i)+rho(a)`

shows that, for `i!=j`, the sign of the full cycle-order difference is determined by `rev_m(i)-rev_m(j)`. For `i=j`, its sign is determined by `rho(a)-rho(b)`. The diagonal local term contributes `1/(1-h)` before this distinction; replacing it by `h/(1-h)` subtracts exactly one. Thus direct entry comparison gives

`Theta(J_N)_ab = r^(rho(a)-rho(b)) [(X E Y^T)_ab - 1_(rho(a)<rho(b))(X Y^T)_ab]`.

No limiting argument at r=0 is used. For these particular character profiles, bit reversal in X cancels that of E, so the inner Fourier-conjugated operator is

`B=F_m T_m(v,h) F_m^-1`,

where `(F_m)_ai=omega^(ai)`. If k=m, the Gram subtraction is zero. If k=2m, let P duplicate the m rows and put

`Q=[[0,I_m],[0,0]]`, `D=diag(r^rho(a))`.

Then

`Theta(J_N)=D [P B P^T-Q] D^-1`.

The form of Q follows from `rho(a+m)=rho(a)+1` and the fact that the only off-diagonal nonzero Gram entries pair a with a+m.

### Homogeneous branch

If `h=v^m=r^N!=1`, the local Green is `(I-v Z_m)^-1`. Positive Fourier convention gives

`B=diag(lambda_a)`, `lambda_a=1/(1-v omega^a)`.

When k=m this makes `Theta(J_N)=diag(lambda_a)`. When k=2m, each paired coordinate `(a,a+m)` has the exact block

`[[lambda_a, (lambda_a-1)/r], [r lambda_a, lambda_a]]`.

All denominators are nonzero because `v^m!=1`. The block equals the inverse of `I-[[0,v omega^a/r],[r,0]]`.

### Nonresonant local edge

If `v^m!=1` and `h!=1`, put

`kappa=(h-v^m)/[(1-h)(1-v^m)]`, `u_i=v^i`, `w_j=v^(-j)`.

Comparing both triangles of T gives

`T_m(v,h)=(I-v Z_m)^-1+kappa u w^T`.

Thus B is diagonal plus rank one, with vectors `F_m u` and `w^T F_m^-1`. Their entries are finite geometric sums. Their O(m) scalar preparation uses only guarded denominators and explicitly generated powers. The repeated-profile lifting and outer diagonal conjugation preserve the rank-one bound.

### Resonant local edge with valid total wrap

If `v^m=1`, `h!=1`, the previous decomposition is invalid and must not divide by `1-v^m`. Set

`b=h/v^(m-1)`, `tau=(b-v)/m`, `z_a=omega^a`, `d_a=1-v z_a`.

There is exactly one index a0 with `d_a0=0`, and `tau!=0`. Fourier conjugation of the weighted denominator gives

`F_m (I-P_weighted) F_m^-1 = diag(d_a)-tau*1*z^T`.

For a right-hand side t, solve this system by

`x_a=(t_a-t_a0)/d_a` for `a!=a0`,

`x_a0=[-t_a0/tau-sum_(a!=a0) z_a x_a]/z_a0`.

The a0 row determines `sum z_a x_a`; substituting it in every other row proves the formula. This is a diagonal map plus at most two rank-one maps and costs O(m) arithmetic. No zero denominator is inverted. Lifting by P, subtracting Q, and conjugating by D leaves paired two-by-two blocks plus rank at most two and an O(k) evaluator.

## 4. Natural-middle compression

Define `U_m(s)[i,j]=s^(i-j)` and `J_m^+(s)[i,j]=s^(i-j)` for `i<j`, zero otherwise. Comparing physical blocks gives

`T_N(s,c)=T_k(s^m,c) tensor U_m(s)-I_k tensor J_m^+(s)`.

Consequently

`Theta(T_N)=diag(f) T_k(s^m,c) diag(g)-diag(d)`,

where coarse indices are interpreted modulo m and

`f_a=sum_i s^i omega^(a rev_m(i))`,

`g_a=(1/m)sum_i s^(-i)omega^(-a rev_m(i))`,

`d_a=(1/m)sum_(i<j) s^(i-j)omega^(a(rev_m(i)-rev_m(j)))`.

The possibly zero f and g entries are used only as diagonal multipliers. No inverse of either is required.

## 5. Exact coefficient recurrences and arithmetic cost

Write `D_m(a;s)=m*d_a` and `ell=m/2`. For `0<=a<ell`, set `z=s^ell omega_m^a`. Bit reversal on the first natural half has even values, and on the second half has the corresponding odd values. Therefore

`f_m(a;s)=(1+z)f_ell(a;s)`,

`f_m(a+ell;s)=(1-z)f_ell(a;s)`.

Generate the reciprocal-edge f array in the same pass. Put

`K_a=f_ell(a;s) f_ell(-a;s^-1)`.

The two internal half-triangles each contribute `D_ell`; the cross-half rectangle factors as `z^-1 K_a`. Hence

`D_m(a;s)=2D_ell(a;s)+z^-1 K_a`,

`D_m(a+ell;s)=2D_ell(a;s)-z^-1 K_a`.

The base is `f_1=1,D_1=0`. One jointly computed pair of half-length geometric-character arrays is reused at the next level. The arithmetic recurrence is therefore `C(m)=C(m/2)+O(m)=O(m)`, not two independent transforms. Powers, inversions, storage, and writes must be counted; exact bit complexity depends on the coefficient representation. This is a special transform of a geometric input, not a linear-time transform of arbitrary data.

## 6. What the projected closure establishes

For a middle stage `A_N=T_N(s,c)+t J_N(r,h)`, the chosen projection is a diagonally sandwiched coarse weighted Green plus a diagonal or paired two-by-two map and at most rank two. The separately appended identity branch adds `t I_k`. With valid coefficients supplied and prepared as above, this is an O(k) coarse evaluator after O(m) preparation and O(N) mask generation.

This can be inserted into the previously factored natural-boundary path `U_0 C_0 Theta(A_N) C_2 V_2`, while charging the O(N) contractions and expansions. It does not cover the paths involving natural principal blocks or bit-reversal terms from either outside stage. It does not imply that the sum of every path equals the Fourier matrix.

## 7. Reviewed auxiliary obstructions, narrowly stated

### Ordinary scalar-quadratic profiles

For a nonzero mask `D[n]=alpha^(n^2) beta^n`, half restriction remains in the same quadratic family, with second-half factor `alpha^(m^2)beta^m` and updated beta `beta*alpha^(2m)`. Conjugating a constant ordinary cycle edge introduces the ratio `alpha^(2n-1)beta`. For N>=3, it is constant across ordinary edges only if `alpha^2=1`. The N=2 one-ordinary-edge case needs this qualification; it cannot impose a comparison between two ordinary edges.

At a balanced split k=m, a quadratic profile has the form `X_ai=A_a B_i z^(ai)`. Suppose this X is invertible and diagonalizes a nonzero-edge weighted bit-reversal Green by similarity. With nonzero wrap, the weighted cycle has distinct eigenvalues in characteristic zero. Its left eigenrows are bit-reversed characters times a common column gauge and row scales. Comparing any two rows, then canceling their common factors, gives

`z^i=omega^(Delta rev_m(i))`.

At i=1, `rev_m(1)=m/2`, so `z^2=1`. For m>=4, the resulting Vandermonde profile has at most two distinct rows before row scaling, contradicting invertibility. Zero wrap instead gives a nontrivial nilpotent weighted successor and a non-diagonalizable Green, so it does not restore the missing diagonal branch. This excludes only this direct profile-diagonalization mechanism, not every quadratic-mask circuit.

### A bounded common-cycle pole representation of the index transition

Applying a natural principal Green to its matching local geometric mode gives

`[T_m(s,c) u_s]_i=s^i [i+(1+c(m-1))/(1-c)]`.

The extra i factor becomes `B_m=F_m diag(rev_m(j)) F_m^-1` under the character-profile basis. As a circulant, its eigenvalue at the Z_m eigenvalue `omega^j` is `rev_m(-j mod m)`.

Suppose this transition were a scalar identity plus p scalar finite-pole Green terms on that same cycle, with no pole on the m-th roots. After canceling redundant poles its eigenvalue is `R(z)=P(z)/Q(z)`, with `deg P<=deg Q=t<=p`. In `R(omega z)-R(z)+m/2`, clearing denominators produces a nonzero polynomial of degree exactly `2t`: the leading terms in the difference cancel, and the added multiple of `Q(omega z)Q(z)` has nonzero leading coefficient.

For each odd j, bit reversal gives `rev(-(j+1))-rev(-j)=-m/2`. The polynomial therefore vanishes at m/2 distinct roots `omega^j`, forcing `2t>=m/2` and `p>=m/4`. This is a restriction on an uncanceled common-cycle scalar-pole representation. It is not a streaming or general arithmetic-circuit lower bound, and it leaves cancellations, shared evaluators, and different cycle orders open.
