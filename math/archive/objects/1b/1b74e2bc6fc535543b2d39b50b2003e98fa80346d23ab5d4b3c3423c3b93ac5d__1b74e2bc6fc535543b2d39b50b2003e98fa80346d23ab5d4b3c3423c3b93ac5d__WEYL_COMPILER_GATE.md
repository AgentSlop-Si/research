# Exact gate for the proposed Weyl-certified phase-bank compiler

9 October 2026. Bounded mathematical audit against master version 10. No master or Library item was changed; no uploaded program was run, imported or compiled.

## Result

The proposed fusion of non-affine Green scans, exact complementary phase banks and algebraic compatibility is a research roadmap. It does not yet give the missing construction. Three existing ingredients do different jobs:

- The phase bank gives an exact identity and complete information, with an ordinary Cooley–Tukey evaluator already known.
- Green scans supply cheap individual operators; non-affine order can evade an affine-cut hypothesis but does not supply Fourier phase coherence.
- Weyl identities and algebraic compatibility certify a specified operator; they do not synthesize coefficients or reduce its evaluation cost.

Moreover, a pure product of weighted one-cycle resolvents remains subject to the existing inverse-support obstruction even when every cycle order is genuinely non-affine. A surviving candidate must therefore change more than its permutations: sums with sufficiently complicated inverses, unrestricted separated-generator scans, or a genuinely shared circuit are the relevant remaining forms.

This report adds one bounded diagnostic: for fixed invertible outside factors, complete diagonal Weyl-word fingerprints are a necessary and sufficient middle-monomial compatibility test. They are always collision-free. This completes the older short-signature screen, without claiming a new fast evaluator, a uniform parameter construction or publication novelty.

## 2. Exact certificate and conventions

For N≥2, set

    omega = exp(-2*pi*i/N)
    W[j,k] = omega^(j*k)
    F = W/sqrt(N)
    Z e_k = e_(k+1 mod N)
    D e_k = omega^k e_k.

Thus `(Zx)[j]=x[j-1]`, `(Z^-1 x)[j]=x[j+1]`, and `D Z = omega Z D`. The correct negative-exponent Fourier identities are

    F Z = D F,
    F D = Z^-1 F.

For a fixed linear candidate T, an exact sufficient and necessary certificate is

    T Z - D T = 0,
    T D - Z^-1 T = 0,
    T[0,0] = 1/sqrt(N).

The first equation gives `T[j,k]=b_j omega^(jk)`. The second gives `b_(j+1)=b_j`; calibration fixes their common value. No invertibility premise is needed for this characterization. In particular, testing both equations without a nonzero normalization accepts the zero map, and without the exact normalization accepts every scalar multiple of F.

An equivalent certificate is the first whole-matrix identity plus the full calibrated first column `T e_0 = (1/sqrt(N))*1`. This should not be confused with checking only one entry after only one covariance identity.

For finite-field diagnostics, use a field containing a certified primitive Nth root, with characteristic not dividing N. The unnormalized target W avoids an unnecessary square-root choice. If a normalized target is desired, explicitly supply the compatible nonzero normalizer. The N=1 case is the scalar identity and should be handled separately.

A candidate submission must also contain:

1. Its operator grammar, exact coefficients, permutations and complete straight-line application program.
2. Every inverse/denominator guard and all exceptional parameter cases. A skipped singular chart is not an all-parameter exclusion.
3. The exact residual certificate with its field/root/normalizer convention.
4. A charged coefficient-generation and fresh-input evaluator ledger.
5. The allowed length family and a uniform construction rule. Isolated successful sizes do not imply an asymptotic result.

For a nonlinear or input-adaptive implementation, the fixed-linear-map premise must first be established. A linear randomized oracle test does not establish it.

## 3. Cheap inherited rejection before any coefficient fitting

Let a weighted cycle resolvent have denominator `I-W_j`, where W_j is any weighted permutation matrix. Its order need not be affine, and the sparse-support conclusion does not require a single cycle. Each row of the denominator has at most two nonzeros. If an invertible candidate is a product of t such resolvents, with arbitrary invertible monomial factors between and outside them, each inverse row has support at most `min(N,2^t)`.

Every entry of F^-1 is nonzero. Hence exact Fourier equality requires

    2^t >= N, i.e. t >= ceil(log_2 N).

More generally, inverses with row support at most s_j require `product_j s_j >= N`. Cancellations can shrink the actual support and only strengthen this necessary condition. Permutations and nonzero diagonal scalings preserve the support-count bound.

For complex unitary F, the already-proved stronger estimate is

    ||T-F||_2 >= sqrt(max(0, 1-2^t/N)).

It follows from the inherited one-inverse-row lemma `||T-F||_2 >= sqrt(1-s/N)` whenever an inverse row has support at most s<N. No conditioning assumption is needed. Fixed spectral error delta<1 consequently requires `2^t >= N(1-delta^2)`.

These statements rule out sublogarithmic pure-resolvent stage count, including non-affine orders. A stage-by-stage implementation that materializes N entries per stage then has its usual N-times-depth charge. They are not a lower bound for arbitrary shared evaluation DAGs, nor do they automatically apply to a sum whose total inverse is dense. The sparse-plus-low-rank inverse and affine-character cut gates must be checked separately for a proposed more general grammar.

## 4. What phase-bank completeness certifies

Write N=KM. For each block r, choose unit-modulus phases s_0(r,u), 0≤u<M, and set

    s_h(r,u) = s_0(r,u) exp(2*pi*i*h*u/M),
    (C_h x)[r] = sum_u s_h(r,u) x[Mr+u],
    P_h = C_h* C_h.

The physical output-v/input-u matrix entry is `conj(s_h(r,v))*s_h(r,u)`, not its transpose/conjugate. Character orthogonality gives

    C_h C_l* = M delta_(h,l) I_K,
    P_h P_l = M delta_(h,l) P_h,
    sum_h P_h = M I_N,
    F x = (1/M) sum_h F P_h x.

Here P_h is a scaled projector; P_h/M is the idempotent projection. Fewer than M such rank-K linear summaries cannot determine all arbitrary inputs when the decoder sees only that transcript.

The known independent-row schedule costs `O(MN+N log K)`. Joint block analysis already costs `O(N log M)` under the ordinary short-transform evaluator; the complementary short-transform bank costs `O(N log K)`. Eliminating the separate aggregate scans by the known identity gives ordinary Cooley–Tukey, with total `O(N log N)`.

Therefore “do not materialize M output vectors” is not by itself a new speedup. The missing part is an explicit shared evaluator that reduces the remaining transform work, with all coefficients and routing charged. A common downstream operator H followed by exact averaging also gives only `(1/M) sum_h H P_h=H`; its being equal to F remains to be proved and evaluated.

One-row phase conventions must be checked before bank averaging: reversing every bank phase can permute the full bank and leave the average unchanged while certifying the wrong row executor.

## 5. Complete fixed-factor middle-monomial diagnostic

### Statement

Work over any field containing a primitive Nth root omega, with characteristic not dividing N. Let A and B be specified invertible matrices, and use unnormalized W for this theorem. Define

    X = A^-1 D A,       Y = A^-1 Z^-1 A,
    U = B Z B^-1,       V = B D B^-1.

Both ordered pairs obey the SAME convention

    XY = omega^-1 YX,   UV = omega^-1 VU,
    X^N=Y^N=U^N=V^N=I.

For every coordinate i define the complete ordered diagonal fingerprint

    sigma_i(X,Y) = ((X^a Y^b)[i,i])_(0<=a,b<N).

The word order `X^a Y^b` is essential; changing order introduces powers of omega.

Then:

1. Within either pair, the N fingerprints are pairwise distinct, with no genericity assumption.
2. There exists an invertible monomial C=Q P such that `X C=C U` and `Y C=C V` if and only if the two complete fingerprint multisets are equal.
3. In the matching case, the matching coordinate permutation is unique. C is unique up to one global nonzero scalar, and calibration gives `A C B=W` or the chosen normalized target.

The claim is for exact conjugates of these irreducible Weyl pairs. Arbitrary matrix pairs or the older symmetrized Harper pairs need not satisfy it.

### Proof

The N² matrices D^a Z^-b form a basis of all N-by-N matrices: different b occupy disjoint cyclic diagonals; along each such diagonal the N characters omega^(a*j) are independent. Conjugation preserves this basis property, so the X^aY^b and U^aV^b are also full matrix bases.

If two coordinates i≠j had identical complete fingerprints for X,Y, the nonzero functional `H -> H[i,i]-H[j,j]` would vanish on a basis of all matrices. This is impossible, proving collision-freedom.

The invertible matrix

    C_0 = A^-1 W B^-1

always intertwines the two pairs. Suppose fingerprint i of X,Y equals fingerprint pi(i) of U,V. For every basis word H=U^aV^b,

    tr((C_0^-1 E_ii C_0) H) = tr(E_pi(i),pi(i) H).

The trace pairing on the full matrix algebra is nondegenerate, so

    C_0^-1 E_ii C_0 = E_pi(i),pi(i).

Equivalently, `E_ii C_0=C_0 E_pi(i),pi(i)`. Thus row i of C_0 has its only nonzero in column pi(i), and invertibility makes these columns a permutation. C_0 is monomial. The reverse implication follows immediately by diagonal conjugation and relabeling.

Any two invertible intertwiners differ by an element commuting with both U and V, hence with their full matrix algebra; that element is scalar. This proves uniqueness up to scale, and explains why a nonzero full-Weyl pair cannot leave independent component scales undetermined.

### Recovering the gauge and a lower-storage variant

After the fingerprints identify pi, set `U'=P U P^-1`, `V'=P V P^-1`, with `P[i,pi(i)]=1`. The equations are

    X[i,j] q_j = q_i U'[i,j],
    Y[i,j] q_j = q_i V'[i,j].

Check support agreement and propagate `q_j/q_i=U'[i,j]/X[i,j]` or its Y,V analogue over nonzero edges, checking repeated ratios and cycles. The union support graph is connected: otherwise the pair would preserve a proper coordinate subspace, contradicting its generating all matrices. Set q_0=1, recover every other q_i and check both equations exactly. One scalar calibration then determines the target normalization.

For a cheaper screening implementation, start with the old six words, then add Weyl words only when needed to separate a remaining coordinate collision in X,Y. At most N-1 added separating words suffice, since each selected word splits at least one class. Any true monomial solution must match those labels; after obtaining its unique possible permutation, the complete support/ratio/equation test remains necessary. Matching a shortened separating signature alone is NOT a sufficiency certificate.

### Explicit counterfeit for the old six signatures

For odd N≥5, take

    (X,Y)=(Z,DZ),
    (U,V)=(Z,DZ²).

Both pairs have the required commutation relation and Nth powers I, and both are irreducible: their generators recover D and Z. For each pair the six matrices `X,Y,X²,XY,YX,Y²` have identically zero diagonals. Thus every coordinate has the same six-entry zero fingerprint, in both pairs.

But

    X^-1 Y = omega D,
    U^-1 V = omega D Z.

The first diagonal is everywhere nonzero, the second everywhere zero. That single extra Weyl word rejects common monomial similarity exactly. Complete fingerprints are collision-free and disagree. This example exposes short-signature incompleteness without suggesting that an earlier inconclusive short screen was unsound.

### Cost and scope

Complete fingerprints contain N³ field entries per pair. A direct implementation building powers and computing product diagonals uses O(N⁴) arithmetic and O(N³) storage, apart from the input inversions/conjugations. Reading, sorting/comparing canonical exact field elements, inversion costs and field representation must be charged. Directly forming the known dense C_0 and checking whether it is monomial is also a valid reference test; it explicitly uses Fourier data and is not a fast transform construction.

The complete-fingerprint result is a finite compatibility diagnostic for FIXED A,B. It does not choose Green parameters, make their matrix entries cheap, certify an unspecified family, or turn polynomial verification into sub-N-log-N construction.

## 6. Exact verification versus construction versus runtime

A finite-field challenge check of the two Weyl equations uses one calibration call and three candidate applications per challenge: T r, T(Zr), T(Dr). For fixed deterministic linear T and independent uniform field challenges, if the stacked defect has rank rho, h challenges pass with exact probability `p^(-h*rho)`. An equivalent first-identity/full-column verifier uses one full seed-column call and two applications per challenge. All candidate-application costs, field/root preparation, randomness and O(N) per-challenge auxiliary work remain charged.

These checks do not prove characteristic-zero identities from modular passes, do not establish linearity and do not give a uniform all-N construction. A modular rejection transfers only when the proposed exact coefficients, roots, inverses and all denominators specialize validly. Exact symbolic residual identities or independently checkable exact algebraic coefficients are needed for a complex proof.

An alleged arithmetic improvement must display, for its declared length family,

    T_total(N) = T_parameters(N) + T_permutations(N)
                 + T_coefficients(N) + T_fresh_input(N)
                 + T_reconstruction(N) + T_output(N)
                 + T_required_certification(N).

Certification is included when the proposed algorithm actually performs it, or when it forms part of coefficient synthesis. An independently proved theorem need not be re-proved on every fresh input, but nonuniform precomputed coefficient tables cannot be silently substituted for a charged uniform generator. One-time setup and amortization assumptions must be explicit.

For a genuine unamortized improvement this total must be `o(N log N)`, not merely the number of Green calls or the cost of one part of a fused scan. A scalar-field count is distinct from bit complexity; exact root representation, growing coefficient heights, field inversions and output representation must not be hidden in a “linear-time” claim.

## 7. Next falsifiable gate and stopping condition

The note supplies no candidate coefficient family. The next task should therefore fix a specific still-admissible architecture before searching: for example an existing unresolved N=8 separated-generator or sum-of-scans family whose inverses genuinely escape the pure-resolvent support restriction, with a declared non-affine wiring and all earlier residual/exception charts preserved.

The bounded deliverable is one of:

- An exact admissible parameter witness, a reproducible application DAG, vanishing full Weyl defects plus calibration, and all guards verified; or
- An exact infeasibility certificate covering every chart of that specified family; or
- An explicitly incomplete result identifying the unresolved equations or branch.

For fixed outside factors, the complete fingerprint test above supplies a decisive middle-monomial yes/no gate without factorial permutation search. It does not eliminate continuous parameters, so its failure at sampled A,B is only a sampled veto.

A positive N=8 witness would be a Fourier-specific finite feasibility result. The subsequent, separately necessary milestone is a single coefficient/wiring rule extending to a growing family such as N=16,32,..., with a proved ledger below N log N. Having high graph cut ranks, passing modular samples, or independently solving each finite size would not establish that milestone.

No broad factorization search was performed in this pass. The work stops at this exact gate, the bounded new fixed-factor diagnostic and its controls.
