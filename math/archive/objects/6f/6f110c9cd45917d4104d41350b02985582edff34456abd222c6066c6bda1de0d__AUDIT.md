# Independent audit of the routed root-packet representation bounds

## Verdict

The packet factorization, the general `r²` column-support bound, the common-route `r` bound, and the stated spectral and normalized-Frobenius approximation bounds are correct under the assumptions below. The low-rank extension is also correct. Its product uncertainty bound holds for every flat unitary; its additive uncertainty bound requires the prime-order discrete Fourier transform, or a transform with the same additive uncertainty property. No numerical experiment or program output is used in the proofs.

The important qualifications are:

1. The packet index is exactly `i = 0, …, r−1`. With `m` branches, the general bound is `mr`, not automatically `r²`.
2. “Normalized Frobenius” means `||X||F / √N`, equivalently Frobenius error relative to the unitary target's Frobenius norm.
3. In the common-route calculation, retain the order `P^s R`. Commuting with `P^r` does not imply commuting with `P`.
4. The additive low-rank result does not follow from flatness alone; explicitly assume the prime-order Fourier transform.
5. These are restrictions on a specified matrix representation, not scalar-operation or general circuit lower bounds.

## 1. Assumptions and notation

Let `N ≥ 1` and `r ≥ 1` be integers, `P` an `N × N` coordinate permutation matrix, `ζ` a primitive `r`th root of unity, and `a ∈ C` with `0 < |a| < 1`. Put

`b = |a|^r`,

`Q = I − a^r P^r`,

`H_i = Σ_{s=0}^{r−1} (aζ^i)^s P^s`.

A partial monomial matrix has at most one nonzero in every row and every column; its nonzero weights need not have unit modulus. A flat unitary `U` satisfies `U*U = I` and `|U_{jk}| = 1/√N` for all entries. All norms below are over complex matrices; `||·||2` is the induced Euclidean/operator norm.

Since `P^r` is unitary and `b < 1`,

`σmin(Q) ≥ 1 − b > 0` and `||Q||2 ≤ 1 + b`.

In particular, `Q` is invertible. Every `I − aζ^iP` is likewise invertible.

## 2. Packet factorization and routed support

The finite geometric identity gives

`(I − aζ^iP) H_i = H_i (I − aζ^iP) = I − (aζ^iP)^r = Q`.

Thus

`(I − aζ^iP)^−1 = H_i Q^−1`.

Suppose each right route satisfies `R_i P^r = P^r R_i`. Then `R_i` commutes with `Q` and with `Q^−1`, even when `R_i` is singular. Consequently,

`T = Σ_i L_i (I − aζ^iP)^−1 R_i`

`  = (Σ_i L_i H_i R_i) Q^−1 = M Q^−1`.

There is no need for any `L_i` to commute with `P`, `P^r`, or `Q`.

For any coordinate vector `e_j`, `R_i e_j` is either zero or a multiple of one coordinate vector. Applying `H_i` produces at most `r` supported coordinates. Applying the partial monomial `L_i` cannot increase that support. Summing `r` branches gives

`|supp(M e_j)| ≤ r²`.

Repeated coordinates, short permutation cycles, zero weights, and cancellations only reduce this upper bound. The effective bound is `min(N,r²)`.

## 3. Exact representation of a flat unitary

If `T = U`, multiplying on the right by `Q` gives `M = UQ`.

Right multiplication by `P^r` permutes the columns of `U`, so both entries in

`(UQ)_{jk} = U_{jk} − a^r (UP^r)_{jk}`

have respective moduli `1/√N` and `b/√N`. The reverse triangle inequality yields

`|(UQ)_{jk}| ≥ (1 − b)/√N > 0`.

Every column of `M` therefore has all `N` entries nonzero. Together with the routed support bound, this proves `r² ≥ N`.

This conclusion concerns equality as an operator on every input. Agreement on one input vector is insufficient.

## 4. Common input route and diagonal output weights

Now assume all `L_i` are diagonal and all `R_i` equal one common unitary monomial matrix `R` commuting with `P^r`. Define diagonal matrices

`D_s = Σ_{i=0}^{r−1} ζ^{is} L_i`.

Then the numerator is

`M = Σ_{s=0}^{r−1} a^s D_s P^s R`.

Each `P^sR` is monomial, and multiplication on the left by diagonal `D_s` cannot enlarge a column's support. There are only `r` such terms. Therefore every column of `M` has at most `r` nonzeros, and exact equality with a flat unitary forces `r ≥ N`.

The corresponding common-state realization is exact:

`w = Q^−1 x`,

`z = Rw = Q^−1 Rx`,

`Tx = Σ_{s=0}^{r−1} a^s D_s P^s z`.

Equivalently, `T = B R Q^−1` with `B = Σ_s a^s D_sP^s`. Do not replace `P^sR` by `RP^s` unless a separate commutation hypothesis justifies it. The support proof actually works for a common partial monomial `R`; unitarity of `R` is stronger than necessary for this conclusion.

## 5. Robust error lower bounds

Let `M` be any matrix with at most `t` nonzeros in every column, with `t ≥ 0`, and set `T = MQ^−1`. No routing hypothesis is needed here. Write `t₀ = min(t,N)` and

`D = (U − T)Q = UQ − M`.

At each zero entry of `M`, the matching entry of `D` has modulus at least `(1 − b)/√N`. There are at least `N(N − t₀)` such entries. Hence

`||D||F² ≥ (1 − b)² (N − t₀)`.

Using `||D||F ≤ ||U − T||F ||Q||2` gives

`||U − T||F/√N ≥ [(1 − b)/(1 + b)] √max(0,1 − t/N)`.

Also `||D||2 ≥ ||D||F/√N` and `||D||2 ≤ ||U − T||2 ||Q||2`, so

`||U − T||2 ≥ [(1 − b)/(1 + b)] √max(0,1 − t/N)`.

Thus the proposed constants and normalizations are correct. The unnormalized Frobenius lower bound instead has `√max(0,N − t)` in place of `√max(0,1 − t/N)`.

The constant depends on `a` and `r`. It can approach zero as `|a|^r → 1`; a uniform quantitative approximation obstruction needs a separate bound keeping `|a|^r` away from one. When `t ≥ N`, the bound is correctly vacuous.

## 6. Separate low-rank extension

Assume

`U = A Q^−1 + E`,

where `Q` is any invertible `N × N` matrix having at most `s` nonzeros per column, `A` has at most `t` nonzeros per column, and `rank(E) ≤ k < N` for an integer `k ≥ 0`. This section does not require the special permutation form of `Q`.

Choose any set `J` of `k+1` coordinates. The restriction of `EQ` to vectors supported on `J` has rank at most `k`, so its kernel contains a nonzero vector `v` supported on `J`. Define

`x = Qv ≠ 0`.

Multiplying the representation by `Qv` gives

`Ux = UQv = Av`,

because `EQv = 0`. Consequently,

`|supp(x)| ≤ s(k+1)` and `|supp(Ux)| ≤ t(k+1)`.

### Product inequality for every flat unitary

For any nonzero vector `x` and flat unitary `U`, put `p = |supp(x)|` and `q = |supp(Ux)|`. Flatness and Cauchy–Schwarz give

`||(Ux)||∞ ≤ ||x||1/√N ≤ √(p/N) ||x||2`.

Unitarity then implies

`||x||2² = ||Ux||2² ≤ q ||Ux||∞² ≤ (pq/N) ||x||2²`.

Canceling the positive squared norm yields `pq ≥ N`. Applying this to the selected `x` proves

`st(k+1)² ≥ N`.

No Fourier-specific result or normalization beyond flat unitarity is missing here.

### Additive inequality for prime-order Fourier

If `N` is prime and `U` is its discrete Fourier matrix, the prime cyclic uncertainty theorem states, for nonzero `x`,

`|supp(x)| + |supp(Ux)| ≥ N+1`.

Therefore

`(s+t)(k+1) ≥ N+1`.

Reference: Terence Tao, [An uncertainty principle for cyclic groups of prime order](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0308286), Mathematical Research Letters 12 (2005), 121–127. Scaling the Fourier normalization does not change supports. Left/right coordinate permutations and nonzero diagonal scalings likewise preserve the additive uncertainty property. Prime dimension alone, with an arbitrary flat unitary, is not the stated hypothesis of this theorem and must not be substituted without a separate argument.

### Edge cases and application to the packet numerator

- `k < N` is essential to selecting `k+1` coordinates; for `k ≥ N`, a full-rank residual gives no such conclusion.
- Invertible `Q` has `s ≥ 1`. If `t = 0`, the representation would force `U = E` and hence `rank(E) = N`, contradicting `k < N`.
- For the packet matrix `Q = I − a^rP^r`, one may always take `s = 2`; if `P^r = I`, take `s = 1`.
- The low-rank residual is exact and rank-bounded. An arbitrary small-norm residual does not satisfy the kernel argument.
- These low-rank bounds are independent necessary conditions. With `k = 0` and the special packet `Q`, the reverse-triangle support argument in Sections 3–4 is stronger and should be retained.

## 7. Arbitrary routes via a low-rank commutator correction

The commutation assumption can be replaced by an explicit rank penalty. Define commutators by `[R,Q] = RQ − QR`. For any matrix `R`,

`Q^−1 R = RQ^−1 + Q^−1 [R,Q] Q^−1`.

This sign is correct: the second term on the right equals `Q^−1R − RQ^−1`.

For arbitrary partial monomial right routes `R_i`, retain

`M = Σ_i L_i H_i R_i`.

Then, without any route commutation assumption,

`T = M Q^−1 + E`,

`E = Σ_i L_i H_i Q^−1 [R_i,Q] Q^−1`.

The numerator still has at most `r²` nonzeros per column. Moreover,

`[R_i,Q] = −a^r [R_i,P^r]`,

so rank subadditivity and rank monotonicity under multiplication give

`rank(E) ≤ Σ_i rank([R_i,P^r]) = K`.

If this arbitrary-route operator equals a flat unitary `U` and `K < N`, Section 6 applies with `A = M`, `s = 2`, `t = r²`, and `k = K`, giving

`2r²(K+1)² ≥ N`.

For the prime-order Fourier target, it also gives

`(r²+2)(K+1) ≥ N+1`.

If `K ≥ N`, these two displayed inequalities hold trivially, so they are valid necessary conditions in every case. `K` is an upper bound on the actual correction rank; replacing it by `rank(E)` gives a potentially stronger condition. The premise `T = U` must be stated before invoking either inequality.

## 8. What the audit does not establish

The conclusions limit the stated routed common-denominator representations. They do not establish a lower bound on scalar additions/multiplications, total computational work, all possible inverse-based algorithms, or unrestricted linear circuits. Such claims would need an independently specified computational model and a valid reduction from that model to these representations.

The finite geometric/root-packet identity and the uncertainty principles are established ingredients. This audit makes no novelty claim for them or for an operation-count lower bound.
