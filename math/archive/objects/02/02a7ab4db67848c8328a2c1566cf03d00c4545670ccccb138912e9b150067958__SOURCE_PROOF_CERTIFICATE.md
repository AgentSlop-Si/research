# Phase 18 proof certificate — exact finite Weyl magnetic-twirl fusion

**10 October 2026.** Statements are mathematical within the stated model; historical novelty is not established. **No universal faster FFT is claimed.**

## Model and notation

Let `N=K M`, where `K,M` are positive integers. Work over a field `F` whose characteristic does not divide `N` and which contains a primitive `N`-th root `omega`. We use exact field arithmetic. In coordinates indexed modulo `N`, define

- `S e_i = e_(i+1)`, the cyclic shift;
- `q=omega^M`, a primitive `K`-th root;
- `U e_i=q^i e_i`, so `US=q SU` and `U^K=I`.

Set `W_j=alpha S+beta q^j U`, `Q_j=I-W_j`, `G_j=Q_j^-1` wherever defined. The negative-exponent Fourier matrix is `F_N[k,t]=omega^(-kt)`.

## Theorem 1 — shared nonsingularity criterion

All `Q_j` have the determinant

`det Q_j=(1-beta^K)^M-alpha^N`.

Moreover, with `D=(1-beta^K)I-alpha^K S^K`,

`det D = ((1-beta^K)^M-alpha^N)^K`.

Consequently all `G_j` and `D^-1` exist exactly when

`(1-beta^K)^M != alpha^N`.

**Proof.** `Q_j` has diagonal entries `1-beta*q^(i+j)` and exactly one cyclic subdiagonal coefficient `-alpha`. In the Leibniz determinant expansion, the only two permitted permutations are the identity and the full cyclic shift. Hence `det Q_j = prod_i(1-beta q^(i+j))-alpha^N`. The diagonal product repeats each `K`-th root `M` times and equals `(1-beta^K)^M`. For `D`, `S^K` consists of `K` cycles of length `M`, and each cycle contributes `(1-beta^K)^M-alpha^(KM)`. Multiply the `K` contributions. QED.

## Theorem 2 — full growing-branch fusion

For any `K|N` and parameters satisfying Theorem 1,

`R_0 := (1/K) sum_(j=0)^(K-1) G_j`

`= ((1-beta^K)I-alpha^K S^K)^-1 * sum_(r=0)^(K-1) alpha^r S^r`.

**Proof.** The root-of-unity quantum binomial identity applied to `alpha*S` and `beta*q^j*U`, whose commutation ratio is `q`, gives

`W_j^K=alpha^K S^K+beta^K I`.

Thus `D=I-W_j^K` is independent of `j` and commutes with `W_j`. For invertible `D`,

`G_j=(I-W_j)^-1 = (sum_(t=0)^(K-1) W_j^t) D^-1`.

In the noncommutative expansion of `W_j^t`, each term with exactly `h` occurrences of `U` carries a factor `q^(j h)`. For `0<=t<K`, one has `0<=h<K`. Averaging over `j` annihilates all `h>0`; the `h=0` term is `(alpha S)^t`. Finally commute `D^-1` with the shift polynomial. QED.

**Remark.** The branches really are pairwise noncommuting for `K>1` and generic `alpha,beta != 0`: `Q_j` and `Q_l` have commutator proportional to `alpha*beta*(q^j-q^l)*[S,U]`, nonzero when `j != l`. The fusion is not obtained by merely summing commuting Green paths.

## Theorem 3 — completely charged O(N) evaluation

The full vector `R_0 x` is computable in `O(N)` exact field operations and `O(N)` memory, uniformly for `1<=K<=N`.

**Proof.** First form `z=P_K(S)x` for `P_K(S)=sum_(r=0)^(K-1) alpha^r S^r`. Its entries satisfy

`z_i=alpha*z_(i-1)+x_i-alpha^K*x_(i-K)`.

Compute `z_0` directly in `O(K)` operations and propagate the recurrence for all remaining `i` in `O(N)`. No division is required in this stage, including when `alpha^N=1`.

Next solve `D y=z`, i.e.

`d*y_i-c*y_(i-K)=z_i`, `d=1-beta^K`, `c=alpha^K`.

This separates into `K` independent cyclic recurrences of length `M=N/K` each. If `d != 0`, write `r=c/d` and do a zero-initial-value pass to determine the periodic closure, then a second pass to emit the solution, using the nonzero denominator `1-r^M`. If `d=0`, `c != 0` and `y_i=-z_(i+K)/c`. The scalar powers and inverses require `O(log N)` field arithmetic, while all passes sum to `O(N)` work. The implementation also validates the supplied root using `O(sqrt(N))` integer trial division and `O(log^2 N)` scalar powering work. These are within `O(N)`. Every output is emitted and charged `O(N)` memory accesses. QED.

The Python ledger counts actual additions, multiplications and inversions of the implementation. It does not model bit complexity or finding a root from scratch; those are different contracts.

## Theorem 4 — exact rank of the averaged operator

Under the conditions of Theorem 1,

`rank(R_0) = N` if `alpha^N != 1`, and `rank(R_0)=N-K+1` if `alpha^N=1`.

**Proof.** Since `D` is invertible, rank equals that of `P_K(S)`. The shift is diagonalizable over `F` with distinct eigenvalues `lambda` ranging through all `N`-th roots of unity. Its eigenvalue is `P_K(lambda)=sum_(r=0)^(K-1)(alpha*lambda)^r`. A zero requires `(alpha*lambda)^K=1` and `alpha*lambda !=1`. Such a zero forces `alpha^N=1`. If `alpha^N=1`, exactly `K` values have `(alpha*lambda)^K=1`, and precisely one of those instead gives the nonzero eigenvalue `K` (the characteristic does not divide `K`). Thus exactly `K-1` eigenvalues vanish. QED.

## Theorem 5 — weighted-character sectors and their fast evaluator

For `0<=ell<K`, define

`R_ell=(1/K) sum_j q^(-j ell) G_j`.

Then

`R_ell = D^-1 * beta^ell * [sum_(r=0)^(K-1-ell) alpha^r * [r+ell choose ell]_q * S^r] * U^ell`,

where `[. choose .]_q` denotes the Gaussian `q`-binomial. For each fixed `ell`, the complete vector `R_ell x` is computable in `O((ell+1)N + ell^2 + K)` field operations, with `O(N+ell)` retained storage.

**Proof.** The weighted root-of-unity filter in the proof of Theorem 2 selects exactly `ell` occurrences of `U` in all words of degree less than `K`. The noncommuting `q`-binomial theorem gives the coefficient displayed. Now

`[r+ell choose ell]_q = prod_(i=1)^ell (1-q^(r+i))/prod_(i=1)^ell(1-q^i)`.

Because `ell<K`, the denominator is nonzero. The numerator is a degree-`ell` polynomial in `q^r`, say `sum_(h=0)^ell c_h q^(hr)`. Therefore the bracketed shift polynomial is a linear combination of `ell+1` geometric windows, each of length `K-ell`, with ratios `alpha*q^h`. Each window admits the Theorem 3 sliding recurrence in `O(N)` operations; computing coefficients takes `O(ell^2)` field work, constructing `U^ell x` takes `O(N)`, and the common `D^-1` costs `O(N)`. QED.

**Covariance.** Each `R_ell` has the form `f_ell(S) U^ell`, so `R_ell S=q^ell S R_ell` and `[R_ell,S^K]=0`.

## Theorem 6 — exact subgroup-commutator rank and feedback obstruction

For every divisor `K|N`, over the same field of characteristic not 2,

`rank(F_N S^K-S^K F_N) = N-K` if `N-K` is even, and `=N` if `N-K` is odd.

Consequently, if `A` commutes with `S^K`, any additive correction `E` for which `F_N=A+E` satisfies

`rank(E) >= ceil(rank(F_N S^K-S^K F_N)/2)`.

This applies to any linear combination of Theorem 5's `K` weighted sectors, even using every sector.

**Proof.** Fourier intertwining gives `F_N S^K=T^(-K)F_N` and `S^K F_N=F_N T^K`. Thus the commutator equals `(T^(-K)-S^K)F_N`. Fourier is invertible. The matrix `T^(-K)-S^K` decomposes into `K` length-`M` cycles indexed by residue modulo `K`. In any cycle, the kernel recurrence fixes every coordinate from one starting coordinate. The consistency product is

`prod_(t=0)^(M-1) omega^(-K(r+Kt)) = (-1)^(K(M-1))=(-1)^(N-K)`.

When it is `+1`, each cycle contributes nullity one and the total rank is `N-K`; when it is `-1`, every cycle contributes nullity zero and rank is `N`. Because `[A,S^K]=0`, one has `[E,S^K]=[F_N,S^K]`; its rank is at most `rank(E S^K)+rank(S^K E)<=2rank(E)`. QED.

For the unweighted twirl, `[R_0,S]=0` (stronger than `[R_0,S^K]=0`), so the same theorem with `K=1` yields a rank-`ceil(N/2)` additive-correction barrier at even `N`, and `(N-1)/2` at odd `N`, regardless of how many unweighted twirls are summed.

## Theorem 7 — projective Weyl-line covering barrier (odd prime N)

Let `N` be an odd prime, and use standard Weyl monomials `W_(a,b)=S^a T^b`. Every Weyl coefficient of the exact DFT is nonzero:

`c_(a,b) = (1/N) tr(W_(a,b)^(-1) F_N)`

`= (1/N) sum_t omega^(-t^2-(a+b)t)`

`= (G/N) omega^((a+b)^2/4)`, where `G=sum_t omega^(-t^2) !=0`.

The nonvanishing follows from the standard quadratic Gauss-sum identity, valid in characteristic coprime to `N`. Consequently:

1. A sum of `B` one-direction operators `f_j(S^a_j T^b_j)`, each `(a_j,b_j)!=(0,0)`, cannot equal the DFT unless `B >= N+1`.
2. If each term is additionally multiplied on the left/right by arbitrary *Weyl monomials* (not arbitrary dense or arbitrary diagonal matrices), at least `N` such terms are necessary.

**Proof.** The powers of a nontrivial Weyl monomial occupy the `N` points of one one-dimensional linear subspace of the Weyl index plane `F_N^2`. Two distinct subspaces meet only at `(0,0)`, so `B` such lines cover at most `1+B(N-1)` points. All `N^2` Fourier coefficients are nonzero, forcing `B>=N+1`. Left/right Weyl multiplications shift the support into an affine line of `N` points; `B` affine lines cover at most `BN` points, so `B>=N`. These are sharp bounds for *support coverage*, not claims that these many Green resolvents suffice to reproduce exact Fourier coefficients. QED.

**CRITICAL counterexample:** The product `(I-alpha S)^-1 (I-beta T)^-1` has a nonzero coefficient at every Weyl index for nonzero parameters away from singularities, yet it applies to arbitrary inputs in `O(N)` work (one diagonal scaling and one cyclic solve). Thus full Weyl support is not a general lower bound for arbitrary multistage or noncommuting FFT circuits. The line theorem applies to *sums of single-direction operators only*.
