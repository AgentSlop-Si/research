This appendix does not alter the previously sealed main theorem or audit. It reviews the additional complex-field family Q_(h,alpha)=I-alpha P_h, where 0<alpha<1/sqrt(2), and in particular alpha=1/2.

## Exact-rank preservation

The support remains precisely the same bipartite tree: all diagonal entries are 1 and all parent-from-child entries are -alpha, which is nonzero. The already accepted forest-matching lemma gives equality of every submatrix rank with Q_h=I-P_h. Consequently every independent row/column order pair has exactly the same separation width, and inversion preserves this equality of optimized widths. The all-height lower and upper bounds therefore apply unchanged.

There is also an independent direct check requiring no forest theorem: with d(v) the depth of vertex v, let D be diagonal with D[v,v]=alpha^(-d(v)). For a parent u of v, D[u,u]/D[v,v]=alpha. Thus

    Q_(h,alpha)=D Q_h D^(-1).

Any submatrix is multiplied by nonsingular diagonal matrices on its rows and columns, which preserves its rank. This argument works over any field where alpha is nonzero; only the subsequent condition-number statement requires the complex Euclidean setting. The diagonal similarity itself need not be well-conditioned, but the direct norm estimate below is independent of its condition number.

Nilpotence gives Q_(h,alpha)^(-1)=sum_(j=0)^h alpha^j P_h^j. The unique-path property therefore yields entry alpha^d for an ancestor-descendant pair at distance d, and zero otherwise, as claimed.

## Uniform spectral conditioning

Distinct rows of P_h have disjoint child supports. For h>=1, P_h P_h* is diagonal, with a 2 at every internal vertex and a 0 at every leaf. Therefore ||P_h||_2=sqrt(2). For any vector x, the triangle and reverse triangle inequalities give

    (1-alpha sqrt(2))||x||_2
      <= ||(I-alpha P_h)x||_2
      <= (1+alpha sqrt(2))||x||_2.

The lower factor is strictly positive under the stated hypothesis. Hence the smallest singular value is at least 1-alpha sqrt(2), and the largest is at most 1+alpha sqrt(2). Their ratio bounds the spectral condition number. Inversion reciprocates singular values, so it leaves this ratio unchanged:

    kappa_2(Q_(h,alpha))=kappa_2(Q_(h,alpha)^(-1))
      <= (1+alpha sqrt(2))/(1-alpha sqrt(2)).

At alpha=1/2 this ratio simplifies exactly to 3+2sqrt(2)<6. For h=0, P_h=0 and Q_(h,alpha) is the scalar identity, so its condition number is 1. The manuscript states this exception explicitly and correctly.

## Scope

This is a valid strengthening: unbounded optimized exact separation rank occurs with uniformly bounded condition numbers and coefficients drawn from the fixed set {0,1,-1/2}. It eliminates the need to rely on a potentially ill-conditioned unit-edge witness.

It does not prove a lower bound on tolerance-dependent numerical separation rank. The distant-ancestor entries decay with depth; nonzero exact minors can be arbitrarily small. The manuscript correctly retains that distinction. No new claim about optimal SSS/HSS storage, a TQS open question, publication priority, or unrestricted arithmetic lower bounds is added.
