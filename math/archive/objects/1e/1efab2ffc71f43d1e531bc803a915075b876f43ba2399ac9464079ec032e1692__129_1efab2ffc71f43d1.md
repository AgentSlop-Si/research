# Exact experimental summary — interleaved scans and global broadcasts

**Result:** New rejection theorem for our specific global-scan/shuffle architecture; **not** a new FFT upper bound or a general lower bound.

## Algebraic certificates

- Scan inverse: bidiagonal, per-row support ≤2.
- Arbitrary interleaved permutation: inverse is still monomial, support remains 1 per row.
- Any t scan-only network: inverse row support ≤2^t. Because F_N^-1 is dense, t≥ceil(log2 N), and for N=power of two the boundary-row mass argument sharpens this to t≥log2 N+1.
- Each added invertible rank-r broadcast remains a rank-r perturbation of identity after inversion. Removing all broadcasts changes the inverse computation by rank at most B.
- Every s-row-sparse correction S to a Fourier matrix F satisfies rank(F−S)≥floor(N/(s+1)). Proof uses a nonzero Vandermonde minor on q consecutive rows and q unmodified columns.
- **Hybrid stage constraint:** B≥floor(N/(2^t+1)).

## Finite verification

- 12 project regression tests pass.
- 254 exact complete-transform basis columns tested by the audit, plus 35 fresh random field vectors; additional 48 random vectors and 127 basis vectors in unit tests, among other controls.
- 60 random invertible matrix words of scans, permutations, diagonal factors and broadcasts pass inverse support and rank-difference bounds.
- 288 independent Vandermonde minor witnesses over two separate primes and ten primitive-root lengths, with independent Gaussian elimination code.
- An ordinary radix-two transform was reconstructed as *two* one-way scans per butterfly level, and independently compared against full O(N²) DFT results. N=128 uses 14 scans, 896 scalar data-path multiplications, 896 scalar additions, 120 additional twiddle-table-generation multiplications, and 14 pair-shuffle calls (each entails O(N) memory movement).
- O(N)-work bidirectional *sums*, unlike compositions, were exhibited with entirely dense inverses for N=4,8,16,32. No one of these is a DFT, and the current theorem does not cover the family.

These are **algebraic correctness and operation-count experiments**, not wall-time benchmarks or novelty priority claims.
