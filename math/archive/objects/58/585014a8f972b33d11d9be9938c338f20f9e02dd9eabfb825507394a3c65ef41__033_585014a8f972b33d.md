# Phase-rotated commuting words: exact short-word classification and logarithmic gate

Independently verified, 9 October 2026. This addendum extends the fixed-operator obstruction in `PROOF.md` to phase-rotated operators, with a different conclusion: a logarithmic factor-count lower bound, rather than impossibility at every depth.

## Result

Let N >= 3 and let M be a unit-modulus weighted full N-cycle with M^N=-I. Set K(X)=(2I-X)(I-X)^-1. Consider a word of t stages chosen from

    K(lambda M), K(lambda M)^-1,
    K(lambda M*), K(lambda M*)^-1,       lambda^N=1.

Unitary monomial separators are allowed only when, after pushing them to an endpoint by conjugation, **every resulting stage is still exactly in this family for the same fixed M**. This is a substantive restriction, not a consequence of the stages merely commuting.

1. If t<N/2 and the word is scalar-unitary, then it is actually unitary and can be reordered into opposite-sign pairs. Each pair is either an identity or V(lambda M) or V(lambda M)^-1, where V(X)=K(X)K(X*)^-1. In particular t is even.
2. If such a word, at any depth, is a nonzero scalar multiple of a flat unitary, then t >= log_2 N. In the branch t<N/2, the sharper necessary inequality is

       c_N^t >= N,       c_N=2-3/(2^N+1)<2,

   and hence t>log_2 N.
3. The strict cutoff is essential. At t=N/2 for every even N, there are scalar-unitary words with only forward factors and with scalar modulus greater than 1. They do not admit the short-word pairing argument.

This proves an Omega(log N) gate for the entire stated phase-rotated commuting family. An Omega(N log N) execution consequence requires separately charging Omega(N) for each explicit full-array stage. It does not forbid algebraic fusion, arbitrary other commuting cycles, or genuinely noncommuting changes of cycle.

## 1. Rational functions and the exact pairing indices

Let a be an Nth root of unity. Use the circle location a, rather than the phase of the operator, as the index:

    A_a(z)=K(a^-1 z)=(2a-z)/(a-z),
    B_a(z)=K(a z^-1)=(2z-a)/(z-a).

Both forward factors have their unique unit-circle pole at z=a. Their inverses have their unique unit-circle zero there. The remaining zero or pole has radius 2 for A_a and radius 1/2 for B_a; it cannot contribute to a circle divisor.

At the matrix level,

    A_a(M)=K(a^-1 M),
    B_a(M)=K(a M*)=K((a^-1 M)*).

Consequently the cross-orientation pairing is

    A_a(M) B_a(M)^-1 = V(a^-1 M),
    B_a(M) A_a(M)^-1 = V(a^-1 M)^-1.                 (1)

A factor paired with the inverse of the same orientation cancels exactly. Equivalently, K(lambda M) pairs with K(lambda^-1 M*)^-1, **not generally** with K(lambda M*)^-1. This inverse-phase index is essential.

All original denominators are nonzero at every eigenvalue of M. Circle poles satisfy a^N=1, whereas the N distinct eigenvalues satisfy z^N=-1; off-circle poles cannot be eigenvalues of a unitary matrix.

## 2. The sampling-to-circle step

Replacing every z^-1 factor by its displayed degree-one numerator and denominator gives

    R(z)=P(z)/Q(z),         deg P <= t, deg Q <= t.

There is no extra factor z or z^-1: it cancels within each B_a stage and its inverse. In the unreduced product used here both degrees are exactly t and their constant terms are nonzero, although only the upper degree bound is needed. The original Q does not vanish on the spectral grid.

Suppose R(M)*R(M)=c^2 I with c>0. Since M is unitary with the N distinct roots of z^N+1 as eigenvalues,

    |P(z_j)|^2=c^2 |Q(z_j)|^2       for all z_j^N=-1.

For a polynomial p of degree at most t, define its length-t reciprocal conjugate

    p#(z)=z^t conjugate(p(1/conjugate(z))).

This is an ordinary polynomial. On |z|=1 it equals z^t conjugate(p(z)). Therefore

    H(z)=P(z)P#(z)-c^2 Q(z)Q#(z)

is a polynomial of degree at most 2t that vanishes at all N spectral points. If 2t<N, the distinct-root count forces H identically zero. Thus R has constant modulus c on the entire unit circle wherever the original rational expression is defined. Apparent circle singularities will be removable by the next step.

No Fourier grid interpolation or numerical observation is being substituted for this polynomial identity. Strict inequality N>2t is required.

## 3. Circle multiplicities force a complete pairing

For a circle point a, the zero multiplicity of p# at a equals that of p at a, because reciprocal conjugation fixes a. Taking multiplicities in P P#=c^2 Q Q# therefore gives

    2 ord_a(P)=2 ord_a(Q).

The only numerator circle zeros in P come from inverse stages; the only denominator circle zeros in Q come from forward stages. It follows that, separately at every a, the number of forward stages equals the number of inverse stages. This proves that t is even.

Because all stages commute, pair every forward stage with an inverse stage at the same a. The complete list of possibilities is

    A_a A_a^-1=I,       B_a B_a^-1=I,
    A_a B_a^-1=V(a^-1 M),
    B_a A_a^-1=V(a^-1 M)^-1.

This proves the classification. Each pair is unitary. Indeed, K(X*)=K(X)* and K(X) is normal, so K(X)K(X*)^-1 is unitary. Hence c=1 exactly; a nontrivial positive scalar modulus is impossible in the short-word branch.

The determinant gives an independent consistency check. Each forward stage has determinant D_N=(2^N+1)/2; each inverse stage has determinant D_N^-1. The number of forward and inverse stages is t/2 each, so det R(M)=1. If R(M) is scalar-unitary, this too gives c^N=1 and c=1. Endpoint monomials need not have determinant 1, but their determinant has modulus 1 and they do not change this modulus conclusion.

A convenient normal form is

    R(M)=product over a [V(a^-1 M)]^(p_a),

where p_a is the signed number of A_a stages. Circle balance makes the signed number of B_a stages equal -p_a. After exact cancellations, the number of nontrivial pairs is

    r=sum_a |p_a| <= t/2.                              (2)

This is an identity of rational functions before evaluation at M. The converse, that any circle-balanced word is unitary, holds at every depth; only necessity used t<N/2.

## 4. From the pairing to a flatness gate

For every X=lambda M with lambda^N=1, X is still an allowed weighted full cycle and X^N=-I. The explicit coefficients proved in `PROOF.md`, Section 4, give

    ||V(X)||_1=||V(X)^-1||_1=c_N=2-3/(2^N+1)<2,
    kappa_1(V(X))=c_N^2<4.

These bounds are independent of the cycle gauge and of lambda. Unitary monomial endpoints have induced-1 condition number 1, as do nonzero global scalars.

A flat unitary F has ||F||_1=||F^-1||_1=sqrt(N), hence kappa_1(F)=N. If the normalized word is a scalar multiple of such an F and t<N/2, submultiplicativity and (2) imply

    N <= c_N^(2r) <= c_N^t < 2^t.                    (3)

Here t>0 because a monomial cannot be flat for N>1. Thus t>log_2 N, and the stronger inequality c_N^t>=N holds in this branch.

If instead t>=N/2, then t>=log_2 N for N>=4, since N/2>=log_2 N. For N=3, t>=N/2 and integrality imply t>=2>log_2 3. Thus every depth is covered and t>=log_2 N for N>=3. One may combine the branches more precisely as

    t >= min(N/2, log(N)/log(c_N)),

with the understanding that t is an integer. The original factors, not just preselected locally unitary blocks, are counted here.

## 5. Why the strict cutoff cannot be removed

Let N=2m, and use all m phases lambda satisfying lambda^m=1. These phases also satisfy lambda^N=1. A product of t=m forward factors is

    R_m(z)=product_(lambda^m=1) K(lambda z)
          =(2^m-z^m)/(1-z^m).

On the spectrum z^N=-1, w=z^m satisfies w^2=-1, so w=+i or -i. Therefore

    |R_m(z)|^2=(4^m+1)/2

at every spectral point. Equivalently, with X=M^m and X^2=-I,

    R_m(M)=[(2^m+1)I+(2^m-1)X]/2,
    R_m(M)*R_m(M)=[(4^m+1)/2] I.

This scalar-unitary word has no inverse stages at all. Its rational function has actual circle poles and is not constant-modulus on the entire circle. In fact the interpolation polynomial is exactly H(z)=((2^m-1)^2/2)(z^N+1), of degree 2t=N. Thus equality t=N/2 is a genuine boundary, not a technical omission. These examples do not violate the factor-count gate; their depth is already N/2.

The closed form has only two nonzero entries per row, so it is not Fourier-flat for N>2. It is nevertheless a constructive example of sharing work across stages: after preparing the coefficients, the entire product applies in O(N) scalar arithmetic, instead of the Theta(N^2) cost of m separately applied linear passes. For a weighted cycle, an O(N)-prepared monomial gauge or prefix-product representation applies M^m in O(N) scalar work; it must not be built by N separate length-m multiplications. The integer 2^m can be generated by repeated squaring in O(log m) field multiplications (O(1) for m=1), but has m+1=Theta(N) bits. This is neither an O(N) bit-complexity nor a bounded-word arithmetic claim. The standalone `apply_fused_halfcycle.py` implements the exact-capable linear evaluator.

At t=N, taking all N phases also gives the scalar matrix ((2^N+1)/2)I. This further illustrates why unrestricted-depth circle pairing would be false.

## 6. Scope and verification

Monomial factors at the two endpoints only permute entry magnitudes and preserve unitarity up to a scalar, induced-1 condition number, and flatness. Interior monomials are covered only under the explicit common-family hypothesis after conjugation. Simultaneous diagonalizability alone does not imply that hypothesis. No claim is made for K(M^k), other rational functions sharing M's eigenbasis, nonunit-modulus phase multipliers, or genuinely noncommuting different cycles.

The independently written `check_commuting_phase.py` checks the pairing identities symbolically; verifies exact small complex-rational matrix examples; exhaustively checks short commuting multisets numerically for N=3,...,10 through length min(4,floor((N-1)/2)); checks phase-rotated matrix norms; checks the cutoff examples; and verifies the reusable fused half-cycle evaluator on exact weighted inputs. It imports standard-library code, NumPy, SymPy, and the independently authored fused evaluator, never uploaded executable source. The saved JSON distinguishes exact checks from numerical checks. Finite checks corroborate the proof but do not establish the all-N claim.

The result is a scoped factorization obstruction, not a general lower bound for Fourier algorithms and not a construction of an o(N log N) Fourier method. No publication or literature-priority claim is made.
