# Exact all-length obstruction for three bit-reversal quasiseparable scans

**Date:** 10 October 2026  
**Status:** A characteristic-zero theorem with an explicit scalar-network cut, independently executable certificates, and exactly verified Fourier minors. This does **not** prove an unrestricted FFT lower bound or yield a new fast FFT.

## Main theorem

Let `N = 2^k >= 64`. Let `F_N=(zeta_N^(i*j))_{0<=i,j<N}` be the unnormalized complex DFT, with `zeta_N` a primitive `N`th root of unity. Let `P_N` be the bit-reversal permutation matrix. Let `S_1,S_2,S_3` be **arbitrary** `N × N` matrices whose every strictly upper-right and lower-left off-diagonal cut block has rank at most **one** (order-one quasiseparable matrices). Then

\[
\boxed{F_N\ne S_3P_NS_2P_NS_1.}
\]

This includes *all* scalar lower and upper generator values, zeros, singular stages, arbitrary complex magnitudes, arbitrary diagonal entries, and arbitrary transition generators. It is **not** restricted to the narrower `a_i b_j / c_i e_j` separated-generator chart.

It assumes precisely two internal **bit-reversal** permutations, Fourier inputs and outputs in **natural** order, and exactly three rank-one quasiseparable stages. It does **not** exclude other permutations, independently permuted endpoints, rank-two stages, four or more stages, or completely different FFT circuits.

## 1. A complete scalar-state realization of order-one quasiseparable matrices

A classical scalar quasiseparable generator representation writes the strictly lower entries as

\[
 S_{ij}=p_i\,a_{i-1}a_{i-2}\cdots a_{j+1}\,q_j\qquad(i>j)
\]

and the upper entries as

\[
 S_{ij}=g_i\,b_{i+1}b_{i+2}\cdots b_{j-1}\,h_j\qquad(i<j),
\]

with diagonal `d_i`; empty products are one. All these scalars may vanish. The representation is equivalent to order-one quasiseparability, including reducible or block-diagonal cases. Standard references are listed below.

For an input vector `x`, compute

\[
 f_0=0,\quad f_i=a_{i-1}f_{i-1}+q_{i-1}x_{i-1} \quad(i\ge1),
\]
\[
 b_{N-1}^*=0,\quad b_i^*=b_{i+1}b_{i+1}^*+h_{i+1}x_{i+1}\quad(i\le N-2),
\]
\[
 y_i=d_ix_i+p_if_i+g_ib_i^*.
\]

We denote the **one-scalar forward state wire** at coordinate `i` by `f_i`, the one-scalar backward wire by `b_i`, and the scalar input wire by `x_i`. The recurrence means that any influence from `x_j` to `y_i` moves:
- directly, when `j=i`;
- monotonically forward through `f_{j+1},...,f_i`, when `j<i`;
- monotonically backward through `b_{j-1},...,b_i`, when `j>i`.

Zeros remove some paths but can never add paths. Scalar multipliers do not alter this connectivity over-approximation.

### Rank-cut lemma

If a directed linear-arithmetic DAG has `q` **one-dimensional scalar wires** whose removal disconnects a selected set `C` of input coordinates from a selected set `R` of output coordinates, then

\[
\operatorname{rank}(T[R,C])\le q.
\]

Indeed, with the other input coordinates zero, all information from `C` reaching `R` must cross those `q` scalar wires. Since every gate is linear, the output restrictions factor through the values on these `q` wires; this gives a linear factorization through `\mathbb C^q`. This argument is independent of coefficients and does not require stable or invertible stages.

## 2. Exact bit-reversal identities

Write `N=32m`, where `m=2^(k-5)`. If `rev_s(a)` denotes reversal of the `s`-bit representation of `a`, the identity

\[
\boxed{P_N(am+b)=32\,\operatorname{rev}_{\log_2m}(b)+\operatorname{rev}_5(a)}
\]

holds for `0<=a<32` and `0<=b<m`. The same identity, with the roles of the blocks exchanged, gives

\[
P_N(32z+s)=m\operatorname{rev}_5(s)+\operatorname{rev}_{\log_2m}(z).
\]

We choose the **first `13m` input coordinates**

\[
C=\{0,1,\ldots,13m-1\}
\]

and the **last `13m` output coordinates**

\[
R=\{19m,19m+1,\ldots,32m-1\}.
\]

## 3. The explicit cut (no graph optimization required)

Delete the following scalar wires:

| Stage | Wires removed | Count |
|---|---|---:|
| 1 | Forward wire at `13m` | `1` |
| 2 | Input wires `32z+24`, `32z+26`, `32z+28` for every `z=0,...,m-1` | `3m` |
| 2 | Forward wire `32z+21` for each `z=0,...,m-1` | `m` |
| 2 | Backward wire `32z+31` for `z=0,...,m-2` | `m-1` |
| 3 | Input wires in the seven `m`-blocks numbered `20,22,24,25,26,28,30` | `7m` |
| 3 | Forward wire at `19m` | `1` |
| **Total** | | **`12m+1`** |

### Stage 1

Because inputs outside `C` are zero, the only possible way to carry influence beyond coordinate `13m-1` is the first-stage forward state `f_(13m)`. Cutting that scalar wire confines the affected outputs of the first stage to indices in `C`.

### First bit reversal and stage 2

The first shuffle sends `C` to **exactly 13 residue positions in each 32-coordinate block**:

\[
A=\{0,2,4,6,8,10,12,16,18,20,24,26,28\}.
\]

This follows by evaluating `rev_5(a)` for `a=0,...,12` in the first bit-reversal identity.

Cutting stage-2 input wires at residues `24,26,28` leaves sources only at

\[
A'=\{0,2,4,6,8,10,12,16,18,20\}.
\]

For each 32-block, cutting the forward wire at residue `21` blocks all influence to later residues from these sources. Cutting the backward wire at the boundary residue `31` prevents influence from the **next** block from propagating backward. Thus the only possible stage-2 output influence lies in residues `0,...,20` of every 32-block.

### Second bit reversal and stage 3

Applying the second identity to the stage-2 output positions sends them to complete `m`-blocks with block indices

\[
V=\{0,1,2,4,5,6,8,9,10,12,14,16,17,18,20,22,24,25,26,28,30\}.
\]

Exactly seven of these indices are at least `19`:

\[
V\cap\{19,...,31\}=\{20,22,24,25,26,28,30\}.
\]

Remove those seven `m`-blocks of stage-3 input scalar wires. Every remaining influenced stage-3 input position is strictly less than `19m`. Cutting its forward state at `19m` prevents any input influence from reaching output coordinates in `R`. Backward states can move information only **toward smaller indices**, so they cannot circumvent this final cut.

The original input set `C` and final output set `R` have now been disconnected by only `12m+1` scalar wires. Therefore

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}((S_3P_NS_2P_NS_1)[R,C])\le12m+1.}
\]

## 4. Fourier requires rank `13m`

For `i=19m,...,32m-1` and `j=0,...,13m-1`,

\[
F_N[i,j]= (\zeta_N^i)^j.
\]

These `13m` numbers `zeta_N^i` are pairwise distinct, so `F_N[R,C]` is a square Vandermonde matrix with nonzero determinant

\[
\det(F_N[R,C])=\prod_{19m\le i<j<N}(\zeta_N^j-\zeta_N^i)\ne0.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}(F_N[R,C])=13m.}
\]

For `m>=2`, `13m>12m+1`. Contradiction. **QED.** At `m=1` (`N=32`), this particular cut has capacity exactly 13 and gives no contradiction.

## 6. A rank-aware extension of this fixed cut

For stages with forward/backward channel ranks `r_1,r_2,r_3`, the same cut counts

\[
\boxed{10m+r_1+(2m-1)r_2+r_3}
\]

scalar channel dimensions: stage-2 and stage-3 input cuts still cost `3m+7m=10m`, and the state cuts have their corresponding channel ranks. For all ranks equal to 1 this is `12m+1`. With ranks 2 the inequality need not contradict Fourier's `13m` rank. This theorem is consequently about the **rank-one** (not unrestricted-rank) architecture.

## 7. Secondary structural certificates

### A cheap scan with maximal bit-reversed cut rank

Let `S_N[i,j]=1` for `i>j`, `=3` for `i=j`, and `=2` for `i<j`. For any permutation putting all even original indices before all odd original indices, including bit reversal, both off-diagonal blocks at **every** cut `k` attain rank `min(k,N-k)`.

For a selected even set `E={2a_1<...<2a_s}`, select the odd partner columns `E+1`. The resulting matrix has `1` below and `2` on/above its diagonal. Its determinant is **`2`**, not an alternating sign. For the opposite orientation, use odd partner rows `E+1`; the resulting matrix has `1` on/below and `2` above, with determinant `(-1)^(s-1)`. These are never zero. This shows why single-matrix cut-rank magnitude is not a certificate of high arithmetic cost: `S_N` applies using just two scans.

The determinant of this specific scan also has the exact formula `det(S_N)=2^(N+1)-1`. Indeed, `S_N=(I-L)+2J`, with `L` the strictly lower all-ones matrix and `J=11^T`; the matrix determinant lemma and `(I-L)^(-1)1=(1,2,4,...,2^(N-1))^T` give the formula.

### A bounded-generator amplitude tradeoff (restricted, not a new FFT lower bound)

For a separated-generator scan `S` with `|a_i|,|b_i|,|c_i|,|e_i|,|d_i| <= B` and `B>=1`, introduce one forward and one backward auxiliary state per coordinate. The exact `3N × 3N` sparse block determinant has Schur complement equal to `S`. Rescale the two state-variable groups by `B` and their equations by `1/B` (net determinant factor one). Each of its first `N` rows has Euclidean norm at most `sqrt(3) B^2`; each other row at most `sqrt(3)`. Hadamard's inequality yields

\[
|\det S|\le 3^{3N/2}B^{2N}.
\]

For `t` such stages with coefficient bounds `B_i>=1`, permutations do not change determinant magnitude, while `|det F_N|=N^(N/2)`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{\prod_{i=1}^{t} B_i\ge 3^{-3t/4}N^{1/4}.}
\]

If `B_i<=B` this gives `B >= 3^(-3/4) N^(1/(4t))`. It is restricted to **unnormalized** Fourier output, this parameterization, and coefficient size. It is analogous to classical determinant-based bounded-coefficient arguments (Morgenstern 1973), not a lower bound for unrestricted exact complex FFT arithmetic.

### Exact count of zero `2×2` Fourier minors

For `N=2^k`,

\[
\det F_N[\{r_1,r_2\},\{c_1,c_2\}]
=\zeta_N^{r_1c_1+r_2c_2}
\bigl(1-\zeta_N^{(r_1-r_2)(c_2-c_1)}\bigr).
\]

It vanishes exactly when `N | (r_2-r_1)(c_2-c_1)`. For `a=0,...,k-1`, exactly `N²/2^(a+2)` unordered index pairs have 2-adic difference valuation `a`. Summing pairs with valuations `a+b >= k` gives

\[
\boxed{Z_N=\frac{N^3}{8}(k-2)+\frac{N^2}{4}}
\]

zero `2×2` Fourier minors (for `N>=2`), out of `binom(N,2)^2`. The tests verify the formula against brute-force enumeration at `N=4,8,16,32`.

### Exceptional-branch-complete quartic compatibility for three stages

For any invertible order-one QS outer stages, define `A=S_1^(-1)`, `C=S_3^(-1)`. The nullity theorem preserves their QS cut ranks under inversion. Then

\[
F_N=S_3PS_2PS_1
\iff
S_2=PCF_NAP.
\]

Thus a three-stage factorization exists **if and only if** there are invertible order-one QS `A,C` such that `PCF_NAP` is also order-one QS. The QS conditions on `A,C` are quadratic `2×2` minors, while those on the middle product are **quartic** in the entries of `A,C`, with coefficients in `Q(zeta_N)`. No division by a generator entry or nonzero-corner assumption is made; singular intermediate candidates are excluded by the invertibility requirement.

By Cauchy–Binet, the quartic relations are bilinear in the exterior-square (Plücker) minors of `PC` and `AP`, with **exact Fourier `2×2` minors** as known coefficients. This is a reusable nonlinear feasibility formulation for other fixed permutations and for the four-stage problem, although the present bitrev three-stage case is already excluded by the stronger rank-cut theorem.
