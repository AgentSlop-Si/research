# FFT research checkpoint — inverse-stage elimination and Fourier-support equations

**Date:** October 9, 2026  
**Status:** Verified exact algebraic reformulations, not a faster FFT and **not a proof that the proposed two-stage factorization is impossible**. The source archive includes all previous checkpoints.

## Goal and model

Keep the original goal: a fixed, input-independent transform of every fresh input with every DFT output exact, eventually with `o(N log N)` **fully charged** arithmetic—not cache reuse, threads, input-specific speedups, or a disguised radix-2 FFT.

This checkpoint investigates the narrower finite-dimensional feasibility question for `N=8`:

\[
 F_8 = B P A,\quad (P x)_i=x_{p_i},\quad p=[7,0,6,3,4,1,2,5],
\]

where each `A,B` belongs to the **global separated-generator rank-one** stage family

\[
 S_{ij}=\begin{cases} \ell_i v_j & i>j,\\ d_i & i=j,\\u_i w_j&i<j.\end{cases}
\]

This family is a dense chart of order-one quasiseparable matrices, but not the entire order-one class at zero-corner boundary points. The previous checkpoint established that all the ordinary information-flow min-cut tests can pass while this family's image still has dimension only 60 in the ambient 64-dimensional matrix space.

## A. Exact degree-28 to degree-4 reduction (main new result)

Put `C=A^{-1}` and rearrange:

\[
 B=F_8 C P^{-1}.
\]

**Classical rank-inversion fact.** For an invertible `S`, the complementary-block nullity theorem gives at every cut `k`:

\[
 \operatorname{rank}(S^{-1}[k{:}8,0{:}k]) = \operatorname{rank}(S[k{:}8,0{:}k]),
\]

and likewise for the upper crossing block. Thus `S^{-1}` has quasiseparable order at most one whenever `S` does. See Strang–Nguyen, *The Interplay of Ranks of Submatrices*, SIAM Review 2004, and Vandebril et al., *A note on the nullity theorem*, JCAM 2006. Crucial restriction: preservation of **QS cut ranks** does **not** imply preservation of the entire global separated-generator parameterization at degenerate corners; see D below.

On the dense open patch where `C` has nonzero diagonal and lower/upper corner anchors, use an intermediate-column diagonal gauge to set `diag(C)=1`, and the two scalar generator gauges to fix `\ell_7=1`, `u_0=1`. Hence `C` depends on exactly **26 coordinates**: six remaining lower row generators, seven lower column generators, six remaining upper row generators, and seven upper column generators.

Let `B=F_8 C P^{-1}`. If `B_{7,0}B_{0,7}\neq0`, membership of `B` in the rank-one separated-generator family is equivalent to the **30 identities**

\[
 B_{ij}B_{7,0}-B_{i,0}B_{7,j}=0\quad(2\le i\le6,1\le j<i),
\]

\[
 B_{ij}B_{0,7}-B_{i,7}B_{0,j}=0\quad(1\le i\le5,i<j\le6).
\]

The identities are both necessary and sufficient on that open patch: set the lower generator row factors from column zero divided by the pivot `B_{7,0}`, and column factors from row seven; use row zero and column seven for the upper part. This matches the earlier chart proof, except we have **no adjugate in the equations**.

Each entry of `C` is a polynomial of degree at most 2 in the 26 variables, and `F_8 C P^{-1}` is linear in entries of `C`. Thus the 30 membership equations have total **degree at most 4**. Their exact coefficients lie in `Z[z]/(z^4+1)`, where `z` is a formal primitive eighth root of unity; no numerical root approximation enters the exported polynomials.

**Generated exact certificate:** `quartic_cyclotomic_equations.json` consists of **1,268 nonzero coefficient–monomial terms involving 389 distinct monomials**. Maximum total degree is exactly 4. File SHA-256 is recorded in `results.json`. Each coefficient is a four-integer vector in the basis `(1,z,z²,z³)`.

**Equivalence conditions:** Any solution satisfying `det(C)\neq0` and the required nonzero chart anchors, **including the inverse-stage anchor conditions**, gives `A=C^{-1}` and `B=F_8 C P^{-1}` in the separated-generator family and an exact Fourier factorization. Conversely, any factorization lying in this same regular locus can be normalized to a root of these equations. Without these inequations, the equations admit singular false positives.

This is a **computational simplification of an exact feasibility problem**, not a proof of its feasibility or infeasibility and not a statement about arithmetic cost.

## B. A further Fourier-specific elimination: 25 equations in 21 variables

Use the structure of `B` rather than generic polynomial elimination. Let `\zeta=z`, `\xi=z^{-1}`, and assume `B_{7,0}\ne0`. Define

\[
 \beta = B_{7,1}/B_{7,0}.
\]

The five lower-stage identities for column `j=1`, combined with this defining equality for row seven, are equivalent to

\[
 (F_8(C_{:,0}-\beta C_{:,7}))_i=0,\qquad i=2,3,\ldots,7.
\]

**Fourier-support lemma:** In characteristic zero or characteristic not dividing eight, this is equivalent to

\[
 C_{k,0}-\beta C_{k,7}=a+b\xi^k,\quad k=0,\dots,7.
\]

Let `t=C[7,0]=v_0`, `s=C[0,7]=w_7`. Using the diagonal normalization, the `k=0` and `k=7` cases force

\[
 b=\frac{t-1+\beta(s-1)}{\zeta-1},\qquad a=1-\beta s-b.
\]

The remaining six cases then force the **explicit** first-stage lower generators

\[
 \boxed{\ell_k=\frac{a+b\zeta^{-k}+\beta s u_k}{t}},\qquad k=1,\ldots,6.
\]

Thus `\ell_1..\ell_6` are completely eliminated, while a single `\beta` variable is introduced: **26 variables become 21**, and **30 membership equations become 25**. This is necessary and sufficient for exactly the five eliminated identities on the patch `t\ne0,B_{7,0}\ne0`; the other identities and invertibility conditions still need solving.

To generate an **honest polynomial system without denominators**, let `h_i=s u_i` for `i=0..6` (so `h_0=s`), `\eta_j=w_j/s` for `j=1..6`, `\eta_7=1`, and let `D=t(\zeta-1)`. Construct `\widetilde C=D C` using

\[
 T_i=(\zeta-1)(1-\beta h_0+\beta h_i)
 +(t-1+\beta(h_0-1))(\zeta^{-i}-1),\qquad i=1,\ldots,6.
\]

Then, for `i>j`,

\[
 \widetilde C_{ij}=\begin{cases}T_i v_j,&1\le i\le6,\\D v_j,&i=7,\end{cases}
\]

and for `i<j`,

\[
 \widetilde C_{ij}=\begin{cases} D h_i \eta_j,&j\le6,\\D h_i,&j=7,\end{cases}
\]

with diagonal `\widetilde C_{ii}=D`, `v_0=t`. By exact symbolic expansion over the cyclotomic ring, **all five eliminated B identities vanish identically**, not merely at sampled points.

**Generated second certificate:** `reduced_sextic_equations.json` contains exactly **25 nontrivial equations in 21 variables**, maximum total degree **6**, **1,749 nonzero terms**, **550 distinct monomials**. We verified both the cleared matrix entries and all 25 equations over two primes and two primitive-root embeddings per prime. Degree rises from 4 to 6; this is a trade-off, not an unconditional Gröbner speedup.

## C. Full exceptional-case encoding (no normalization or saturation needed)

For a proof that excludes *every* two-stage separated-generator factorization at the fixed permutation, checking only a regular patch is insufficient. `complete_quadratic.py` provides an **equivalent, exception-complete system**:

- 64 independent entries for `A`, 64 for `B`, and 28 lower/upper generator coordinates for each stage: **184 unknowns**.
- For every off-diagonal entry of both stages, enforce `A_ij=\ell_i v_j` or `A_ij=u_iw_j`, and analogously for `B`: **112 quadratic equations**.
- Enforce every entry of `B P A=F_8`: **64 further quadratic equations**.

**Total: 176 equations in 184 variables, all degree at most 2, with 800 nonzero terms.** No corner needs to be nonzero; no singular spurious stage solution exists because `F_8` is invertible, forcing both `A` and `B` to be invertible. The system's equivalence is immediate by interpreting the matrix-entry variables and generator variables.

This is standard polynomial quadratization with more variables—not a claim that 184 variables are easier to eliminate. It is an essential independent control for any future full-class impossibility claim. It addresses the separated-generator stage family, not the larger *entire* QS-order-one class at degenerate corners.

## D. Two exact exceptional-case traps

**D1. Inverse of a separated-generator stage may exit that global chart.** Take

\[
 C=\begin{pmatrix}2&1&1\\1&1&1\\1&1&3\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 C^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}1&-1&0\\-1&5/2&-1/2\\0&-1/2&1/2\end{pmatrix}.
\]

`C` has separated rank-one upper and lower entries and determinant 2. But the inverse has nonzero entries at `(1,0)` and `(2,1)` and zero entry at `(2,0)`. A single global lower representation `\ell_i v_j` cannot realize this pattern, although every cross-cut lower rank remains at most one. So the inverse-chart technique genuinely requires checking corner regularity or retaining the larger QS class.

**D2. The 30 quartics have a false positive on the singular locus.** Set **every** normalized first-stage coordinate equal to 1. Then `C=J`, the all-ones matrix, has rank one. For any primitive eighth root, `F_8 J` is zero except for its first row, where every entry equals eight. Thus `B=F_8 J P^{-1}` obeys every lower and upper separated-stage quadratic identity even though `det(C)=0` and `B_{7,0}=0`. So a Gröbner calculation that reports *some* root of the 30 quartics does **not** produce a Fourier factorization. Conversely, one cannot declare the regular patch impossible merely by discarding these solutions without a rigorous saturation proof.

Both examples are checked in `exceptional_charts.py` and `tests/test_inverse_gate.py`.

## E. Exact verification

- `inverse_quartic.py`: full quartic export, 4 field/root embeddings, 24 fresh random samples per embedding, 2,880 direct polynomial vs matrix evaluations, 96 independently constructed exact synthetic products; cut ranks of the inverse checked at every split. None of the 96 random Fourier first-stage guesses solved the 30 equations, **not evidence of nonexistence**.
- `fourier_support_elimination.py`: 120 exact random constructions satisfy the five Fourier-support-eliminated lower equations (30 per embedding).
- `reduced_sparse_sextic.py`: the five equations vanish **as formal polynomials** in `Z[z]/(z^4+1)`; all 25 remaining cleared equations and all 64 cleared matrix entries are checked in 64 random samples across 4 embeddings.
- `complete_quadratic.py`: the exception-complete formulation is checked against exact synthetic targets with both generic stages and zero-anchor diagonal stages.
- `exceptional_charts.py`: tests the exact 3×3 inverse counterexample and the 8×8 singular Fourier false positive.
- `tests/test_inverse_gate.py`: 10 independent new regression tests. The full `run_all.py` additionally runs 37 tests from all five prior checkpoint generations: **47 tests total**.

## F. What the results do and do not establish

**Established:** We have two explicit sparse cyclotomic polynomial systems for one Fourier-specific regular patch, a complete auxiliary-variable system for every separated-generator chart (including zero anchors), exact inverse/corner caveats, and independent finite-field verification of all generated identities.

**Not established:** The 25 equations have not been solved or excluded on the determinant/anchor-saturated locus; the 30 equations have not been solved or excluded either. Neither system extends to every degenerate representation of the broader QS family. There is no new all-length FFT algorithm, no verified sub-`N log N` runtime from this research, and no new general lower bound.

**Next decisive gate:** Solve the 25-equation/21-variable system with saturation by the product of all required nonzero coordinates/determinants **over `Q(z)` modulo `z^4+1`**, or obtain a certified exact solution. Distinguish the regular-patch conclusion from the full-stage conclusion. Independently attack the complete 176-quadratic ideal or stratify exceptional generators with proven exhaustive coverage. Reject numerical near-solutions and unverified modular nonexistence as proof. If an exact factorization is constructed, check the polynomial identities, all Fourier entries, stage implementability, and honest arithmetic scaling for growing `N`.
