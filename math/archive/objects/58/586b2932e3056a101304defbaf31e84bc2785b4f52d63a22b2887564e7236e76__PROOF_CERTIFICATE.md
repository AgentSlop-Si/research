# Phase 8 proof certificate — exact scope

## Lemma 1 (cross-cut transport through arbitrary layers)

Suppose matrices `T_i = B_i + E_i` act on a fixed direct sum `K^m ⊕ K^(N-m)`, where each `B_i` preserves the two summands and `rank(E_i) <= r_i`. For `P=T_L ... T_1`, `B=B_L ... B_1`, expand the noncommutative telescoping difference as

```
P-B = sum_{i=1}^L (T_L ... T_{i+1}) E_i (B_{i-1} ... B_1).
```

All products are in their original order. Each term has rank ≤`r_i`. Rank subadditivity gives `rank(P-B) <= sum r_i`. Since `B` is block diagonal, the top-right `m×(N-m)` off-diagonal block of `P` also has rank ≤`sum r_i`.

Take `P=F_N`. This off-diagonal block has entries `z_j^k`, `k=0..m-1`, `j=m..N-1`, and the nodes `z_j=omega^(-j)` are distinct. A rectangular Vandermonde with consecutive exponents has rank `min(m,N-m)`. This proves the cut rank bound.

**Excluded:** changing cuts, arbitrary whole-array GL gauges counted as free, nonlinear or out-of-place register operations, and claims about the overall scalar gate count.

## Lemma 2 (sharpness with invertible block diagonal baseline)

For `N=2m` partition `F=[A B; C D]`, all four m×m blocks are Vandermonde up to nonsingular diagonal scaling, so A and D are invertible. Let `H=CA^(-1)B`. For a scalar `t ≠ 0,1`, set

```
base(t) = diag( t A, D - H/(1-t) ).
```

Then

```
F-base(t) = [ (1-t)A , B ; C , H/(1-t) ].
```

Its Schur complement relative to invertible `(1-t)A` is zero; its rank is exactly m. The lower block of `base(t)` tends to D as `t` tends to infinity, so its determinant is not the zero rational function of t. Over complex numbers, choose any sufficiently large t away from isolated bad values. In our finite fields, `p≥N+1` and at most m values of `(1-t)^(-1)` can make `det(D - H/(1-t))` vanish (the constant term is `det D ≠ 0`); choose a valid scalar from `F_p\{0,1}`. The code constructs and checks one.

**Caution:** Dense A, H, Schur and baseline are not free operators. This is rank-only sharpness.

## Lemma 3 (CRT transfer invertibility)

With `N=2m`, a complete two-child polynomial-remainder transform of a degree `<2m` polynomial has coefficient matrix

```
T = [I, R_A; I, R_B].
```

Let `V_A,V_B` be Vandermonde evaluation matrices for the two disjoint sets of m distinct Nth roots. Then row-permuted Fourier evaluation factors as `diag(V_A,V_B)T`. Both Vandermonde blocks and the full Fourier matrix are invertible. Thus `T` is invertible. Subtract its first block row from the second, leaving `diag(I, R_B-R_A)` in block triangular form. This proves `rank(R_B-R_A)=m` for *every* balanced partition.

## Lemma 4 (minimum transfer support and unique equality)

`R_A` is the matrix of multiplication by `z^m` in `K[z]/P_A`, since its column t represents `z^(m+t) mod P_A`. Its eigenvalues under the evaluation-basis isomorphism are `z_j^m`, all nonzero. Hence `R_A` is invertible, as is `R_B`, so the total number of nonzero entries is ≥`2m`.

Equality forces each square invertible transfer to have exactly m entries: it is a monomial matrix. Its first column is exactly the negative vector of the lower coefficients of the monic root polynomial `P_A`, with nonzero constant term. Therefore the first column's unique nonzero entry must be its constant entry, and all other lower coefficients vanish. Hence `P_A=z^m-c`. For N=2m, `z_j^m=(-1)^j`, so these roots are exactly one parity class; the complement is the other parity class. Conversely both parity classes give `R_A=I`, `R_B=-I` up to their assignment. The sparse transfer is uniquely the classical radix-2 butterfly split.

This minimizes **literal monomial-basis transfer support**, not general linear-circuit complexity.

## Lemma 5 (computer-assisted 16-point root support certificate)

For primitive 16th root `ζ`, `Φ_16(ζ)=ζ^8+1=0`; `1,ζ,...,ζ^7` are an integral basis of `Z[ζ]`. The exhaustive routine multiplies `(z-ζ^j)` for every 8-element root subset with integer coefficient vectors, where multiplying by `ζ^j` is an exact signed shift in that eight-dimensional basis. A vanishing polynomial coefficient is zero iff all 8 integer entries vanish. This establishes the exact 16-point root-polynomial histograms saved in `AUDIT_RESULTS.json`.

For the transfer matrix, the independent audit enumerates all 6,435 complementary pairs and all `R_B-R_A` ranks over `F_17`. A characteristic-zero transfer entry reducing to a nonzero residue mod 17 cannot have been zero in `Z[ζ_16]`; hence the observed minimal finite-field support supplies a *lower bound* on characteristic-zero support. No converse inference from finite-field zeros is made.

## Lemma 6 (semantic interning stop rule)

At stage `s` of ordinary in-place radix-2 FFT, each intermediate wire is a linear form supported on exactly `2^s` distinct original input symbols. Different stages have different support sizes; within a stage, the N wire forms are rows of an invertible linear transform and are pairwise linearly independent. Thus no pair of distinct stored wire forms can be proportional by an input-independent nonzero scalar. Equality-only union-find saves zero scalar computations in this specific graph, although shared *templates* remain abundant.

## Historical unitary entropy lemma (Ailon 2013 — not original)

For a unitary matrix the squared magnitudes in each row form a probability distribution. The normalized DFT has N rows each uniform over N inputs: total entropy `N log2 N`; the identity begins with 0. A two-row unitary transformation preserves the per-input sum of the two affected squared amplitudes, changing their combined row entropy by at most 2 bits. At least `N log2 N / 2` two-row unitary gates are needed. Our numerical reproduction confirms each normalized radix-2 butterfly increases this potential by exactly 2 bits. It is incorrect to extend this to arbitrary nonunitary circuits or quantum readout without additional proof.
