# Verified extensions of the same obstruction

Date: 2026-10-10 (UTC). These are addenda to `SEALED_PROOF.md`; the original
cycle theorem remains accepted with no change to its hypotheses.

## A. Every invertible matrix with at most two nonzero entries per column

**ACCEPT.** Replace each Q_i=D_i+E_iP_i by an arbitrary invertible N-by-N
matrix Q_i having at most two nonzero entries in every column. All conclusions
and endpoint-routing allowances of the main theorem remain valid:

\[
F_N=\sum_{i=1}^{r}L_iQ_i^{-1}R_i
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad N\le r(r+1)^r.
\]

No row-sparsity hypothesis on Q_i is needed. Tree branches of arbitrarily
high vertex degree are allowed.

### Proof of the generalized structural lemma

An invertible Q has no zero column. Build a support multigraph with one vertex
for every row coordinate and one labeled edge for every column. A two-entry
column is an edge between its two supported rows. A singleton column is
represented by a loop at its supported row. Loops and parallel edges retain
their multiplicity.

For any connected component with v vertices and e columns, all its columns
are independent and supported on those v coordinates, so e<=v. Summing over
all components gives sum(e)=sum(v)=N; consequently e=v in every component.
Every connected component is therefore unicyclic. This uses the ordinary
multigraph convention that a loop or a pair of parallel edges can form the
unique cycle.

Select k rows S of Q^{-1} and delete the corresponding k **column-labeled
edges** from Q. The remaining graph is a pseudoforest: each component is a
tree or is still unicyclic. The number of tree components is exactly k,
because every tree contributes one and every unicyclic component contributes
zero to

\[
\sum_C(|V(C)|-|E(C)|)=N-(N-k)=k.
\]

All retained columns remain independent, including after restriction to
their support component. A tree component has v-1 columns and local left
nullity one. A surviving unicyclic component has v columns and local left
nullity zero. Hence the left nullspace of Q[:,S^c] is a direct sum of k
one-dimensional spaces with disjoint vertex supports. As before,
Q^{-1}[S,:] is a basis of that nullspace. Its nonzero columns therefore have
exactly k independent projective directions. Endpoint routing, zero absorption
and the Fourier common-refinement argument are identical to the main proof.

**Wording correction:** a surviving unicyclic component need not be
*untouched*. Removing an edge in a tree attached to the cycle can leave a
smaller unicyclic component and a separate tree. The rank calculation remains
valid and does not depend on the unicyclic component being unchanged.

This graph encodes support and edge counts only. No nonzero-cycle-gain
assumption is imported: the needed full ranks follow from Q's invertibility.
In particular the proof also covers GF(2), where gain-graph conventions for
singleton columns require special treatment in some references.

### Independent exact checks

`fresh_extension_checks.py` is new audit-authored code. It reuses only the
auditor's own newly authored elementary exact-arithmetic helpers from
`fresh_exact_checks.py`; no supplied program is executed or imported.

- Exhaustive all column-support-size-one-or-two matrices over GF(2), N<=4,
  and GF(3), N<=3: 16,124 matrices, 6,417 invertible matrices and 68,685
  nonempty inverse-row restrictions. All satisfy the exact-k-directions lemma.
- Of the invertible exhaustive cases, 2,418 have a row with more than two
  nonzeros, so the check genuinely exceeds the permutation-cycle setting.
- 3,600 randomized exact rooted-tree-plus-loop matrices over GF(2), GF(3),
  GF(5), GF(7), GF(17), 2<=N<=25, with independent column permutations.
- 10,800 random deletion checks, plus 3,600 designated bridge deletions that
  leave a proper surviving unicyclic component, all pass. The latter explicitly
  test the wording correction above.

Full results are in `extension_results.json` and `extension_checks.log`.

### Primary-source context

Peter Nelson and Stefan H. M. van Zwam, *On the existence of asymptotically
good linear codes in minor-closed classes*, Section 2, pp. 4–5:
[author-hosted paper](https://www.math.uwaterloo.ca/~apnelson/asympgood.pdf).
This source defines a frame matrix as a matrix with at most two nonzero
entries per column and describes its graph representation, including loops
and parallel edges. The column-two hypothesis is therefore an established
matrix class, not a newly introduced class. The restricted inverse-row and
Fourier sum argument here was proved directly; the source is background
for the structural language, not a claim that it contains this exact theorem.

More directly, Zaslavsky's *Biased Graphs IV*, Section 2.1, describes the
componentwise contraction map behind the projective-column lemma. See the
updated `PRIMARY_SOURCE_COMPARISON.md` for the exact reference and comparison.
Credit this structural mechanism as classical; the combined Fourier-sum
obstruction's priority remains unresolved.

## B. An additional row-sparse summand, including a free diagonal

**ACCEPT.** Suppose additionally

\[
F_N=S+\sum_{i=1}^{r}L_iQ_i^{-1}R_i,
\]

where S has at most delta nonzero entries in each row, delta is a nonnegative
integer, and either the original inverse-cycle or the generalized column-two
hypothesis holds. Then

\[
\boxed{N\le\delta(r+1)+r(r+1)^r}.
\]

Proof: for k=r+1 selected consecutive Fourier rows, at most delta k column
indices contain a nonzero entry of S on those rows. Discard those indices.
On all remaining columns, S vanishes, and each inverse branch still admits
at most k rank-one column classes. Their common refinement has at most k^r
cells and each cell has at most r surviving Fourier columns. Thus there are
at most r k^r surviving columns, and restoring the discarded columns proves
the bound. If k>N, the bound is automatic, so selecting k rows is unnecessary.

In particular, an arbitrary added diagonal matrix gives

\[
N\le(r+1)+r(r+1)^r.
\]

This residual-sparsity corollary is an immediate exact proof; no numerical
decomposition search was performed or is needed for it. It remains a
restricted representation theorem and adds no general arithmetic lower bound.
