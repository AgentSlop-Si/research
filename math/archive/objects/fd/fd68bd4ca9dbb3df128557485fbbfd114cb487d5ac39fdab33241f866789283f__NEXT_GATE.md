# Hero #1 Phase 25 — falsifiable research targets

## Priority 0: beat the strongest honest baseline

Compare an exact new circuit with **native radix-2 or split-radix** on dyadic lengths, and with optimized mixed-radix/Rader/Bluestein on other lengths. The native radix-2 reference used in Phase 24 is `3N log2 N/2` field operations with one multiplication and two additions per butterfly, including trivial twiddle multiplications; note which constants are optimized. Do not headline a victory based only on a slower generic-length Bluestein fallback.

## Priority 1: cross-input-independent sharing beyond the existing FFT DAG

The Phase 24 deferred graph eliminates *recomputation*, but its full completion still executes exactly all native radix-2 butterflies. A qualifying breakthrough must actually remove a superconstant fraction of those necessary operators on arbitrary fresh inputs, with a valid circuit that is not a conjugated Cooley–Tukey transform or a graph solve with hidden dense forcing. Verify the full linear map on basis vectors, not only structured signals.

## Priority 2: account for lower-bound hypotheses

The bounded-coefficient determinant lower bound and well-conditioned circuit lower bounds constrain any all-input complex-number scheme in their particular models. To depart from those models legitimately, specify coefficient heights, condition numbers, intermediate dynamic range, precision/bit costs, extra memory and the full cost of recovering exact outputs. Finite-field gate counts and complex-number stability require separate certificates.

## Priority 3: adaptive budgeted discovery

Implement a certificate compiler that can **abort while retaining a complete radix-2 fallback**, given an explicit arithmetic work budget. Charge partial determinant evaluation, all root tests, failed certificates and node-state bookkeeping. Aim for a worst-case certified bound of `native FFT + o(N log N)` *extra* field work while preserving the structured fast path, not an unbounded detector before fallback.

## Priority 4: odd-factor and prime lengths

Generalize Phase 24's deferred-DIF mechanism to `N=2^t*m` for small odd `m`, retaining exact no-recomputation semantics and `O(N)` scratch. For prime `N`, investigate whether a pruned Rader or Bluestein graph has a worthwhile reusable prefix; full transforms must not be credited as free. Root and field setup costs matter.

## Priority 5: reduce the high constant of the Phase 21 Hankel compiler

The Phase 24 `s=4` compiler costs tens of thousands of field operations before any full certificate. A symbolic, displacement-rank or batch-inversion circuit may materially improve structured runtimes, but it must report full charged work and independent exact resultants.

### Research scope gate

Asymptotic victory requires one exact, all-input DFT evaluator with charged `o(N log N)` cost in a precisely stated model. Phase 24 does not satisfy that gate; its improvements are exact no-duplicate scheduling, Green-conjugated probes, and corrected comparison accounting.
