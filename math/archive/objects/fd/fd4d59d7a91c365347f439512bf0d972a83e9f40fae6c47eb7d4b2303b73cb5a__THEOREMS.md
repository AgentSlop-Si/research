# HERO #0 — Phase 13: Cut capacity, universal approximation barriers, and expander-surviving Green architectures

**Date:** 2026-10-10  
**Status:** Derived exact matrix-rank inequalities, proved finite-precision-independent approximation obstructions for a restricted **input-independent** Green-resolvent grammar, and supplied a uniform explicit topology which evades those specific inequalities. Implementations and tests are reproducible. **No universal sub-`N log N` DFT circuit was found.** Novelty relative to the entire mathematical literature is **not established**.

## 0. Computational model and notation

Let

\[
 U_N[k,j]=N^{-1/2}e^{-2\pi i kj/N}
\]

be the *unitary* DFT. The **unnormalized** matrix `F_N = sqrt(N) U_N` has identical ranks. Fix any family of input-independent weighted permutation matrices `P`, each having at most one potentially nonzero entry per row, in distinct columns. A Green resolvent is `G_P=(I-P)^-1`, defined only when every weighted directed cycle has product different from 1.

Consider any finite-depth circuit

\[
T=T_L T_{L-1}\cdots T_1,\qquad
T_\ell=\sum_{j=1}^{r_\ell}c_{\ell j}(I-P_{\ell j})^{-1}
\]

where `c_{ell j}` are arbitrary coefficients; diagonal terms and other stages with zero upper cut rank can be included without changing the argument. Invertible Green solvers apply in `O(N)` arithmetic operations each via cyclic recurrences, and we count every solver and coefficient. Fixed `L` and fixed `r_ell` give `O(N)` application **for such a matrix T**, not necessarily for the Fourier matrix.

For any subset `R` of the coordinate labels, define `C=[N]\R`, `m=|R|`, and the *directed crossing count*

\[
b_P(R):=\#\{i\in R:\pi_P(i)\in C,\ P[i,\pi_P(i)]\neq0\}.
\]

The **total crossing budget** is `b(R)=sum_{ell,j} b_{P_ellj}(R)`. Treat repeated occurrences as separate edges; this yields a safe, sometimes loose upper bound. The reverse budget is `b(C)`.

## 1. The resolvent-crossing lemma — arbitrary permutations and weights

**Lemma.** For every invertible weighted-permutation Green resolvent and every coordinate partition `(R,C)`,

\[
\operatorname{rank}G_P[R,C]\le b_P(R),\qquad
\operatorname{rank}G_P[C,R]\le b_P(C).
\]

**Proof.** Partition `(I-P)G_P=I` into the `R,C` row/column blocks:

\[
(I-P_{RR})G_{RC}=P_{RC}G_{CC}.
\]

The matrix `I-P_RR` is invertible. Indeed, the directed graph induced by `R` consists of directed paths and full cycles. Its paths are nilpotent and each full cycle is one of the original cycles, whose product is not 1 by the Green invertibility assumption. Consequently,

\[
G_{RC}=(I-P_{RR})^{-1}P_{RC}G_{CC},
\]

so `rank(G_RC) <= rank(P_RC) <= b_P(R)`. The reverse follows by swapping `R,C`. No genericity, positivity, nonzero-weight, cycle-length, or monotonic-order hypothesis is needed. QED.

## 2. Addition and composition preserve the **sum of crossing budgets**

For arbitrary square matrices `A,B` and partition `(R,C)`,

\[
(AB)_{RC}=A_{RR}B_{RC}+A_{RC}B_{CC}.
\]

Thus `rank((AB)_RC) <= rank(A_RC)+rank(B_RC)`; and `rank((A+B)_RC) <= rank(A_RC)+rank(B_RC)`. Induct over the Green sums and all product stages:

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}T[R,C]\le b(R),\quad
\operatorname{rank}T[C,R]\le b(C).}\tag{2.1}
\]

**Scope:** fixed common coordinate labeling across the matrix product; arbitrary noncommutation and arbitrary permutation cycles are allowed. Separate global permutations/relabelings inserted as actual computational stages have their own cut rank and must be included in the budget. This is a restriction on the specified Green-sum grammar, *not* a lower bound on arbitrary arithmetic/FFT circuits.

## 3. All-subset Fourier rank and graph expansion

For every subset `R`, the block `B=U_N[R,C]` has `m(N-m)` entries of squared absolute value `1/N`. Since `U_N` is unitary,

\[
\|B\|_F^2=\frac{m(N-m)}{N}=:h,
\qquad \|B\|_2\le 1.
\]

All nonzero singular values are <=1, hence `rank(B)>=||B||_F²`. This gives the all-size, all-subset rank inequality

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}U_N[R,C]\ge
\left\lceil\frac{m(N-m)}N\right\rceil.}\tag{3.1}
\]

The same holds for the reverse block, and for any flat unitary matrix (complex Hadamard). Combining (2.1) and (3.1) gives the **necessary graph expansion condition**

\[
\boxed{b(R),b(C)\ \ge\ \left\lceil\frac{m(N-m)}N\right\rceil
\quad\text{for every }\emptyset\neq R\subsetneq[N].}\tag{3.2}
\]

For `m <= N/2`, its required outgoing crossing density is at least about `m/2`. In particular, a disconnected or poorly expanding union of permutation graphs cannot implement the full Fourier matrix, regardless of its weights.

### The sharper natural-prefix condition

For `R={0,...,m-1}` and `C={m,...,N-1}`, a Vandermonde argument shows *exactly*

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}F_N[R,C]=\min(m,N-m)}.\tag{3.3}
\]

If `m<=N/2`, select any `m` columns of `C`: the consecutive Fourier rows indexed `0,...,m-1` form a nonsingular Vandermonde matrix on distinct roots. For `m>N/2`, select the first `N-m` rows, and use the consecutive suffix columns: after extracting a column-start phase these again form a Vandermonde. The reverse cut follows analogously. This applies to every integer `N` over the complex numbers, and to exact modular roots in fields of suitable characteristic.

Therefore a **second necessary test** is

\[
\boxed{b(R_m), b(C_m)\ge\min(m,N-m)
\text{ for every }m=1,\dots,N-1.}\tag{3.4}
\]

The `O(LrN)` prefix-capacity checker uses one difference array and never materializes a dense matrix.

## 4. Genuine operator-norm and Frobenius approximation barriers

Suppose a candidate matrix `T` satisfies `rank(T[R,C]) <= c`. Set `q=min(m,N-m)` and `h=m(N-m)/N`. Write the singular values of `B=U_N[R,C]` as `sigma_1>=...>=sigma_q>=0`. The classical Eckart--Young theorem and `sigma_i<=1` imply

\[
\begin{aligned}
\|U_N-T\|_F^2&\ge \max(h-c,0),\\
\|U_N-T\|_2^2&\ge\frac{h-c}{q-c}\qquad(c<h).
\end{aligned}\tag{4.1}
\]

For the second inequality, `h=sum sigma_i² <= c+(q-c)sigma_(c+1)²`. Note the error is an *absolute operator norm* of the unitary transform; the Frobenius relative bound divides the first by `||U_N||_F=sqrt(N)`.

At an even-length middle cut, `m=q=N/2` and `h=N/4`, giving

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\|U_N-T\|_F}{\|U_N\|_F}
\ge\sqrt{\max\!\left(\frac14-\frac cN,0\right)}
}\tag{4.2}
\]

and, for `c<N/4`,

\[
\boxed{\|U_N-T\|_2\ge
\sqrt{\frac{N/4-c}{N/2-c}}}.\tag{4.3}
\]

Thus any family of Green-product networks with middle-cut capacity `o(N)` has a worst-case *operator-norm error* bounded away from zero (asymptotic lower limit `1/sqrt(2)` in this estimate), and relative Frobenius error bounded asymptotically below by `1/2`. **This concerns uniform operator approximation, not the earlier randomized or distributional approximate FFT guarantees.**

### Finite exact witness (N=128)

Take two layers containing resolvents of weighted shifts by `1,2` and `3,4` respectively. Across the middle cut, the four directed crossing capacities are exactly `1+2+3+4=10`, even though the Green inverse matrices themselves can be dense and noncommuting if weights vary with position. The true Fourier cut rank is `64`, so exact equality is impossible for *all* weights.

For the unitary target, (4.2) and (4.3) give

- relative Frobenius error `>= 0.414578098794425`;
- spectral/operator norm error `>= 0.6382847385042254`.

The code verifies exact rank inequalities against actual random-weight two-layer matrices over `GF(65537)` and checks spectral singular-value inequalities separately using complex floating point.

## 5. A non-prefix bottleneck missed by every prefix test

At `N=128`, take the irregular eight-vertex set

`R={3,19,35,51,67,83,99,115}`.

The program `hidden_bottleneck_graphs()` generates four independently randomized permutation graphs, initially respecting this partition but then swapping a single target across it in each permutation. Therefore every graph has *exactly one* directed crossing each way, and the total budget is 4. Every natural prefix cut **passes** the sharper necessary test (3.4) for the chosen deterministic seed `1307`. Nonetheless

\[
\operatorname{rank}T[R,C]\le4
<\left\lceil\frac{8\cdot120}{128}\right\rceil=8.
\]

The rank gap holds over `C` for all allowed nonzero weights. The spectral bound is

\[
\boxed{\|U_{128}-T\|_2\ge\sqrt{7/8}\approx0.935414.}
\]

The code checks the same failure using **exact finite-field matrices** (not just the combinatorial formula) and an independent Fourier matrix.

## 6. Escaping the obstruction: explicit expander graph topology

The cut budget is necessary, not sufficient. A constant-degree expander can make *all* crossing budgets large while preserving `O(N)` per-resolvent application.

For `N=s²`, index the coordinates by `(x,y) in Z_s x Z_s`. The classic Margulis--Gabber--Galil topology consists of these **four permutations**, together with their inverse edges:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\pi_1(x,y)&=(x+2y,\,y),\\
\pi_2(x,y)&=(x+2y+1,\,y),\\
\pi_3(x,y)&=(x,\,y+2x),\\
\pi_4(x,y)&=(x,\,y+2x+1),
\end{aligned}
\qquad \pmod{s}.
\]

Set the symmetric 8-regular adjacency `A=sum_{j=1}^4(P_pi_j+P_pi_j^T)`. The established Gabber--Galil spectral theorem is

\[
\max_{v\perp\mathbf 1}\frac{|v^*Av|}{\|v\|²}\le 5\sqrt 2.
\]

Therefore for the *graph-cut Laplacian* `L_1=4I-A/2`,

\[
\lambda_2(L_1)\ge 4-\frac{5\sqrt2}{2}
\approx0.464466094.
\]

For `c` copies of the four permutation edge sets, with potentially **different nonzero Green weights** on each copy, the graph-cut Laplacian is `L_c=c L_1`. For the indicator `1_R`, the identity

\[
1_R^T L_c 1_R=\sum_{\text{all copied permutations}} b_\pi(R)
\]

and the Rayleigh principle give the *all-subset* inequality

\[
b(R)\ge c\left(4-\frac{5\sqrt2}{2}\right)\frac{m(N-m)}N.
\]

Two rigorous topology certificates follow:

1. **Three copies = 12 resolvents:** `lambda_2 >= 1.393398... >1`. Every subset passes the **weaker all-subset** necessary rank test (3.2). This guarantee alone does **not** imply it passes the *stronger* prefix test for all `N`.
2. **Five copies = 20 resolvents:** `lambda_2 >=2.322330... >2`. Since `m(N-m)/N>=min(m,N-m)/2`, every subset satisfies the stronger bound
   \[\boxed{b(R),b(C)\ge\min(m,N-m).}\]
   Therefore **no cut-capacity test derived solely from (2.1)** can rule out this explicit topology, at any square length.

Every affine permutation and every cycle weight can be generated in `O(N)` uniform word/arithmetic operations, and each resolvent is applied in `O(N)` operations. Twenty is a constant, so *the candidate topology* is a uniform `O(N)` **operator**. This is **not** an `O(N)` Fourier transform unless suitable weights are found and the full Fourier matrix identity is proved. In the saved modular example at `N=16`, **all 256 Fourier entries differ** from the 20-resolvent candidate with the default weights.

The expander spectral theorem and the quasiseparable rank closure are established prior mathematics; this checkpoint derives their precise application to our Green-FFT search. No new expander theorem is claimed.

- Pernet and Storjohann, *Time and space efficient generators for quasiseparable matrices*, J. Symbolic Computation 85 (2018): https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0747717117300767

## 7. A denominator-free rank gate for arbitrary two-stage Green sums

Let `a_j=I-P_j`, `j=1,...,r`, and `d_k=I-Q_k`, `k=1,...,s`, each invertible. Define

\[
X=\sum_{j=1}^r a_j^{-1},\qquad Y=\sum_{k=1}^s d_k^{-1}
\]

and let `A=diag(a_1,...,a_r)`, `D=diag(d_1,...,d_s)`. Define `B=1_r\otimes I_N`, `C=1_r^T\otimes I_N`, `E=1_s\otimes I_N`, and `G=1_s^T\otimes I_N`. Then the lifted sparse-polynomial matrix

\[
H(F)=\begin{pmatrix}
A&0&-B\\
-EC&D&0\\
0&-G&F
\end{pmatrix}
\]

satisfies the **exact** Schur-complement rank identity

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}H(F)=
(r+s)N+\operatorname{rank}(F-YX).}\tag{7.1}
\]

Hence `F_N=YX` **if and only if** `rank H(F_N)=(r+s)N`, with invertible `a_j,d_k` as explicit hypotheses. This is an *input-independent, denominator-free compatibility certificate* whose entries depend polynomially on weighted-permutation coefficients. It generalizes the Phase 12 two-plus-two block gate, and is validated with both positive controls (`F:=YX`, rank exactly `(r+s)N`) and actual independent Fourier targets (nonzero residual rank) in exact finite fields.

**It is not a fast DFT evaluator**: building a symbolic or numerical rank certificate can cost superlinear work. The advantage is that failed sparse graph topologies can be ruled out **before** trying expensive algebraic searches, whereas viable expander topologies can be subjected to this sharper exact compatibility gate.

## 8. Research limits and next gate

- This work **does not** prove that the Fourier matrix has an `O(N)` Green-sum factorization, nor that it cannot have one.
- The cut-rank closure of quasiseparable matrices and the Margulis expander theorem are classical. The graph-cut / Fourier-flatness composition is presented as a **project-level deduction**, not a verified publication novelty claim.
- Topology feasibility says nothing about numerical stability, all-input exactness, bounded coefficient sizes, algebraic compatibility, or actual evaluation equivalence.
- Finite-field tests are exact for the chosen field, but a satisfying modular factorization would not automatically lift to the complex numbers. Conversely, the complex no-go cut inequalities are unconditional.
- A low-capacity graph family is excluded even from uniformly accurate spectral approximation. This does **not** contradict earlier project-specific randomized `O(N log log N)` weak-norm approximate FFTs, which use a different model and guarantee.
- **Next decisive target:** For an explicit expanding set of noncommuting permutation Green solvers, eliminate the weight variables from `H(F_N)` and determine whether the rank-minor ideal has a solution over the appropriate cyclotomic field for a whole infinite family of lengths. Search for truly new compatibility identities; do not settle for spectral expansion or another verified structural input class.
