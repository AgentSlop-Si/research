# Composite-modulus extension audit

The unit-step decoder itself does **not** require a prime-power length: if `a` is a unit modulo `N`, the map `m -> m+a` is one cycle for every integer `N`.

The sharp covariance theorem is different. For a general modulus, averaging over the units gives the normalized Ramanujan sum

\[
\frac{c_N(q)}{\varphi(N)}
=
\frac{\mu(N/g)}{\varphi(N/g)},
\qquad g=\gcd(N,q),
\]

rather than the two-level prime-power character identity. Different divisor shells are no longer mutually orthogonal equal-eigenvalue projectors.

`composite_modulus_audit.py` evaluates the resulting covariance exactly at the Ramanujan-sum level and diagonalizes it numerically. The direct claim

\[
Q^{(m)}=\frac MN\Pi_m
\]

fails for ordinary composite moduli.

Examples:

| `N` | `M` | `||Q||` | `M/N` | Ratio |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 12 | 2 | 0.250000 | 0.166667 | 1.5000 |
| 12 | 3 | 0.394338 | 0.250000 | 1.5774 |
| 18 | 3 | 0.333333 | 0.166667 | 2.0000 |
| 30 | 5 | 0.250000 | 0.166667 | 1.5000 |
| 60 | 5 | 0.217539 | 0.083333 | 2.6105 |

The trace remains `M-1`, as expected from the total projection error, but the positive spectrum generally splits into several values and the operator norm can exceed `M/N`.

This closes the tempting shortcut “the same proof works for every modulus.” The current rigorous extension beyond prime powers therefore remains the coprime-factor/Good–Thomas route in `RESEARCH.md`, with a charged cofactor transform. An all-length theorem still requires a new covariance decomposition or a different evaluator.

Run:

```bash
python composite_modulus_audit.py --output composite_modulus_results.json
```
