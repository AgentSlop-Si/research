# Phase 33 — Two-sided Green gauges: exact certificates and a uniform cut obstruction

**Research date:** 10 October 2026.  
**Status:** project-specific exact finite-instance exclusion theorem, generic algebraic dimension result, constructive partial extension, and a specialization of the earlier Phase 17 all-length obstruction. **NOT a new general-input sub-\(N\log N\) FFT.**

## 1. What is being tested

Let \(W_s\) be independent Hamiltonian-cycle permutation matrices, \(\alpha_s\ne0\) with \(\alpha_s^N\ne1\), and

\[
 G_s=(I-\alpha_sW_s)^{-1},\qquad
 M=\sum_{s=1}^{q} L_s G_s R_s.
\]

Each \(L_s\) and \(R_s\) is **any invertible diagonal complex matrix**. Each cycle may be an independently chosen, non-affine permutation and may not commute with the other cycles. Individually, the Green solves use one \(O(N)\) cyclic recurrence each. The question is whether both sets of gauges can turn few such resolvents into the full Fourier matrix \(F_N[i,j]=\omega^{ij}\), \(\omega=e^{-2\pi i/N}\).

## 2. Exact six-variable certificate for q=2

Write \(M=L_1G_1R_1+L_2G_2R_2\), and suppose its columns \(0,1,2\) agree with \(F_N\). Because each input diagonal is invertible, absorb its column-0 entry into the corresponding left diagonal. Without loss of generality,

\[
 R_1[0,0]=R_2[0,0]=1,\quad
 R_1[1,1]=a,\ R_2[1,1]=b,\
 R_1[2,2]=c,\ R_2[2,2]=d
\]

with \(a,b,c,d\ne0\). In any output row \(i\), abbreviate the three entries of \(G_1\) by \((A,C,E)\), those of \(G_2\) by \((B,D,H)\), and the three Fourier entries by \((f_0,f_1,f_2)=(1,\omega^i,\omega^{2i})\).

The 3x3 matrix

\[
 \begin{pmatrix}
 A&B&f_0\\aC&bD&f_1\\cE&dH&f_2
 \end{pmatrix}
\]

has rank at most two, since the Fourier column must be a linear combination of the two Green columns with coefficients given by the output-diagonal entries. Its determinant must vanish. Expanding gives

\[
 f_0CH(ad)-f_0DE(bc)-f_2BC(a)+f_2AD(b)
    +f_1BE(c)-f_1AH(d)=0.
\]

Define the \(N\times6\) matrix

\[
 K_i=\bigl(f_0CH,\;-f_0DE,\;-f_2BC,\;f_2AD,\;f_1BE,\;-f_1AH\bigr)
\]

and the **compound-variable vector** \(z=(ad,bc,a,b,c,d)^T\). Every valid two-sided factorization must satisfy \(Kz=0\). This implication does **not** divide by an interpolation minor and therefore is valid even when a per-row response matrix is singular.

**Theorem 33A.** If \(\operatorname{rank}K=6\) over the exact coefficient field, **no** choice of any four invertible diagonal gauges can make this fixed two-Green sum equal the DFT. In fact it cannot match even the first three Fourier columns.

A rank-six matrix forces \(z=0\), contradicting \(a,b,c,d\ne0\). This is a necessary-condition certificate; a rank-deficient \(K\) does not certify that a valid solution exists.

### Exact characteristic-zero witnesses

We used the deterministic Hamiltonian cycles in `phase33_two_sided.py`, together with rational couplings \(\alpha_1=3,\alpha_2=5\). These are non-affine, distinct and noncommuting. Over each prime \(p=998244353\) and \(p=2013265921\), a primitive \(N\)-th root is available. The **first six rows** of \(K\) have a nonzero 6x6 determinant for all tested powers of two \(8\le N\le1024\). The resulting integer residue certificates are in `audit.json`.

The entries are rational functions of \(3,5,\zeta_N\) with denominators \(1-3^N\) and \(1-5^N\). Neither denominator vanishes in the audit fields or over \(\mathbb C\). A nonzero determinant in either exact reduction implies the original determinant in \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_N)\) is nonzero. Thus the conclusion is an **exact complex-number exclusion for each specified finite instance**, not merely an exclusion modulo a prime.

For each fixed pair of these cycle orderings, the same determinant is a nonzero rational function of the two couplings. Therefore a nonempty Zariski-open set of couplings also fails. **Exceptional, specially tuned coupling choices are not excluded** by this statement, nor are arbitrary different cycle orderings.

Positive controls: when arbitrary matrices are deliberately chosen as \(G_1=F-H\), \(G_2=H\), with all gauges equal to identity, the certificate is rank-deficient. Independent 3x3 determinants agree with the six-monomial expansion for random gauge choices.

## 3. Gauge-fixing and the q²-dimensional first-missing-column map

Let \(q\) **fixed** Green operators be given, and require their two-sided sum to match \(q\) selected target columns \(j_0,\dots,j_{q-1}\). We may rescale \(L_s\) and \(R_s\) oppositely and fix \(R_s[j_0,j_0]=1\), eliminating one trivial gauge redundancy per term.

Then the remaining input-side coefficients on these \(q\) columns number \(q(q-1)\). On the **nonsingular interpolation chart**, for every row \(i\), its \(q\) left-gauge values are *uniquely* recovered from the \(q\times q\) matrix

\[
 E_i[\ell,s]=G_s[i,j_\ell] R_s[j_\ell,j_\ell],\qquad
 u_i=E_i^{-1}F_N[i,J]^T.
\]

For one further column \(j_*\), there are \(q\) new right-gauge parameters, and its output vector is a rational function of **at most**

\[
 \boxed{q(q-1)+q=q^2}
\]

parameters. Thus its attainable vectors form a constructible set whose Zariski closure in \(\mathbb C^N\) has dimension at most \(q^2\). If \(N>q^2\), this is a *proper* subvariety: a **generic** additional target column is impossible. This does **not** rule out the special additional Fourier column, and does not cover singular row interpolation charts.

For \(q=5\) we also computed the exact Jacobian. Let the base input-side gauges equal one, \(B_i=E_i\), \(l_i=B_i^{-1}f_i\), and \(h_{is}=G_s[i,j_*]\). For \(\ell\ge1\),

\[
 \frac{\partial y_i}{\partial r_{s\ell}}
 =- (h_i^TB_i^{-1})_\ell B_i[\ell,s] l_{is},\qquad
 \frac{\partial y_i}{\partial r_{s*}}=G_s[i,j_*]l_{is}.
\]

This provides an \(N\times25\) matrix. For the saved non-affine five-cycle family with integer couplings \(7,9,11,13,15\), the Jacobian has exact rank 25 and the matrix augmented by the missing Fourier-column residual has rank 26 at \(N=32,64,128,256,512,1024,4096\). Nonzero finite-field minors lift to nonzero characteristic-zero minors, so the local rational map has full possible 25-dimensional image at these parameter points. The augmented rank is **a first-order correction failure at that point, not a global nonlinear impossibility theorem**.

A positive two-sided control sets the right gauges of the extra column by solving one \(5\times5\) system from the first five output rows. All original five Fourier columns remain exact; the sixth now matches exactly at those five rows, while the other \(N-5\) rows disagree for the checked family through \(N=4096\). This is precise partial progress, not a full transform.

## 4. The all-length additive obstruction survives both diagonal gauges

This is an **explicit cyclic-Green specialization of the already established Phase 17 simultaneous-cut theorem**, not a new universal FFT lower bound.

In the linear order of a Hamiltonian cycle, for coordinates \(i,j\),

\[
 G_s[i,j]=\frac{\alpha_s^{(p_s(i)-p_s(j))\bmod N}}{1-\alpha_s^N}.
\]

If two disjoint subsets \(R,C\) are separated in that order (every element of \(R\) occurs before every element of \(C\), or vice versa), this formula **factorizes** on the rectangular block \(R\times C\) into a row factor times a column factor. Thus \(\operatorname{rank}(G_s)_{R,C}\le1\), even after independent left and right diagonal gauges.

For \(q\) arbitrary cycle orders, the balanced-halving argument of Phase 17 provides two disjoint groups of size

\[
 m\ge\left\lfloor N/2^q\right\rfloor
\]

that are separated in **every** order. Therefore, for the entire additive matrix, \(\operatorname{rank}(M_{R,C})\le q\).

For normalized Fourier \(\widehat F_N\), every entry has squared magnitude \(1/N\), while every rectangular block has spectral norm at most one. Consequently, every \(m\times m\) Fourier block has rank at least \(m^2/N\). Exact equality \(M=\widehat F_N\) therefore requires

\[
 \boxed{\left\lfloor N/2^q\right\rfloor^2\le qN}.
\]

For \(N\) divisible by \(2^q\), necessarily \(N\le q4^q\). Hence **any exact representation in this sum-of-two-sided-cyclic-Green class needs \(q=\Omega(\log N)\) terms**. The lower bound holds for arbitrary non-affine Hamiltonian orders, arbitrary coupling values, arbitrary conditioning, and arbitrary nonzero two-sided diagonal gauges.

If \(m>q\) and \(m^2>qN\), best-rank approximation additionally yields

\[
 \boxed{\|M-\widehat F_N\|_2\ge
 \sqrt{\frac{m^2/N-q}{m-q}}}.
\]

The saved tests construct the common separated sets for \((q,N)=(2,64),(3,256),(4,2048),(5,8192)\) and verify the cyclic rank-one block identity exactly. The error floors and thresholds through \(q=6\) are recorded in `audit.json`.

**Scope:** this does not cover products of noncommuting Green sums with internal phases, input-dependent nonlinear algorithms, or an algorithm sharing arithmetic across all \(q\) terms more efficiently than evaluating them separately. A naïve term-by-term executor costs \(\Theta(qN)\), hence at least \(\Theta(N\log N)\) at the necessary term budget; an improved shared-state executor would require a separate operation count and exact Fourier proof.

## 5. Research consequence

The specific **single sum of independently ordered, two-sided diagonal-gauged cyclic Green resolvents** is asymptotically closed as a route to a constant- or sublogarithmic-term exact FFT. Reparameterizing both sides can improve local interpolation, but cannot defeat the common-cut rank bound for all large \(N\).

The next genuinely different architecture must permit **products or other non-additive shared-state composition** that destroys the common cut, and must satisfy both Fourier Weyl equations globally. Do not confuse a nonzero rank certificate with a novel general arithmetic lower bound.
