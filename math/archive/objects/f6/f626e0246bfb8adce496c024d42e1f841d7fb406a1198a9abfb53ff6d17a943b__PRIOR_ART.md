# Phase 26 — attribution and novelty boundary

The mathematical principles used here have substantial prior art:

- Jacques Morgenstern, *Note on a Lower Bound on the Linear Complexity of the Fast Fourier Transform*, JACM 20(2), 1973, https://doi.org/10.1145/321752.321761 — bounded-coefficient/determinant lower bound for the unnormalized transform.
- Nir Ailon, *A Lower Bound for Fourier Transform Computation in a Linear Model Over 2x2 Unitary Gates Using Matrix Entropy*, 2013, https://arxiv.org/abs/1305.4745 — normalized Fourier lower bound in an in-place unitary two-register model. Its restriction to unitary gates and absence of extra workspace are essential.
- Nir Ailon, *An n log n Lower Bound for Fourier Transform Computation in the Well Conditioned Model*, 2014, https://arxiv.org/abs/1403.1307 — more general well-conditioned restrictions, not reconstructed or formally verified here.
- Steven G. Johnson and Matteo Frigo, *A Modified Split-Radix FFT With Fewer Arithmetic Operations*, IEEE TSP 55(1), 2007, https://www.fftw.org/~athena/abstracts/abstract5.html — asymptotic arithmetic improvements for real floating-point operation models. This does not imply savings in every field-operation model.
- Good–Thomas / prime-factor FFT, Winograd codelets, conventional radix-2 and split-radix recurrences — existing classical algorithms.

**New project-specific deliverables, not claims of worldwide research priority:** executable prime-field comparison of two distinct full Fourier DAGs with *separately identical addition/multiplication tallies*; an explicit tested elementary nonunitary lift with a negative work result; a high-arity determinant/entropy budget that rejects proposed uncharged gates; and repeatable two-field complete-basis verification linked to the Phase 25 lineage.

All determinant and entropy inequalities are elementary extensions/applications of established potential arguments. Finite tests verify the implementations, not their global mathematical novelty. Nothing in this package proves an unrestricted `o(N log N)` transform.
