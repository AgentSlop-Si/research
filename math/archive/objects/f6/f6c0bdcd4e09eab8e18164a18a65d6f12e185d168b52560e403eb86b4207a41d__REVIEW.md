# Remaining algorithm source audit

## Result and scope

All 46 assigned exact source hashes in batch_03.json were read first-to-last as inert text: 339,474 bytes. The originals, prior audit seals, and central coverage registry were not changed. Sources were not executed, imported, compiled, or AST-parsed. Independently authored archive/data checks and arithmetic demonstrations were run. Each source has an exact snapshot, a numbered snapshot, verified archive-member provenance, and line-anchored findings in SOURCE_REVIEWS.json / SOURCE_REVIEWS.md.

This completes this assigned source-read batch. It does not complete all research-material review, runtime dependency closure, theorem certification, or novelty evaluation. Capturing a stored result or document as evidence is not claiming a full prose review of that document. Historical PASS records remain historical.

## Findings that change interpretation

1. First-jet linearization (source02) is correctly scoped to formal polynomial/rational identities at a regular point. Equality only as functions on a finite field is insufficient: x^3=x over F3, but formal derivatives are 0 and 1. This is a model/hypothesis correction, not an FFT counterexample. The three-linear-gates-per-gate bound does not by itself account for all uniform derivative-constant construction or basepoint selection costs.
2. Projected and multi-consumer Fourier stores (sources12,34) scan all retained event records even when the current query's lag H is one. Their actual replay path includes O(E) old-history traversal. Without explicit compaction or a sufficiently small cap, one edit/query per step can have quadratic cumulative scanning, despite advertised O(qH) replay. The relation identities remain intact.
3. The offline code generator (source18) emits C++ sparse-position modulo and an array access without validating nonnegativity. A malformed sparse_position=-1 with zero shifts produces C++ out[-1]. This is a guard defect in the exported native ABI; higher-level validation can exclude it. No generated code was compiled or run.
4. Convolution subscription overflow rejection (source39) occurs after cached output mutation. A finite huge delta can change the output and then raise while leaving metadata unchanged. An independent floating arithmetic example confirms the ordering hazard without invoking supplied methods.
5. The fixed-inner certificate guard (source19) does not require q<1 separately before testing e/(1-q)<rho. A q>1 record can pass that inequality vacuously. Its supplied historical certificate has a valid q<1, so the defect is in malformed-certificate rejection, not a counterexample to the accepted seed.
6. Shrinking-error prototype symbolic budgets (source35) cap total at .95 while allocating decoder error equal to half that cap. Four stored model rows have sample+decoder greater than the labeled total. At log2N=64 in linear mode, .75+.475=1.225>.95. This finite-parameter accounting issue does not refute a sufficiently-large-N asymptotic regime.
7. General-argument guards are narrower than their names: source04's primitive-root check is insufficient beyond dyadic lengths; source08's minimum-singular-value formula is used only at even N; sources26/38 have integer or dyadic preconditions that are not fully validated. These are scoped assumptions, not demonstrated failures of the supported fixtures.

## Mathematical results kept within their bounds

- The commuting-phase t=N/2 scalar-unitary example (source00) is a genuine boundary example against extending a strict short-word claim. The N2 instance gives K=(3I+S)/2 and K* K=(5/2)I. It is not a generic FFT construction.
- The b=1 holonomy case (source03) genuinely has two cycles of length four rather than four of length two. A fresh independent permutation check confirms [4,4]. The finite D8 equalities do not automatically become all-size equality.
- The full-rank tiny-matrix case in source07 refutes extending the individual-norm rank-deficiency inequality to rank N; it does not refute its correctly restricted version.
- N16 numerical search (source14), exact local contraction (source13), N16-to-N64 necessary-gate system (source15), outer-only rejection (source16), and fixed-inner/row controls (sources19,20) are separate conclusions. Recorded rational scalar contraction and nonsingularity budgets reconcile. The outer rejection fixes the two literal inner pullbacks; the full N64 family remains open. Matrix/chart recomputation was not performed in this source audit.
- Common-pole source21 establishes a polynomial-minor route for minus rank8 at all nonzero legal common poles. Its plus rank7 claim is certified at t=1/2, with all-pole upper bound7. The two scopes must remain separate.
- Binary-orbit sources22/23/43 concern information, one-input algebraic recovery, and Lipschitz conditioning under their specific access models. They do not establish unrestricted FFT lower bounds.
- Lazy, prepared, projected, persistent, pruning, and convolution implementations require known relations or existing transformed bases; their mathematical identities do not eliminate initialization, metadata discovery, output materialization, or numeric error. Approximate sampled/lookup/chirp branches have different error/output goals from exact FFT.
