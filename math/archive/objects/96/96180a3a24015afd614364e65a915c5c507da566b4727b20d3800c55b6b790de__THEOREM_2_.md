# Simultaneous Separation-Rank Barrier for Fourier and Global Scan Sums

**10 October 2026 · Independent mathematical research draft · originality unverified**

**Scope:** exact and operator-norm-approximate Fourier matrices; sums of arbitrary independently permuted, independently diagonally gauged quasiseparable/Green-type operators. This note proves a family-specific *representation lower bound*, not an unrestricted FFT arithmetic lower bound. It is not a faster FFT construction. Its project-local novelty is the simultaneous halving lemma applied to arbitrary non-affine, separately permuted additive scan branches, including the dense-inverse examples that evade previous sparse-inverse results. External publication-level novelty has not been established.

## Abstract and main statement

Fix `N>=2`. Write `U_N[j,k] = exp(-2 pi i jk/N)/sqrt(N)` (the unitary DFT). A matrix `H` has *separation rank at most r under a pair of row/column orders* if two bijective orderings `a,b : {0,...,N-1} -> {0,...,N-1}` exist such that, whenever nonempty row and column sets `R,C` satisfy either

- `max_{i in R} a(i) < min_{j in C} b(j)`; or
- `max_{j in C} b(j) < min_{i in R} a(i)`,

we have `rank H[R,C] <= r`. This definition allows **independent** permutations of rows and columns. It includes all independently endpoint-monomial-transformed invertible weighted Hamiltonian-cycle Green matrices `L(I-DP)^-1R` with `r=1`; it also includes globally separated-generator rank-r quasiseparable matrices.

**Theorem (separation-rank obstruction).** Let

    M = H_1 + ... + H_Q,

where branch `H_j` has separation rank at most `r_j` for its chosen row/column order pair. Let `R_total=sum_j r_j`, and let `d<=Q` be the number of **distinct order pairs** across the branches (the order pairs may be grouped). Set

    m = floor(N / 2^d).

There are sets of rows and columns, each of cardinality `m`, satisfying

    rank M[R,C] <= R_total.                              (1)

Consequently:

(a) If `M=U_N` over complex numbers, **for every N**,

    m^2 <= N R_total.                                      (2)

(b) If `N=p` is prime and `M=U_p`, then Chebotarev full spark gives the stronger bound

    m <= R_total.                                          (3)

(c) **For all N and every such M** (not only exact representations),

    ||U_N-M||_2^2 >= max{0, m/N - R_total/m},              (4)

where the bound is read as zero if `m=0`.

These statements allow arbitrary sizes and values of complex coefficients, arbitrary numerical conditioning, zeros in the Green weights, arbitrarily non-affine Hamiltonian cycles, and **independently chosen monomial endpoint transformations in each branch**.

**Special case: a sum of Q Green branches.** Take `r_j=1` and `d<=Q`. For prime p, (3) implies

    floor(p/2^Q) <= Q,
    p < (Q+1) 2^Q,                                         (5)
    Q >= log_2(p) - O(log log p).                          (6)

For arbitrary N, (2) implies

    floor(N/2^Q)^2 <= N Q,
    Q >= (1/2) log_2 N - O(log log N).                     (7)

For **fixed Q** and any sequence of candidate matrices with N tending to infinity, (4) yields the robust impossibility of vanishing operator error:

    liminf_{N -> infinity} ||U_N - M_N||_2 >= 2^(-Q/2).   (8)

This applies even if the arbitrary cycles and all complex coefficients are chosen separately for each N.

## Proof 1: Green inverse has one-dimensional separated rectangles

Take any weighted Hamiltonian N-cycle `sigma` and its associated weighted shift `T=diag(w)P_sigma`, with `(P_sigma)[i,sigma(i)]=1`. Write the cycle as `v_0 -> v_1 -> ... -> v_{N-1} -> v_0` and let `lambda=prod_{t=0}^{N-1} w[v_t]`. Then `T^N=lambda I`. If `lambda !=1`,

    G=(I-T)^-1 = (I+T+...+T^(N-1))/(1-lambda).

The entry `G[v_a,v_b]` is `1/(1-lambda)` times the product of weights along the unique directed arc from `v_a` to `v_b` of length in `{0,...,N-1}`.

If all row positions `a` are before all column positions `b`, choose any cut position `h` between them. The arc product factors as

    (prod_{t=a}^{h-1} w[v_t]) * (prod_{t=h}^{b-1} w[v_t]).

The first factor depends only on the row and the second only on the column. Therefore this entire rectangle has rank at most one. If all rows come after all columns, the wrapped arc factors as

    (prod_{t=a}^{N-1} w[v_t]) * (prod_{t=0}^{b-1} w[v_t]),

again rank at most one. The argument holds even if some weights vanish: **no division by an individual weight** is used. Independent invertible monomial left/right factors only permute and scale the respective row/column orders and preserve rank. QED.

For globally separated-generator quasiseparable matrices of rank r, the analogous off-diagonal rectangles have rank at most r by their generator representations. In particular, the matrix with constant entries below and above the diagonal but a different diagonal (the project sparse-defect witness) belongs to this wider separation-rank-one class, even though its inverse is globally dense.

## Proof 2: the simultaneous half-selection lemma

**Lemma.** Let `R` and `C` be two sets of *equal* cardinality `n>=2`, and let `a` and `b` be any respective injective integer-valued orderings of their elements. There exist subsets `R' subset R, C' subset C` of cardinality `floor(n/2)` each, such that either every `a(R')` is smaller than every `b(C')`, or every `b(C')` is smaller than every `a(R')`.

**Proof.** Put `h=floor(n/2)`. Sort R by a, and C by b. Let `R_lo` and `C_lo` be the lowest h indices in their respective orders, and `R_hi,C_hi` the highest h. Since each individual ordering is injective,

    max a(R_lo) < min a(R_hi),
    max b(C_lo) < min b(C_hi).

If `max a(R_lo) < min b(C_hi)`, select `(R_lo,C_hi)`. Otherwise `max a(R_lo) >= min b(C_hi)`. If also `max b(C_lo) >= min a(R_hi)`, then

    max a(R_lo) >= min b(C_hi) > max b(C_lo)
               >= min a(R_hi) > max a(R_lo),

an impossibility. Hence `(R_hi,C_lo)` is separated in the opposite orientation. QED.

Apply the lemma repeatedly to the `d` distinct order pairs, starting with all N rows and N columns. Previously achieved strict separation survives when taking subsets. Each application halves the equal cardinality, leaving `m=floor(N/2^d)` rows and columns. Every branch is rank at most `r_j` on the resulting common rectangle, and therefore their sum has rank at most `sum r_j`. This proves (1).

## Proof 3: consequences for Fourier matrices

The unitary Fourier matrix `U_N` has entries of absolute value `1/sqrt(N)` and operator norm one. Thus for any m×m submatrix `U_N[R,C]`:

    ||U_N[R,C]||_F^2 = m^2/N,   ||U_N[R,C]||_2 <= 1.

Since any matrix has `||A||_F^2 <= rank(A) ||A||_2^2`, we obtain

    rank(U_N[R,C]) >= m^2/N.                              (9)

If `U_N=M`, combine (1) and (9) to get (2). For prime p, the classical Chebotarev theorem says every square submatrix of the p-point Fourier matrix is nonsingular, yielding `rank(U_p[R,C])=m` and therefore (3).

For arbitrary M with rank at most `R_total` on the certificate rectangle, best rank-R approximation (Eckart–Young) and the norm-one upper bound on each Fourier-submatrix singular value give

    ||U_sub - M_sub||_F^2 >= m^2/N - R_total.

As `||E||_2 >= ||E[R,C]||_2 >= ||E[R,C]||_F/sqrt(m)`, we conclude

    ||U_N-M||_2^2 >= max{0,m/N-R_total/m}.

This proves (4). If d<=Q, then `m>=floor(N/2^Q)`. For fixed Q and N->infinity, `m/N>=2^-Q-o(1)` and `Q/m=o(1)`, so (8) follows. Finally, solving (2)-(3) up to lower-order logarithms proves the advertised branch-count asymptotics. QED.

## Additional structural diversity bound

The theorem separates **number of terms Q** from **number of distinct row/column ordering templates d**. If many Green branches share the same pair of orders but have unrelated cycle weights/diagonal gauges, we perform the half-selection only once for that shared order. For prime p, we necessarily have

    p < (R_total+1)2^d.                                   (10)

Therefore, a constant number of distinct order templates requires `R_total = Omega(p)`, even though each individual matrix may be globally dense. If `r_j=1`, this means Omega(p) branches. Independent non-affine permutations are permitted, but they must supply logarithmically many genuinely distinct templates when the rank budget remains small.

## Finite exact verification

The independent Python program `verify_separation_barrier.py` checks the constructed rectangles and exact ranks over the prime field 998244353; arbitrary non-affine Hamiltonian cycles and *independent monomial endpoint permutations and scales* are sampled. It also covers zero edge weights and independently weighted branches sharing the same order template. Additionally, for primes p in {5,13,37,83}, it checks exact p-th cyclotomic Fourier minors symbolically as integer polynomials modulo z^p-1; these checks do not confuse modular nonvanishing with characteristic-zero full spark.

The saved `VERIFY_RESULTS.json` holds every checked (N,Q,m) schedule, exact finite-field rank, and checksum-grade reproducibility inputs. **Finite checks are not the proof of the all-N theorem; the algebraic arguments above are.**

## Research interpretation and boundaries

- **Closed by this theorem:** exact or vanishing-error Fourier representation as a *constant-branch additive sum* of arbitrary independently gauged and independently permuted Green inverses; more generally, sums of low-separation-rank global scans. For exact prime lengths it even requires nearly log_2 N branches, not merely `log N/log log N`.
- **Still open:** a product of two or more such sums; arbitrary interstage permutations; networks that fuse the branches into an unrelated computation circuit; a single stage whose separated rank grows with N; FFT-specific nonlinear compatibility of global scan products; and all unrestricted arithmetic circuits.
- **No general lower bound:** A required logarithmic number of additive terms does **not** prove Omega(N log N) scalar arithmetic. Branch evaluations could be fused, and a computational network need not take this additive form. The known algebraic gate models and word-/bit-complexity models must not be conflated.
- **Not a new O(N) FFT:** This is a rigorous, architecture-specific no-go theorem, with a separate constructive search target.
- **Priority/novelty:** The halving lemma is elementary; quasiseparable/semiseparable factorization is established; Fourier full spark is known (Tao/Chebotarev). This exact application to *independently permuted Green sums with a spectral approximation gap* appears absent from the project material inspected here, but **we have not established that it is unpublished**. An expert prior-art search and independent proof review are necessary before claiming novelty.
