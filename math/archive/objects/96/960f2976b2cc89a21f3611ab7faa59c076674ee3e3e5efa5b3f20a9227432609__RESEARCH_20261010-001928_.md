# FFT parity-oracle and binary-affine mixing research

**Date:** October 10, 2026  
**Focus:** Exact, fresh-input DFT matrix factorization through linear-work global scans, arbitrary coefficient values, and moving bit-linear layouts.  
**Status:** Proved necessary depth restrictions and exact input-independent complex Fourier-submatrix ranks. **No new faster FFT constructed.**

## Scientific advance in this checkpoint

Previous research found a four-stage N=256 network using three densely mixing, invertible GF(2) bit permutations. It passed 255 parity-mask capacity tests, all 32,895 nonempty intervals, and eight full-network max-flow samples. This **only established necessary rank feasibility**.

This checkpoint replaces expensive (and sometimes misleading) finite-field rank checks for parity blocks with the exact formula proved in `PROOF_CERTIFICATE.md`:

\[
\mathrm{rank}_{\mathbb C}F_{2^k}[R_b,C_a]
=\begin{cases}2^{k-2},&\mathrm{msb}(a)+\mathrm{msb}(b)\le k-2,\\2^{k-1},&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}
\]

It is an application of the published universal-sampling theorem to a precise family of parity sets, not a claim of general priority.

An additional kernel-of-binary-constraints argument proves a new restriction within our project:

\[
k\le t\lceil\log_2(4rt)\rceil
\]

for all length-\(2^k\) DFT factorizations into \(t\) two-sided order-\(r\) quasiseparable stages, with arbitrary **GF(2)-affine** index permutations. The stronger heterogeneous rank/bit-budget version is also proved. In particular, *four* rank-one stages are ruled out for **every such shuffle pattern** when \(N\ge2^{17}\). This does not exclude nonlinear permutations.

## Finite-field audit warning

We independently found a 16-point Fourier minor whose columns form a universal sampling set, so its complex rank is **8**, but its rank over the valid NTT prime 12,289 is **7**. Over 65,537 it is **8**. Hence a single finite-field specialization can *understate* complex rank and lead to false acceptance of an architectural candidate. Our exact parity oracle eliminates this risk for the parity family.

## Boundaries and next gate

Our original goal remains an actual exact FFT implementation with all arithmetic costs counted and asymptotic improvement over \(N\log N\), preferably linear. This checkpoint makes such a construction **less plausible within fixed-order quasiseparable scans and GF(2)-affine routing**, but establishes no lower bound for unrestricted FFT circuits and produces no faster transform.

Next: investigate **genuinely non-affine, cross-partition index permutations** or growing-rank stages while keeping full arithmetic costs visible. For finite-size rank-feasible layouts (including the N=256 candidate), any factorization claim must be backed by exact Fourier coefficient identities, including degenerate generator branches. Never interpret full network min-cuts as proof of coefficient compatibility.
