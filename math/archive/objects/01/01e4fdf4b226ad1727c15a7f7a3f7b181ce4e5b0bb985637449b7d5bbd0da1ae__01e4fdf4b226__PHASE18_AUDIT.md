## 1. Exact defect decoding and all resonance cases

With the positive-sign Fourier convention and `(Sr)_j=r_(j-1)`, `FS=DF`, where `D_kk=omega^k`. Therefore for any nonzero polynomial `q` with nonzero constant coefficient,

\[
Fq(S^\delta)r[k]=q(\omega^{k\delta})Fr[k].
\]

At nonzero denominators, division is exact. At zero denominators the filter has erased that Fourier coordinate, so computing the corresponding dense Fourier sum from the original residual is a valid repair. No value of the erased coordinate is inferred from the sparse defect.

If `g=gcd(N,delta)`, the eigenvalues of `S^delta` are the `N/g` distinct roots, each repeated `g` times. A degree-`d` polynomial therefore has at most `min(N,dg)` zero denominator coordinates. This holds whether the defect support has size zero, one, or more. A zero defect can still require nontrivial repairs. If `q(z)=1-z^(N/g)` and `g>1`, the filter is the zero operator and every frequency is repaired; the algorithm remains exact but does dense quadratic work.

A binomial `b-a z`, with `b!=0`, has exactly `g` resonances when `a!=0` and `(a/b)^(N/g)=1`, and none otherwise. The source handles `a=0` separately. One batch inversion of the nonzero denominators costs one field inverse and linear many products; excluding zeros before the batch is essential and is done correctly.

The inherited `LF=FP` identity then gives `Fx=L^{-1}F(Px)` whenever the implemented Green solver is admissible. Dense residuals are permitted throughout.

## 2. Discovery and charged complexity

For an everywhere-nonzero residual, the projective pairs `(r_j,r_(j-delta))` represent ratios without inversions. Equality is tested by cross multiplication. If a binomial relation has fewer than `N/2` defects, its ratio appears strictly more than half the time; pair-cancellation majority therefore returns it. The separate full defect scan certifies the candidate. The production selector additionally applies its support and repair-cost thresholds, so this is a candidate-discovery guarantee, not a guarantee that every mathematically valid candidate is chosen.

The bounded-prefix Berlekamp-Massey routine only proposes a recurrence. Its full-vector audit is necessary: an early defect, an uninformative prefix, or a sample from only one of several shift cycles can produce an unsuitable proposal. Static inspection confirms that failure does not bypass the full Fourier fallback.

For an accepted degree-`d` filter with defect support `K` and actual resonance count `Z`, the useful arithmetic form is

\[
O\bigl(N(d+1)+N\log(K+1)+K\log^2(K+1)+NZ\bigr).
\]

Here `Z<=dg`. For a fixed bank of `B` candidate shifts and degree cap `dmax`, add the discovery bound `O(BN(dmax+1)+B dmax^2)`. The source default has fixed bank size and degree cap, so failed discovery adds only linear field work before its ordinary FFT fallback. There is no efficient discovery theorem over all unknown shifts or all unknown factor lists.

The roots for a long shift can be initialized with a logarithmic-length power and streamed over all output frequencies; this is absorbed by the linear scan for a fixed number of filters. The fast sparse-forcing term remains conditional on the convolution/root engine and the qualifications inherited from Phase17.

## 3. Supplied filter unions are a genuine additional structured family

For supplied factors `Q_s=I-alpha_s S^delta_s`, apply the factors consecutively to obtain the defect. Each application is a linear scan, so a product of `m` filters costs `O(mN)` without expanding its potentially exponential monomial list. In Fourier coordinates their scalar factors multiply. Nonzero product denominators are inverted in one batch, and every distinct zero denominator is repaired once. Thus

\[
O\bigl(mN+N\log(K+1)+K\log^2(K+1)+NZ\bigr)
\]

is valid, with `Z` bounded by the sum of the individual resonance counts. All these filters commute; the noncommuting part remains the outside Green pair.

Our independent exact examples at N128, N256 and N384 have dense `x` and dense `r=Px`, each individual filter defect dense, and the combined defect supported at three entries. At N384 the delays are 191 and 383, and coefficients are 2 and 3, as in the source's family. The jointly filtered result, followed by exact division and Green inversion, equals a direct independently authored Fourier transform. This confirms that the union branch is more than selecting whichever individual filter already sparsifies the residual.

These examples receive their factors as structural information. Generating the example from a sparse defect is a test-data construction, not an uncharged transform step on an arbitrary input. The source correctly separates this supplied branch from its small-bank adaptive branch.

## 4. Dimension bound and the noncoprime qualification

For the ordinary shift, fix a defect support `T` of size at most `K` and the exact set of `e` Fourier roots where `q` vanishes. Normalizing `q(0)=1`, the root conditions impose `e` independent linear conditions on its `d` remaining coefficients. This follows from a nonzero Vandermonde minor, since the roots are distinct and nonzero. The polynomial stratum has dimension at most `d-e`; the kernel of `q(S)` has dimension `e`. The compatible defect vector has at most `K` parameters, and each nonempty preimage has dimension `e`. The incidence dimension is therefore at most `(d-e)+K+e=d+K`. Finite union over supports and root sets and projection to `r` preserve this upper bound.

The same proof works for a coprime long shift, which is a relabeled single cycle. It also allows any finite union of candidate coprime delays at fixed N. It proves a measure-zero structured family when `d+K<N`, not a lower bound for arbitrary Fourier algorithms.

For a noncoprime shift, the eigenvalue multiplicity is `g`, so the correct same-proof bound is

\[
\dim \mathcal C_\delta(d,K)
\le\min\{N,\ d+K+(g-1)\min(d,N/g)\}.
\]

Indeed the polynomial root constraints still cost `e` parameters, but the kernel has dimension `ge`. A simple exact counterexample to the unqualified `d+K` bound is `delta=N/2`, `q=1-z`, and `K=0`: every vector repeated across the two halves is in the kernel, a family of dimension `N/2`, although `d+K=1`. The source theorem formally defines its class using `q(S)`, so its proof is sound; its shorter README and gate descriptions should be read with this scope retained. The independent checker explicitly verifies these counterexamples.

For a nonresonant supplied filter union, fixed parameters give an invertible linear map, so the K-defect family has dimension at most K. Allowing `m` unknown scalar filter parameters gives at most `K+m` on nonresonant strata; the finite choices of integer delays do not increase algebraic dimension. Singular strata require separate kernel and repair accounting, as the source states.

## 5. Inherited implementation and arithmetic qualifications

Because the two Phase17 implementation files are unchanged, the following previously verified qualifications remain:

- `CyclicSolver` inverts every diagonal entry before checking closure, a stronger condition than operator nonsingularity. For example at `p=17,N=16,omega=3`, the L diagonal vanishes at index 3 and the P diagonal at index 11, although each operator determinant is 16 modulo 17. The source therefore does not implement every nonsingular finite-field case. A reverse recurrence using the nonzero shift repairs this in principle; our independent checks use that repair, and do not imply that the uploaded source was patched.
- Literal NTT twiddle planning raises separate powers at successive transform lengths. A growing-field version must supply the required short roots or include the corresponding `O(log p log(K+1))` field-multiplication setup term for the rational branch. Common Green/root setup also relies on an admissible root supply. The fixed finite-field experiments do not supply a uniform bit-complexity result.
- Inherited `PolyStats` omits some rational-series sign/subtraction operations. Restoring `O(N+K log K)` additions preserves the accepted asymptotic bound, but the printed numbers remain incomplete arithmetic proxies rather than exact full scalar-operation counts. The new source does charge rejected candidates, repeat certificate scans, accepted filter applications, direct repairs, and Green application/solve in its own proxy.
- The source's comparison shares cached roots and Green setup on both sides. Its finite ratios were not rerun here and are not independently certified instruction counts or worst-case speedups.

Near a complex resonance, a nonzero denominator can be arbitrarily small. Exact quotient identities therefore give no numerical stability bound; inherited polynomial coefficient-growth issues also persist. The source acknowledges this and does not infer stable complex or bit-complexity performance from finite-field exactness.
