# Hero #17 Phase 12 — Noncommuting Green-Union rigidity

**Date:** October 10, 2026  
**Objective:** Find an exact, fully charged O(N) full DFT for arbitrary fresh inputs using Graph + Union + long-range sparse shifts + noncommuting Green solves, without FFT disguises or uncharged preprocessing.

**Verdict:** The universal O(N) target remains UNSOLVED. Phase 12 derives an exact *restricted-circuit obstruction* that survives low-rank Union corrections at intermediate stages, and proves that full-rank Weyl/Green noncommutativity is insufficient to escape it. Verified in modular arithmetic, with a 65,536-element streaming Green execution serving as a positive control for linear-time Green solves and a **negative control** for Fourier equivalence.

## Central theorem

Let a candidate compute the unnormalized complex DFT `F_N` as a product of L invertible factors `H_L ... H_1`. Suppose `H_i=G_i+R_i` with `rank(R_i)<=r_i`, `G_i` invertible and its inverse having at most `s_i` nonzeros in each column. Define `r=sum r_i`, `s=prod s_i`. Then:

- **Every N:** `s(r+1)^2 >= N`.
- **Prime N:** `(s+1)(r+1) >= N+1`.

In particular if each `G_i` is a (possibly permuted, gauged, long-stride) one-pole cyclic Green inverse, so `s_i<=2`, then

`2^L(r+1)^2>=N` and `(2^L+1)(r+1)>=N+1` for prime N.

Proof: inverse-of-rank-update has rank-`r_i` difference, hence the inverse of the entire product is a rank-`<=r` perturbation of an `s`-column-sparse matrix. An `r+1`-sparse nullvector of the perturbation gives a vector whose Fourier inverse has support at most `s(r+1)`. Standard and prime-specific Fourier uncertainty yield the inequalities. See `PROOF_CERTIFICATE.md` for the precise proof and caveats.

## Why the quantum/Weyl trick is insufficient

Define cyclic shift S and diagonal modulation T by `S e_j=e_(j+1)` and `T e_j=omega^j e_j`. They satisfy `TS=omega ST`. Let

`G_S=(I-alpha S)^-1`, `G_T=(I-beta T)^-1`.

These O(N)-applicable operations DO NOT commute, and for invertible denominators, `rank([G_S,G_T])=N`. The commutator is as algebraically large as possible, yet the inverse of `G_S G_T` has only two nonzeros per column. The Green-Union theorem forces a rank correction growing with N for an exact Fourier representation.

**Important positive control:** applying `G_T` and then `G_S` to 65,536 arbitrary modular input entries took linear work. The full output satisfied **all 65,536 equations** for each stage; 18 outputs were checked independently against a dense O(N²)-style kernel formula. Four direct Fourier bins differed. This is an efficient noncommuting Green network, **not** a Fourier algorithm.

## A constructive Graph–Union fusion that genuinely saves work

Let `K` divide N, let `U=diag(zeta^j)` with `zeta` a primitive Kth root, and `S` be the cyclic shift. We proved the exact **Weyl-orbit averaging identity**

`(1/K) sum_(j=0)^(K-1) U^j (I-alpha S)^-1 U^(-j) = (I-alpha^K S^K)^-1`.

The left side is **K independently applicable dense Green operators**. The right side is just **one long-range stride-K Green solve**. Its complete application, including all N output entries and invertibility preparations, costs O(N), rather than the O(KN) literal separate-path evaluator. This exact identity was tested for K=2,4,8,16 at N up to 1,024, and for K=16 at N=65,536: all 65,536 outputs matched the explicit 16-path sum and the stride-16 recurrence.

This is a valid, universal-input positive improvement **for this Green-operator average**, not for the Fourier transform. It shows how Graph+Union can yield real cross-branch savings while simultaneously revealing a trap: the fused result returns to a single sparse-inverse operator already covered by the no-go theorem. See Theorem 6 in `PROOF_CERTIFICATE.md`.

## Bluestein/chirp escape route audited

For every odd N, `F_N=D C D` with `D_jj=omega^(j^2/2)` and `C_(i,j)=omega^(-(i-j)^2/2)` (exponents modulo N). That is the known Bluestein chirp factorization; it is not a new transform algorithm. The circulant `C` is dense **and its inverse is dense**. Therefore at least `ceil(log_2 N)` one-pole commuting Green inverse factors would be needed to express `C` exactly, no matter how long their cyclic shift distances are. This is a restricted factorization lower bound, not a prohibition on all fast chirp convolutions.

## Literature and novelty boundary

The ingredients are standard: Fourier uncertainty (Donoho–Stark), prime full-spark Fourier minors (Chebotarev/Tao), low-rank inverse perturbation identities (Woodbury/resolvent), Weyl commutation, and Bluestein chirps. There is also extensive research into Fourier matrix rigidity and sparse-plus-low-rank decompositions, including Dvir–Liu and Chandrasekaran et al. This report **does not assert an historically novel theorem** without a more exhaustive scholarly audit. What is new within our research lineage is a combined, directly testable Green-network + Union-rank no-go condition that applies with internal rank corrections and arbitrary monomial gauges.

Previous `fft_green_phase23` already established the r=0 inverse-sparsity obstruction. Phase11 established that rank alone does not lower-bound arithmetic complexity. The present theorem respects both limitations.

## Why O(N) is still possible in principle

The obstruction excludes only circuits expressible as a short product of individually invertible, sparse-inverse Green stages with small total additive rank. It leaves room for more general **parallel sums of independent Green paths**, noninvertible overlapping stages whose product isn't a single invertible-chain decomposition, stage inverses with large support but cheap joint evaluation, and other genuinely global circuits. Any future positive claim must include source-to-output costs, not just support/rank.

## Phase 13 research target

Build a **branching Green circuit** of a small number of parallel paths and develop a *joint evaluation theorem* that is not reducible to one invertible sparse-inverse chain plus a low-rank correction. Count branch formation, common-subexpression reuse, exact arithmetic, preprocessing and N outputs. Test all standard basis vectors and independent random dense inputs. For complex arithmetic, compare to the published uniform O(N(log N)^(1-1e-13)) exact DFT benchmark in the same model, not merely radix-2 FFT.
