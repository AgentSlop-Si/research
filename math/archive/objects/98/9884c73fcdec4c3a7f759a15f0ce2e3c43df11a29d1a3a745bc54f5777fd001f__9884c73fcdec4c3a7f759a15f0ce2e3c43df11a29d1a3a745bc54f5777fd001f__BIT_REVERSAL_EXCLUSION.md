# Repeated bit reversal: a boundary-discrepancy exclusion

Independently derived 9 October 2026 UTC from the already verified boundary rank gate. This is a restricted factorization theorem, not an unrestricted Fourier arithmetic lower bound or a novelty claim.

## Theorem

Let N=2^m, let P be the m-bit reversal permutation, and let F_N[i,j]=omega^(ij) for a primitive Nth root omega in a field. Let S1,S2,S3 be square quasiseparable matrices of orders at most r1,r2,r3 in their displayed natural coordinates. If

F_N = S3 P S2 P S1,

then r1+r2+r3 >= ceil(m/2). In particular, three order-one stages cannot realize F_N for any dyadic N>=128.

The proof covers all generator zero and degenerate branches; it does not assume separated generators, stage inverses, nonzero pivots, or bounded coefficients. Since F_N is invertible, any actual factorization would in fact have invertible stages, but that is not used in the exclusion.

Natural Fourier endpoints and arbitrary invertible endpoint diagonal scalings are covered. In fact either one endpoint may have affine indexing with coprime slope while the other endpoint has an arbitrary permutation: a Fourier prefix-row block is Vandermonde for any distinct selected columns, and transposition covers the opposite case. With both endpoints arbitrarily non-affine, the relevant block can be rank deficient; no transfer is made without checking its rank.

## Elementary boundary rank bound

For a prefix I and suffix J of the same width W, write the row restriction S3[I,:] as its part supported in I plus an exterior error of rank <=r3. Write S1[:,J] as its part supported in J plus an exterior error of rank <=r1. Expanding the product and grouping each error into one term gives

rank((S3 P S2 P S1)[I,J]) <= r1+r3+rank(S2[R,C]),

where R=P(I), C=P(J). There is no compression-dimension exception or division in this argument.

At any separator T, let A=|R intersect [0,T)| and B=|C intersect [0,T)|. Delete W-A rows and B columns. All remaining row indices are below T and all remaining column indices are at least T, so the remaining middle block has rank <=r2. Restoring deleted rows and columns gives

rank(S2[R,C]) <= r2+W-A+B.

The Fourier block F_N[I,J] is a W-by-W Vandermonde matrix times invertible column phases and therefore has rank W. Thus every such prefix/suffix pair imposes r1+r2+r3 >= A-B.

## Exact growing discrepancy

For odd m, take N=2^m and W=T=(N+1)/3. Define

A_m = #{0<=i<W : rev_m(i)<W},
B_m = #{N-W<=i<N : rev_m(i)<W}.

At m=1, (N,W,A,B)=(2,1,1,0). Suppose m is odd and advance by two bits. Then N'=4N and W'=4W-1=N+W. Write i=4j+r, 0<=r<4. Bit reversal gives

rev_(m+2)(4j+r)=N rev_2(r)+rev_m(j).

The prefix i<W' includes all four residues for j<W-1 and residues 0,1,2 for j=W-1. In counting rev_(m+2)(i)<N+W, residue r=0 contributes W, residue r=2 contributes A_m, and residues 1,3 contribute nothing. Hence A_(m+2)=W+A_m.

Use rev_m(N-1-i)=N-1-rev_m(i) to rewrite B as the number of prefix indices whose reversed value is at least N-W. In the enlarged prefix, the threshold is N'-W'=3N-W. Residue r=3 contributes W-1, residue r=1 contributes B_m, and the other two contribute nothing. Hence B_(m+2)=W-1+B_m.

Therefore A_m-B_m=(m+1)/2 for odd m.

For even m, start from the odd (m-1)-bit witness with length n=N/2, width w=(n+1)/3 and separator t=w. Use width W=2w at length N and retain separator T=t. Since

rev_m(2j+r)=n*r+rev_(m-1)(j),

only r=0 lies below T. The prefix and suffix counts are exactly the smaller witness counts. Thus A-B=m/2. This proves the theorem for every m.

## Concrete N=128 certificate

I=[0,43), J=[85,128), and separator T=43. The mapped sets have 43 elements each:

R=[0,2,4,8,10,12,16,18,20,24,28,32,34,36,40,42,44,48,50,52,56,60,64,66,68,72,74,76,80,82,84,88,92,96,98,100,104,108,112,114,116,120,124].

C=[3,7,11,13,15,19,23,27,29,31,35,39,43,45,47,51,53,55,59,61,63,67,71,75,77,79,83,85,87,91,93,95,99,103,107,109,111,115,117,119,123,125,127].

Here A=16, B=12. Delete 27 rows at least 43 and 12 columns below 43. The retained block is16-by-31 and is strictly above a single diagonal cut. Thus a rank-one middle stage has full43-by-43 mapped-block rank at most 40. The two boundary-stage losses yield rank at most 42. The Fourier block has rank 43, a contradiction.

## Scope and algorithmic meaning

This closes repeated-bit-reversal, fixed-order, three-stage factorizations as an asymptotic route under the stated endpoints. It does not exclude other permutation pairs, more stages, stages that are not quasiseparable in the required coordinates, arbitrary non-affine endpoints, or alternative Fourier arithmetic models. In particular, non-affine Green sums need not lie in this quasiseparable class.

The order lower bound alone is not an arithmetic-operation lower bound: a high-order stage might admit a different fast representation. The ordinary O(Nr) scan upper bound does not establish a matching lower bound.

The original uploaded numerical layout records used widths6 and8 through N=128 and correctly reported no violations in those tested cases. The width43 boundary certificate is a new analytic consequence of the strengthened gate and contradicts no exact claim of universal survival. Finite checks support, but do not replace, the proof.
