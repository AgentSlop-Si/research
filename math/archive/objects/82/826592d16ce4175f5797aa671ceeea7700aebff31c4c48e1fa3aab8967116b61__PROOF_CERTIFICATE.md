# Phase 13 — Exact proof certificate: branching Weyl–Green gate

**Date:** 2026-10-10. **Status:** Self-contained mathematical proofs for scoped path grammars. No uniform sub-`N log N` full DFT algorithm, no universal FFT lower bound, no independently established novelty claim.

Throughout the main results, `N>=3` is **odd**; `ω` is a primitive `N`-th root of unity over `C`. The unnormalized transform is `F[i,j]=ω^(ij)`. Let `S e_j=e_(j+1 mod N)` and `T e_j=ω^j e_j`, so `TS=ω ST` and `S^N=T^N=I`. We use unit-cost field arithmetic, with root preparation and all online operations charged when discussing algorithms. Exponents /2 and /4 mean modular inverses in `Z/NZ`, not floating-point division.

## Theorem 1 — Full Weyl coefficient rank of the Fourier matrix

Every matrix has the unique Weyl expansion `A=Σ_(a,b) C_A[a,b] S^a T^b`. Coefficient extraction follows from trace orthogonality:

`C_A[a,b] = (1/N) Tr((S^a T^b)^(-1) A)`.

For the Fourier matrix,

`C_F[a,b]=(1/N) Σ_(t=0)^(N-1) ω^(t²+(a-b)t)`.

Completing the square gives

`C_F[a,b] = Γ_N ω^(-(a-b)²/4)`, where `Γ_N=(1/N) Σ_t ω^(t²)`.

The Gauss factor is nonzero. In complex arithmetic, `|Σ_t ω^(t²)|²=N`: substitute `u=t-s`, `v=t+s` into the double sum, which is invertible for odd `N`, then use character orthogonality. The same algebra shows `G(ω)G(ω^(-1))=N` in exact fields of characteristic not dividing `N`.

Factor the coefficient matrix as

`C_F = Γ_N diag(ω^(-a²/4)) [ω^(ab/2)]_(a,b) diag(ω^(-b²/4))`.

The middle matrix is a column permutation of a Fourier matrix because `2` is invertible modulo `N`; hence

`rank(C_F)=N`.

In addition, every entry of `C_F` is nonzero. This is a Weyl-coordinate statement, not an arithmetic-circuit lower bound. QED.

## Theorem 2 — Canonical ordered two-Green branching needs at least N paths

Suppose `1-α^N != 0` and `1-β^N != 0` and define

`G_S(α)=(I-αS)^(-1) = Σ_(a=0)^(N-1) α^a S^a/(1-α^N)`, 

`G_T(β)=(I-βT)^(-1) = Σ_(b=0)^(N-1) β^b T^b/(1-β^N)`.

Then `G_S(α)G_T(β)` has Weyl coefficient array

`C[a,b]=α^a β^b / ((1-α^N)(1-β^N))`, of matrix rank at most one.

Multiplying this path on the left or right by a Weyl monomial `S^u T^v` only cyclically permutes the two coefficient indices and multiplies the resulting row and column factors by one-dimensional characters. Hence arbitrary Weyl-monomial endpoint gauges still have Weyl-coefficient rank <=1.

Any sum of `B` such paths (with arbitrary scalar weights and arbitrary parameters/gauges satisfying invertibility) has coefficient rank <=B. By Theorem 1, if that sum equals `F_N` for every input, then **`B>=N`**. QED.

**Exact scope:** The relative order is `G_S` followed by `G_T`. The theorem does NOT cover reverse-order paths, mixed orders, different Weyl generator pairs across branches, arbitrary diagonal endpoint gauges, or general multistage networks. Each path can be applied to a fresh input in `O(N)` work, but the lower bound on literal independent branches does not prohibit amortized joint execution.

## Theorem 3 — Rotated Weyl coordinate-rank formula

Let `U=S^u T^v`, `V=S^r T^s`, and assume `D=us-vr` is invertible modulo odd `N`. Then the `N²` words `U^i V^j` form an operator basis. Set

`q=u²+v²`, `t=r²+s²`, `h=ur+vs`, `δ=(D-h)/2`.

The coefficients of `F_N` in this ordered basis are

`C'_(i,j)=Γ_N ω^(-q i²/4 + uv i/2 -t j²/4 + rs j/2 + δ i j)`.

This follows from

`U^iV^j = ω^(uv i(i-1)/2 + rs j(j-1)/2 + vr i j) S^(ui+rj) T^(vi+sj)`

and substitution into Theorem 1. Multiplying rows and columns by nonzero scalars leaves the matrix `[ω^(δ i j)]`, whose rank is the number of distinct `N`-th roots generated by `ω^δ`:

`rank(C') = N/gcd(N,δ)`.

For prime `N=p` the rank is `p` when `δ !=0`, and `1` when `δ=0`. **Therefore Weyl coefficient rank is not invariant under a change of ordered Weyl generators.** E.g. `(U,V)=(S,T)` has full rank, while `(S,ST)` has rank one. QED.

## Theorem 4 — Uniform two-direction Green-branch lower bound at prime lengths

Let `p` be an odd prime, `U=S^uT^v` and `V=S^rT^s` be any fixed generating Weyl directions (`D=us-vr !=0 mod p`). Allow branches

`A_l=c_l (I-α_l U)^(-1)(I-β_l V)^(-1)`,

with `1-α_l^p!=0`, `1-β_l^p!=0`. Their coefficient arrays in the ordered `U^i V^j` basis have rank at most one and, more specifically, are separable geometric arrays `c'_l α_l^i β_l^j`.

If `δ !=0`, Theorem 3 forces at least `p` branches.

If `δ=0`, put `A=u²+v²` and `B=r²+s²` (mod p). The elementary Lagrange identity

`A B = (ur+vs)²+(us-vr)² = h²+D²`

and `δ=0` imply `h=D`, therefore `A B=2D² !=0`, so **both** `A` and `B` are nonzero.

At `j=0`, the Fourier coefficient sequence is a nondegenerate quadratic chirp

`f_i=Γ_p ω^(-A i²/4+uv i/2)`.

Set `m=(p+1)/2`. Its `m x m` nonwrapping Hankel matrix `H_(a,b)=f_(a+b)` uses indices `a+b<=p-1` and factors into two invertible diagonal matrices and a Vandermonde matrix `[ω^(-A ab/2)]`. Since `A!=0`, those `m` Vandermonde nodes are distinct. Thus `rank(H)=m`.

Each separable Green term contributes, at `j=0`, one geometric exponential `κ_l α_l^i`, whose nonwrapping Hankel matrix has rank <=1. Therefore `B>=m`.

Combining both cases:

**For every fixed generating Weyl pair `(U,V)` at odd prime length `p`, an exact sum of this ordered two-resolvent grammar needs at least `(p+1)/2` branches.** QED.

This is independent of which pair `(U,V)` is selected **before** evaluating the branch sum. It is NOT proved for a sum whose *individual branches choose different generator pairs* or have additional arbitrary Weyl endpoints. The general-composite analogue is more subtle because `δ` may be a nonzero nonunit; Theorem 3 still holds there.

## Theorem 5 — Rank barriers are not operation-count lower bounds

Reverse-order Green paths `G_T(β)G_S(α)` have canonical Weyl coefficients

`C[a,b]=α^a β^b ω^(ab)/((1-α^N)(1-β^N))`.

If `αβ!=0` and denominators are invertible, this matrix has rank **N**, being diagonally equivalent to `F_N`, yet the operator is evaluated by one cyclic Green solve plus one diagonal multiplication in **O(N)** arithmetic. It is **not** the Fourier matrix in general.

Even the three-stage path `G_S(α)G_T(β)G_S(γ)` has full Weyl coefficient rank N for nonzero invertible parameters, because its array factors, up to a nonzero common scalar, as

`[α^((a-c) mod N)]_(a,c) · diag(γ^c) · [ω^(bc)]_(c,b) · diag(β^b)`.

The first factor is a nonsingular cyclic Green matrix; the other factors are nonsingular. Its evaluation is still **O(N)** using two cyclic Green recurrences and a diagonal multiplication. So high Weyl coefficient rank is not a measure of the complexity of applying an operator. QED.

## Theorem 6 — A genuinely branching linear-work network with dense inverse

Let `H(α,β)=G_S(α)+G_T(β)`. Fix any `N>=2` over `C`. For `0<α<1` and `β=0`,

`H(α,0)^(-1) = (I-αS)(2I-αS)^(-1) = I-(2I-αS)^(-1)`.

The inverse of `2I-αS` has a nonzero coefficient at each cyclic shift, explicitly `α^k/[2^(k+1)(1-(α/2)^N)]`. Every off-diagonal entry of `H(α,0)^(-1)` is nonzero. Its diagonal is `1-1/[2(1-(α/2)^N)]`, which is also nonzero for `N>=2` and `0<α<1`.

All entries of the inverse are rational functions of `β` continuous near `β=0`; hence for sufficiently small nonzero complex `β` the two Green factors are invertible, the inverse of their sum has **no zero entries**, and the two Green operators do not commute (`TS=ω ST`).

Despite its dense inverse, H applies to an arbitrary fresh input in `O(N)` arithmetic: compute `G_S(α)x` by a cyclic first-order recurrence, `G_T(β)x` pointwise, and add the N outputs. Precompute N modulation denominators with one batch inversion plus O(N) arithmetic, and charge all output writes.

This provides an explicit **branching escape** from a short *uncorrected* chain of two-sparse-inverse Green factors: such a chain of length L would have at most `2^L` inverse nonzeros per column, so cannot equal H when `2^L < N`. This says nothing about arbitrary low-rank-corrected circuits, nor does H equal the DFT. QED.

## Negative boundary / publication status

- These results concern matrix/operator identities, not a universal exact O(N) FFT and not a new integer-multiplication bound.
- The specific two-resolvent path grammars are essential to the lower bounds. Reverse orientation and a three-stage path are explicit O(N) counterexamples to treating Weyl rank as computational cost.
- Modular tests support, but are not a substitute for, the characteristic-zero proofs.
- The discrete Fourier/Weil representation, Weyl operator basis, quadratic Gauss sums, Prony/Hankel rank, and cyclic Green inversion are established mathematics. No claim of historical novelty is made for the combined scoped certificates without deeper literature checking.
