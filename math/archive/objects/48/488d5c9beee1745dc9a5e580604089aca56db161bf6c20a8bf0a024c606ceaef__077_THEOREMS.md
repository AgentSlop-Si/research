# HERO #0 / Phase 5 — Joint Green-frame fusion and its exact limits

**Date:** 10 October 2026  
**Status:** Exact identities, a constructive elimination of the `QM`-scan bottleneck, two scoped rank/accuracy obstructions, and independent finite-field/numerical checks. **No new exact `O(N)` or `O(N log log N)` Fourier algorithm.**

## 0. The contract we are testing

The original target remains **fresh arbitrary input**, all `N` explicit outputs, one sequential processor, all input inspection, coefficient generation, preprocessing, temporary memory, and arithmetic charged. Exact complex arithmetic and arbitrary coefficient arithmetic must be distinguished from approximate finite-precision/word-RAM algorithms.

Phase 4 proved the *complete Green-phase frame*:

\[
  \frac1M\sum_{r=0}^{M-1}P_r=I_Q, \qquad Q=KM.
\]

It could obtain all `M` input summaries with `O(Q log M)` arithmetic, but its implementation then ran `M` length-`K` Fourier transforms and `M` length-`Q` Green scans. The hoped-for new algorithm needed to fuse these scans.

**Phase 5 closes the scan question for the exact aggregate.** The scans *can* be eliminated, but the resulting transform is exactly classical mixed-radix Cooley--Tukey and still costs `O(Q log Q)`. Independently, a new local transfer analysis shows why replacing all phase channels by a low-rank surrogate cannot work in uniform operator norm.

## 1. Exact joint-phase cancellation: no separate Green scans

Let `Q=KM`, with indices `n=bM+t` (`0<=b<K`, `0<=t<M`). Define `ω_d=exp(-2πi/d)`. Fix **any** nonzero unit-modulus phases `s_{b,t}`; the original Green-chirp choices are a special case. Define phase summaries

\[
 D_{b,r}=\sum_{t=0}^{M-1}s_{b,t}x_{bM+t}\omega_M^{-rt},\qquad 0\le r<M.\tag{1}
\]

The sign convention makes `D` an inverse length-`M` DFT up to a scaling factor. Therefore each local block recovers **exactly**:

\[
 x_{bM+t}=\bar s_{b,t}\,\frac1M\sum_{r=0}^{M-1} D_{b,r}\omega_M^{rt}.\tag{2}
\]

The complete phase-frame projectors are

\[
 (P_rx)_{bM+t}=\bar s_{b,t}\omega_M^{rt}D_{b,r},
 \quad \frac1M\sum_rP_rx=x.\tag{3}
\]

Instead of executing the Green recurrence to compute `U_Q P_r x` for every `r`, **sum the phase contributions before carrying out the long Fourier operation**. Substitute (2), and reorganize the resulting double sum using the output index `k=ell+jK`:

\[
\boxed{
 (U_Q x)_{\ell+jK}=\frac1{\sqrt Q}
 \sum_{t=0}^{M-1}\omega_Q^{\ell t}\omega_M^{jt}
 \left(\sum_{b=0}^{K-1}x_{bM+t}\omega_K^{\ell b}\right).
}\tag{4}
\]

Proof: `ω_Q^{(ell+jK)bM}=ω_K^{ell b}`, because `ω_Q^{jK bM}=1`, and `ω_Q^{(ell+jK)t}=ω_Q^{ell t} ω_M^{j t}`. This is exact for arbitrary complex input and all dimensions, without any approximation, stochastic assumption, or chirp identity.

**Charged work:** Given the phase summaries, recover the local blocks by `K` length-`M` FFTs in `O(Q log M)`, compute `M` length-`K` FFTs in `O(Q log K)`, multiply `Q` twiddles, then compute `K` length-`M` FFTs in `O(Q log M)`. Total `O(Q(log K+log M))=O(Q log Q)` and `O(Q)` working memory. Producing the summaries in the first place adds another `O(Q log M)`; it does not change the bound.

**Classification:** Equation (4) is Cooley--Tukey in another basis, **not a faster exact FFT**. Phase 4's `M` explicit Green scans were unnecessary if only their aggregate is required, but their removal exposes the previously identified `M` length-`K` FFTs as an unavoidable cost **in this particular execution graph**.

## 2. Complete phase channels contain independent information

Each block map `x_b -> D_b` in (1) is an invertible `M`-point inverse Fourier transform composed with an invertible diagonal. The complete `Q`-coordinate transformation `x -> D` is consequently an isomorphism.

This proves:

- On arbitrary fresh input, the `M` channels are **not** linearly dependent, even though all arise from one input.
- For formal algebraic indeterminates `x_0,...,x_{Q-1}`, the `D_{b,r}` are also algebraically independent: an invertible linear coordinate change preserves algebraic independence.
- For continuously random inputs, any prescribed nontrivial equality of two distinct channel linear forms occurs with probability zero.

This excludes free collision-based merging or deletion of channel values. It does **not** prove that no clever global arithmetic circuit can compute the DFT faster; structural sharing of *different* linear expressions remains an unrestricted research question.

## 3. A full-rank **unitary** phase-transfer gate

Fix a block `b` and a base-frequency index `ell`. The contribution of its `M` phase summaries to the `M` outputs `ell+jK`, ignoring the nonzero common prefactor `ω_K^{ell b}/sqrt(Q)`, has transfer entries

\[
  T_{\ell,b}[j,r]=\frac1M\sum_{t=0}^{M-1}
    \bar s_{b,t}\omega_Q^{\ell t}\omega_M^{(j+r)t}.\tag{5}
\]

Let `U_M` denote the **unitary** `M`-point DFT and set `h_t=\bar s_{b,t}ω_Q^{ell t}`. Then

\[
 \boxed{T_{\ell,b}=U_M\operatorname{diag}(h)U_M^T.}\tag{6}
\]

Since `|h_t|=1`, both `U_M` and `U_M^T` are unitary, so

\[
 \boxed{T_{\ell,b}^*T_{\ell,b}=I_M.}\tag{7}
\]

**Consequence:** every singular value equals one, for **every** `ell,b,Q,M` and every unit-modulus base chirp. For `r<M`, Eckart--Young yields the exact operator-norm obstruction

\[
 \boxed{\min_{\mathrm{rank}(A)\le r}\|T_{\ell,b}-A\|_{2\to2}=1.}\tag{8}
\]

Thus a purported low-rank `r=o(M)` surrogate for the **local phase-transfer operator** cannot have uniformly vanishing spectral-norm error, even at one block. This does not exclude special-input approximations, global cancellation across blocks, or a different circuit architecture.

The 10-length complex suite verified 160 distinct local transfers, with unitarity residuals at the `10^-15` level and direct single-block Fourier responses accurate to `2.5×10^-14` or better.

## 4. Exact orthogonal-frame moments and partial-phase sampling

Within one block let `e_r(t)=\bar s_{b,t}ω_M^{rt}`. The columns `e_r/√M` form an orthonormal basis and `P_r=e_re_r^*` on each block. Across all blocks,

\[
  P_rP_{r'}=M\,\mathbf 1_{r=r'}P_r,
  \qquad \sum_{r=0}^{M-1}P_r=M I_Q.\tag{9}
\]

Let `S` be a **uniform size-`s` subset without replacement** of the `M` phases, and form the unbiased mean `\bar P_S=(1/s)\sum_{r\in S}P_r`. Then

\[
 \mathbb E_S \bar P_S=I_Q,\qquad
 \bar P_S^2=\frac Ms\bar P_S.
\]

For *every fixed* `x∈C^Q`, taking the expectation gives the **exact** identity

\[
\boxed{
 \mathbb E_S\|U_Q(\bar P_S-I)x\|_2^2
 =\frac{M-s}{s}\|x\|_2^2.
}\tag{10}
\]

With `s` independent uniform draws **with replacement**, the corresponding mean squared error is `(M-1)||x||²/s`. Sampling distinct phases therefore reduces global variance by the exact finite-population factor `(M-s)/(M-1)` compared with replacement.

A per-frequency analogue is also exact. If `y_r(k)=(U_QP_rx)_k` and `\mu(k)=(U_Qx)_k`, then

\[
\boxed{
 \operatorname{Var}_S\Big[\tfrac1s\sum_{r\in S}y_r(k)\Big]
 =\frac{M-s}{s(M-1)}\,\frac1M\sum_r|y_r(k)-\mu(k)|^2.
}\tag{11}
\]

The identity explains why **constant** `s` can give small error per Fourier bin but cannot promise a small *global relative ℓ2* error as `M` grows; with `s=M`, the error vanishes identically at `O(Q log Q)` cost by (4). Equation (10) also provides a principled variance-reduction route for limited numbers of phase rows, though constant `s` changes only constants in Phase 4's asymptotic bound.

For a *fixed* subset `S` with `s<M`, the operator norm `||\bar P_S-I||_{2→2}` is at least one, because all omitted phase subspaces lie in its kernel. This is a per-subset uniform obstruction, not a refutation of per-fixed-input randomized ℓ∞ guarantees.

The exhaustive Python checks enumerated all subsets of sizes `1,...,M` for `M=3,4,5,7` and agreed with (10) to floating-point precision.

## 5. A distributional `alpha>1/2` barrier for compressed-input Green algorithms

Phase 4 uses a centered zero-padded vector of length `Q≈4N` with original arbitrary input supported on `N` coordinates. Each sampled Green phase records at most **one complex linear measurement per occupied length-`M` input block**. Fix any nonadaptive choice of `s<M` such phases, with no later access to raw input except through these recorded measurements. The decoder may be arbitrary, including nonlinear and random processing.

Let `B` be the number of fully occupied length-`M` blocks. In the centered input embedding, `B>=floor(N/M)-2`. Since each full block contributes `M` unknown complex values but at most `s` independent observations, the total measurement-map rank deficit is

\[
 \boxed{d=N-\operatorname{rank}(A)\ \ge\ B(M-s).}\tag{12}
\]

Take a **fresh isotropic complex Gaussian input** `x ~ CN(0,I_N)` independent of the measurement-state choice. Conditioning on observations `Ax` leaves an independent Gaussian component with covariance equal to the orthogonal projector `\Pi_{\ker A}`. Since `U_N` is unitary,

\[
\operatorname{tr}(U_N\Pi_{\ker A}U_N^*)=d.
\]

Thus at least one Fourier coordinate has conditional complex variance `nu >= d/N`. No estimator based only on `Ax` can make the unknown Gaussian at that coordinate vanish. Its conditional complex-Gaussian density is at most `1/(πnu)`.

For any constant `C>0`, exponent `alpha>1/2`, and success event

\[
  \|\hat X-U_Nx\|_\infty \le C N^{-\alpha}\|x\|_2,
\]

restrict to the high-probability event `||x||₂<=2√N`. The success disk at the coordinate with largest conditional variance has radius at most `2C N^{1/2-alpha}` and Gaussian probability at most its area times maximum density. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
 \Pr[\text{all-bin success}]
 \le\frac{4C^2N^{1-2\alpha}}{d/N}
  +e^{-cN}.
}\tag{13}
\]

For `M=Theta(log N)`, `s<=M-1`, and sufficiently large `N`, (12) implies `d/N=Omega(1/M)`. Hence

\[
\boxed{
 \Pr[\text{all-bin success}]
 \le O(C^2 M N^{1-2\alpha})+e^{-cN}\to0
 \quad\text{for every fixed }\alpha>1/2.
}\tag{14}
\]

This establishes a **distributional impossibility** for that measurement-only compressed architecture, not merely an adversarial input chosen after seeing the seeds. Because success probability averaged over `x` and independent states tends to zero, the architecture cannot satisfy a high-probability guarantee for *every fixed input* in this accuracy regime.

**Scope is essential.** A general FFT algorithm reads the complete input; it is not limited to `s` scalar Green measurements per block, so (14) places **no lower bound** on arbitrary complex FFT arithmetic complexity. Neither does it disprove Phase 4's positive `alpha<1/2` approximate result. For `alpha=1/2`, the bound is inconclusive, and we make no claim.

A numerical test at `N=509`, `Q=2057`, `M=11`, `s=3` exhibited 368 unseen complex directions. Independently computed posterior Fourier variances summed to exactly 368 at numerical precision, and an omitted input direction had nonzero Fourier norm one.

## 6. Classical bounded-coefficient gate (not a general lower bound)

Even after eliminating the Green scans, formula (4) explicitly evaluates `M` **independent unnormalized** `K`-point DFTs. Those `M` length-`K` input vectors are arbitrary because the preceding local `M`-point phase transformation is invertible.

If one insists on **explicitly producing every one** of these intermediate unnormalized DFTs with a linear straight-line circuit whose gate coefficients have modulus at most one, their direct-sum matrix has determinant magnitude

\[
 |\det(I_M\otimes F_K)|=K^{MK/2}=K^{Q/2}.
\]

Morgenstern's bounded-coefficient volume argument then gives at least

\[
 \boxed{\tfrac Q2\log_2 K}\tag{15}
\]

binary linear-combination gates (up to the precise gate convention). This is a **classical model-restricted consequence**, not a new theorem about all Fourier algorithms. It does **not** carry over to freely normalized intermediates, arbitrary/unbounded scalar coefficients, or algorithms avoiding these particular intermediate vectors. Numerical determinant logs agree with the formula at all seven tested size pairs.

Reference: J. Morgenstern, *Note on a Lower Bound on the Linear Complexity of the Fast Fourier Transform*, JACM 20(2), 1973, https://doi.org/10.1145/321752.321761.

A modern unrestricted-coefficient comparison is OpenAI's all-length exact DFT result at https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/lean/docs/130.md, reported as `O(N(log N)^(1-10^-13))` operations in its own theoretical model. Phase 5 does not improve that exponent.

## 8. What we should research next, without repeating the dead ends

1. **Do not** attempt more collision-merging of the `D_{b,r}` as though they were dependent fresh-input values; they are an invertible coordinate system.
2. **Do not** approximate the local transfer `T_{ell,b}` by rank `r<M` and announce uniformly vanishing operator-norm error; every singular value is one.
3. **Do not** target the M Green scans alone: the exact aggregate scan is eliminated, but `M` independent `K`-FFTs remain. The full computation is Cooley--Tukey.
4. **For exact all-input progress**, find a direct *global* contraction that avoids explicitly computing those `M` intermediate K-FFTs **and** has proven total work `o(Q log Q)`, with all arithmetic and coefficient costs charged. This is an open construction request; it may require a model/circuit radically different from the phase-frame pipeline.
5. **For approximate all-input progress**, test whether Gaussian FIR interpolation admits a uniform `O(N)`-work evaluator at polynomially shrinking error `N^-alpha` without violating the Phase 4 covariance/model contract. Improved polynomial accuracy must not be claimed from fixed-width filters with nonvanishing deterministic error.

**Final outcome:** Phase 5 resolves the exact-scan bottleneck *inside the Green frame*, proves a tight orthogonal-frame variance identity, supplies a stronger distributional accuracy barrier for measurement-only compression, and explains the full-rank unitary phase coupling. **It does not solve arbitrary exact sub-`N log N` FFT.**
