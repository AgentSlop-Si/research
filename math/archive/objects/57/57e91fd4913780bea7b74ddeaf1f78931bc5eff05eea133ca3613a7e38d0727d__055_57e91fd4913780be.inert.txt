# Mixed-order Green stage: a fully charged linear-arithmetic inverse

Independent audit and extension, 9 October 2026. This implements a useful extension of the uploaded mixed-order Green primitive. It does not factor the Fourier matrix and does not establish a faster FFT or literature priority.

## 1. Definitions and exact identity

Let N=2m>=2, T=tridiag(-1,3,-1), and let P gather coordinates in the order

    sigma = (0,2,...,N-2,1,3,...,N-1).

Put B=P T P^T, epsilon=1/20, and

    M = B^-1 + epsilon T^-1.

The uploaded forward circuit applies M in O(N) scalar operations using two tridiagonal solves. The inverse is also linear-arithmetic:

    C = T + epsilon B,
    M = B^-1 C T^-1,
    M^-1 = T C^-1 B.                                      (1)

Multiplication by B or T takes O(N), including P and P^T. The remaining issue is a fully charged O(N) solver for C. Equation (1) preserves matrix order; incorrectly commuting T and B would invalidate it.

## 2. The intermediate solve is banded plus rank two

Conjugate C back to interleaved coordinates:

    Ctilde = P^T C P = P^T T P + epsilon T.

The natural path edges of T become all distance-two edges plus one exceptional edge connecting a=1 and b=N-2 (zero-based indices). Thus

    Ctilde = C0 - e_a e_b^T - e_b e_a^T,

where C0 has diagonal 3(1+epsilon), first off-diagonals -epsilon, and second off-diagonals -1. The formula includes N=2 and N=4; in those cases the exceptional edge happens to be nearby, and its contribution adds to the existing first off-diagonal.

C0 is symmetric and strictly diagonally dominant with positive diagonal, hence SPD. Indeed each row's off-diagonal absolute sum is at most 2+2epsilon, below 3+3epsilon. Ctilde is also SPD because C=T+epsilon B is a positive sum of two SPD matrices. Therefore all pivots in the no-pivot LDL^T factorization of C0 are positive over the rationals/reals.

Let

    U = [e_a,e_b],  V = [-e_b,-e_a],
    Z = C0^-1 U,  K = I_2 + V^T Z.

Prepare the bandwidth-two LDL^T factorization of C0, the two columns of Z, and the inverse of K. All cost O(N) scalar operations and storage. The determinant lemma gives

    det(K) = det(Ctilde)/det(C0) > 0,

so the 2x2 correction is invertible for every even N>=2. A fresh solve is

    y = C0^-1 rhs,
    Ctilde^-1 rhs = y - Z K^-1 V^T y.                       (2)

Here V^T y just reads and negates two coordinates, K^-1 acts on two scalars, and multiplication by the N-by-2 Z is one linear pass. Scatter the input by P^T and gather the result by P to solve C. Combine with (1) to obtain M^-1.

This is ordinary banded elimination and the rank-two Woodbury identity applied to this specific path geometry. The new project deliverable is the explicit geometry, charged inverse, and independent implementation, not a claim that these general matrix techniques are new.

## 3. Explicit banded recurrence

For diagonal d=3(1+epsilon), first off-diagonal c=-epsilon, and second off-diagonal b=-1, write C0=L D L^T with unit lower triangular L and two subdiagonals l1_i,l2_i. For i>=2:

    l2_i = b / D_(i-2),
    l1_i = (c - l2_i D_(i-2) l1_(i-1)) / D_(i-1),
    D_i  = d - l1_i^2 D_(i-1) - l2_i^2 D_(i-2).

Use the corresponding truncated formulas at i=0,1. This takes constant arithmetic per index. Forward substitution, diagonal scaling, and backward substitution each take O(N). Prepare reciprocal pivots once. The implementation uses the same bandwidth-at-most-two routine for T with zero second off-diagonal; it does not construct a dense matrix in the executor.

## 4. Reflected and phase-dressed versions

The source uses z=ceil(sqrt(N-1)), u=(z,1,...,1)^T and H=I-2uu^T/(u^Tu). Since H^2=I,

    (H M)^-1 = M^-1 H.

For source phases D_L[i,i]=omega^(i^2), D_R[j,j]=omega^j,

    (D_L H M D_R)^-1 = D_R^-1 M^-1 H D_L^-1.

Every additional factor has a charged O(N) application. The diagonal entries and reciprocals can be generated by recurrences in O(N) operations from a supplied primitive Nth root. The rational Householder data need one integer square-root computation on an O(log N)-bit integer, plus O(N) scalar/index work. No complex root extraction is silently supplied by this extension.

## 5. Stronger spectral conditioning

The uploaded kappa_2<=7 estimate is valid but loses positivity by treating epsilon G as an arbitrary perturbation. Set c_N=cos(pi/(N+1)). Eigenvalues of T lie in

    [3-2c_N, 3+2c_N].

Both B^-1 and T^-1 therefore lie, in the positive-semidefinite order, between I/(3+2c_N) and I/(3-2c_N). Adding them with positive weights gives

    (1+epsilon)/(3+2c_N) I <= M <= (1+epsilon)/(3-2c_N) I,
    kappa_2(M) <= (3+2c_N)/(3-2c_N) < 5.

The same condition bound holds for M^-1, H M, and the phase-dressed version because H and unit-modulus diagonals are unitary. The cosine is only used in the proof; preparing coefficients does not require its evaluation. The source's induced-1 condition bounds and their rank-one gauge warning remain applicable. A bounded matrix condition number is not itself a proof of the numerical stability of a particular floating-point implementation of (2).

In particular, the **bare M still has uniformly bounded induced-1 condition**. The upload's bound kappa_1(M)<=7 holds for any permutation P: ||B||_1<=5, ||T^-1||_1<=1, and the Neumann series for (I+epsilon B T^-1)^-1 gives ||M^-1||_1<=5/(1-5epsilon)=20/3; also ||M||_1<=21/20. Consequently a product of t bare M-type stages with interposed permutations, unit phases and scalar global rescalings has kappa_1 at most 7^t, while Fourier has kappa_1=N. It needs t>=log(N)/log(7). High natural cut rank alone therefore does not escape that earlier norm gate. The H wrapper supplies the growing induced-1 condition in the uploaded construction. This particular product obstruction does not cover added reflections or arbitrary nonunit-magnitude diagonal gains.

## 6. Full-cost and representation model

The bound is O(N) exact scalar-field operations, O(N) indexing/data movement, O(N) input-independent preparation, and O(N) memory. All N input and output slots are read/written. Preparation includes both banded factorizations, reciprocal pivots, two correction solves, the 2x2 inverse, permutations, and Householder data. No dense inverse is part of the algorithm.

The independently instrumented implementation uses 42N-40 scalar-field operations for this shared preparation, 20N-24 for a forward M application, and 19N-8 for an inverse M application, for even N>=2. These counts include field inversion as one scalar operation and deliberately include some unnecessary zero-coefficient operations in the generic banded routine. They exclude the separately linear array/index operations and integer square-root work; together these still cost O(N) in the stated scalar-arithmetic/index model. Phase and reflection applications add linear work. These are implementation counts, not minimality claims.

This is **not** O(N) bounded-word or bit complexity. For example, T^-1[0,N-1]=1/D_N, where D_0=1, D_1=3, D_N=3D_(N-1)-D_(N-2). The integer D_N grows exponentially, so this single exact rational coefficient has Theta(N) denominator bits. Exact rational arithmetic cannot silently be treated as bounded-word arithmetic. Large bit complexity, finite-precision stability, practical constants, and a Fourier factorization remain separate questions.

The all-N proof is over characteristic zero, with arbitrary fresh complex input and rational stage coefficients. Modular tests are finite certificates at primes where all required denominators/pivots are nonzero. They do not establish that one fixed finite field supports the construction at every length.

## 7. Independent implementation and verification

Run `python3 mixed_inverse_check.py`. It imports no source files from the upload and uses only the Python standard library. Its dense reference M is generated from the closed continuant formula for a tridiagonal inverse, separately from the banded solver. The executor itself uses only vectors and fixed-width bands.

Verified results in `mixed_inverse_results.json`:

- Rational exact tests at N=2,4,6,8,10,12,16 and modular tests at N=2,4,6,8,10,16,32,64,128.
- Five fresh vectors per case, 80 total: forward-versus-dense agreement, both inverse compositions, the intermediate C-solve equation, and reflected inversion.
- Phase-dressed forward/inverse round trips at N=8,16,32,64.
- Complete inverse-column verification for both M and H M at N=8,16,32,64, 240 column checks total. These independently reproduce full density of M, H M and both inverses at the tested lengths, and full half-cut rank of H M. Neither inverse density nor exact full half-cut rank is promoted to an all-N theorem.
- Linear field-operation counts recorded separately from the dense test oracle.

The algebra above, rather than finite tests, establishes the all-N linear-arithmetic inverse and the improved condition bound.
