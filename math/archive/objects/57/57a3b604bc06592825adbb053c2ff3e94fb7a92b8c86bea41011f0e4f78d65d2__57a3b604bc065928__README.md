# Exact FFT research: new materials, constructive gains, and the scan question

9 October 2026. This continuation incorporates the six initially attached
archives and the subsequently added wide-scan archive. The two joint-Galois
ZIPs have identical SHA-256 hashes, so the seven attachments contain six
unique research packages. Their extracted contents and source hashes are
preserved in `fft_new_materials_20261009/`; source files were not modified.

**Main goal remains unresolved:** this package does not establish a new
independent o(N log N) exact transform for arbitrary fresh complex inputs.
It contains measured exact-field gains on verified input/support classes,
new full-output algorithms, and a stronger all-input theorem about the
specific scan architecture proposed in the latest material. No integer
multiplication bit-complexity breakthrough or mathematical priority is claimed.

## 1. Cold recurrence reconstruction now handles every scalar shift

`fft_recurrence_extension_research/` improves the earlier verified pair
FE=JF. It detects inputs with sparse `(E-lambda I)x`, now allowing both
lambda=0 and singular root-of-unity shifts. Sparse inputs retain a direct path.
Automatic exact majority comparisons find the candidate lambda, followed by
a full residual check. Accepted work remains O(N(s+1)) for s defects.

At zero shift, a first-order target recurrence closes directly in one scan.
At a nonzero shift, the actual input sum supplies y0. A second-order target
recurrence leaves one scalar seed; cyclic closure usually determines it.
If that closure is singular, one explicitly computed inverse-Fourier
coordinate sum, using the known input coordinate, determines the missing
kernel component. That sum is O(N) paid work, not a hidden inverse transform.

The author audit checks 368 exact outputs, 13 declines and four invalid
controls. A separate exhaustive review checks all 625 inputs over F5^4 at
two support caps: 1,250 acceptance/output decisions, including singular cases.
The two reviews and cost formulas are included.

## 2. An additional automatically detected dense input family

`fft_qlinear_recurrence_research/` recognizes the exact condition

    (p0+p1*q^i)*x_i + (r0+r1*q^i)*x_(i+1)=0,  0<=i<N-1,

for some nonzero coefficient quadruple. A streaming four-column nullspace
calculation finds the parameters in O(N). Fourier conjugation turns this
source recurrence into a cyclic bidiagonal target equation. The endpoint
defect is retained, and singular cases use a paid input-coordinate constraint.
All N outputs cost O(N) exact field/word work for accepted inputs, including
discovery and preparation.

This includes geometric modes, chirps and linear-fractional q-ratios. It is
an additional class, not a strict superset of every previous scalar-shift
defect family. Parameters and inversions depend on the input; it is not a
fixed linear circuit for all inputs. The exact audit checks 380 full outputs
(244 singular reconstructions), 60 declines and explicit degenerate cases.

## 3. Joint output decoding with a sparser annihilator multiple

`fft_joint_decode_research/` begins with the supplied fused reconstruction
identities. It does not count their rediscovery as progress. For modal roots
z_s it tests powered-root polynomials

    R_b(t)=product_(distinct lambda=z_s^b)(1-lambda*t).

The higher-degree polynomial R_b(t^b) still annihilates the sequence, and may
have far fewer nonzero coefficients than its minimal denominator. The code
pays every candidate search, coefficient construction and initial prefix,
then generates the complete output directly by the selected sparse recurrence.

For the two-fiber example, every mode has z^16 equal to omega^16 or omega^48.
After 32 paid seed values, the explicit recurrence is

    f_m=(omega^16+omega^48)*f_(m-16)-omega^64*f_(m-32).

This uses two multiplications and one addition per additional output, with
no final FFT decoding. For logarithmic k and a constant number of discovered
taps, the total is O(N)+polylog(N). Arbitrary supports need not have that
property. A paid hybrid chooses the conventional decoded FFT when cheaper,
preserving its arbitrary-support asymptotic bound under the stated auxiliary
root assumptions. Exact tests include 112 amplitude basis vectors, 71,080
output-coordinate comparisons and 45 independently checked edge cases.

## 4. Explicit side information makes some orbit reconstruction cheap

`fft_joint_sideinfo_research/` uses the retained input coefficients to form
the signed substitution defect d=(P5-I)r at each dyadic conductor. Its exact
identity is `Y_(5k)-Y_k=DFT_odd(d)_k`. Two directly evaluated anchors and a
sparse defect advance the two odd-frequency cycles. All levels, full support
discovery, anchors and outputs are paid. Bounded defect support gives O(N)
field work on a verified fresh-input class.

Its per-conductor hybrid retains cheap levels and uses ordinary modulated
FFTs elsewhere; its arbitrary-input overhead is at most O(N) over the
selected radix-two evaluator. A separate binary-index rank-one tensor route
discovers and verifies all factors, then shares periodic suffix products to
produce the entire Fourier vector in O(N) work. Neither decoder reconstructs
arbitrary input from a few Fourier measurements. Both exact prototypes and
their independent reviews are included. Numerical error statements in their
documents are conditional analyses, not implemented floating-point certificates.

## 5. New wide-scan material: the internal-shuffle escape is closed

`fft_inverse_support_research/` strengthens the latest archive's scan result.
The old cut-rank invariant did not exclude arbitrary intermediate shuffles.
For the stated ordinary weighted scan, however, invertibility forces its
inverse to be bidiagonal: at most two nonzeros per row. Inverse permutations
and diagonals have one nonzero per row. After t scan factors, inverse row
support is at most 2^t under any sequence of intermediate layouts.

Every Fourier-inverse row has N nonzeros. The one-supported boundary row of
an ordinary scan sharpens this to

    t >= 1+ceil(log2 N).

A fully implemented factorization uses 2log2(N) scans by expressing each
radix-two butterfly layer as a backward and forward scan with resets and
explicit routing. Thus the architecture needs Theta(log N) passes even with
arbitrary internal shuffles. With the stated full-array pass charging, it
costs Theta(N log N) work. The construction is an ordinary FFT realization,
not a speedup; its coefficients, permutations and every operation are counted.

This rules out that particular o(log N)-scan route. It is not a general FFT
lower bound: additive branches, general feedthrough filters, auxiliary-state
methods and linear-work stages with dense inverses are outside the theorem.
The independent review confirms both the lower bound and matching-order
construction; exact checks cover 136 basis vectors and 72 internally shuffled
scan products, among other controls.

## 6. The thin-matrix source lead stays conditional

`fft_thin_matrix_research/` recovers and reads the official 1978 erratum and
checks Chan's explicit O(n^2) total-operation citation. It also proves a
constant-overhead transfer between the two matrix orientations by reverse
differentiation of an already available total arithmetic circuit.

The original corrected 1976 construction was not recovered in full. We
therefore do not claim to have implemented or independently audited its
addition/decoding costs, and do not dismiss the later total-operation citation.
The supplied linear-time logarithmic-support consequence remains conditional
on that full circuit premise. There is no placeholder fast-matrix black box
inside any of the executable algorithms above.
