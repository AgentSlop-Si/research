# Predecode-median Fourier sketch

**Checkpoint:** 9 October 2026  
**Status:** Algebraically derived and reproducibly tested continuation of the v5 frequency-shift sketch. The new mechanism defers the short Cooley–Tukey decoder until *after* robust coordinate recovery. This lowers the cost of each additional robust row from `Theta(N log M)` to `Theta(N + (N/M) log(N/M))`, gives a new full-output energy-relative approximation regime with polylogarithmic rather than `N^{o(1)}` overhead, and gives high-probability exact recovery for locally Fourier-sparse structured inputs.  
**Not claimed:** an exact arbitrary-input FFT, ordinary floating-point accuracy, an all-length theorem, a bit-complexity theorem, a practical win over FFTW/cuFFT, or established novelty.

---

## 1. Why v5 could not simply be “analyzed harder”

The v5 estimator already had the exact per-output variance

\[
\mathbb E|\widehat X_m-X_m|^2
=\frac{M-1}{N}\|x\|_2^2,
\qquad N=KM.
\]

The obvious next move was to seek a universal fourth-moment or subgaussian tail bound for one row. That route is false in the worst case.

### 1.1 A one-column local Fourier tone

Let all local spectra vanish except

\[
A_{t_0}[s_0]=1.
\]

Equivalently, the time-domain input occupies one interleaved column and is a single length-`K` Fourier mode. Fix an output whose local index is `p=s_0`. In one v5 row, every other column `t != t_0` contributes precisely when

\[
q_t=q_{t_0}.
\]

Hence the collision count is

\[
C\sim\operatorname{Bin}(M-1,1/K),
\]

and the output error has the form

\[
E=\frac1{\sqrt M}\sum_{j=1}^{C}\zeta_j,
\]

where the `zeta_j` are independent unit-modulus variables with zero first and second moments. Conditional on `C`,

\[
\mathbb E\left|\sum_{j=1}^{C}\zeta_j\right|^4
=2C^2-C.
\]

Therefore

\[
\mathbb E|E|^2=\frac{M-1}{MK},
\]

\[
\mathbb E|E|^4
=\frac1{M^2}
\left(
\frac{M-1}{K}
+
\frac{2(M-1)(M-2)}{K^2}
\right),
\]

and the standardized fourth moment is exactly

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\mathbb E|E|^4}{(\mathbb E|E|^2)^2}
=
\frac K{M-1}+2-\frac2{M-1}.
}
\tag{1.1}
\]

When `K/M -> infinity`, the kurtosis diverges. Moreover, the event `C=1` has probability

\[
\frac{M-1}{K}\left(1-\frac1K\right)^{M-2},
\]

and on that event `|E|=1/sqrt(M)`. Relative to the standard deviation this is a deviation of order `sqrt(K/M)` occurring with probability of order `M/K`, exactly the Chebyshev scale `1/lambda^2`.

### Consequence

A one-row, variance-scale, distribution-free subgaussian theorem is impossible for this estimator. The v5 generic median analysis was not merely losing a logarithm through careless proof; robust repetition is genuinely needed on the full arbitrary-input class.

The prototype verifies (1.1) for five `(K,M)` pairs. The largest tested case, `K=512,M=16`, has theoretical kurtosis `36.0` and measured kurtosis `35.9434` over 60,000 trials.

## 2. The surviving idea: robust recovery before the short decoder

The v5 row first produced estimates of all local length-`K` Fourier coordinates and then immediately ran the `K` short length-`M` decoders. Repeating that row `R` times therefore paid the `N log M` decoder cost `R` times.

But the nonlinear operation—the median—is only needed to recover the local coordinates. The deterministic short decoder can be postponed until after those medians.

The new order is:

```text
R independent mixed length-K spectra
                ↓
recover R estimates of each local coordinate A_t[p]
                ↓
median across rows in the local-coordinate domain
                ↓
one set of K length-M decoders
                ↓
all N Fourier outputs
```

This is the whole v6 architectural change. It looks small, but asymptotically it changes how many robust rows are affordable.

## 3. Local-coordinate estimator

Assume

\[
N=KM,
\]

reshape

\[
x_{r,t}=x_{rM+t},
\qquad
0\le r<K,
\quad
0\le t<M,
\]

and define the unitary length-`K` column transforms

\[
A_t[p]
=\frac1{\sqrt K}\sum_{r=0}^{K-1}
 x_{r,t}e^{-2\pi i rp/K}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

For one randomized row draw independently

\[
q_t\sim\operatorname{Unif}(\mathbb Z_K),
\qquad
\xi_t\sim\operatorname{Unif}\{1,-1,i,-i\}.
\]

Construct

\[
z_r
=\sum_{t=0}^{M-1}
\overline{\xi_t}e^{-2\pi i r q_t/K}x_{r,t}
\]

and compute

\[
Z=U_Kz.
\]

The modulation identity gives

\[
Z[s]
=\sum_{u=0}^{M-1}
\overline{\xi_u}A_u[s+q_u].
\tag{3.2}
\]

Instead of decoding a full length-`N` estimate immediately, recover each local coordinate by

\[
\widehat A_t[p]
=\xi_t Z[p-q_t].
\tag{3.3}
\]

Substituting (3.2),

\[
\widehat A_t[p]
=A_t[p]
+
\sum_{u\ne t}
\xi_t\overline{\xi_u}
A_u[p+q_u-q_t].
\tag{3.4}
\]

### 3.1 Unbiasedness

For every `p,t`, even conditional on all shifts,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_\xi\widehat A_t[p]=A_t[p].
}
\tag{3.5}
\]

### 3.2 Exact local variance

Let

\[
E_t=\sum_{r=0}^{K-1}|x_{r,t}|^2
=\sum_{p=0}^{K-1}|A_t[p]|^2,
\qquad
E=\|x\|_2^2=\sum_tE_t.
\]

The fourth-root phases remove all cross terms. Averaging the uniform frequency differences then gives

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E|\widehat A_t[p]-A_t[p]|^2
=\frac{E-E_t}{K}.
}
\tag{3.6}
\]

The right-hand side is independent of `p`.

The reproducible experiment checked this identity on 18 combinations of factorization and signal family. The empirical/theoretical ratios ranged from `0.97761` to `1.03393`.

## 4. Predecode median algorithm

Take

\[
R=2s-1
\]

independent compressed rows. For every local coordinate `(p,t)`, form

\[
\widetilde A_t[p]
=
\operatorname{med}_j\Re\widehat A_t^{(j)}[p]
+i\operatorname{med}_j\Im\widehat A_t^{(j)}[p].
\tag{4.1}
\]

Only after all local medians are available, apply the ordinary Cooley–Tukey short decoder:

\[
\widetilde X_{p+Kv}
=
\frac1{\sqrt M}
\sum_{t=0}^{M-1}
\widetilde A_t[p]
 e^{-2\pi i(p+Kv)t/N}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

If the local medians equal the true `A_t[p]`, (4.2) is the exact unitary DFT.

## 5. Simultaneous arbitrary-input error theorem

Let

\[
C_s={2s-1\choose s}.
\]

The median-moment inequality for `2s-1` iid real estimators gives

\[
\mathbb E|\operatorname{med}(Y_1,\ldots,Y_{2s-1})-\mathbb EY_1|^{2s}
\le
C_s(\mathbb E|Y_1-\mathbb EY_1|^2)^s.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Apply this separately to the real and imaginary parts of every local coordinate, use (3.6), Markov's inequality, and a union bound over the `N=KM` local coordinates.

### Theorem 5.1 — local simultaneous recovery

For every `0<delta<1`, with probability at least `1-delta`, simultaneously for all `p,t`,

\[
|\widetilde A_t[p]-A_t[p]|
\le
\sqrt2
\left(\frac{2NC_s}{\delta}\right)^{1/(2s)}
\sqrt{\frac{E-E_t}{K}}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

### Theorem 5.2 — all `N` Fourier outputs

Under the same event,

\[
\boxed{
\|\widetilde X-U_Nx\|_\infty
\le
\frac{\sqrt2}{\sqrt{MK}}
\left(\frac{2NC_s}{\delta}\right)^{1/(2s)}
\sum_{t=0}^{M-1}\sqrt{E-E_t}.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

Using

\[
\sum_t(E-E_t)=(M-1)E
\]

and Cauchy–Schwarz gives the input-independent simplification

\[
\boxed{
\|\widetilde X-U_Nx\|_\infty
\le
\sqrt2
\left(\frac{2NC_s}{\delta}\right)^{1/(2s)}
\sqrt{\frac{M(M-1)}N}\,\|x\|_2.
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

Compared with the postdecode median in v5, this proof loses an extra factor approximately `sqrt(M)`. The benefit is that additional rows are now much cheaper, and that asymptotic trade is favorable.

## 6. Fully charged time and memory

For each randomized row:

1. construct `z`: `O(N)`;
2. compute one length-`K` FFT: `O(K log K)`;
3. retain only `Z`, the `M` shifts, and the `M` phases.

After all rows:

4. gather and median `R` values for each of the `N` local coordinates: `O(RN)` using linear-time selection;
5. run the `K` length-`M` decoders once: `O(N log M)`;
6. write the `N` outputs: `O(N)`.

Thus

\[
\boxed{
T(N,M,R)
=O\!\left(
R\left[N+\frac NM\log\frac NM\right]
+N\log M
\right).
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

### 6.1 Memory does not need to be `RN`

It is unnecessary to retain `R` full local arrays or `R` full output vectors. Store only

\[
R\cdot K
\]

compressed spectrum values plus `R*M` shifts/phases. Then process one `p`-slice at a time, gather its `R*M` estimates, take medians, run one length-`M` decoder, and emit those `M` outputs.

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
S(N,M,R)=O\!\left(N+R\left(\frac NM+M\right)\right).
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

The `N` term is the explicit output itself.

## 7. Main asymptotic corollary: polylogarithmic energy-scale error

Take powers of two `N`. Fix constants

\[
a\ge0,
\qquad
\varepsilon>0,
\]

and require failure probability

\[
\delta=N^{-a}.
\]

Let `h(N)` satisfy

\[
1\le h(N)=o(\log\log N).
\]

Choose a power-of-two factor

\[
M=\Theta\!\left(\frac{\log N}{h(N)}\right),
\qquad
K=N/M,
\tag{7.1}
\]

and choose

\[
s=
\Theta\!\left(
\frac{(1+a)\log N}{\varepsilon\log\log N}
\right),
\qquad
R=2s-1.
\tag{7.2}
\]

Since `C_s <= 2^(2s-1)`, the amplification term in (5.4) is

\[
O((\log N)^\varepsilon).
\]

Combining (5.4), (7.1), and (7.2) gives

\[
\boxed{
\|\widetilde X-U_Nx\|_\infty
=
O\!\left(
\frac{(\log N)^{1+\varepsilon}}{h(N)}
\frac{\|x\|_2}{\sqrt N}
\right)
}
\tag{7.3}
\]

with probability at least `1-N^(-a)`.

The time is

\[
\boxed{
T(N)
=O\!\left(
\frac{1+a}{\varepsilon}
\frac{h(N)}{\log\log N}
N\log N
+N\log\log N
\right)
=o(N\log N),
}
\tag{7.4}
\]

and (6.2) gives

\[
\boxed{S(N)=O(N).}
\tag{7.5}
\]

### Clean fixed choice

Setting `h(N)=1` gives

\[
T(N)=O\!\left(\frac{N\log N}{\log\log N}\right)

times a constant depending on `a,epsilon`, and

\[
\|\widetilde X-U_Nx\|_\infty
=O\!\left(
(\log N)^{1+\varepsilon}
\frac{\|x\|_2}{\sqrt N}
\right).
\tag{7.6}
\]

Choosing a slowly growing `h=o(log log N)` improves the error by `h` while preserving strict little-`o(N log N)` time.

### What improved relative to v5

At the fastest v5 factorization, every robust row cost `Theta(N log log N)`, so only `o(log N/log log N)` rows fit inside strict sub-`N log N` time. The resulting worst-case amplification remained superpolylogarithmic (`N^{o(1)}`).

Here each additional robust row costs only `Theta(N h)`, because the `M`-point decoder is shared. This permits `Theta(log N/log log N)` rows and reduces the arbitrary-input overhead to a true polylogarithm.

## 8. Exact structured result: locally Fourier-sparse columns

Suppose every local spectrum `A_u` has at most `h_0` nonzero entries.

For a fixed target local coordinate `(p,t)`, a row is exactly correct unless some source column `u != t` lands one of its nonzeros at the queried shifted frequency. By a union bound,

\[
\rho
\le
\frac{(M-1)h_0}{K}.
\tag{8.1}
\]

If at least `s` of the `R=2s-1` rows are collision-free, both the real and imaginary medians equal the target exactly. Independent rows therefore give

\[
\Pr[\widetilde A_t[p]\ne A_t[p]]
\le
C_s\rho^s.
\]

Union over all `N` local coordinates:

\[
\boxed{
\Pr[\widetilde X\ne U_Nx]
\le
N C_s
\left(\frac{(M-1)h_0}{K}\right)^s.
}
\tag{8.2}
\]

This is exact algebraic recovery, not approximation, on the stated structured class.

For constant `h_0`, `M=Theta(log N)`, and only three rows (`s=2`),

\[
N\left(\frac{Mq}{K}\right)^2
=
O\!\left(\frac{h_0^2(\log N)^4}{N}\right)
\to0.
\]

The resulting exact structured transform costs

\[
O(N\log\log N)
\]

because only a constant number of compressed rows is needed.

The prototype generated signals with exactly `h_0` nonzero local Fourier coordinates per column. At `K=4096,M=8`, all 12 trials were exactly recovered to numerical roundoff for:

- `h_0=1` with 3, 5, and 7 rows;
- `h_0=2` with 5 and 7 rows;
- `h_0=4` with 5 and 7 rows.

For the hardest tested point `h_0=8`, seven rows recovered 11 of 12 trials; the rigorous union upper bound there was approximately `0.0401`, so a single observed failure in 12 trials is statistically compatible with the bound and is not evidence against it.

## 10. Prior-art boundary

The local estimator is CountSketch-like: random shifts act as hashes, fourth-root phases act as signs, and coordinatewise medians provide robust point recovery. CountSketch point estimation and median amplification are established techniques. The median-moment inequality used in Section 5 is also established prior work.

What this checkpoint contributes to the project is the **factorization-specific scheduling observation**:

> recover the intermediate Cooley–Tukey coordinates robustly, retain only compressed length-`K` rows, and execute the expensive family of short decoders once.

A limited search did not identify this exact full-output Fourier scheduling/result, but that is not a novelty proof. The theorem should be treated as a project-originated candidate until independently reviewed against the broader sketching, approximate matrix-vector multiplication, and Fourier literature.

Primary references consulted:

- M. Charikar, K. Chen, M. Farach-Colton, *Finding Frequent Items in Data Streams*, ICALP 2002.
- K. G. Larsen, R. Pagh, J. Tětek, *CountSketches, Feature Hashing and the Median of Three*, ICML 2021, arXiv:2102.02193.
- G. T. Minton, E. Price, *Improved Concentration Bounds for Count-Sketch*, SODA 2014, arXiv:1207.5200.
- H. Hassanieh, P. Indyk, D. Katabi, E. Price, *Nearly Optimal Sparse Fourier Transform*, STOC 2012, arXiv:1201.2501. That work targets sparse Fourier recovery rather than explicitly approximating all `N` outputs.

## 11. Honest boundary and next question

This checkpoint gives, for factorizable lengths, a randomized fresh-input full-output approximation with:

- strict `o(N log N)` algebraic time;
- `O(N)` memory including explicit output;
- arbitrary dense inputs;
- simultaneous energy-relative error at most a polylogarithmic factor above `||x||_2/sqrt(N)`;
- exact high-probability recovery for locally Fourier-sparse structured inputs.

It still does **not** give:

- exact arbitrary-input DFT;
- relative error on every coefficient;
- ordinary FFT roundoff accuracy;
- finite-precision/bit-complexity accounting;
- prime/all-length support;
- a measured wall-clock win over optimized FFT libraries.

The remaining mathematical gap is now much narrower and more concrete:

> Can one replace the coordinatewise local median plus worst-case short-decoder triangle inequality by a robust intermediate estimator whose error is controlled directly in the decoded `ell_infinity` norm, while still allowing the short decoder to run only `o(log N/log log N)` times—or once?

That is the route to remove the remaining approximately `log N` factor without falling back to `Theta(N log N)` work.
