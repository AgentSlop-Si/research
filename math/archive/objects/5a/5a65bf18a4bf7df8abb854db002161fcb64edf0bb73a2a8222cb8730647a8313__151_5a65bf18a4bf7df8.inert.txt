# Sharp F32 rank gate and complete coordinate-bit two-stage exclusion

## Verified results

1. Every 11-by-11 submatrix of the complex 32-point Fourier matrix has rank at least five, including mixed-parity row and column sets. The minimum is exactly five.
2. Consequently all 120 unsigned coordinate-bit internal shuffles fail for an exact F32 factorization by two cross-cut-rank-one bidirectional stages, even with arbitrary independent endpoint permutations and nonsingular endpoint diagonal scalings.
3. The result is a scoped lower bound. It is not a faster fresh-input FFT or a general lower bound for arbitrary multistage Fourier circuits.

## Files

- `PROOF.md`: self-contained proof, exact sharpness example, complete architectural application, and the smallest alternate certificate.
- `certify_gate.py`: sharp moment bound 3377 via 63 integer vertices, independently checked against all 617 dyadic difference profiles.
- `certificate.json`: exact output of that checker.
- `certify_single_polytope.py`: smaller independent certificate, 22 rational vertices and the sufficient moment bound 3425.
- `single_polytope_result.txt`: output of the smaller checker.
- `certify_bit_permutations.py`: fresh exact enumeration of all 120 five-bit coordinate permutations, eliminating 115 by the earlier discrepancy screen and the final five by the new rank gate.
- `bit_permutation_certificate.json`: per-permutation results and witnessing cuts.

The Python checkers use only the standard library, exact integers or rational numbers, and no external data or downloaded programs. Run them with Python 3.8 or later. Their scope is explicit and finite; no approximate rank inference is used.

The proof's core is the Galois-averaged Gram second moment: rank four would force an average of at least `14641/4 = 3660.25`, whereas the independent certificates bound it by 3377 or, more simply, 3425. A row/column set achieving rank five is `{0,2,4,8,10,12,16,18,20,24,28}`.

No claim of literature priority is made.
