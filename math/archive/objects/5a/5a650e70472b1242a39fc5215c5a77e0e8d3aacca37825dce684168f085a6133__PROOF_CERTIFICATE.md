# Proofs and exact certificates — nonlinear-routing Fourier rank

Date: 2026-10-10. Target: exact complex DFT on arbitrary fresh inputs. **Status: exact necessary rank obstructions; no new FFT factorization, no arithmetic speedup.**

This package extends the archived `fft_parity_affine_depth_no_go_2026-10-10.zip`. The imported mathematics is identified below. Any claim of research priority requires an independent literature review.

## Model

Let `N=2^k`, `F_N[r,c]=ζ_N^(rc)`. An order-`r` two-sided quasiseparable stage has every strictly lower/upper crossing block of rank at most `r`, and admits a two-directional scan representation with `r` channels per direction. We test

`M = S_t P_{t-1} S_{t-1} ... P_1 S_1`,

where the permutations are explicitly oriented as `x_next[j] = y_previous[P_j[j]]`. The stage parameters are arbitrary complex numbers, may be zero, and the stages need not be invertible.

## Theorem A: exact rank oracle for arbitrary output sets

Let `h|N`, `d=N/h`, and let `E ⊂ {0,...,h-1}` have cardinality `m`. Suppose that the `h×m` Fourier matrix restricted to its columns `E` is **full spark**: every selection of `m` distinct rows is nonsingular. Put

`C = { e + h*j : e∈E, 0<=j<d }`.

For any set `R⊂{0,...,N-1}` of distinct output indices define `n_ρ = |{r∈R : r=ρ (mod d)}|`. Then

> **rank_C F_N[R,C] = Σ_(ρ=0)^(d-1) min(n_ρ,m).**

**Proof.** Write `r=ρ+dℓ`, `c=e+hj`. Then

`ζ_N^(rc) = ζ_N^(ρe) ζ_h^(ℓe) ζ_d^(ρj)`.

Apply the invertible length-`d` Fourier change of basis across the `j` index. This block-diagonalizes the restricted transform by `ρ`. In block `ρ`, the surviving row indices are the `n_ρ` distinct values of `ℓ`, and the column matrix equals the corresponding restriction of `F_h[:,E]`, times invertible nonzero diagonal scalings. Full spark gives rank `min(n_ρ,m)`. Summing independent blocks proves the formula. QED.

For `h=2^a`, `m=h/2`, any transversal `E={s+m e_s : 0<=s<m, e_s∈{0,1}}` is a **universal sampling set** by the prime-power balanced-residue criterion of Osgood, Siripuram, and Wu (2012). The full-spark premise is therefore satisfied. This imported sampling theorem is NOT claimed as a new result.

In particular, if `a` is a nonzero `k`-bit parity mask with highest bit position `u`, the halfset `C_a={x: parity(a & x)=0}` repeats with period `h=2^(u+1)`, and its within-period set is a transversal modulo `h/2`. The formula therefore applies to `C_a` and **arbitrary** `R`.

**Useful corollaries for arbitrary `R` of size `N/2`:**

- `rank F_N[R,C_a] ≥ N/4`, since each residue class contains at most `h` indices and `min(n_ρ,h/2)≥n_ρ/2`.
- If the highest bit of `a` is `k-1`, `h=N,d=1`, giving **rank exactly `N/2` for every such `R`**.
- The archived parity-vs-parity rank formula follows when `R` is another parity halfset; it is rechecked in `test_new.py`.

The exact rank oracle never rounds complex numbers and requires no Gaussian elimination. The modular ranks in tests are independent *controls*, not substitutes for this proof.

## Theorem B: moving-partition obstruction works with arbitrary nonlinear shuffles

Let `A_1` be any input halfset, transported through stage shuffles to `A_j` (`A_(j+1) = P_j^{-1}(A_j)`). Let `d(A)` count the adjacent membership changes along `0,...,N-1`. Then

`rank M[A_t^c,A_1] ≤ Σ_j r_j d(A_j)`.

**Proof.** Every `S_j[A_j^c,A_j]` can communicate across at most `r_j` forward/backward scan channels at each boundary of `A_j`. The rank of the restricted product is at most the sum of its crossing ranks (insert each coordinate projection and telescope). Permutations transport the partitions exactly and cost zero arithmetic in this rank inequality. No regular generator chart or invertibility is needed. QED.

Take `A_1=C_a`, a parity halfset. Theorem A supplies the exact Fourier rank for the arbitrary output subset `A_t^c`, even when `P_j` are nonlinear. If the Fourier rank exceeds the capacity sum, **there is no exact factorization**.

## Exact nonlinear 256-point obstruction

In `EXACT_CERTIFICATES.json`, three interstage affine shuffles are given by their eight GF(2) basis columns (and zero affine offset). The third shuffle is modified by the following five Toffoli gates, applied in order as `P <- P ∘ gate`:

1. controls `(2,7)`, target `6`
2. controls `(3,6)`, target `2`
3. controls `(2,4)`, target `3`
4. controls `(3,4)`, target `2`
5. controls `(2,3)`, target `4`

All are individually reversible nonlinear permutations of 8-bit indices. The resulting third shuffle is genuinely nonaffine: its values disagree at 160 of 256 arguments with the GF(2)-linear map inferred from its action on `0,1,2,4,...,128`.

For `a=112` (`0b01110000`), the transported partitions have crossing counts

`[11,53,43,17]`, total **124**. The final output set `R` has 128 elements, 64 even and 64 odd. Here `h=128`, `d=2`, `m=64`, so Theorem A gives

`rank_C F_256[R,C_112] = min(64,64)+min(64,64)=128`.

This gives **128 > 124**, excluding the exact four-stage product for all complex generator coefficients. An independent 8,176-node scan-network minimum-cut computation equals **124**. The 128×128 Fourier submatrix has rank 128 independently in both finite fields GF(12289) and GF(65537). The characteristic-zero theorem above is the logical proof; the modular ranks corroborate it.

**Scope:** the *original* affine shuffle layout still has no contradiction from the tested rank oracles; after the five Toffoli modifications it is a **different** architecture. The original layout's coefficient compatibility is unresolved.

## Theorem C: robustness under low-rank nonlinear permutation edits

Fix stage matrices `S_j`. Let `P_j` be arbitrary permutations and `A_j` any baseline permutations of equal length. Write `M` for the product using `P_j`, and `M_0` for the product with `A_j`. By expanding differences one shuffle at a time and using `rank(XYZ)≤rank(Y)`, we obtain

`rank(M-M_0) ≤ H := Σ_j rank(P_j-A_j)`.

Therefore for any selected output/input sets `R,C`,

`rank M[R,C] ≤ rank M_0[R,C] + H`.

For actual permutation matrices, `rank(P-A) = N - c(A^{-1}P)`, where `c` counts *all* cycles including fixed points. The kernel of `I-A^{-1}P` consists precisely of vectors constant on each cycle, over any field. This yields an exact integer rank without forming a matrix.

An `m`-control reversible NOT on `k` bits exchanges `N/2^(m+1)` disjoint pairs and fixes all other indices. Thus

`rank(P_MCNOT-I)=N/2^(m+1)`.

In particular, one 2-control Toffoli has edit rank `N/8`. Interleaving any number of GF(2)-affine gates with such nonlinear gates cannot increase the edit rank beyond the sum of the nonlinear-gate edit ranks: telescope products and use invariance of rank under permutation multiplication.

### Robust affine-depth obstruction

Assume all baseline `A_j` are arbitrary invertible GF(2)-affine permutations. Let stage orders be `r_1,...,r_t`. Choose integers `q_j≥1` satisfying `Σ q_j < k`. As in the previous archive, GF(2) linear algebra provides a nonzero input parity mask whose transported baseline stage masks have all of their bottom `q_j` bits zero. At stage `j` its boundary count is ≤ `N/2^q_j - 1`.

Let `H=Σrank(P_j-A_j)`. If

> **Σ_j r_j (N/2^q_j - 1) + H < N/4,**

no product using the actual (possibly nonlinear) `P_j` can equal the Fourier transform. Theorem A gives Fourier rank ≥N/4 even with an independently arbitrary output-coordinate permutation. This conclusion includes singular and zero-generator stages.

Suppose the actual shuffles are constructed from binary-affine gates plus nonlinear controlled NOTs with control counts `m_1,...,m_g`. We may take

`H ≤ N δ`, where `δ=Σ_ℓ 2^(-m_ℓ-1)`.

For uniform stage rank `r` and stage count `t`, choose `q=ceil(log2(8rt))`. If `k>tq` and `δ≤1/8`, then

`rt(N/2^q - 1) + Nδ ≤ N/8 - rt + N/8 < N/4`.

**Corollary:** one Toffoli (two controls, `δ=1/8`) anywhere amid otherwise binary-affine interstage routing cannot enable a fixed-depth, fixed-rank exact Fourier factorization for all sufficiently large powers of two. E.g. `t=4,r=1`, `q=5`, impossible at every `k≥21`, i.e. `N≥2^21`. This does NOT cover two generic Toffoli gates uniformly, all arbitrary nonaffine permutations, or unrestricted FFT circuits.

### Exact per-layout two-Toffoli certificates

The archived seeded GF(2) basis columns at `N=2048,4096` give parity witnesses of ranks 1024 and 2048 and baseline scan capacities 390 and 887. Replacing any two of their affine shuffles by affine-mixers interleaved with **one two-control Toffoli apiece** changes the full network by rank at most `2N/8=N/4`.

| N | Fourier rank | Baseline cap | H (two Toffolis) | Edited network cap | Strict gap |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2,048 | 1,024 | 390 | 512 | 902 | 122 |
| 4,096 | 2,048 | 887 | 1,024 | 1,911 | 137 |

These conclusions are for **those particular affine baselines**, not all 4-stage shuffles. Each edit's exact rank is checked from permutation cycles.

## Positive controls, runtime model, and limitations

A random four-stage product with independent upper/lower generators and two Toffoli-edited shuffles was explicitly evaluated over GF(12289) and GF(65537) at `N=64`. Its chosen Fourier-corner block has rank 19; the baseline block has rank 4; the whole-matrix perturbation has rank 16; the Fourier block has rank 32. These satisfy `19≤4+16<32`. The exact calculations check orientations and zero-case safety.

The rank oracles and certificates do not synthesize stage coefficients. No exact global-scan DFT implementation with sub-`N log N` charged arithmetic has been found. An arbitrary permutation itself can be executed in `O(N)` index accesses, so an Ω(N) edit-rank budget is *not* an arithmetic lower bound. That caveat matters: the theorem only rejects certain ways of obtaining cheap mixing.
