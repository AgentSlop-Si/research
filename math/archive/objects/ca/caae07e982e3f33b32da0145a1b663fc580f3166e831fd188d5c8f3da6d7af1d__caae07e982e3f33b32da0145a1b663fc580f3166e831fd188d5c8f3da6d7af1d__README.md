# Shared-graph research continuation

9 October 2026. This pass adds exact dense Fourier-query reuse, automatically
detected structured-input transforms, fused noncommuting correction sums,
multiple-parent integer products, changing feedback couplings, and an
arbitrary-length exact multiplication backend.

**The independent exact single-input goal remains unresolved:** no result
here gives o(N log N) for every fresh arbitrary Fourier input, with all N
outputs and preparation charged. The constructive results below have explicit
batch, operator, or input-structure promises. They are proved and executable;
they are not claims of mathematical priority for the underlying classical
techniques.

## Full Fourier query result, with an actual same-target comparison

For F[k,j]=q^(kj), define the upper-bidiagonal E by

    E[i,i]=-q^i,
    E[i,i+1]=-q^i(q^(i+1)-1),

and the cyclic two-band J by

    (Jy)_i=(q^(-i-1)-1)y_(i+1)-q^(-i-1)y_(i+2).

Direct finite algebra gives F E=J F. For a nonsingular supplied shift lambda,

    F(E-lambda I)^-1 x=(J-lambda I)^-1 F x.

Both inverses have linear-work solvers. The proposed relation is checked on
all input coordinates before the cached output is used. Given an arbitrary
fresh query, manufacturing its anchor input does not manufacture that anchor's
Fourier output. The general related-query route pays its first FFT.

There is also a cold structural fast path. Discover u=(E-lambda I)x. If u has
s nonzeros, its full Fourier vector costs O(Ns), and

    F x=(J-lambda I)^-1 F u

costs O(N(s+1)) including preparation, residual discovery, phase construction
and all outputs. This requires no FFT anchor. A final-coordinate impulse u
produces a fully dense x, so the accepted class includes fresh dense inputs.
Fixed s gives linear exact field work. A supplied support cap bounds the
attempt; unstructured residuals decline after paying discovery. The underlying
plan uses a nonzero lambda with lambda^N!=1 and a supplied primitive Nth root.
The 40 accepted and 30 declined supplied-shift audit cases check every accepted
output against the full Fourier matrix in exact fields. This does not extend
the result to arbitrary dense inputs or establish a floating-point or bit-cost
bound.

`detected_fourier.py` removes the need to supply a favorable lambda. If the
input has m>2s nonzero entries and an admissible shift has at most s defects,
the exact ratios (E x)_i/x_i have that shift as a strict majority. A
Boyer-Moore scan compares ratios by cross multiplication, computes one
candidate, rejects excluded shifts, and verifies the entire residual. This
costs O(N); candidate selection alone is never accepted as a certificate.
When m<=2s, direct sparse Fourier evaluation already costs O(Ns). Together
these branches recognize and evaluate the union of these sparse and
low-defect input families in O(N(s+1)) cold work, without an FFT anchor or
unpaid shift search. Unrelated dense inputs still require a general fallback.
This is an adaptive exact-field algorithm, including paid input-dependent
inversions. Its present constants lose in the tested cold comparisons: at
N=8192 it uses 344,080 field operations versus 167,949 for the ordinary FFT.
The result is a linear asymptotic bound for the recognized family, not a
measured practical speedup. The detector audit checks 51 exact accepted
outputs, 40 declines and four invalid-argument controls; a separate reviewer
also checks eight edge cases and independently counts every field call.

For N=8192 and sixteen additional related queries, the graph used
**2,306,004 counted field operations versus 3,190,765** for separate ordinary
radix-two FFTs. Both workloads pay their first FFT, common query generation,
shared setup and every output. The graph additionally pays full relation
verification. Every query changed all 8192 coordinates. The N=128 case loses
on constants: 34,830 versus 30,247. These are exact arithmetic ledgers, not
CPU timings or a claim against the best constant-optimized FFT.

The method uses only a supplied primitive Nth root and exact fields. Its
132-basis-vector, 30-mixed-vector small audit includes characteristic-zero
cyclotomic fields; larger comparisons use a prime field. The original
H_q(C)-specific rational family remains included as a separate verified result.

## Why cross-term fusion matters

The previous rank budget did not prohibit fusion; it only charged programs
that separately materialize N-vector results for many corrections. We now
have a concrete exact example that avoids those passes. Write

    L=C^(N-1)=a b^T,  Q=diag(q^i).

Then

    sum_(j=0)^(N-1) Q^j L Q^j
        = N diag(a) R_neg diag(b),

where R_neg maps index i to -i modulo N. N rank-one terms become one weighted
permutation. More generally, weights given by t Fourier modes produce t
weighted permutations in O(tN), with the supplied weight description and
preparation charged. Nonzero single-mode maps have full rank N. Literal
weights on only s orbit terms instead use a pruned FFT and its transpose in
O(N(1+log(s+1))). These are classical Fourier orthogonality/pruning mechanisms
applied to the exact correction graph, not a new fast general DFT theorem.

The more general noncommuting tail sum shares a small projected input window,
its intermediate updates and one final decode. Its README proves the support
conditions and full cost. The same scalar-weighted highest-power orbit family
does not contain H_q(C), so the successful fusion is a component rather than
a solution of the full middle operator.

## Multiple-parent reuse handles a missed family

If R_i=A_i-2A_(i-1)+A_(i-2) is verified sparse, then

    A_i B=2(A_(i-1)B)-A_(i-2)B+R_i B.

Both coordinate and canonical numeric residual representations are checked.
The audit includes the exact same targets as the previous one-parent planner:
every first difference is dense and that planner chooses eight fresh products;
the new secant schedule uses two seed products plus linear digit work. Because
the seed backends differ, their operation ledgers are not divided into a
misleading speed ratio. Two changing affine operands produce a quadratic
product sequence, with three paid seeds; the mixed product term is retained.
Unrelated controls pay all fresh products plus unsuccessful discovery.

## Exact-goal attempts that did not close the gap

`fft_branch_fusion_research` derives finite H-specific even/odd relations,
including E(D)^2-D O(D)^2=H_(q^2)(D). It checks the final coefficient that
vanishes on nilpotent D but not on a cyclic shift. The direct implementation
needs four odd-kernel calls and is slower than the previous three-call split.
A determinant certificate excludes one precise two-identical-kernel
factorization; it does not exclude all two-call algorithms or distinct kernels.

`fft_exact_recovery_research` propagates the strong surrogate error through
forward transforms, pointwise multiplication, inverse transformation and
integer rounding. Under that particular worst-case certificate, any nonempty
integer residual tail requests too many samples and triggers the exact-input
bypass. This does not prove every approximation/refinement approach impossible.
The useful positive result there is the separately implemented exact structural
fast path described above.

The next unresolved constructive step is a representation of the complete
H_q(C) that admits the demonstrated kind of shared decoding, or a branch
factorization using genuinely cheaper distinct kernels. Dense related-query
reuse alone cannot supply a missing arbitrary-input anchor.

## Verification, preservation, and reproduction

Run `python3 run_all.py`. It uses Python's standard library, executes each new
audit in its own process, and records logs plus `verification_summary.json`.
No old timing benchmark is rerun. The exact proof statements and independent
reviews are included alongside the code; tests check implementations rather
than establish asymptotic claims by extrapolation.

The new feedback package's local rank-one helper removes an unnecessary sort
of source indices. Its independent review caught the resulting setup-cost
issue. Older checkpoint files remain unchanged; their compiler setup needs
the additional sorting cost when source indices are arbitrarily ordered and
their count grows. This change preserves circuit semantics and gate counts.

The six earlier helper folders are dependencies and comparison code, with
historical results retained for provenance. Their old audits are not counted
as new experiments. This continuation follows `fft_reuse_frontier_20261009.zip`,
SHA-256 `bb691b0b3d8349c8a820cdd6f0afd36b91df0708154b7ea6963c5a4606448983`.
The original supplied materials are preserved in that checkpoint. This archive
adds the new work and required executable dependencies; it contains no native
binaries or Python cache files. `MANIFEST.json` fingerprints every portable file.

Field-element costs, byte-digit costs, seed multiplication calls, bit bounds
and timings are different models. Component reports state which is used and
charge preparation and all output work. No priority claim is made for finite
differences, low-rank inverse updates, cyclic-band solving, pruned FFTs,
root-of-unity filtering, or repdigit multiplication identities. External
Swapnil/#130 routes have not been imported as our independent result.
