# Prior-art and claim boundary — v21

## Classical ingredients

1. **Bluestein linear-filtering DFT.** L. I. Bluestein, “A Linear Filtering Approach to the Computation of Discrete Fourier Transform,” *IEEE Transactions on Audio and Electroacoustics* 18(4), 451–455 (1970), DOI `10.1109/TAU.1970.1162132`. It rewrites the DFT as a quadratic-chirp filtering/convolution problem and uses an FFT for `O(N log N)` work.
2. **Chirp z-transform.** L. R. Rabiner, R. W. Schafer, and C. M. Rader, “The Chirp z-Transform Algorithm and Its Application,” *Bell System Technical Journal* 48 (1969), 1249–1292.
3. **Finite Weyl/Heisenberg operators and quadratic Gauss sums.** Shift/modulation operators, their projective commutation relations, and the fact that the DFT of a nondegenerate quadratic chirp is another full-support quadratic chirp are classical.
4. **Roots-of-unity filtering and Vandermonde determinants** are standard algebraic tools.

## Project-specific synthesis

The package derives and checks the following combination inside the user's Green-resolvent program:

- generic completeness of all `h!=0` Weyl–Green character packets for arbitrary off-diagonal matrix data;
- exact reduction of a diagonally gauged Fourier matrix to `N-1` Green resolvents `G_(h,-h)`;
- proof that the Fourier-compatible family lies on one common Weyl line;
- identification of its fused polynomial as the Bluestein chirp convolution;
- a two-nonzero-per-column gate for separately evaluated selected character packets.

A limited search did not establish whether the generic packet-basis theorem appears in prior literature. **No novelty or priority claim is made.** The decisive algorithmic collapse is explicitly classical Bluestein territory.
