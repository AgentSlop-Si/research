# Exact certificates: moving Fourier partitions and twisted-bit-reversal depth

**Date:** 2026-10-10  
**Status:** Characteristic-zero mathematical arguments plus exact modular verification  
**Scope:** Products of quasiseparable matrices with fixed interstage coordinate shuffles; **not** unrestricted FFT algorithms, and no claim of original priority.

## 1. The moving-partition rank theorem

Let `N >= 2`, let `F_N[i,j] = omega^(ij)` for a primitive complex N-th root `omega`, and suppose

\[
F_N=S_t P_{t-1}S_{t-1}\cdots P_1S_1,
\]

with upper and lower quasiseparable cut ranks of `S_i` at most `r_i`. We represent each permutation by `pi_i` such that a signal in old coordinate `pi_i(j)` enters new coordinate `j`.

For **any** input subset `A_0`, set

\[
A_{i}=\pi_i^{-1}(A_{i-1}),\qquad i=1,\ldots,t-1.
\]

Define the boundary count in the natural coordinate order by

\[
d(A)=\#\{0\le j<N-1:\mathbf1_A(j)\ne\mathbf1_A(j+1)\}.
\]

**THEOREM (arbitrary interstage permutations).** Every such factorization must satisfy

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}\,F_N[A_{t-1}^c,A_0]
\ \le\ \sum_{i=1}^{t}r_i\,d(A_{i-1}).}\tag{1}
\]

No generator chart, nonzero parameter, stage invertibility, bound on coefficient magnitude, or invariance of the partition is assumed.

**Proof.** For any order-r quasiseparable stage S, the off-group block `S[A^c,A]` has rank at most `r d(A)`: split the row subset `A^c` into consecutive runs. Columns in A lie strictly left or right of each run, and each of these subblocks is contained in a quasiseparable cut block of rank at most r. Across all runs the count of left/right contributions equals the number of boundaries. Repeat with the sets swapped if needed.

Now retain only the columns originally in `A_0`. At the first stage the output rows in `A_0^c` have rank at most `r_1 d(A_0)`. After the first permutation, their rows map into `A_1^c`, with unchanged rank. At the next stage, the output in `A_1^c` is the sum of (a) signals crossing from `A_1`, whose rank is at most `r_2d(A_1)`, and (b) signals already in `A_1^c`, whose rank is bounded by the previous total. Iterate. The final off-group block has rank at most the right side of (1). QED.

### Consecutive Fourier input columns

If `A_0={a,a+1,...,a+M-1}` is an interval, the selected Fourier block has rows `A_{t-1}^c`, which contain `N-M` distinct output indices. Its entries, after nonzero row rescaling, are consecutive powers `1,z,z^2,...,z^(M-1)` of distinct roots `z=omega^i`. A Vandermonde minor proves

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}\,F_N[A_{t-1}^c,A_0]=\min(M,N-M).}\tag{2}
\]

Consequently, for **every arbitrary interstage permutation program**, one must have

\[
\boxed{\min(M,N-M)\le\sum_i r_i d(A_{i-1})}\tag{3}
\]

for every consecutive input interval of length M. In particular, taking the first N/2 inputs gives a full-rank block. This result is valid over C for any N, not just powers of two.

### Layout interaction graph

Pull all stage-coordinate paths back into the original input coordinate labels. Each stage contributes a Hamiltonian path whose consecutive vertices are neighboring positions in its current layout. Assign its edges weight `r_i`. Then the sum on the right of (1) equals the graph's cut weight for the initial subset `A_0`. This converts the search for cheap moving partitions into a weighted multigraph cut search. A small cut is a **necessary-condition obstruction**, not a proof of general matrix rigidity.

## 2. Exact binary-parity boundaries

For `N=2^k` and nonzero k-bit mask `m`, define `A(m)={j: popcount(j&m) even}`. The transition from j to j+1 toggles the first `s+1` low bits if j has exactly s trailing 1s. Thus

\[
\boxed{d_k(m)=\sum_{s=0}^{k-1}2^{k-1-s}
 \left[\operatorname{popcount}(m\mathbin{\&}(2^{s+1}-1))\text{ is odd}\right].}\tag{4}
\]

For a single bit `m=2^b`, `d_k(m)=2^{k-b}-1`. A GF(2)-linear bit transformation y=Mx pulls this parity mask back to `M^T m`; an affine offset may change the selected parity sign but cannot change the boundary count.

`test_gate.py` exhaustively verifies (4) at every nonzero mask through k=10 and checks random exact stage products against (1).

## 3. Local binary mixing needs logarithmic depth

Assume `N=2^k` and each interstage coordinate permutation comes from an invertible affine binary transformation `y=M_s x+b_s`, with `M_s[i,j]=0` whenever `j<i-c`, where c is a fixed positive integer. In words, output bit i may depend on input bit positions no more than c below i; dependencies on higher positions are allowed.

Start with the first half of input indices, i.e. the parity mask `2^(k-1)`. After s shuffles, the pulled-back mask is supported in bit positions at least `k-1-cs` (prove by induction using `M_s^T`). Formula (4) implies

\[
d_s\le 2^{cs+1}-1.
\]

If every stage has quasiseparable order at most r, the exact Vandermonde property in (2) gives

\[
\boxed{2^{k-1}\le
r\left(\frac{2(2^{ct}-1)}{2^c-1}-t\right).}\tag{5}
\]

For fixed c,r, this forces `t=Omega(k/c)=Omega(log N)`. At c=r=1, substituting `t=k-2` violates (5), so **t >= k-1** is necessary.

This applies to changing XOR mixers as well as bit-position permutations whenever the above locality condition holds. It does **not** cover arbitrary global bit reversals.

**Separate cyclic bit-rotation corollary.** For repeated `rotl` shuffles, the *specific ancestral top-bit mask* moves down by one bit per stage until it wraps, even though the permutation as a whole is not locally banded. While t<k, the stage boundaries are `1,3,7,15,...,2^t-1`; hence t>=k-1 is necessary for order-one stages.

## 4. Twisted bit reversal needs Omega(sqrt N) stages

Let `T(x)=rotl(bitrev(x))` on k-bit coordinates, and repeat T between t stages. On bit **positions**, the induced permutation is the reflection `j -> -j (mod k)`.

**Even k=2m.** This reflection fixes the middle bit m. The parity mask `a=2^m` is invariant, and

\[
d_k(a)=2^m-1.
\]

The corresponding half-by-half Fourier block has rank N/2. To show this over C, write `N=4q^2` with `q=2^(m-1)`, and index rows/columns as

\[
i=4qz+uq+a_0,\quad j=4qw+vq+b_0,\quad0\le z,w,a_0,b_0<q.
\]

For the row group with bit m equal to 1, `u in {2,3}`; for the column group with bit m equal to 0, `v in {0,1}`. Applying invertible q-point Fourier changes of basis in the z and w variables leaves q² independent 2-by-2 matrices. Up to invertible diagonal scalings each is the 4th-root-of-unity matrix `eta^(u v)`, with eta primitive fourth root and u=2,3 and v=0,1. Its determinant is a nonzero multiple of `eta-1`. All N/2 dimensions survive.

Thus

\[
\boxed{\sum_i r_i\ge\frac{2^{2m-1}}{2^m-1}.}
\]

For order-one stages, the integer depth condition is

\[
\boxed{t\ge2^{m-1}+1.}\tag{6}
\]

**Odd k=2m+1.** The same reflection swaps bit positions m and m+1. The parity mask `a=2^m+2^(m+1)` is invariant and has `d_k(a)=2^m`.

Set `L=2^m`, `q=L/2`, and `N=2L²=8q²`. Group membership is the parity of the two central bits. Index

\[
i=4Lz+uL+a_0,\quad j=4Lw+vL+b_0,
\quad 0\le z,w<q,\quad0\le a_0,b_0<L.
\]

For output rows in the odd-parity group, `u in {1,2}`; for input columns in the even-parity group, `v in {0,3}`. Invertible q-point Fourier changes of basis in z,w isolate the residues `a_0 mod q` and `b_0 mod q`. Each resulting block has dimensions four by four. Write `a_0=a'+epsilon_a q`, `b_0=b'+epsilon_b q`. After nonzero row/column scaling its entries are

\[
\zeta_8^{XY},\qquad X=2u+\epsilon_a\in\{2,3,4,5\},\quad
Y=2v+\epsilon_b\in\{0,1,6,7\},
\]

where `zeta_8=omega^(q²)` is a primitive 8th root. Since X=2,3,4,5 are four consecutive exponents and the nodes `zeta_8^Y` are distinct, this is a 4-by-4 **Vandermonde matrix** after nonzero column rescaling. Every block is invertible; the full Fourier half-block has rank N/2.

Therefore the necessary order-one depth is

\[
\boxed{t\ge2^m.}\tag{7}
\]

**Theorem:** repeated twisted bit reversal needs `Omega(sqrt N)` order-one quasiseparable stages for arbitrary exact complex Fourier transforms. Four stages fail at every power of two `N>=64`. The rank-sum versions of (6),(7) apply to nonuniform stage orders; the argument also permits intermediate permutations preserving or swapping the indicated invariant parity partition.

These statements do not exclude all other cross-partition shuffles.

## 5. Characteristic-zero meaning of modular nonzero determinants

`EXACT_CERTIFICATES.json` records 13 separate finite-size obstructions. Each selected Fourier submatrix has size N/2-by-N/2, with entries in the cyclotomic integer ring `Z[zeta_N]`. For a prime `p=1 mod N` and primitive N-th root `w in F_p`, the substitution `zeta_N -> w` gives a ring homomorphism. If the specialized determinant is **nonzero modulo p**, its determinant cannot be zero over the complex numbers. This is a logically valid way to certify nonvanishing (unlike using finite-field nonexistence to claim no complex solution).

Every recorded determinant is nonzero modulo **both** 12,289 and 65,537. `verify_certificates.py` recomputes stage boundaries directly from sets and checks both independent field ranks. The network min-cut oracle additionally matched the moving-partition capacity for the six representative 128/256-point instances tested.

**Caveats.** These are restricted-architecture rank barriers and finite-size certificates. They establish no new o(N log N) Fourier arithmetic upper bound, no linear-time FFT, and no universal lower bound against arbitrary linear circuits. Similar cut/rank techniques have appeared in the literature; priority has not been established. Relevant existing work includes Delvaux and Van Barel, *Rank-deficient submatrices of Fourier matrices* (Linear Algebra and its Applications, 2008, DOI: 10.1016/j.laa.2008.04.043), and butterfly-structured transform factorization work (Dao et al., *Learning Fast Algorithms for Linear Transforms Using Butterfly Factorizations*, 2019).
