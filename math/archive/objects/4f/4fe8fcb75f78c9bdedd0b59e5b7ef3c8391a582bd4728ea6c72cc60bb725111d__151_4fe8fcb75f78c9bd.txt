Cyclic block displacement and its exact rank
October 9 2026

Let z_1,...,z_k be distinct nonzero field elements, B>=1, and V[r,s]=z_s^r for 0<=r<B. Let D=diag(z_s), and let C be the cyclic left shift on B rows, so (CV)[r,s]=V[(r+1) mod B,s].

For 1<=t<=B, the defect R_t=VD^t-C^t V has entries
  R_t[r,s]=0,                              r+t<B;
  R_t[r,s]=(z_s^B-1) z_s^(r+t-B),          r+t>=B.

The bottom t rows form a t-by-k Vandermonde matrix times diag(z_s^B-1). If all z_s^B!=1, its rank is min(t,k). More generally, delete resonant columns; its rank is min(t,number of nonresonant nodes).

At prime length N, for 1<=B<N, every nontrivial Nth root is nonresonant. Thus a one-sample shift has rank-one defect, while a whole-block jump t=B has rank k when B>=k and the support excludes frequency zero. If zero is present, its constant contribution can be peeled off and the remaining statement applies.

This directly tests the proposed reuse mechanism: cheap one-step displacement does not remain rank-one under a block jump. It DOES NOT imply that applying the resulting full-rank map requires k squared operations, nor that an O(N) Fourier algorithm is impossible. Full-rank maps can have very fast circuits. The result only invalidates keeping a fixed number of rank-one correction directions in this specific update representation.

The accompanying independently written check_displacement.py exhaustively tests all nonempty subsets of the nonzero frequencies for prime N=5,7,11, every B=1,...,N-1 and t=1,...,B. Exact fields are respectively F_11, F_29 and F_23. All 57,738 entry-identity and rank checks passed. These finite checks supplement the proof; they are not timing benchmarks or an asymptotic argument.

No novelty claim is made for this elementary Vandermonde identity.
