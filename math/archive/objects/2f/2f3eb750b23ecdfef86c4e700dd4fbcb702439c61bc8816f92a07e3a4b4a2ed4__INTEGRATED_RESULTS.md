## Results worth retaining

1. **Restricted odd-cycle chirp classification, independently checked.** With a free diagonal term, the legal simple scalar-resolvent bank minimum is m or m+1 for N=2m+1. The exact resonance gcd is classified for every odd N; prime-order squarefreeness is proved and finite odd orders through 1001 have exact checked evidence. A uniform composite-order squarefreeness proof is still absent. The resulting application has quadratic cost and does not produce a faster FFT.

2. **Priority narrowed.** The annihilator formula is precisely a classical partial-theta Padé/Rogers–Szegő specialization. The possibly distinctive part is the half-degree resonance and ordinary-squarefreeness theorem, not a new polynomial or Padé formula. Two close original papers remain insufficiently inspected in full. A short mathematical note remains a conditional candidate, not a verified novelty claim.

3. **Young-feedback obstruction, independently checked.** Exposing the specified outputs for independent arbitrary jets requires Omega(N log N) bounded-coefficient linear work, with a matching charged construction. The input independence assumption is essential. This result cannot be transferred unchanged to jets constrained by a single N-dimensional source. The volume method is classical; the packet selection is the project-specific component.

4. **F8 restricted factorization work.** The half-anchor diagonal orbit is excluded for the fixed support pair, and the adjacent-anchor guarded polynomial interface is exact. The new adjacent obstruction is supported by an explicit unit-ideal combination; all 57 proof nodes, 20 resultants and their canceled factors have now passed independent exact checking. Its four-factor nonzero guard leaves boundary strata open. No conclusion about all factorizations or all lengths follows.

5. **Composite chirp route clarified.** A concrete repeated-eigenvalue example shows that the sector argument alone cannot prove simplicity. Characteristic-two reduction remains a promising precise subproblem; finite experiments are not a uniform proof.
