# Research report — From one Fourier rank gate to an exact outer-stage stability certificate

**Date:** October 10, 2026  
**Target:** A new exact o(N log N) Fourier algorithm on arbitrary fresh inputs, not a replay of prior kappa/CrocSwap constructions.  
**Research outcome:** Stronger necessary coefficient constraints for a fixed N=256 global-scan architecture; no valid factorization discovered.

## Starting point

The previous checkpoint gave 15 ordinary-matrix rank inequalities under diagonal-stage replacement and a single selected middle-product rank-16 gate. Those rank inequalities do not imply a scan arithmetic lower bound, because a quasiseparable matrix may have high ordinary rank yet use O(N) operations. The task here is to attack **the actual Fourier coefficient constraints**.

## Main finding A: simultaneous equations from 32 parity masks

The previous mask 64 was not unique. For each nonzero parity mask `m` we choose the 128 input columns and 128 output rows transported through the **unchanged saved middle permutation**. We compute boundary count `b_m`, a coefficient-independent upper bound on the rank of the two middle stages.

Exactly **32 masks have b_m < 128**. The corresponding normalized Fourier blocks, with the saved outer shuffles and identity outer inverse stages, all have exact complex rank 128. This produces 32 simultaneous algebraic constraints on unknown outer stages C4,C1; no generator divisions are used. The top seven boundaries are 16,47,49,52,53,59,61. All 32 Fourier ranks were independently verified mod two primes (64 checks) and synthetic two-stage controls passed.

Each rank constraint is equivalently the vanishing of **all (b_m+1)-minors** of the corresponding outer-transformed Fourier block. Even testing all of them is only a necessary condition.

## Main finding B: nontrivial singular-value gap, certified over characteristic zero

Rather than testing approximate coefficients numerically, we study how far the original Fourier matrix lies from the rank-16 set in operator norm. For the mask-64 block, 128×128 with entries each of modulus 1/16, the Frobenius/energy argument alone yields `sigma_17 >= sqrt(3/7) ~= 0.654654`.

An **exact cyclotomic fourth-moment calculation** improves this to:

`boxed: sigma_17 > 3 sqrt(2)/5 ~= 0.848528`.

The verifier computes pair-difference counts of the selected Fourier rows and columns, reduces the moment to 128 integer cyclotomic coefficients, then certifies the strict rational inequality `tr((H*H)^2)>1264/25=50.56`. The cosine bounds use Machin's identity for pi, alternating arctangent series, and rational Taylor remainder bounds—no floating-point proof step. Numeric SVD gives an informative `sigma_17 ~= 0.924760` but is not invoked in the theorem.

## Main finding C: quantitative outer-stage coefficient obstruction

For normalized U=F/16, factor U as S4 P3 S3 P2 S2 P1 S1, and put C4=S4^-1, C1=S1^-1. Then every diagonal-unitary baseline pair D4,D1 satisfies

`||C4-D4||*||C1|| + ||C1-D1|| > 3 sqrt(2)/5`.

Therefore both inverse outer stages cannot lie within spectral-norm distance `epsilon<=sqrt(1+3sqrt(2)/5)-1 ~=0.359606` of diagonal unitaries. An approximate-output corollary yields an explicit positive lower bound on the normalized FFT operator error for near-diagonal outer inverses. The proof also covers arbitrary nonsingular diagonal baselines after accounting for their smallest selected diagonal magnitudes.

This is a **genuine complex coefficient restriction**, not a graphical-capacity restriction. It does not assert that a fully non-diagonal quasiseparable C4,C1 cannot satisfy the Fourier factorization.

## What remains unsolved

1. Whether the **32 simultaneous rank equations** have a solution in the full arbitrary zero-generator two-sided quasiseparable inverse families C4,C1.
2. Whether such a solution could additionally factor the entire middle matrix as S3 P2 S2.
3. Whether any finite-size solution extends constructively to all N and beats N log N *after every scalar, precomputation, and index operation is charged*.

A necessary next proof gate is an **exact algebraic feasibility decision** beyond neighborhoods of diagonals. The 32 masks provide multiple constraints for elimination; the rational spectral certificate can prune approximate-search branches rigorously. We should not spend more time treating rank-feasible permutations alone as FFTs.

## Existing mathematical context

Quasiseparable matrices and compact generators are standard in exact structured linear algebra; see C. Pernet, *Computing with quasiseparable matrices* (2016), https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.01246.
