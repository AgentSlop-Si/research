# Exact four-column compatibility gate for a two-stage Fourier factorization

**October 9, 2026 · independently reproducible algebraic research**  
**Status:** New necessary rank certificate, exact characteristic-zero excluded slice, and a codimension bound in the regular chart. **No complete impossibility proof, no new FFT complexity bound.**

## 1. What we resumed

The previous generation studied the fixed eight-point Fourier matrix

\[
F_{ik}=\zeta^{ik},\quad \zeta=e^{2\pi i/8},\qquad
P=(7,0,6,3,4,1,2,5),\qquad F=BPA.
\]

Put \(C=A^{-1}\), so \(B=FCP^{-1}\). Both stages are modeled by **globally separated rank-one generators**: for a stage \(S\),

\[
S_{ij}=d_i\ (i=j),\qquad S_{ij}=U_iW_j\ (i<j),\qquad
S_{ij}=L_iV_j\ (i>j).
\]

This is **narrower than all order-one quasiseparable matrices**. Our previous regular normalization set \(d_i=1\), \(U_0=L_7=1\), and introduced eight variables \((t,h_0,h_1,h_2,h_6,\beta,\gamma_1,\gamma_2)\). The earlier adjacent-column equations \(E_3=E_4=0\) and quartic \(Q=0\) yielded a **31-term necessary cubic**

\[
W(h_0,h_1,h_2,h_6)=0.
\]

The last checkpoint enumerated 4,596 candidates satisfying those three necessary conditions on one nonzero-pivot branch over \(\mathbb F_{17}\). It found no complete valid factorization, but that was not an impossibility theorem over \(\mathbb C\).

## 2. New structural lemma: an 11×9 matrix must be singular

For an arbitrary 8×8 transform \(Q_0\), write \(D=Q_0 C P^{-1}\). For each input-column index \(j\), define three basis vectors

\[
e_j,\qquad u^{<j}_k=\begin{cases}U_k&k<j\\0&k\ge j\end{cases},\qquad
\ell^{>j}_k=\begin{cases}L_k&k>j\\0&k\le j\end{cases}.
\]

Every column \(C_{:,j}\) is a linear combination of those three vectors, including its diagonal coefficient. Define

\[
Y_j=(Q_0)_{0:4,:}[e_j,u^{<j},\ell^{>j}],\quad j=1,2,5,
\]

as 5×3 matrices. Define \(Y_4\) analogously but with **only the first four rows**, so it is 4×3. Put \(A=Y_1,\ B_2=Y_2,\ C_5=Y_5\). Let \(\eta\in\mathbb K^4\) be the cofactor left-nullvector of \(Y_4\):

\[
\eta_i=(-1)^i\det(Y_4\text{ with row }i\text{ deleted}),\qquad \eta^T Y_4=0.
\]

Construct the following 11×9 matrix:

\[
\boxed{K=\begin{pmatrix}
A&-B_2&0\\ A&0&-C_5\\ \eta^T A_{0:3,:}&0&0
\end{pmatrix}.}
\]

**Theorem (necessary compatibility):** If \(D\) has globally separated upper rank-one generators, then \(\operatorname{rank}K\le8\).

**Proof, including zero columns.** Output columns 5, 6, and 7 correspond to C columns 1, 2, and 5. On output rows 0 through 4, each is a scalar multiple of the same upper-stage row-generator vector. If all three projected columns are nonzero, rescale their nonzero three-coefficient decompositions to the same common vector \(q\). These decompositions produce a nonzero vector in the kernel of the first ten rows of \(K\). Output column 4 is also proportional to \(q\) on rows 0 through 3, and its first-four-row projection belongs to \(\operatorname{col}(Y_4)\). Hence \(\eta^T q_{0:3}=0\), satisfying the last row. If any of the three projected columns is zero, use its nonzero column-coefficient triple in its corresponding block and zero triples in the other blocks; it gives a nontrivial kernel vector for *all eleven rows*. Thus \(K\) is singular in every case. This also covers zero generator entries and zero diagonal entries. □

The lemma is purely algebraic and does not depend on positivity, conditioning, or whether \(Q_0\) is a Fourier transform. It was tested on **200 exactly factorable, invertible positive controls** over primes 12,289 and 65,537, including forced-zero upper columns; the rank bound passed every case. The positive controls reconstruct \(Q_0=D P C^{-1}\) from independently generated separated stages.

## 4. Polynomial lifting: 250 exact terms, degree 8

The new gate can be reduced to a polynomial in just the four structural variables \(h=(h_0,h_1,h_2,h_6)\). Write the prior affine tail equations as

\[
E_3=a(h)t+b(h)\beta+c(h),\qquad
E_4=d(h)t+e(h)\beta+f(h).
\]

Set

\[
\Delta=ae-bd,\quad T=-ce+bf,\quad V=-af+cd,
\qquad t=T/\Delta,\quad\beta=V/\Delta.
\]

The coefficients \(a,d,c,f\) are constant and \(b,e\) are linear in \(h\), hence \(\Delta,T\) are linear and \(V\) is constant. Let \(H_0=h_0,H_1=h_1,H_2=h_2\); extend \(H_3,H_4,H_5\) using the previous three-frequency recurrence, and let \(H_6=h_6\).

On the regular chart \(h_0\Delta T\ne0\), rescale the upper and lower basis directions by \(h_0\) and \(M=(\zeta-1)T\), respectively:

\[
\widehat U=(H_0,H_1,\ldots,H_6,0),\quad
\widehat L_0=0,\quad \widehat L_7=M,
\]

\[
\boxed{\widehat L_k=(\zeta-1)\,[\Delta+V(H_k-h_0)]
+[T-\Delta+V(h_0-1)](\zeta^{-k}-1),\quad 1\le k\le6.}
\]

Direct algebra gives \(\widehat U=h_0U\), \(\widehat L=M L\), preserving column spans. The rescaled 11×9 matrix \(\widehat K\) has **polynomial entries in \(\mathbb Z[\zeta_8][h]\)**. Its selected 9×9 minor, using zero-indexed rows \([0,1,2,5,6,7,8,9,10]\), is

\[
\widehat D(h)=\det\widehat K_{[0,1,2,5,6,7,8,9,10],:}.
\]

We expanded the determinant **exactly**, obtaining **250 terms, total degree 8**. A block-column expansion factors it:

\[
\boxed{\widehat D=-D_B D_H,}
\]

where \(D_B\) is a 3×3 determinant (9 terms, degree 2) and \(D_H\) a 6×6 determinant (105 terms, degree 6). All 250 exact cyclotomic coefficients are in `exact_polynomial_gate.json`. Independent evaluations of the sparse expression against the original 11×9 determinant match over three prime fields.

## 5. **Characteristic-zero** obstruction on an entire slice

Specialize the three base parameters to

\[
\boxed{h_0=1,\qquad h_1=h_2=0,\qquad h_6=x.}
\]

The necessary cubic \(W\) becomes a quadratic in \(x\). Under the valid ring homomorphism

\[
\mathbb Z[\zeta_8]\longrightarrow\mathbb F_{17},\qquad\zeta_8\mapsto9,
\]

its reduction is

\[
W(1,0,0,x)\equiv10+14x+16x^2
=16(x-2)(x-12)\pmod{17}.
\]

At **both** roots, the exact denominator-cleared gate minor is nonzero:

| \(x\pmod{17}\) | Tail pivot \(\Delta\) | \(T\) | \(\widehat D\pmod{17}\) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2 | 14 | 8 | **3** |
| 12 | 9 | 10 | **2** |

Consequently the two polynomials \(W(1,0,0,x)\) and \(\widehat D(1,0,0,x)\) have a **nonzero resultant** modulo \((17,\zeta_8-9)\):

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Res}_x(W,\widehat D)\equiv6\pmod{17}.}
\]

Because both polynomial expressions were generated with coefficients in the *integral* cyclotomic ring and resultants commute with coefficient reduction, a nonzero reduction proves the characteristic-zero resultant is nonzero. Hence the polynomials have **no common complex root** on that fixed slice. **No factorization exists on that regular slice.** This is a genuine complex-algebraic exclusion, not an extrapolation from a finite-field enumeration. It does **not** exclude other \(h_0,h_1,h_2\), or \(h_0\Delta T=0\) exceptional branches.

## 6. New codimension bound for regular complex candidates

Eliminate \(x=h_6\) symbolically:

\[
R_B(h_0,h_1,h_2)=\operatorname{Res}_x(W,D_B),\qquad
R_H(h_0,h_1,h_2)=\operatorname{Res}_x(W,D_H).
\]

The exact exports are:

| Polynomial | Terms | Total degree | Witness at \((1,0,0)\pmod{17}\) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| \(R_B\) | 37 | 5 | 16 |
| \(R_H\) | 476 | 15 | 11 |

Both are therefore **nonzero polynomials over \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_8)\)**. Every regular complex factorization must satisfy

\[
\boxed{R_B(h_0,h_1,h_2)R_H(h_0,h_1,h_2)=0.}
\]

This projects all regular factorization candidates into a **proper algebraic hypersurface union** in the three-dimensional base space.

A dimensional subtlety matters: above a base point where \(W(h_0,h_1,h_2,x)\not\equiv0\) as a polynomial in \(x\), only finitely many values of \(x\) exist, so the solution locus has dimension at most two there. If \(W\equiv0\) in \(x\), the fiber could be a curve. But the leading coefficient \(W_2(h_0,h_1,h_2)\) is a **nonzero linear polynomial**, and \(W_1\) is not divisible by it: at \((h_0,h_1,h_2)=(1,0,10)\pmod{17}\), \(W_2=0\) but \(W_1=2\), and \(W_2\) has unit leading coefficient modulo 17. Thus their common-zero base has dimension at most one; including a free \(x\) adds at most one. Overall:

\[
\boxed{\dim\bigl(\text{regular candidate locus in }\mathbb C^4\bigr)\le2.}
\]

This dimension bound concerns the **fixed permutation and globally separated rank-one generator chart only**. It does not establish emptiness.

## 7. Validation and limitations

The new verifiers use two independent determinant routes (original 11×9 modular computation and sparse exact 3×3/6×6 expansion) and an independent SymPy resultant implementation for random specializations. They also test deliberately zeroed upper-stage columns. The complete previous checkpoint, including all previous regression tests, is included in the package.

**Not proved:** that \(F_8\) is impossible with all parameters and all exceptional branches; anything about arbitrary endpoint or internal permutations; anything about all quasiseparable stages; or any exact or practical sub-\(N\log N\) FFT. Published Fourier factorizations and quasiseparable representations remain useful background, not something this work supersedes.
