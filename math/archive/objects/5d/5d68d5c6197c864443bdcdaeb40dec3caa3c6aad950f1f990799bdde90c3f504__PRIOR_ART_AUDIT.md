# Prior-art boundary — v22

The mathematical background is classical:

1. Finite Weyl/Heisenberg operators and the finite Weil representation.
2. The discrete Fourier transform as the operator associated with the Weyl element.
3. Quadratic Gauss sums and quadratic-phase/metaplectic operators.
4. Bluestein/chirp-convolution representations of the DFT.

Useful primary references:

- S. Gurevich, R. Hadani, R. Howe, *Quadratic reciprocity and the sign of the Gauss sum via the finite Weil representation*, arXiv:0808.2447.
- N. A. Lopez, I. Y. Dodin, *Exactly unitary discrete representations of the metaplectic transform for linear-time algorithms*, arXiv:2012.06028.
- L. I. Bluestein, *A linear filtering approach to the computation of discrete Fourier transform*, IEEE Trans. Audio and Electroacoustics 18 (1970).

The project-specific results are:

- the exact rank-`1` versus rank-`N` dichotomy for the dephased Fourier coefficient tensor relative to two chosen Weyl lines;
- the classification `v=t(a-b,a+b)` of all successful ordered line pairs in the chosen conventions;
- the deduction that every successful factorization exposes at least one factor already monomially/diagonally equivalent to Fourier;
- the fixed-pair sum-of-products rank gate.

A limited audit is not a proof of mathematical priority. No novelty claim is made.
