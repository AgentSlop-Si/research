# Exact gauge-closed stage recursion, crossblocks, and charged application

Independent paired review, 9 October 2026 UTC.

## Stage family and guards

For N a power of two, let Z_N be the natural successor and V_N its
bit-reversal conjugate. Define D_d=diag(1,d,...,d^(N-1)) and

A_N(p,q,t,d)=D_d (I-pZ_N)^(-1) D_d^(-1)+t(I-qV_N)^(-1).

The guards are d!=0, p^N!=1, q^N!=1. The family explicitly enlarges
the preceding two-resolvent pencil by gauging only its natural-cycle
term. At d=1 it contains that original family and its certified N4
point. This is a stage identity, not a Fourier synthesis theorem.

## Both diagonal and both off-diagonal blocks

Write N=2m and E=(I-q^2 V_m)^(-1). First assume p!=0 and put

A'=A_m(p^2,q^2,t,d/p),
U_ij=d^(i-j) p^(m+i-j)/(1-p^(2m)), 0<=i,j<m.

Then the exact contiguous-half block identity is

A_(2m) = [[A', d^(-m)U+t q V_m E],
          [d^m U+t q E, A']].

For the natural term, each entry in either principal half is
d^(i-j) p^((i-j) mod 2m)/(1-p^(2m)). The corresponding entry of
D_(d/p) G_(p^2)(Z_m) D_(d/p)^(-1) is

(d/p)^(i-j) p^(2((i-j) mod m))/(1-p^(2m)).

The two coincide separately for i>=j and i<j. The lower principal
half is identical since the added m cancels in its gauge exponent.
For crossblocks, the natural Green exponent is m+i-j in both
directions; the gauge contributes the additional d^(-m) or d^m.

For the bit-reversal term, V_(2m)=[[0,V_m],[I,0]]. Direct multiplication
by I-qV_(2m) verifies its blocks [[E,qV_mE],[qE,E]]. This proves all
four blocks for every allowed size and parameter.

The new guards persist exactly:

(p^2)^m=p^(2m), (q^2)^m=q^(2m), d/p!=0.

After k halvings, the stage parameters are

p_k=p^(2^k), q_k=q^(2^k), t_k=t, d_k=d/p^(2^k-1).

All remain in the same coefficient field for supplied initial
parameters, although powers, representation sizes, and inversions are
still charged preparation work.

## The p=0 boundary is separate and valid

At p=0 the natural term is I, independently of d. Both diagonal
blocks are I+tE and the off-diagonal blocks are tqV_mE and tqE.
The boundary therefore closes as A_m(0,q^2,t,d), or with any other
nonzero smaller gauge. There is no division by p on this branch.
The case d=0 is not admitted because the original gauge is singular.
The q=0 and t=0 boundaries are also valid wherever the stated pole
guards hold; their terms may simplify without any cancellation of a
forbidden denominator.

## Crossblock structure and cost

For p!=0, U is nonzero rank one. A factorization without negative
p powers is

u_i=(dp)^i,
v_j=d^(-j) p^(m-j),
U=u v^T/(1-p^(2m)).

The exponents m-j are positive, so the original entry formula for U
also correctly gives zero when p=0. For tq!=0, the other part of each
crossblock is invertible: E is invertible and V_m is a permutation.
Thus each full crossblock has rank at least m-1. Gauge closure does
not restore bounded cut rank.

Despite this growing rank, each crossblock has an O(m) application:
one rank-one product, one cyclic Green application in bit-reversal
order, one scaling, and optionally a permutation. With its rank-one
vectors and scalar coefficients supplied, a direct bound is

5m-1 scalar multiplications and 4m-3 additions per crossblock,

plus O(m) index/data work. This counts 2m multiplications and m-1
additions for the rank-one product, 2m-1 multiplications and 2m-2
additions for E, m multiplications for tq, and m additions to combine.
Rank-one vector construction is an additional O(m) preparation task;
the pole/gauge powers and needed reciprocals must also be supplied or
computed. This is an upper bound, not an optimized schedule.

## Full-stage weighted-cycle application retains the prior ledger

The gauged natural resolvent is the inverse of a weighted cyclic
shift denominator. The ordinary edge coefficient is a=pd and the
wrap edge coefficient is b=p d^(1-N). Their product is

a^(N-1)b=p^N.

For input y, a Horner pass forms

h=y_(N-1)+a y_(N-2)+...+a^(N-2)y_1,
x_0=(y_0+b h)/(1-p^N),
x_j=y_j+a x_(j-1), 1<=j<N.

This costs exactly 2N-1 scalar multiplications and 2N-2 additions
once a,b and the boundary reciprocal are supplied. No explicit dense
diagonal-gauge application is necessary. Applying the second cyclic
resolvent and combining with t gives 5N-2 multiplications and 5N-4
additions for one stage, and therefore

17N-6 multiplications and 15N-12 additions

for three stages and two diagonal masks. Index generation, data
movement, storage, and output writing remain O(N).

Preparation includes six pole-boundary reciprocals and the wrap
coefficients for three stages. If gauge inverses are not supplied,
one additional reciprocal per stage suffices to generate d^(1-N)
by repeated squaring. All required pole/gauge powers take O(log N)
multiplications per stage. One may batch inversions, but they are not
free. Arbitrary mask and parameter synthesis and exact field/bit
costs remain outside this supplied-coefficient ledger.

## What still has to match Fourier

The new family removes the previous scalar-only principal-half
closure obstruction by an explicitly enlarged gauge parameter. It
does not yet supply compatible diagonal masks, Fourier phases, or a
recursion for the complete three-stage product.

There is a concrete target distinction. With contiguous row and
column halves, the principal block of F_(2m) is X_ij=omega_(2m)^(ij),
not F_m. If D=diag((-1)^i), the four target blocks are

[[X, D X], [X D, (-1)^m D X D]].

Under even/odd input-column order instead, they are

[[F_m, T F_m], [F_m, -T F_m]], T=diag(omega_(2m)^i).

Any proposed product recursion must specify which ordering it uses,
carry the permutation and twiddle costs, and satisfy the crossblock
equations. Principal-stage closure alone cannot replace those
identities. Naively recursing on two smaller diagonal stages with
O(m) extra block work gives the usual recurrence T(2m)=2T(m)+O(m),
which is O(N log N), even though each already-specified stage has
the direct O(N) weighted-cycle schedule above.

## Independent controls and replay

check_gauge_recursion.py uses exact rational arithmetic and no
constructor program. Twenty controls at N=4,8,16,32 cover nonzero,
negative, p=0, q=0 and t=0 parameters. They check all four blocks,
the weighted inverse identity, guard propagation, rank-one structure,
and an operation-counted schedule against dense matrix application.
The all-size result follows from the entrywise proof above, not from
these finite controls. GAUGE_RECURSION_RESULTS.json records them.
