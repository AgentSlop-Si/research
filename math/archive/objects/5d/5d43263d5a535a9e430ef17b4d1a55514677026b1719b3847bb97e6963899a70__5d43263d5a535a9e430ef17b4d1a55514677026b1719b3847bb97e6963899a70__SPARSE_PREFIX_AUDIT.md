# Sparse prefix and additive tail audit

## Result

**Accept the stated representation obstruction**, with its full-spark, fixed-linear-factorization and row-sparsity assumptions. For a totally nonsingular matrix T, an invertible matrix P having at most s nonzero entries per row, and a terminal sum H of branches with total separation-rank budget R and d distinct row/column order pairs,

    T = P^(-1) H  implies  floor(N / (s 2^d)) <= R.

For an individual-Green prefix of depth t followed by Q rank-one Green branches, s <= 2^t gives the necessary condition

    p < 2^(t+d) (Q+1) <= 2^(t+Q) (Q+1)

at prime complex Fourier length p. In particular, one prefix and two branches are impossible at all primes p >= 29; two prefixes and two branches are impossible at all primes p >= 53. These thresholds are correct, although the theorem does not assert feasibility at the smaller primes.

The proof needs only minor endpoint bookkeeping: handle m = floor(N/(s 2^d)) = 0 as vacuous before applying a lemma requiring two nonempty sets of size at least two. Integer separation-rank bounds are understood. The source verifier's empty-set early return does not maintain its stated column cardinality outside its default positive-m cases; this does not invalidate the theorem or those default cases.

The independent exact controls all passed. This review accepts a restricted representation theorem, not a general arithmetic-operation lower bound, an exact linear-time FFT, or any claim of historical priority. **Products of two additive Green sums and the supplied bridge formulation are outside this audit's scope.**

## Checked proof

### Simultaneous separation

An order pair consists of an injective row-position map a and injective column-position map b into a common ordered label set. Positions may coincide across the two maps. A branch has separation rank at most r when every rectangle lying strictly on either side of a common cut has rank at most r.

For row and column sets of cardinalities u,v >= 2, put h=floor(u/2), k=floor(v/2). Let row low/high denote the first/last h positions, and column low/high the first/last k. If row low does not precede column high, then column low must precede row high. Otherwise their endpoint inequalities form a strict cycle. Within each map, the low and high subsets are disjoint, so the strict comparisons used in this contradiction remain valid even when row and column positions tie each other.

Applying this argument to each distinct order pair halves both cardinalities at most once per pair. Previously achieved separations survive restriction to subsets. Starting from floor(N/s) rows and all N columns gives

    |X| = floor(N/(s 2^d)) = m,
    |Y| = floor(N/2^d) >= s m.

If m > 0, every preceding stage has both set sizes at least two, so the selection lemma's hypotheses hold throughout. If m = 0, the desired inequality is immediate; no selection argument is required. Reusing an order pair should not incur another halving, which explains why d rather than Q is the sharper count.

### Preservation of row rank after sparse mixing

Let S be the union of support positions of the m selected rows of P. Then

    m <= |S| <= s m <= |Y|.

The selected rows of an invertible P are independent, so P[X,S] has rank m. Total nonsingularity of T implies that T[S,Y] has full row rank |S|: choose any |S| columns of Y and use the corresponding nonzero square minor. Consequently it has a right inverse V. The identity

    (PT)[X,Y] V = P[X,S]

implies rank((PT)[X,Y]) >= m, and equality follows from its number of rows. This is an exact algebraic statement over any field in which the assumed minors are nonzero. Invertibility of all of P is a sufficient condition; independence of the chosen rows is the property actually used.

Each terminal branch on X by Y has rank at most its order bound. Hence rank(H[X,Y]) <= R. Since H=PT, m<=R. For integral R, floor(N/(s 2^d))<=R is equivalent to N<s 2^d(R+1). If real-valued rank bounds are allowed, replace each by its integer floor rather than using the displayed equivalence unqualified.

### Why Green branches meet the separation assumption

Order a Hamiltonian cycle consecutively and write its cyclic difference matrix as A=I-D S. In a split into a proper leading and trailing segment, the two diagonal blocks of A are triangular with unit diagonal, and each crossing block has rank at most one. The block equations for A^(-1) then bound each crossing block of A^(-1) by rank one. This remains true when some edge weights vanish; division by individual weights is unnecessary. The only inversion condition for the full cycle is that the product of its edge weights differs from one.

Taking subrectangles preserves rank bounds. Independent nonzero left and right diagonal gauges preserve rank, and independent row and column permutations transport the two position maps. Thus arbitrary non-affine cycle permutations and independent monomial gauges are covered.

Each inverse of an individual Green factor has at most two nonzero coefficients per row. Inverses of intervening monomial gauges have one per row. Row-support size of a product is at most the product of the individual bounds, so t individual Green factors give an inverse prefix with at most 2^t entries per row. The order in which the factors are inverted changes their product but not this support bound. Actual cancellations can only improve the bound. The transposed conclusion is valid for column-sparse inverse suffixes.

### Prime Fourier specialization

For prime p, every square minor of the complex p-point Fourier matrix is nonzero. This classical input is explicitly stated and proved as Lemma 1.3 in [Tao's paper](https://arxiv.org/html/math/0308286v6), which also identifies Chebotarev's earlier result. Complex normalization and changing the Fourier sign do not affect nonsingularity.

The requirement cannot be replaced by mere invertibility of T or by arbitrary composite Fourier length. At N=4, rows {0,2} and columns {0,2} of the Fourier matrix form an all-ones 2-by-2 matrix and have rank one. Our exact control reproduces this over GF(17), using the primitive fourth root 4.

For t=1,Q=2 and worst-case d=2, the necessary condition is p<24. The first excluded prime is 29. For t=2,Q=2 it is p<48; the first excluded prime is 53. With shared order pairs, the d refinement is strictly stronger. A necessary representation-budget consequence is t+Q+log2(Q+1)>log2(p). It does not by itself lower-bound all arithmetic implementations of the represented matrix.

## Approximation and complexity limits

For fixed invertible P and an extracted rectangle with q=R<m, the stated inequality

    ||T-P^(-1)H||_2 >= sigma_(q+1)((PT)[X,Y]) / ||P||_2 > 0

is correct. Restricting rows and columns cannot increase operator norm, and any rank-at-most-q matrix is at least the indicated singular value away from (PT)[X,Y]. This is a fixed-preconditioner certificate. The argument supplies no uniform lower bound as the weights and conditioning vary. For example, scaling the selected rows of a diagonal P by epsilon while leaving other rows at unit scale keeps its norm one and can scale the numerator singular values to zero as epsilon tends to zero.

All matrices and order maps must be fixed independently of the input, with equality on all input vectors. Adaptive input-family solvers, nonlinear branching, or a success guarantee only on selected vectors do not furnish the required matrix identity. Passing a graph capacity test cannot replace an exact matrix identity either.

This is a term/depth/order obstruction in a specified formula. It is not sound to combine an O(N) upper bound for applying one Green factor with a factor-count lower bound and call the result an unrestricted arithmetic lower bound. Fused computation, alternative formulas and coefficient models require their own arguments.

The source does not provide an implementation speedup or a full preprocessing/precision analysis. If an allowed architecture were constructed, its root and coefficient generation, gauges, permutations, inverse products, reads/writes, divisions and bit/precision costs would still have to be paid. Dense verification is not an FFT algorithm. The independent checker deliberately uses dense inversion and exhaustive minors; its runtime is evidence-generation cost, not a proposed transform cost.

The familiar quasiseparable structure has established algorithms and representations, as described by [Pernet](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.01246). Lower bounds under conditioning restrictions use a different explicit circuit model; [Ailon's paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1403.1307) bounds circuits with controlled intermediate condition numbers. These references do not establish priority for this particular combination of sparse mixing and separated rectangles, and this audit makes no such priority claim.

## Independent exact controls

`independent_checks.py` uses only Python's standard library and has no input dependency on the uploaded verifier or saved results. It was authored for this review. It chooses rectangles by searching separating cuts, rather than copying the source's low/high subset procedure, and constructs Green matrices by ordinary Gauss-Jordan inversion, rather than the source's path-product formula.

All controls passed:

- 79,472 exhaustive pairs of relative row/column position subsets on label sets of sizes 2 through 8, including cross-map ties.
- 2,340 iterated selection tests for N=1 through 65, six sparsity bounds and depths 1 through 6. The 1,446 m=0 cases preserve the separate floor cardinalities in our helper.
- Every nonempty square complex Fourier minor at prime lengths 2,3,5,7, totaling 5+19+251+3,431 minors. Determinants are computed by signed permutation sums of powers of a formal root. For prime p, reduction modulo 1+z+...+z^(p-1) is exact: a coefficient vector is zero precisely when all its entries agree. These are characteristic-zero checks, not floating-point or modular proxies.
- All 3,431 nonempty square minors of an explicit 7-by-7 Cauchy matrix over GF(101), followed by 15,585 sparse-mixing rectangle checks with invertible upper-triangular preconditioners of row support at most 1,2,3,4.
- Ten independently inverted, independently row/column-permuted and gauged Green branches at lengths 3,5,8,13,29; 212 maximal strict rectangles have rank at most one. Five branches include a zero edge weight.
- Twelve complete sparse-prefix/additive-tail rank certificates at prime lengths 13,29,53,101,131,257, with t up to 3 and Q up to 3. Six use all distinct order pairs; six deliberately repeat a pair. Every case verifies the actual Fourier support rectangle's rank over its chosen prime field before using it, all branch ranks, the tail rank bound, row-support growth and the preconditioned Fourier rank m>Q. The finite-field ranks are finite-case certificates only, not a proof of Chebotarev over C.
- 59,880 checks of the integral floor/strict-bound equivalence.

Assumption-failure controls are essential:

1. Composite Fourier: the 4-point Fourier 2-by-2 rectangle described above has rank one, so invertibility of a Fourier matrix alone is insufficient.
2. Too few columns: for the verified GF(101) Cauchy matrix, an invertible row-two-sparse P has first row (45,63,0,0,0,0,0) and cancels its first Fourier-analogue output column exactly. Thus m=1,c=1<s*m=2 can have rank zero.
3. Dependent preconditioner rows: duplicating two rows produces rank one where the theorem's independent-row hypothesis would force rank two.

These tests support the audited proof and reject specific dropped-assumption mutations. They do not constitute a formal proof assistant verification, a claim that the source's own saved random trials were reproduced, or a general Fourier algorithm.

## Source implementation caveat

The source helper `simultaneous_asymmetric_halving` returns `[],[]` as soon as either next half-size is zero. This is adequate when the only desired result is a vacuous rank bound, but it does not preserve both advertised cardinalities. With one row and eight columns, one halving should produce cardinalities 0 and 4; the helper returns 0 and 0. The default experiments never reach this situation because their m exceeds Q. A general-purpose implementation should either document the empty-certificate convention or return appropriately sized subsets separately. Source files are preserved untouched.
