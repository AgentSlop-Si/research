# Two-Sided Fourier–Green Spectral Separator (TSFG)

**Research date:** 10 October 2026  
**Status:** Exact algebraic theorem in a restricted family, independently checked by finite-field/singular-value tests; a stronger rejection mechanism for the user's proposed exact two-stage Green-sum FFT architecture. **No exact arbitrary-input O(N) FFT, no unconditional FFT lower bound, and no publication-level originality claim.**

## 1. The primary problem and what this paper changes

We seek an exact, uniform, all-output DFT for fresh arbitrary input with total sequential work `o(N log N)` (ideally `O(N)`), including coefficient generation. The unresolved architecture in earlier research is

`M_N=(G_1+G_2)(G_3+G_4)`

with each `G_j` a weighted, independently permuted, cyclic Green inverse. Prior work proves rank bounds on fixed/affine/balanced/dyadic cuts; none proves that a universal Fourier factorization exists. This paper certifies obstructions using **different row/column cosets** and an **intermediate partition that moves between network stages**, with the individual Green rectangle ranks computed using a sparse matching problem instead of forming dense inverses.

There are three reusable lemmas and one combined theorem. The ingredients are classical: subgroup Fourier orthogonality, complementary inverse-nullity, acyclic matching/rank, submodularity of linear matroid rank, and network rank propagation. The particular integration and benchmark instance are new within the project checkpoints surveyed, **not confirmed new to mathematics**.

## 2. Fourier rectangular coset spectrum — exact formula

Let `U_N[j,k]=N^{-1/2} exp(-2π i jk/N)`. Let `d_r,d_c` be positive divisors of N. For any residue offsets `0<=a<d_r`, `0<=b<d_c`, define

`R={a+d_r u:0<=u<N/d_r}` and `C={b+d_c v:0<=v<N/d_c}`.

Put

- `r=N/d_r`, `c=N/d_c`;
- `h=N/gcd(N,d_r d_c)`;
- `s=sqrt(rc/(Nh))`.

**Theorem A.** The singular values of the rectangular complex matrix `U_N[R,C]` consist of **h copies of s** and `min(r,c)-h` copies of zero. Therefore, `rank U_N[R,C]=h`.

**Proof.** Expand the phase of entry `(u,v)`:

`(a+d_r u)(b+d_c v)=ab + d_r b u + a d_c v + d_r d_c uv`.

The first three terms produce a global unit-modulus scalar and diagonal unitary row/column multipliers, which do not change singular values. The remaining core is `N^-1/2 z^(uv)`, where `z=exp(-2π i d_r d_c/N)` has exact order `h`. Since `h|r` and `h|c`, reordering rows and columns by residue modulo h produces a tensor product of the all-ones matrix of shape `(r/h)×(c/h)` and an h-point (unnormalized, possibly permuted) Fourier matrix. The respective nonzero singular values are `sqrt((r/h)(c/h))` and `sqrt(h)`, establishing `s=sqrt(rc/(Nh))`, repeated h times. □

**Dyadic consequence.** For `N=2^k`, `d_r=2^a`, `d_c=2^b` with `a+b<=k`, all `h=2^(k-a-b)` nonzero singular values are exactly 1; thus any submatrix approximation with rank smaller than h has operator-norm error at least 1, for every offset pair `(a0,b0)`.

This is a familiar consequence of Fourier subgroup geometry; **the formula itself should not be marketed as a new Fourier discovery**.

## 3. Exact rectangular rank of an arbitrary cyclic Green inverse in O(N)

Let σ be a single Hamiltonian cycle on `{0,...,N-1}`, let weights `w_i` be arbitrary field elements, and assume `K=I-diag(w)P_σ` is invertible (equivalently `prod_i w_i !=1`). Put `G=K^-1`. Let A and B be **arbitrary**, possibly unrelated row and column subsets; set `a=|A|`, `b=|B|`.

**Theorem B.** The exact rank over any field is

`rank G[A,B] = a+b-N + μ(K[B^c,A^c])`,

where μ is the maximum cardinality of a matching in the bipartite nonzero-pattern graph of the sparse indicated denominator rectangle. That matching is computable in `O(N)` sequential work without forming G.

**Proof.** The complementary inverse-nullity theorem gives

`rank K^-1[A,B] = a+b-N + rank K[B^c,A^c]`.

The bipartite graph of K contains diagonal edges `(row i,column i)` and active cycle edges `(row i,column σ(i))`. When all weights are nonzero this is a single undirected cycle on 2N bipartite vertices; with zero weights it is a disjoint union of paths. Restricting to a proper submatrix removes at least one graph vertex and leaves a forest of paths. Every nonzero square minor of a forest-supported matrix has at most one perfect matching in its bipartite support, hence every maximal matching of cardinality μ supplies a nonvanishing μ×μ determinant (one nonzero monomial, no cancellation). Conversely a nonzero minor implies a matching. Thus `rank K[B^c,A^c]=μ`. When A=B=empty, the full K may retain the cycle, but its assumed invertibility implies both full rank and a perfect matching, giving the same formula. The maximum matching in the proper path forest is obtained in O(N) by repeated leaf-edge selection, removing matched endpoints, and discarding isolated vertices. □

For a monomially dressed branch `H=LGR`, the rank of `H[A,B]` is computed by mapping A and B through the independent row and column permutations; invertible diagonal gauges do not affect it. There is no assumption of an affine cycle or a shared row/column order.

**Novelty caveat:** the nullity theorem and the fact that forest-supported matrices have rank equal to matching number are published results. Here they yield a concrete per-candidate O(N) rank oracle for *arbitrary two-sided Green rectangles*.

## 4. Moving-interface submatrix rank for products of sums

Let `M=T_1 T_2 ... T_t`, with

`T_i = sum_{j=1}^{q_i} H_ij`, and `H_ij` any monomially dressed invertible cyclic Green operation.

Choose **arbitrary intermediate coordinate sets** `S_0,...,S_t`, with `S_0=R` and `S_t=C^c`. Let

`B(S_0,...,S_t) = sum_{i=1}^t sum_{j=1}^{q_i} rank H_ij[S_{i-1}, S_i^c]`.

**Theorem C.**

`rank M[R,C] <= B(S_0,...,S_t)`.

**Proof.** For a product AB and any intermediate set S,

`(AB)[R,C] = A[R,S] B[S,C] + A[R,S^c] B[S^c,C]`.

Thus `rank (AB)[R,C] <= rank B[S,C]+rank A[R,S^c]`. Apply this inductively, then apply subadditivity of rank to each stage's sum of branches. □

Each branch rank on the right is EXACTLY computable via Theorem B. Hence **one submitted interface certificate** can be validated with O(N Σ q_i) work, with no dense Green matrix materialization, no numerical Fourier, and arbitrary non-affine permutation and independent endpoint gauge freedom.

## 5. A complete two-sided Fourier obstruction and robust error bound

Use row/column cosets R,C from Theorem A, with Fourier coset rank h and common nonzero singular value s. Let B be any moving-interface rank budget from Theorem C.

**Theorem D (Two-Sided Fourier–Green Spectral Separator).** For every such Green product-of-sums matrix M,

`||M-U_N||_2 >= s` if `B < h`;

`||M-U_N||_F >= s*sqrt(max(0,h-B))` for every B.

In particular, `B<h` makes exact Fourier equality impossible over the complex numbers independently of the actual nonzero cycle weights, diagonal gauges, and conditioning.

**Proof.** Theorem C gives `rank M[R,C] <= B`. Theorem A and the singular-value optimality of rank-B approximations (Eckart–Young) force squared Frobenius block error ≥`s²(h-B)` and operator norm block error ≥s when B<h. Passing to a submatrix cannot increase either ambient matrix norm, proving the bounds. □

### Optimizing the interface in the two-stage architecture

Let `M=(sum_j H_1j)(sum_j H_2j)` and fix R,C. Define

`f(S)=sum_j rank H_1j[R,S^c] + sum_j rank H_2j[S,C]`.

**Theorem E.** f is a **submodular set function** of S. Therefore its global minimum `β=min_{S⊆[N]} f(S)` is computable in polynomial time using submodular minimization with exact rank oracles. Every oracle evaluation is O(N(q_1+q_2)) via Theorem B. Theorem D remains true with the sharper certificate B=β.

**Proof.** For each first-stage branch, `rank H[R,T]` is the rank function of a linear matroid on its columns, and T=S^c; complementing a submodular function preserves submodularity. For each second-stage branch, `rank H[T,C]` is the rank function of a linear matroid on its rows, evaluated at T=S, hence submodular. Summing preserves submodularity. A general polynomial-time submodular-function minimization algorithm may be used; no claim is made that *finding* the global minimizer runs in O(N). □

This automates searching intermediate partitions, rather than relying only on fixed dyadic cuts or exponentially enumerating S. For q_1=q_2=1, the minimax expression is also the classical linear-matroid-intersection min-max quantity. These optimization methods have substantial prior art.

## 6. Strictly stronger than the previous same-coordinate dyadic test

A complete reproducer constructs at N=64:

- Four **different** weighted Hamiltonian cycles, each with independently permuted endpoint coordinates;
- a candidate `M=(H11+H12)(H21+H22)`;
- output row coset R=even indices and input column coset C=odd indices;
- one **different** intermediate 32-element set S.

For the selected S, each of the four Green cross rectangles has **exact rank 1**, hence B(S)=4, while Theorem A gives `rank U_64[even,odd]=16` with all 16 singular values 1.

Therefore for the corresponding real/complex network with any admissible weights having the same nonzero cycle support and compatible independent endpoint permutations:

`||M-U_64||_2>=1`, and `||M-U_64||_F>=sqrt(12)`.

In the supplied GF(193) instantiation the two aggregate cross-stage ranks are 1 and 1; the product block rank is 2. By contrast, keeping the old identical parity cut S=R gives individual stage ranks 17 and 17, thus the previous fixed-partition upper bound 34 ≥16 gives **no obstruction**. This is a strict strengthening demonstrated within exactly the proposed *two sums multiplied* architecture, not just with one Green per stage.

GF(193) contains a primitive 64th root (11), and an independent check reports `rank U_64[even,odd]=16` over GF(193). These finite-field checks support the implementation; the spectral-norm result is a complex-analytic statement justified by Theorems A–D, not by modular rank alone.

## 7. Independent tests and mathematical boundaries

Run `python two_sided_verify.py`:

- 596 numerical singular-spectrum checks over N=8,12,16,18,24,32,45,64; maximum absolute discrepancy <8×10^-15;
- 1,728 Green submatrix tests over GF(193), comparing exact matrix inverse/rank with an independently implemented forest-matching formula, including zero-weight edges and arbitrary cycles;
- 65,536 exhaustive submodularity inequalities for a randomly generated N=8 two-stage network with two Green branches per stage;
- an exact N=64 finite-field Fourier coset rank check using a primitive 64th root in GF(193);
- two explicit moving-interface N=64 witnesses, including the four-branch two-sum product, with independent exact modular ranks.

Tests do not prove publication-level originality and cannot establish an unrestricted FFT lower bound. The general lemmas above supply the proof.

## 8. Main next gate for the user's exact FFT objective

**Improved falsification** is valuable, but a fastest-FFT breakthrough needs **construction**, not another restricted negative theorem. For every candidate parameter family `M_N=(G1+G2)(G3+G4)`:

1. Run fixed dyadic and balanced cuts; optimize moving-interface certificate for selected Fourier coset pairs. Any B<h kills the full parameter family, no fitting required.
2. If it survives all tested cuts, verify actual phase/covariance identities exactly: `M_N S=D M_N` and `M_N e_0=N^-1/2 ones` (with sign convention fixed).
3. Eliminate parameter families failing small exact fields. Only surviving all-length symbolic families merit a claim of `o(N log N)`.
4. Count O(N) coefficient generation, preprocessing, numerical stability, and bit complexity independently of the O(N) application of each Green stage.

**Current answer:** no universal exact O(N) FFT is constructed or proven by this research.
