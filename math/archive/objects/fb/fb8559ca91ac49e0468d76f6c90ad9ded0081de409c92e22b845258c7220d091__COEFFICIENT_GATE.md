# Four-stage Fourier coefficient compatibility: eliminate the outer stages

**Date:** 2026-10-10  
**Status:** Exact necessary polynomial equations, independently tested. **No nonexistence theorem or working FFT factorization.**

## Fixed exact layout and notation

For the unchanged affine permutations \(P_1,P_2,P_3\) defined in `AFFINE_LAYOUT.json`, consider a putative exact factorization

\[
F_{256}=S_4P_3S_3P_2S_2P_1S_1
\]

with all \(S_j\) two-sided quasiseparable of order at most one, over \(\mathbb C\). Here the permutation matrix \(P_j\) acts on vectors as `x[p_j]`, precisely the convention of the previous research code. Since \(F_{256}\) is invertible, all four square stages are invertible—even if individual upper/lower generators vanish. Set \(C_4=S_4^{-1}\) and \(C_1=S_1^{-1}\). By the classical nullity theorem, their inverses also have two-sided quasiseparable order at most one.

Multiplication on the appropriate sides yields the exact identity

\[
\boxed{H=P_3^{-1}C_4F_{256}C_1P_1^{-1}=S_3P_2S_2.}
\]

Thus two of the four unknown stages have been replaced by their independent invertible quasiseparable inverses, leaving a **two-stage** middle product to constrain.

## A concrete middle-stage rank certificate

Select \(C=\{i:\text{bit}_6(i)=0\}\), so \(|C|=128\). Let \(g_i\) be its indicator, let \(p_2\) be the exact saved middle permutation array, and select

\[
R=\{i:g_{p_2[i]}=0\},\quad |R|=128.
\]

The binary sequence \(g\) has exactly **3** natural-order boundaries; \(g[p_2]\) has exactly **13**. For any two rank-one quasiseparable matrices \(S_2,S_3\), boundary-rank subadditivity (including all zero-generator branches) gives

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}(S_3P_2S_2)[R,C]\le3+13=16.}
\]

Hence *every* exact four-stage Fourier factorization with the fixed shuffles necessarily satisfies

\[
\boxed{\det\left[(P_3^{-1}C_4F_{256}C_1P_1^{-1})[R',C']\right]=0}
\]

for every \(R'\subseteq R,\;C'\subseteq C\) with \(|R'|=|C'|=17\). These are **polynomial equations in the entries of the two unknown inverse outer stages**. They can be evaluated without dividing by any generator entries. They are necessary, not known sufficient.

## Nontriviality, proved in characteristic zero

Take \(C_4=C_1=I\), corresponding to the perfectly allowable outer identity stages. Then \(H_0=P_3^{-1}F_{256}P_1^{-1}\). The full 128×128 selected Fourier block has **rank exactly 128** over both \(\mathbb F_{12289}\) and \(\mathbb F_{65537}\). More pointedly, the first 17 selected rows and columns give a minor with determinants:

- \(8799\bmod 12289\),
- \(20067\bmod 65537\).

Both primes admit primitive 256th roots; the residues are obtained by exact modular arithmetic. A nonzero reduction of the cyclotomic determinant polynomial proves that its value in \(\mathbb Z[\zeta_{256}]\) is nonzero. Thus the **same** minor is nonzero over \(\mathbb C\). The outer stages of any valid putative Fourier factorization must therefore create a dramatic rank reduction from 128 to at most 16.

This does **not** preclude such a rank reduction by suitably chosen *invertible* quasiseparable outer factors; it merely supplies explicit nonlinear equations they must solve.

## Positive and negative controls

`fourier_compatibility_gate.py` performs:

1. Direct exact random independent-generator S2,S3 products, showing the selected middle block has rank 16 in two finite fields.
2. Identity-stage degeneracy controls (all off-diagonal generators zero), which also satisfy the rank bound.
3. Exact Fourier F under C1=C4=I, showing rank 128 and the stated 17×17 nonzero determinants.
4. Random **nontrivial but invertible** one-sided outer inverse stages, which do not happen to satisfy the compatibility constraints (no impossibility inference).
5. Construction of their exact unitriangular inverses S1,S4, assembly of a *full four-stage synthetic product*, and proof by entrywise modular equality that algebraically eliminating the outer stages recovers S3 P2 S2. No numerical approximation or root fitting is involved.
