# Independent HERO0 bridge audit and bounded joint-scan construction

9 October 2026. This is an independently derived mathematical supplement. It neither imports nor executes uploaded code. The supplied source was only `HERO0_RESEARCH.md`, 143 lines / 11,664 bytes. Assertions that the source's earlier test suites passed are not independently rerun here.

## Result in brief

1. The Bessel operator recurrence, inverse identity, Cauchy minor argument, and sparse-plus-low-rank obstruction in HERO0 are correct in their stated models.
2. A sharper precision obstruction: an error in the initial b=1 Fourier eigenmode is amplified by at least `(R-1)! (K/pi)^(R-1)` after R orders, for K>=4. The obvious forward cycle therefore needs substantially more than the v11 leading precision budget for a uniform error guarantee.
3. A precise scan-fusion obstruction: the commutator between the cyclic prefix scan and even the degree-one frequency weight has a full-rank selected-output minor. Moving all output-dependent weights before one common scan cannot be repaired by a constant-rank operator on unrestricted forcings.
4. A constructive exact-arithmetic subroutine: the K-period forcing representation admits blocked polynomial-prefix evaluation in

   `O(Q (R/B+B) log^2(M+R))` field operations,

   with O(Q) storage when 1<=B<=R<=M, B divides K, Q=KM, M^2<=Q. Taking a dyadic B comparable to sqrt(R) gives

   `O(Q sqrt(R) log^2(M+R))`.

   This count assumes monomial polynomial coefficients are supplied and fast polynomial multiplication is available. All conversions, forcing preparation and the short-FFT bank are charged separately below. Use the minimum of this cost and the ordinary O(QR) scan. A saving in order requires `log^2(M+R)=o(sqrt(R))`, equivalently `log^4(M+R)=o(R)`. The v11 asymptotic scaling eventually meets that condition, but this says nothing about practical crossover constants.
5. The current total remains O(RN), because the R independent K-point FFTs still cost O(R K log K)=O(RN). The blocked subroutine has no floating-point stability certificate. It is not an O(N log log N) exact FFT, a certified finite-precision v11 improvement, or a practical speedup claim.

## 2. Verification of the HERO0 identities

Let U be the unitary K-point DFT, A=U diag(0,1,...,K-1) U*, H=U diag(0,1,1/2,...,1/(K-1)) U*, and P0=U e0 e0* U*. Then H+P0 and A+P0 have reciprocal diagonal eigenvalues, proving `(H+P0)^-1=A+P0`.

For lambda=pi/K, scalar Bessel recurrence gives

`Y_(r+1)=(2r/lambda) H Y_r-Y_(r-1)+1_(r=1) G0 Ue0`.

The correction is necessary exactly at r=1. No restriction on G is used. The source's weighted geometric sum has the correct sign: for a_i=zeta^i, off-diagonal entries are `a_j/(a_i-a_j)`, and diagonal entries are `(K-1)/2`.

Disjoint square minors are column-scaled Cauchy minors, hence nonsingular. The row-support pigeonhole proof consequently gives

`A=S+E, row_nnz(S)<=s, rank(E)<=r  => K<=(s+2)(r+1)-1`.

Subtracting P0 raises the allowed residual rank by at most one, giving the source's corrected-inverse corollary. These are representation-specific results. Neither full rank nor a dense inverse is an unrestricted circuit lower bound.

The fresh checker verifies 5,460 entry equalities over F_998244353 at K=2,4,8,16,32,64 and 93 disjoint Cauchy minors. It independently verifies the Bessel cycle with 70-digit direct matrices at K=4,8,12, orders through 6. Maximum discrepancy is approximately 2.0e-70. These are separate checks, not executions of the source's absent `cycle_bridge.py`.

## 3. Quantitative forward-precision obstruction

Use an adversarial initial perturbation `delta Y0=0`, `delta Y1=delta Ue1`, with all later operations exact. Unitarity reduces the propagated error to a scalar recurrence

`e0=0, e1=delta, e_(r+1)=(2r/z)e_r-e_(r-1), z=pi/K`.

For K>=4, 0<z<1. Taking delta>0, induction shows e_r>=e_(r-1)>0 for r>=2, and therefore

`(r/z)e_r <= e_(r+1) <= (2r/z)e_r`.

Indeed `2r/z-1>=r/z`, and the subtraction is positive and no larger than e_r. Thus, for R>=2,

`delta (R-1)! z^(-(R-1)) <= e_R <= delta (R-1)! (2/z)^(R-1)`.

Because Ue1 has unit norm, this is also an l2 output-error amplification. A uniform normwise error guarantee epsilon, allowing an arbitrary seed error of magnitude delta, must demand

`delta <= epsilon (pi/K)^(R-1)/(R-1)!`.

Consequently the seed precision requirement includes at least

`log2(1/epsilon)+(R-1)log2(K/pi)+log2((R-1)!)`

bits, up to input scaling and constants. With K=Theta(N/log N), R=Theta(log N/log log N), the extra term is Omega((log N)^2/log log N), much larger than the earlier O(log N) leading working-mantissa order. This is a worst-case conditioning statement about this forward recurrence, not proof that every actual rounding trajectory attains it. Backward recurrence, rescaling, or another formulation may escape; they must be analyzed independently.

Twelve fresh high-precision controls at K=4,16,128,1024 and R=6,12,24 verify the displayed lower and upper amplification inequalities. Replacing H applications with an exact linear-time oracle would not remove this issue.

## 4. What the generating function really collapses to

For real |w|<=1, Jacobi–Anger gives

`Y0+2 sum_(r>=1) (-i)^r T_r(w) Y_r = U diag(exp(-i lambda b w)) G`.

Equivalently, this is `exp(-i lambda w A) U G`. For a single fixed w, the operator is circulant, with multiplier q^b, q=exp(-i lambda w), and entries

`(1-q^K)/(K(1-q zeta^(i-j)))`,

with coincident-root cases interpreted by the original finite geometric sum. This is a dense Cauchy-like convolution; writing it as a matrix exponential does not supply a cheap executor.

If w varies by output, operator functional calculus cannot combine it into one fixed circulant. More concretely, for the all-length nodes j_k=round(Qk/N), theta_k=Qk/N-j_k and Q=KM,

`Y0[j_k mod K]+2 sum_(r>=1)(-i)^r T_r(2theta_k)Y_r[j_k mod K]`

`= (1/sqrt(K)) sum_(b=0)^(K-1) G_b exp(-2pi i M k b/N)`.

This is exact evaluation of a degree-(K-1) polynomial at the N roots with index multiplied by M. For odd N and dyadic M, all N nodes are distinct. It is a rectangular/sparse-support Fourier evaluation problem, not an O(K) application of one fixed exponential. This is only the short-channel combination; the full Green decoder also has local chirp/forcing structure.

The checker compares a 64-order Bessel sum with the direct exponential modulation at K=4,8,12 and three w values; the largest discrepancy is about 5.5e-71. Truncation is numerical corroboration, not proof that 64 orders are exactly sufficient.

There is also an exact-model setup issue: no uniform finite exact-arithmetic generator for the Bessel constants has been supplied. Treating their exact values as free constants changes the arithmetic model relative to a uniformly charged algebraic-root DFT. The earlier certified factorial-series construction is approximate and costs arithmetic/precision; it does not give finite exact Bessel generation. This statement does not assert any unproved transcendence property of a particular Bessel value.

## 5. Exact selected-scan commutator obstruction

In gauge coordinates the v9 decoder is

`z_t=(1/2) sum_u h_u - sum_(u<t) h_u`, for 0<=t<Q.

Let T have ones on the superdiagonal and -1 in its bottom-left corner. Then T^Q=-I, B=I-T is invertible when characteristic is not 2, and `L=B^-1` is exactly the scan matrix: L[t,u]=+1/2 for u>=t and -1/2 for u<t.

For W=diag(w_t),

`[W,L]=L(BW-WB)L=L(WT-TW)L`.

The middle matrix is a signed weighted cyclic permutation. Since det(B)=2,

`det([W,L]) = ((-1)^Q/4) product_t(w_t-w_(t+1))`, cyclic indices.

Thus any neighboring-distinct weights give full rank. The argument remains true after selecting n>=2 output rows and the same n forcing columns: the corresponding principal minor of L has precisely the same +/-1/2 kernel, in inherited order. The selected commutator minor therefore has determinant

`((-1)^n/4) product_i(v_i-v_(i+1))`.

For odd N, gcd(N,Q)=1 implies the N selected theta_k values are pairwise distinct. For if theta_i=theta_j, then Q(i-j)=N(j_i-j_j), forcing N | i-j. Hence n=N selected rows contain a nonsingular N-by-N commutator minor, regardless of how weights at unselected rows are extended. Diagonal nonzero gauges do not change this rank.

The degree-one Chebyshev weight is a nonzero scalar multiple of theta, so even the first nonconstant output coefficient cannot be moved inside one common scan with a bounded-rank correction on arbitrary forcing vectors. Thirty exact finite-field checks at ten odd N=3..61 and three cycle orders reproduce the determinant and full rank.

**Scope:** the actual Bessel channel forcings are correlated through a K-dimensional G, not independent Q-vectors. The unrestricted-forcing rank theorem by itself does not prove that every restricted commutator has rank N, nor prohibit a fast joint evaluator exploiting G. Full rank also does not imply expensive multiplication: WT-TW is itself sparse! The theorem excludes only the specific constant-rank correction shortcut. This is why the construction below is not contradicted by it.

## 6. A constructive blocked polynomial-prefix subroutine

### 6.1 Exact periodic forcing representation

Take dyadic Q=KM, odd a, and a fixed beta. Write omega=exp(-2pi i/Q), or a primitive Qth root in a suitable field. Let F_p(x), 0<=p<K, be degree-<R polynomials formed from the short Fourier channel values and output-basis coefficients. Define

`P_u(x)=F_(a u mod K)(x)-b_u F_((a u+aM) mod K)(x)`,

where `b_u=omega^[M(a u mod Q+beta)+aM(M-1)/2]`. This boundary coefficient is K-periodic in u. Apart from the common normalizer, the forcing polynomial at cycle position t is

`h_t(x)=gamma_t P_(t mod K)(x)`,

`gamma_t=omega^[a t(t-1)/2+beta t]`.

Set `V_u=gamma_u P_u`, `rho=omega^(aK)`, and

`eta_l=omega^[a(lK)(lK-1)/2+beta lK]`.

Direct expansion of the quadratic exponent proves

`h_(lK+u)(x)=eta_l rho^(lu) V_u(x)`.

Here rho has order M. The desired output is evaluation of `H(x)/2-prefix_t(x)` at its output node, where H=sum_t h_t, followed by the already-known gauge and normalization.

This representation needs O(KR+Q) preparation once the F coefficients are present. Powers of rho and the chirps can be generated by first/second multiplicative recurrences in O(Q) field operations after initial roots are supplied/generated. Modular indexing has O(log Q)-bit integer costs, separately from field counts.

### 6.2 Block sums, computed across all repetitions by short FFTs

Choose B dividing K, 1<=B<=R<=M. For each contiguous u-block I of length B, define

`A_(l,I)(x)=eta_l sum_(u in I) rho^(lu) V_u(x)`.

For every one of the R monomial coefficients, all l=0..M-1 values are one M-point DFT of a length-M array. Because B<=M, the B indices u mod M in a block are distinct. Place the coefficient at its u mod M slot and pad the others with zero. There are K/B blocks, so the total is

`O((K/B) R M log M)=O(Q R log M/B)`.

All M outputs, zero-padding, input placement and phase multiplications are charged. This is not a claim that K long transforms disappeared; these are additional short transforms of length M.

### 6.3 Coarse-prefix evaluation

First pass: accumulate for every l its total polynomial over u=0..K-1. From the M totals compute the polynomial prefix preceding each lK and H. This uses O(QR/B+MR) additions besides the block transforms.

Second pass: repeat the block transforms while retaining the current polynomial prefix for each l. At a block boundary, the polynomial `H/2-prefix` has degree<R and is evaluated at up to B relevant query nodes within that block. Generic product/remainder-tree multipoint evaluation, including its product tree, costs O((R+B) log^2(R+B)) over an FFT-friendly field. There are Q/B such (l,block) pairs, so for B<=R the cost is

`O(Q R log^2(R+B)/B)`.

This includes preprocessing of every node set; there is no uncharged common multipoint plan.

### 6.4 Within-block correction

For each source u in a block and each later target v in that same block, evaluate V_u at the target nodes for all repetitions l. There are at most KB/2 source-target pairs and at most M nodes for each pair. Each evaluation uses O((R+M) log^2(R+M)) operations. For R<=M, the total is

`O(K B M log^2(M+R))=O(Q B log^2(M+R))`.

Multiply each value by eta_l rho^(lu) and subtract it from that target output. This accounts for exactly the omitted within-block prefix. All QB scalar accumulations are included. One can discard a pair's values after accumulation, keeping O(Q) output storage.

The all-length offsets have extra structure: for fixed v, the M extended offsets over l form a permuted arithmetic progression with step K/N when N is odd. To see this, the numerator `Q round(Nj/Q)-Nj`, j=a(v+lK) mod Q, is a centered representative of -Nj modulo Q, equivalently -Nat for t=v+lK. As l varies, the representatives run through one residue class modulo K because gcd(Na,M)=1. This structure is independently checked but not needed for the safe generic multipoint bound. It does not justify omitting monomial-to-Newton conversion.

### 6.5 Total, storage, crossover, and coefficient basis

Combining the previous steps gives

`O(Q + KR + Q(R/B+B) log^2(M+R))`

operations, given monomial coefficients. Two passes keep only M prefix polynomials (MR values), one block's transformed coefficients, the KR base bank and Q outputs. Product-tree scratch is at most O(M log M+R log R); under M^2<=Q and R<=M this is O(Q). No Q-by-R table or all-block polynomial table is retained.

For dyadic K, choose B a dyadic divisor within a factor two of sqrt(R). The scan-only upper bound is then

`O(min(QR, Q sqrt(R) log^2(M+R)))`.

Asymptotic improvement requires `log^4(M+R)=o(R)`. A bare inequality without constants is only an indicator of possible crossover. For M~log N and R~log N/log log N the ratio `log^2(M+R)/sqrt(R)` tends to zero, very slowly; small-size tests do not establish a practical speedup.

For the frozen-block Taylor variant, monomial coefficients are already available, with O(KR) synthesis. For the Bessel/Chebyshev variant one safe, elementary conversion is to precompute the R Chebyshev coefficient vectors by their recurrence and convert each of the K polynomials explicitly, at O(R^2+KR^2) operations. This is charged, giving the scan-plus-conversion bound

`O(KR^2 + min(QR, Q sqrt(R) log^2(M+R)))`.

With the v11 scaling, KR^2=Theta(N log N/(log log N)^2)=o(NR), so even this conservative conversion leaves an asymptotic reduction of the scan-plus-conversion portion. Faster basis conversion could improve this subterm, but is not needed or claimed here. The monomial coefficients may grow substantially; that matters below.

### 6.6 What the implementation checks do and do not establish

The checker independently builds arbitrary polynomial channel banks over F_998244353, forms the exact forcing phases, and compares every selected output of this two-pass blocked formula against a direct Q-by-R prefix-polynomial reference. It tests fifteen cases, 831 outputs, including several B values and odd cycle orders. All match exactly. It also verifies the quadratic phase factorization and offset-grid statement.

For small tests, polynomial evaluation and block DFTs are deliberately direct operations. The checker does not implement or benchmark quasi-linear multipoint evaluation or an optimized blocked FFT evaluator. The asymptotic bound is an algorithmic reduction using classical fast polynomial subroutines, with its derivation given above. Finite tests check indexing/algebra and cannot establish asymptotic complexity or numerical stability.

## 7. Fully charged ledger and remaining gates

The current Bessel route has these independent costs:

- Input scan/local channel formation: O(N) for fixed local channel count.
- Coefficient construction: use the earlier certified bounded-argument series if approximating; its charged O(RKL), L=O(b/log b), and guard precision do not disappear. Exact Bessel generation remains unspecified.
- Short Fourier channel bank: O(RK log K). The HERO recurrence replaces this only if an arbitrary-vector H executor and a stable generation scheme are supplied. Using two FFTs per H application does not help.
- Optional elementary Chebyshev-to-monomial conversion: O(KR^2).
- Blocked scan: minimum of the direct O(QR) count and the bound above, plus O(Q+KR) preparation and output materialization.
- Root/phase generation: O(Q) field operations after a suitable primitive root is supplied; primitive-root generation, exact-field selection, or certified complex-root preparation remains charged in the chosen model.

Thus the current overall count is still O(RN). Hypothetically replacing the channel bank by stable O(K)-cost H applications would expose this improved scan bound, but still would not reach O(N log log N). None of this repairs the fixed-summary exact-information barrier.

### Precision and bit complexity

The constructive subroutine theorem is exact field arithmetic. Over a finite field supporting the necessary dyadic roots, with a supplied field/root, each arithmetic operation has its usual bit cost in log p. Using schoolbook modular arithmetic gives a conservative O(log^2 p) bit cost per multiplication, plus explicitly charged initial inversions/root setup and O(log Q)-bit index work. The fixed test prime only supports bounded dyadic sizes; it is not a uniform all-length finite-field setup theorem.

Over C, exact symbolic field operations are a formal arithmetic model, not executable arbitrary-precision Bessel evaluations. The product/remainder trees and basis conversions introduce different rounding and coefficient-growth issues from the existing balanced Green scans. The old v11 summary-decoder contract cannot simply be reused. No sufficient working-mantissa bound, bit-complexity advantage, or stable implementation for the blocked construction is proved here. The absence of that certificate is an explicit remaining gate, not a hidden zero-cost assumption.

The forward Bessel amplification bound in Section 3 does quantify one unavoidable precision cost for the straightforward cycle. That cost must be included before claiming the putative linear H executor lowers total practical or bit complexity.
