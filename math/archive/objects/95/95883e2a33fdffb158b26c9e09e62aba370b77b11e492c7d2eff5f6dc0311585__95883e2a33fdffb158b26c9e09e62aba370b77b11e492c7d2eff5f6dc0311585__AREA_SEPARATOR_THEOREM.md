# A uniform separator obstruction for balanced/complement Green products

## Exact statement

Let M be dyadic, N=M^2, and let F_N be the complex Fourier matrix F[i,j]=q^(-ij) for a primitive N-th root q. Index each M-by-M grid by i=tM+u.

Order vertices by predecessor area a_i=(t+1)(u+1), stably breaking ties by increasing i. If o[k] is this order, define

    sigma(o[k])=o[N-1-k],    rho(i)=N-1-i.

Thus rho is coordinate complement (t,u)->(M-1-t,M-1-u). It is not bit reversal. This theorem applies only to the literal complement-column route used in iteration 7; it does not retroactively exclude the earlier unimplemented bit-reversed-column proposal.

In each of two stages allow arbitrary complex masks and diagonal factors:

    H_l=D_(l,0) G(a_(l,0),b_(l,0)) E_(l,0)
          +D_(l,1) P_sigma G(a_(l,1),b_(l,1)) Q_rho E_(l,1),
    G(a,b)=(I-D_a L_t-D_b L_u)^(-1).

All grid boundaries are zero. Every diagonal factor, edge mask, scalar coupling, and cancellation is allowed, including zeros. Let the middle map be any diagonal matrix, so its permutation is the identity. Optional outer diagonal factors can be absorbed into those displayed.

For every dyadic M>=8, this architecture cannot equal F_N.

The obstruction is coefficient-independent. It excludes arbitrary edge masks in these fixed routes, not only a fixed phase pencil or a numerical search. It does not exclude other column routes, extra stages, extra summands, or a sufficiently crossing middle permutation.

## 1. Rank separator valid for every grid size

Let A be the first N/2 vertices in area order and B its complement. A is a coordinatewise downset, because every proper grid predecessor has strictly smaller area. The reverse-rank permutation obeys sigma(A)=B.

Let partial B be the vertices of B with an incoming grid edge from A. Each grid row and each grid column has at most one A-to-B transition, so

    |partial B|<=2M.

For any zero-boundary Green matrix G, native flow from B to A vanishes:

    G[A,B]=0.

Its A-to-B block has rank at most |partial B|. Indeed, in the A,B ordering the sparse denominator has block form

    U=[U_AA    0; U_BA U_BB],
    G_BA=-U_BB^-1 U_BA U_AA^-1,

and U_BA is supported only on the rows partial B. Both diagonal blocks are triangular and invertible independently of all edge values.

For the balanced term J=P_sigma G Q_rho,

    J[A,B]=G[B,rho(B)].

Set h=|B intersect rho(B)|. Split rho(B) into its A and B parts. The A part contributes rank at most |partial B|; the B part has at most h columns. Therefore, for each whole stage including arbitrary endpoint diagonal factors,

    rank H_l[A,B] <= h+2M.

A sharper literal separator is S=(B intersect rho(B)) union partial B, giving a bound |S|, but h+2M suffices for the all-size proof.

For an identity-permutation middle diagonal, the cross block of H_2 M_0 H_1 is the sum of two terms, each containing one of those low-rank cross blocks. Hence

    rank T[A,B] <= 2h+4M.                         (1)

This is also a literal vertex cut in the four-grid circuit: cut S in each stage's raw balanced grid. Any uncut path entering at B cannot leave the first stage at A or cross from B to A in the second stage. The standalone verifier checks this reachability interpretation directly.

## 2. Fourier rank demand on these nonconsecutive sets

Normalize the complex Fourier matrix by 1/sqrt(N). It is unitary, so every submatrix has operator norm at most one. Its A,B submatrix has squared Frobenius norm

    |A||B|/N=N/4.

The sum of squared singular values is at most its rank times the square of its operator norm. Thus

    rank F[A,B] >= N/4.                           (2)

This argument does not require A or B to be consecutive or a nonvanishing-minor theorem for composite N. It is a characteristic-zero complex rank statement; no analogous arbitrary-finite-characteristic claim is being inferred.

## 3. An explicit all-size bound for h

Write x=t+1,y=u+1 and L=M+1, so rho replaces (x/L,y/L) by (1-x/L,1-y/L). Set

    a=9/50.

The area of {(x,y) in [0,1]^2: xy<=a} is a(1-ln a). Since

    exp(7/4) > sum_(k=0)^5 (7/4)^k/k!
             =700667/122880 >50/9,

we have ln(50/9)<7/4 and therefore

    a(1-ln a)<99/200.

The right-endpoint Riemann sum of this coordinatewise decreasing indicator is no larger than its integral. Thus the number of interior grid vertices satisfying xy<=a L^2 is less than (99/200)L^2. For M>=256,

    99(M+1)^2<100 M^2,

so fewer than N/2 vertices lie at or below that threshold. Every vertex of B consequently has area greater than a L^2.

A vertex in B intersect rho(B), normalized by L, must therefore lie in the fixed lens

    xy>=a,    (1-x)(1-y)>=a.

Let s=sqrt(1-4a)=sqrt(7)/5. Its area is

    A_lens=s-2a ln((1+s)/(1-s))
          =(2/3)s^3+(2/15)s^5+(2/35)s^7+... .

Bounding all coefficients after the first by 2/15 gives

    A_lens <= (2/3)s^3+(2/15)s^5/(1-s^2)
            =679 sqrt(7)/16875
            <5432/50625 <27/250.

The lens's vertical-section height is

    f(x)=max(0,1-a/[x(1-x)]).

It is continuous, unimodal, and has total variation 2(1-4a)=14/25<1. The right Riemann sum error is bounded by total variation divided by L. In a given grid column the lens contains at most L f(x)+1 lattice vertices. Consequently

    h < (27/250)(M+1)^2+2M+1.

Substituting in (1),

    2h+4M < (27/125)(M+1)^2+8M+2.

The gap from the Fourier rank demand N/4 is

    [17M^2-4216M-1108]/500.

It is positive at M=256 (33708/500) and increasing thereafter. This proves the exclusion for every dyadic M>=256 with an explicit threshold, not just an asymptotic limit or unverified extrapolation.

## 4. Exact finite base cases complete all dyadic M>=8

The remaining dyadic sides are M=8,16,32,64,128.

For M=8,16,32, the persisted certificates separate the last N/2 input columns from the first N/2 output rows in the full seven-wire network. They have respectively 24,84,316 vertices, below the required ranks 32,128,512. Those Fourier submatrices have full rank N/2 because consecutive output rows produce a Vandermonde matrix at distinct roots, irrespective of the selected distinct input columns.

For M=64,128, the area separator above gives literal cuts of size 796 and 3048, respectively, below Fourier rank lower bounds 1024 and 4096. The verifier computes the exact area partition, overlap, frontier and union rather than relying on approximate areas.

All cuts are checked by rebuilding every structural circuit edge, removing the listed vertices, and confirming no selected input reaches a selected output. No max-flow program, numerical rank calculation, source packet, or uploaded code is needed for replay. Removing edges or setting coefficients to zero cannot create a new path, so all zero and cancellation branches remain covered.

Combining these finite certificates with section 3 yields the stated all-dyadic M>=8 theorem. Fixed small exceptions at M=2,4 are not asserted to fail and cannot evade the growing-size obstruction.

## 5. What a middle permutation must change

Let the middle monomial have nonzero entry at (p(j),j), so p maps the first stage's output index j to the second stage's input index p(j). Define

    c_p=|{j in B : p(j) in A}|.

Its A,B cross block has rank c_p. A block-product decomposition, or adding these crossing wires to the two-stage separator, gives

    rank T[A,B] <= 2h+4M+c_p.

Therefore every successful middle permutation in this architecture must satisfy

    c_p >= N/4-2h-4M.                             (3)

For M>=256, the explicit right-hand side is strictly greater than

    [17M^2-4216M-1108]/500.

In particular a successful middle permutation must move a positive linear number of high-area vertices into low-area vertices. This is a necessary structural demand, not a recipe for solving the Fourier coefficients. A route that merely changes finitely many middle wires cannot evade the theorem; a route with enough area crossings passes only this separator gate and still needs full Fourier compatibility.

## 6. Cost and research status

The area order and sigma are produced in O(N) using stable counting sort on integer areas. The sets A,B, the frontier, h and c_p are all computable in O(N) time and storage. A specified grid-sum product remains O(N) to apply; the theorem says this particular identity-middle wiring cannot be the desired Fourier product, regardless of its coefficient synthesis.

The cut search that discovered the finite case and the independent replay are charged research computations, not advertised FFT application work. No arbitrary Fourier lower bound, new FFT speedup, or novelty claim follows. The useful continuation is now constrained: any retained balanced/complement two-stage construction must supply the crossing demand (3), nonmonomial intermediate transport, and an actual parameter compatibility identity, or change the architecture in an explicitly different way.

## Reproducibility

- verify_separator_family.py is a self-contained structural replay with rational checks for all constants in the effective tail bound.
- HALF_GRID_CUTS.json contains the literal finite state-vertex separators, including endpoint cuts.
- SEPARATOR_VERIFICATION.json records all five finite base checks and the exact all-size threshold.

State vertices are indexed by seven N-sized blocks: input, first native grid, first raw balanced grid, first sum, second native grid, second raw balanced grid, output. A raw balanced-grid state is indexed before the P_sigma output routing. This differs by a within-wire permutation from the seven-wire denominator coordinates used in iteration 7, and the graph definitions in the verifier state that convention explicitly.
