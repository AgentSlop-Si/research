# Exact fast affine-slope decoder

## Result

Let N=KM, 1≤K≤M, and e_L(z)=exp(2πiz/L). Given the K sketch values c_r, consider

Y_m = N^(−1/2) Σ_{r=0}^{K−1} c_r e_K(−mr) Σ_{t=0}^{M−1} e_M(−a t²−(b+dr)t)e_N(−mt).

For **every integer a,b,d**, all N outputs can be evaluated exactly in **O(N log(2K)) arithmetic operations**, including construction of the needed chirp table. This includes uniform-a distributions and degenerate a=0, and in particular power-of-two M with odd a and d. Sketch formation itself costs O(N).

This is an exact-arithmetic complexity statement over complex roots of unity. It is not a finite-precision stability or bit-complexity guarantee. No O(N+K log K) algorithm for general nonzero d is claimed, and no lower bound excluding one is proved.

For invertible d, the algorithm consists of K independent cyclic convolutions of length M with a K-tap filter, evaluated by overlap-add using FFTs of length Θ(K), rather than K full length-M FFTs. The true all-output charge is O(K M log(2K)) = O(N log(2K)).

## 1. Split the frequency and expose a short cyclic convolution

Define the single base table

H(m)=Σ_{t=0}^{M−1}e_M(−a t²−bt)e_N(−mt),  m mod N.

Write m=p+Kq, where 0≤p<K and 0≤q<M, and define H_p(q)=H(p+Kq). Because adding dr to the slope adds Kdr to the frequency,

Y_{p+Kq}=N^(−1/2) Σ_{r=0}^{K−1} α_{p,r} H_p(q+dr),

where α_{p,r}=c_r e_K(−pr). The argument of H_p is modulo M.

First suppose gcd(d,M)=1. Set h_p(j)=H_p(dj mod M). Reindex the output q=dj mod M. Then

Y_{p+K(dj mod M)}=N^(−1/2) Σ_{r=0}^{K−1} α_{p,r} h_p(j+r mod M).

Thus for each p we need a circular correlation with a contiguous K-tap filter. To use ordinary convolution, set v_p(j)=h_p(−j mod M), and let α_p be the length-M vector whose first K entries are α_{p,r} and whose other entries vanish. If w_p=α_p *_{M} v_p is cyclic convolution, then

Y_{p+K(dj mod M)}=N^(−1/2) w_p(−j mod M).

Every output index is produced exactly once.

## 2. Exact short-filter overlap-add and its true cost

Partition v_p into consecutive blocks of at most K entries. For each block, compute its linear convolution with the K-entry filter α_p, using a zero-padded FFT of length F, the least power of two at least 2K−1. Add the result into the length-M output at the block's offset, reducing output indices modulo M. This folding implements circular convolution exactly.

The FFT of the filter is computed once per p. There are ceil(M/K) data blocks, each requiring O(K log(2K)) arithmetic. Therefore the per-p cost is O(M log(2K)), including additions and indexing. All K values of p cost O(N log(2K)).

This avoids the O(N log M) cost of using a full length-M FFT for every p. It also avoids computing K shifted base-chirp values separately for every one of the N outputs, which would cost O(NK).

## 3. Construct the base table in O(N) for odd dyadic a

The required table is itself generated in O(N), including its initial values. Let z=e_N(−m) and f_t=e_M(−a t²−bt). Since f_{t+1}=f_t e_M(−2at−a−b), f_0=f_M=1, and z^M=e_K(−m), shifting the finite sum gives

H(m+2aK)=e_M(a+b)e_N(m)[H(m)+e_K(−m)−1].

Equivalently, for a fixed p,

H_p(q+2a)=e_N(p)e_M(a+b+q)[H_p(q)+e_K(−p)−1].

All q indices are modulo M. This recurrence is exact, including the endpoint correction e_K(−p)−1; dropping that term at p≠0 would be incorrect.

Let g_a=gcd(2a,M). The map q→q+2a has g_a cycles. Compute one seed H_p(s) directly from its M-term definition for each s=0,...,g_a−1, then traverse each cycle using the recurrence. Across all p, seed construction costs g_a K M=g_a N and recurrence filling costs O(N).

For dyadic M and odd a, g_a=2: the seed choices q=0 and q=1 suffice for every p. There are 2K seeds and each costs M terms, for total 2N seed terms. For odd prime M and a≠0, g_a=1. More generally, this first implementation costs O(g_a N+N log(2K)); the reduction below removes its dependence on g_a.

Twiddle factors can be generated by multiplicative recurrences in the same arithmetic bounds. A direct implementation may instead evaluate the needed roots as unit-cost scalars in the usual FFT model.

Numerical caution: traversing a long recurrence may accumulate rounding error or suffer cancellation. The algebra proves an exact fast circuit; an application requiring a uniform finite-precision error bound must analyze or stabilize this separately.

## 3a. Remove the restriction on a by reducing the quadratic period

The O(N) table bound in fact holds for every a and every M. Set

h=gcd(a,M), L=M/h, J=Kh=N/L.

The quadratic sequence e_M(−a t²)=e_L(−(a/h)t²) has period L. For n=m+bK modulo N, split t=v+Lj, with 0≤v<L and 0≤j<h. This gives the exact factorization

H(m)=Q(n) D(n),
Q(n)=Σ_{v=0}^{L−1}e_L(−(a/h)v²)e_N(−nv),
D(n)=Σ_{j=0}^{h−1}e_J(−nj).

The geometric factor is obtained in O(1) arithmetic:

D(n)=h if n≡0 mod J;
D(n)=(1−e_K(−n))/(1−e_J(−n)) otherwise.

For L>1, a/h is coprime to L, so gcd(2a/h,L)≤2. Apply the construction in Section 3 to Q, replacing its M,K,a,b by L,J,a/h,0. There are at most 2J directly computed seeds of length L, so seed work is at most 2JL=2N; recurrence work is O(N). Generate all Q values and multiply by D at the shifted indices to construct H in O(N).

For a=0 modulo M, take h=M and L=1, hence Q(n)=1 directly; the geometric factor alone gives H. This also handles the L=1 edge case without a nontrivial recurrence.

As before, exact evaluation does not imply that division of two nearly vanishing geometric factors is numerically well-conditioned in an unstabilized floating-point implementation.

## 4. Noninvertible d: decompose into gcd-orbits

Set g_d=gcd(d,M), taking gcd(0,M)=M, and L=M/g_d. The map q→q+d has g_d disjoint cycles of length L. For each representative s=0,...,g_d−1 define

h_{p,s}(j)=H_p(s+dj mod M),  0≤j<L.

Then

Y_{p+K(s+dj mod M)}=N^(−1/2) Σ_{r=0}^{K−1} α_{p,r} h_{p,s}(j+r mod L).

If K≤L, each orbit uses the K-tap overlap-add method. The total per p is O(g_d L log(2K))=O(M log(2K)).

If K>L, first fold the filter once per p:

β_p(v)=Σ_{0≤r<K, r≡v mod L} α_{p,r},  0≤v<L.

This takes O(K). Each orbit is now a length-L circular convolution, costing O(L log(2L)); its filter transform can be reused across all orbits. The per-p total is O(K+M log(2L)). Since K≤M in the theorem, the O(K²) charge across p is at most N. Thus the all-output bound remains O(N log(2K)), and can be sharpened to O(N log(2 min(K,L))) plus chirp-table construction under this size assumption.

For d=0 the specialized factorization is better stated directly: Y_{p+Kq}=N^(−1/2) H_p(q) C_p, where C is the K-point DFT of c. It costs O(N+K log K), including the O(N) chirp table using Section 3a for arbitrary a.

## 5. Storage and implications

Process one original p modulo K at a time. For bounded gcd(2a,M), directly generate its H_p table. For the unrestricted-a reduction, generate the h effective residues p′=p+Kℓ modulo J, 0≤ℓ<h. Each gives a length-L Q sequence, so together they require hL=M entries and O(M) arithmetic, including seeds. Multiplication by D and the shift n→m=n−bK stay in the same original residue p modulo K. Thus the H_p table, permuted vector, convolution accumulator, and temporary FFT blocks use O(M+K) working storage in all cases. Output storage is O(N), or outputs may be streamed in the permuted p/orbit ordering if the consumer supports it.

In the intended regime K=polylog(N), M=N/K large, odd dyadic a and odd d, the exact decoder costs O(N log K), hence O(N log log N) when K is a fixed power of log N. It is slower by a log K factor than the shared-slope O(N+K log K) decoder, but faster than a general length-N FFT in the relevant asymptotic regime.

This computational result is independent of statistical accuracy. In particular, it does not remove the single-seed affine flat-input resonance established in AFFINE_RESONANCE.md. Any repeated-sketch or aggregation argument must charge this decoder cost separately for every seed it evaluates, unless it provides another proven shared computation.

## 6. Independent small-instance verification

An independent worker implemented the correlation/overlap-add decoder and compared it against the direct adjoint followed by a full DFT. Across 117 parameter sets, 10,412 basis inputs and 468 random inputs, the maximum absolute discrepancies were 3.06e−15 and 7.60e−14, respectively. These checks cover all d for M=8,16 and selected zero, even, and invertible d for M=32,31.

The reduced-period base-table construction was separately checked for every a for M=8,16,32,31, at three b values each, totaling 261 parameter sets; the maximum table discrepancy was 5.15e−13. These include a=0 and nonprimitive a.

Reproducible test artifacts are in the sibling tests/ directory: verify_fast_decoder.py, fast_decoder_verification.json, verify_reduced_chirp.py, and reduced_chirp_verification.json. These are finite-instance correctness checks, not an empirical performance result or a uniform numerical-stability bound.
