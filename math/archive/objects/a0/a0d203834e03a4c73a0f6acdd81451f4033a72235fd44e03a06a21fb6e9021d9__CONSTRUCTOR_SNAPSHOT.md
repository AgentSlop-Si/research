# Quadratic spectral gate for conjugated feedback

## Result and scope

The conjugated-involution target has an exact, conjugator-free quadratic spectral equation and a separate integer-rank multiplicity condition. These conditions characterize existence of an abstract conjugator, but not a cheap or admissible one. A deterministic four-point, genuinely noncommuting two-Green ansatz is rejected for every scalar amplitude choice by a six-by-six determinant certificate, valid also over the complex numbers. No allowed positive two-Green instance satisfying all requirements is established here, and the negative control is not a general exclusion.

This note is a bounded follow-on; it does not change the frozen two-Cayley proof. ## 1. Spectral equation and exact multiplicities

Use the unnormalized Fourier matrix F[i,j]=omega^(ij), where char(E) does not divide N and omega is a supplied primitive Nth root. Assume also char(E) != 2. Let lambda != 0 and lambda^2 != 1, and let a fixed diagonal sign matrix J have r plus signs and N-r minus signs, with 0<r<N. Define

    delta_plus = lambda/(1-lambda),
    delta_minus = -lambda/(1+lambda),
    s = delta_plus+delta_minus = 2lambda^2/(1-lambda^2),
    p = delta_plus delta_minus = -lambda^2/(1-lambda^2).

In particular s=-2p, p!=0, p!=1, and delta_plus-delta_minus=2lambda/(1-lambda^2)!=0. Set M=F A. The desired similarity M=C Delta C^(-1), with Delta=lambda J(I-lambda J)^(-1), is equivalent to both

    (M-delta_plus I)(M-delta_minus I)=0,                (S1)
    rank Pi=r,                                        (S2)

where

    Pi=(M-delta_minus I)/(delta_plus-delta_minus).

Condition S1 is exactly M^2-sM+pI=0, or M^2+2pM+pI=0. It implies Pi^2=Pi. Because the two roots are distinct and in the field, E^N splits as image(Pi) plus kernel(Pi), and M acts there by delta_plus and delta_minus respectively. Thus S2 gives the exact prescribed multiplicities. A basis for those two spaces provides some invertible C, proving sufficiency as well as necessity.

The rank in S2 is the actual integer rank of the projector. Trace conditions are useful necessary checks but can lose multiplicity information after reduction to positive characteristic. Equivalently, after S1 is known, one may require the characteristic polynomial

    det(tI-M)=(t-delta_plus)^r(t-delta_minus)^(N-r).

Neither computing dense eigenspace bases nor observing this polynomial supplies a uniform cheap conjugator. These are mathematical characterizations and finite-instance verification tools.

Since p!=0, S1 makes M invertible and hence A invertible; this need not be assumed separately for the spectral gate.

## 2. Riccati-form equation and normalization

Multiplying S1 on the left by F^(-1) gives exactly

    A F A-s A+p F^(-1)=0.                              (S3)

No factors have been commuted. Conversely multiply S3 on the left by F to recover S1. For the unnormalized Fourier convention, F^(-1)=N^(-1) R F, where R reverses indices modulo N. Consequently an inverse-free scaling is

    N A F A-Ns A+p R F=0.

This normalization must not be copied unchanged to a unitary-normalized Fourier matrix.

## 3. The Fourier-compatible B is forced

Given an A satisfying S1, the loop associated with the required delta values is

    K=(1-lambda^2) F A-lambda^2 I.                      (S4)

Indeed this affine polynomial sends delta_plus to lambda and delta_minus to -lambda, so K^2=lambda^2 I. If S2 holds, its two eigenspace multiplicities match J. The Fourier-compatible B is uniquely forced by the feedback inverse relation:

    B=(F^(-1)+A)^(-1)
     =(1-lambda^2)F-lambda^2 A^(-1)
     =(1+lambda^2)F+(lambda^2-1)F A F.                 (S5)

Proof. S1 implies I+M is invertible because neither delta eigenvalue equals -1. Its polynomial inverse is

    (I+M)^(-1)=[(2p-1)I+M]/(p-1).

Hence B=(I+M)^(-1)F=(F^(-1)+A)^(-1), giving the last formula after substituting p. S3 also gives A^(-1)=-2F-p^(-1)FAF, which yields the middle formula. Direct multiplication gives BA=K and (I-K)F=B, so the feedback transfer is F.

These equations remove the abstract conjugator from the specification, but not the algorithmic difficulty. Evaluating them literally uses the target Fourier map or a possibly expensive inverse of A. They are not an acceptable cheap generator for B unless a separate cancellation/factorization theorem supplies the promised representation and cost.

## 4. One small exact noncommuting-order control

Take N=4 over the complex numbers, with F[i,j]=i^(ij), using i^2=-1. Let P be the natural row shift, (P x)[j]=x[j+1 mod4]. Let sigma exchange coordinates 1 and 2, and set Q=sigma P sigma. Both P and Q are Hamiltonian cycles, and PQ!=QP.

Consider the specific two-Green ansatz

    G=(I-3P)^(-1),   H=(I-5Q)^(-1),
    A=xG+yH,

where x,y are arbitrary complex scalar amplitudes. Its Green denominators are 1-3^4=-80 and 1-5^4=-624, both nonzero. This is an allowed two-Green form with scalar diagonal endpoints when x,y are nonzero; allowing either to vanish only enlarges this tested subclass. Its cycle orders genuinely do not commute. This is not the common-cycle Cayley family already excluded.

Put U=FG and V=FH, so M=xU+yV. The equation M^2+2pM+pI=0 expands as a linear combination of six fixed matrices:

    x^2 U^2 + xy(UV+VU) + y^2 V^2
      + px(2U) + py(2V) + pI = 0.                     (4.1)

Those six matrices are linearly independent. Here is a compact exact certificate, requiring no parameter scan or nonlinear fitting. Reduce the Gaussian-rational entries modulo 65537, using i -> 65281=-256, a primitive fourth root. Both denominator factors 80 and 624 remain invertible. Extract entries (0,0),(0,1),(0,2),(0,3),(1,0),(1,1), in that order, from each coefficient matrix in (4.1). The resulting matrix is

    [11469 50056 38545 65536 32768 1]
    [34407 11112 46107 65536 32768 0]
    [37684 11070 61651 65536 32768 0]
    [47515 58837 33924 65536 32768 0]
    [ 3593  9440 44544 39476 27744 0]
    [10779 54667 28467 52379 37708 1]

Its determinant is 19255 modulo 65537, which is nonzero. This proves independence over that finite field and over the Gaussian rationals: a zero characteristic-zero determinant would reduce to zero wherever its denominators are invertible. Therefore independence also holds over the complex numbers. Choosing the other primitive fourth root gives the conjugate characteristic-zero determinant, also nonzero.

Equation (4.1) would force all six scalar coefficients to vanish, in particular p=0, contradicting the lambda guard. Thus no choice of x,y or admissible lambda makes this exact two-amplitude ansatz satisfy the desired quadratic spectral equation. In fact the same independence rules out any monic quadratic annihilator for a nonzero M in this two-dimensional span.

This is one fully specified small-instance rejection. It does not cover arbitrary row/column endpoint masks, variable edge weights, other orders, or all two-Green matrices even at N=4. No failure frequency or asymptotic conclusion is inferred from it.

## 5. Minimal unresolved obligation

The remaining constructive problem is to generate A uniformly in the allowed two-Green class so that S1 and the rank-r condition S2 hold, and to supply an independent allowed two-Green representation of the B forced by S5. All Green denominators and feedback guards must be nonzero, and the coefficients, orders, and every setup step must meet the cold O(N) budget for the degree-two target.

A passing spectral equation alone establishes an abstract eigenspace decomposition. It neither provides the paired-Green B nor licenses a dense Fourier calculation, dense eigensolver, N-dependent advice table, or expensive inverse as free preparation. Conversely, a genuine solution of these class, spectral, multiplicity and cost obligations would be a valid constructive result; no broad impossibility is claimed here.
