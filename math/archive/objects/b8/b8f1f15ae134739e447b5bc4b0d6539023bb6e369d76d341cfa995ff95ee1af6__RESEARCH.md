# FFT global scans: exact nonlinear-compatibility gate

**Checkpoint:** October 9, 2026  
**Status:** A rigorously certified counterexample to the *completeness of whole-network min-cut rank inequalities* for a two-stage separated-generator scan family, together with a new algebraic dimension/Jacobian audit, reproducible exact finite-field certificates, and additional computational scaling evidence. **No new FFT algorithm or `o(N log N)` cost bound is claimed.**

## 0. Mission, scope, and older results

The user's goal is an **input-independent, exact transform of all `N` Fourier outputs on every fresh input** with less than `N log N` total scalar arithmetic, ideally `O(N)`, counting coefficient preparation and indexing; no memoization of related inputs, threads, gauge-dressed ordinary butterflies, or CrocSwap/kappa-style change of the multiplication problem. The previous whole-network research is preserved unchanged in `prior_checkpoint/`; its 64-point strict certificate excludes a **particular** two-stage rank-(1,1) shuffle by `rank(F[R,C])>=17` versus graph min-cut capacity 16. The rank-(2,2) case was **not settled** there.

Our next gate was to test whether the global scan network's necessary rank inequalities miss **shared-coefficient polynomial constraints**.

## 1. Precise stage model

Let `n` be the vector length. A rank-`r` **separated-generator chart** is the set of matrices `S` with

\[
S_{ij}=\begin{cases}
  \sum_{a=1}^r L_{ia}V_{ja},&i>j,\\
  d_i,&i=j,\\
  \sum_{a=1}^r U_{ia}W_{ja},&i<j.
\end{cases}
\]

Each chart matrix has lower and upper quasiseparable order at most `r`. The chart covers the usual nondegenerate generator regime; **our theorems should not be read as excluding every possible degenerate quasiseparable representation**. A two-stage realization with a fixed permutation `P` is `M=S_2 P S_1`, where `P` takes stage-one output index `shuffle[i]` to stage-two input index `i`. Endpoint permutations can be absorbed into row/column relabeling for statements about *all* submatrices.

A stage can be applied to any fresh input in `O(nr)` field operations using prefix/suffix accumulation when its coefficient vectors are already given. `apply_stage.py` implements this and verifies outputs against independent dense matrix-vector products. **This is not an FFT:** no low-rank stages computing a general Fourier matrix have been synthesized here. Finding coefficients, and their bit/precision cost, remain unsolved.

## 2. Rigorous dimension bound, distinct from graph rank cuts

For rank one, the strictly lower triangular separated-generator chart has `n−1` row coefficients and `n−1` column coefficients, with a one-dimensional scaling redundancy. Its dimension is `2n−3`; the upper chart has the same dimension, and the diagonal contributes `n`. Thus

\[
\dim S=5n-6\quad\text{(rank one, generic chart)}.
\]

For two stages, the intermediate diagonal gauge

\[
S_1\mapsto D S_1,\qquad
S_2\mapsto S_2\,P D^{-1}P^{-1}
\]

preserves their product and chart membership for every invertible diagonal `D`. Its generic orbits have dimension `n`. Therefore

\[
\dim \overline{\{S_2 P S_1\}}\le 2(5n-6)-n=9n-12.
\]

At `n=8` this gives **dimension ≤60**, versus `8²=64` independent matrix entries. This is a **global algebraic** restriction, unrelated to whether any individual submatrix has low rank. The closure notation permits possible limiting/degenerate points within this chart model.

For rank `r`, `n≥2r+1`, a generic strictly lower separated-generator chart has dimension `r(2n−2r−1)`: start with `2r(n−1)` row/column coefficients, remove the `r²` change-of-basis gauge and `r(r−1)` further unobservable parameters at the triangular boundaries. Doubling for upper+lower and adding the diagonal, then removing the shared intermediate diagonal gauge, gives

\[
\boxed{\dim\overline{\{S_2 P S_1\}}\le n+4r(2n-2r-1).}
\]

This upper bound is independent of shuffle. At `(n,r)=(16,2),(24,3),(32,4)` the corresponding upper dimensions are `232,516,912` inside ambient dimensions `256,576,1024`.

This bound **does not exclude the Fourier matrix**, which is unusually structured and may belong to a small-dimensional family. Parameter counting is a generic-matrix obstruction, not a DFT complexity lower bound.

## 3. Exhaustive 8-point superconcentrator certificate (new theorem for the chart)

Fix **two rank-one stages** and the middle shuffle

```
[7, 0, 6, 3, 4, 1, 2, 5]
```

Represent them with the exact two-direction register network of `prior_checkpoint/scan_flow.py`, counting unit input/output wires and unit forward/backward register capacity in each stage. For **all** `C,R ⊆ {0,...,7}`,

\[
\boxed{c_G(R,C)=\min(|R|,|C|)}.
\]

### Exact, exhaustive proof certificate

`superconcentrator_gate.py` checks `c_G(R,C)=k` for every pair of subsets with `|R|=|C|=k`. There are exactly

\[
\sum_{k=0}^{8}\binom8k^2=\binom{16}{8}=12{,}870
\]

such pairs, including the empty pair; **12,869 nonempty pairs** are checked by integer maximum flow using SciPy/HiGHS dependencies. The script also cross-checks 32 independently chosen cuts with NetworkX. For arbitrary *unequal* cardinalities, let `k=min(|R|,|C|)`, take any `k`-element subsets `R'⊆R`, `C'⊆C`; flow monotonicity gives min-cut ≥k, while the scalar endpoint wires bound it by ≤k. This proves the statement for **all** submatrix index sets.

The flow certificate alone would be compatible with **every possible 8×8 target matrix**, including the Fourier matrix: the rank inequality becomes `rank(M[R,C])≤min(|R|,|C|)`, which is trivial.

### Independent algebraic certificate

A full `64×80` Jacobian of the polynomial stage generator map, evaluated at a stored deterministic integer parameter point and reduced mod **12,289**, has rank **60**. The independent certificate specifies a `60×60` Jacobian minor whose determinant is **4,779 (mod 12,289)**. Nonzero integer-polynomial reduction ensures this minor is nonzero over characteristic zero, not just over the finite field. Together with the proved dimension upper bound 60, the **complex algebraic image dimension is exactly 60**.

**Conclusion:** the whole-network flow graph has no nontrivial submatrix rank obstructions whatever, yet its actual separated-generator realization family is a proper algebraic subset of codimension **4** in all 8×8 matrices. Therefore at least one genuine polynomial compatibility relation exists beyond the entire collection of cut-rank inequalities. This does **not** exhibit its explicit polynomial, nor prove that it is violated by `F_8`. It is a rigorous *limitation of the min-cut method* and a precise motivation for algebraic elimination.

## 4. Reproducible Jacobian results and shuffle sensitivity

The Jacobian is constructed symbolically by differentiating the generator expressions and multiplying `dS_2 P S_1` or `S_2 P dS_1`; **no finite-difference approximation** is used for the ranks. Forty individual coordinate updates in the small gate and random coordinate updates in larger gates provide independent exact finite-difference validation. `jacobian_gate.py`, `prime_and_lift_audit.py`, and their JSON results are reproducible.

| n | r per stage | shuffle | exact Jacobian rank at saved mod-12,289 point | dimension upper bound |
|---:|---:|---|---:|---:|
| 8 | 1 | N8 superconcentrator | 60 | 60 |
| 16 | 1 | randomized | 132 | 132 |
| 16 | 2 | randomized | 232 | 232 |
| 16 | 2 | identity | 200 | 232 |
| 16 | 2 | block-lift N8 | 230 | 232 |
| 24 | 3 | block-lift N8 | 510 | 516 |
| 32 | 2 | randomized | 504 | 504 |
| 32 | 4 | block-lift N8 | 900 | 912 |

`prime_and_lift_audit.py` repeated the randomized `n=16,r=2` calculation for **three permutations over both primes 12,289 and 65,537** and obtained rank 232 every time. At a random `n=16,r=2` point, the dimension bound is saturated, so the complex algebraic image has dimension exactly 232 for that fixed shuffle; an identity shuffle gives the smaller measured tangent dimension 200, consistent with the product's quasiseparable rank being at most 4. For block-lift shuffles, the measured Jacobian ranks are lower than the universal dimension upper bound; **do not claim those lower values as proved global dimensions without additional gauge analysis**.

## 5. Extending the flow pattern to rank r (computational, not general theorem)

Define `p_r` on `n=8r` indices by replacing each `p_1` item `a` with consecutive entries `(ra,ra+1,...,ra+r−1)`. This gives, for example at `r=2`,

```
[14,15,0,1,12,13,6,7,8,9,2,3,4,5,10,11]
```

We formulated a direct binary-integer **adversarial cut MILP**. For every partition of the full directed-signal graph, let `a` count initial input vertices on the source side, `b` count final output vertices on the sink side, and `c` its finite edge capacity. A violation of universal superconcentration exists iff `c<min(a,b)` for some partition. The MILP minimizes `c−k` subject to `1≤k≤a,b`, using binary side variables and linearized cut-edge variables. Infinite edges have capacity greater than `n`, so forbidding their crossing preserves every possible *negative* witness.

SciPy/HiGHS returned optimal **0 up to roundoff** with matching numerical lower bounds for `(n,r)=(8,1),(16,2),(24,3),(32,4)`. Independent randomized/structured subsets yielded no counterexample for the three larger sizes. **These are solver-backed computational findings**, not an audited formal or paper-proof certificate for all subsets at `r>1` or all `r`. The all-subsets `r=1` theorem has its own independent exhaustive certificate.

At `n=8r` the general dimension bound is `56r²+4r`, while the ambient dimension is `64r²`, leaving codimension **at least** `8r²−4r`. If the block-lift flow property admits a general proof, it will generate a family of arbitrarily large networks whose ordinary rank-cut checks are useless even though global polynomial constraints are extensive. **It would not by itself yield a Fourier algorithm.**

## 6. Recorded first-order witness beyond a finite suite of rank tests

At `n=16,r=2`, a deterministic sample of 20,000 source/output set pairs produced 35 nontrivial cut rectangles. Linearizing their rank constraints at a generic generator point gives an exact field-linear equation matrix of rank **14**, whereas the generator Jacobian has rank **232** (codimension 24).

`nonlinear_tangent_certificate_N16.json` records a **two-entry perturbation** `Δ`, supported at flattened indices **96** and **112**, with coefficients `11818` and `1` modulo 12,289, and a normal covector `q` such that

```
G Δ = 0,      q J = 0,      q Δ = 4599 != 0 (mod 12289).
```

`G` consists of the exact linearized constraints from **those sampled cuts**, and `J` is the exact generator Jacobian. Thus the perturbation satisfies the **tested** cut restrictions to first order but cannot be the derivative of any local parameter perturbation. `tangent_certificate.py` independently recreates and verifies the saved cut rectangles, Jacobian, and normal covector. It does NOT show `Δ` satisfies *every* possible graph cut, and a first-order perturbation need not integrate to a finite matrix factorization.

## 7. Refuted auxiliary hypothesis (preserved rather than concealed)

We tried to show the `n=8` chart has a *uniform* algebraic output matroid of rank 60 by checking all `choose(64,4)=635,376` four-column minors of a `4×64` nullspace basis of the Jacobian. **This hypothesis failed** at the tested finite-field parameter points: one deterministic sample modulo 1,000,003 had **629,241 nonzero** and **6,135 zero** minors. `matroid_census.py` stores the exact count and independently reconstructible normal-space matrix. These zeros at a sampled point **are not certificates of polynomial identities at every parameter point**. They flag promising coordinate sets for *symbolic* elimination and guard against claiming all 60-coordinate projections independent.

## 8. What the evidence does and does not say

**Proved within the stated chart:**

- An explicit 8-point two-stage rank-1 scan network has *every* mincut equal to the trivial full-rank bound (complete exhaustive integer certificate).
- The corresponding two-stage polynomial realization family has complex dimension exactly 60 and algebraic codimension 4 (independent exact modular nonzero Jacobian minor plus gauge bound).
- Therefore checking **all** mincuts is not enough to certify realizability by one compatible generator assignment.

**Computational evidence, scoped:**

- Block-lift patterns at `n=16,24,32` have no negative adversarial cut in the solver model, with solved integral optimization runs. No general combinatorial theorem established.
- Jacobian rank calculations across sizes/fields/permutations and the 16-point sparse tangent witness are independently reproducible.

**Not proved:**

- No exact polynomial identity explicitly violated by the Fourier matrix has been derived.
- The entire class of quasiseparable representations (including singular charts) is not ruled out by the dimension result.
- No Fourier length has been shown *impossible* for every internal permutation or every architecture; no `O(N)` or improved runtime has been built.
- The rank-related mathematical theorem is **not** a lower bound on all exact linear circuits, nor evidence that classical FFT is optimal.

Test `P(F_8)` *exactly* in `Q[zeta_8]` or by a pair of finite-field primitive-root evaluations. A nonzero finite-field evaluation is a valid char-zero certificate; a zero numerical residual is not.
