# HERO #0 — Phase 15: Weyl-symmetry rigidity of mixed-axis Green networks

**Date:** 10 October 2026  
**Status:** Analytical theorems with independent finite-field polynomial identities and complex spectral checks. **No universal exact sub-`N log N` Fourier transform was found. Novelty relative to all prior literature is not established.**

## 0. The target and the surviving architecture

The user's original target remains an exact, sequential `O(N)` or `O(N log log N)` **fresh-input, all-output** length-`N` DFT. Prior Hero #0 phases established fast special-input families and excluded several input-independent Green network grammars. Phase 14 excluded *polarized* (one axis per stage) Green networks but explicitly left the **mixed-axis** Margulis topology open.

This checkpoint adds a **different, weight-sensitive no-go theorem** for the original mixed-axis architecture. It does not claim that arbitrary vertex-dependent weights fail, and it does not exclude entirely different graph architectures.

Take `N=s²`, where `s` is even, and label input and output coordinates `(x,y) in Z_s²` with integer index `sx+y`. The normalized Fourier matrix is

\[
U_N[i,j]=N^{-1/2}\exp(-2\pi i\,ij/N).
\]

The four Margulis/Gabber/Galil affine permutation topologies are

\[
\begin{aligned}
\pi_1(x,y)&=(x+2y,y),& \pi_2(x,y)&=(x+2y+1,y),\\
\pi_3(x,y)&=(x,y+2x),& \pi_4(x,y)&=(x,y+2x+1),
\end{aligned}\pmod s.
\]

A weighted version has `P[i,pi(i)]=w_i`. A Green resolvent is `G=(I-P)^(-1)`, and we consider arbitrary sums and products of such invertible resolvents, with **scalar** stage coefficients. Every resolvent is executable in `O(N)` field operations by cycle solves; no such network is assumed to equal `U_N`.

## 1. Central 2-torsion of the Margulis permutation group

Put `h=s/2`. Let `Q` shift a single `s`-dimensional register by `h`. Define

\[
\Pi_x=Q\otimes I_s,\qquad \Pi_y=I_s\otimes Q,\qquad
\Pi_{xy}=Q\otimes Q.
\]

They are three distinct commuting nonidentity involutions. Since `2h=0 mod s`, **each of the four affine Margulis permutations commutes with all three involutions**. This holds for both shears and both `+1` translations. It does *not* require the two stages to be polarized: the full, mixed-axis topology has this property.

If a weighted permutation satisfies `w_i=w_(Pi(i))` for one of these involutions, the weighted matrix itself commutes with that involution. Then its inverse, any scalar-weighted sum, and any finite noncommuting product of such inverses also commute with it. Thus for every collection of **translation-invariant vertex weights**, the resulting network `T` lies in the commutant of the corresponding `Pi`.

The exact obstruction concerns **the weights** in addition to the graph. Independently varying all vertex weights can break the symmetry and escape the theorem.

## 2. Exact Fourier commutator spectrum: the global half-shift

In the linear index convention, `Pi_x` equals cyclic translation by `N/2`. The unitary DFT conjugates that translation into

\[
U_N^*\Pi_xU_N=D_{\pm},\quad D_{\pm}=\operatorname{diag}((-1)^0,\ldots,(-1)^{N-1}).
\]

Because `N/2` is even, `Pi_x` and `D_±` commute. Their joint eigenspaces contain all four `(+,+),(+,-),(-,+),(-,-)` combinations, each of dimension `N/4`. Hence `Pi_x-D_±` has `N/2` zero singular values and `N/2` singular values equal to 2.

Since left multiplication by `U_N` does not change singular values, the commutator

\[
C_x=U_N\Pi_x-\Pi_xU_N
\]

has the **exact spectrum**

\[
\boxed{\sigma(C_x)=\{\underbrace{2,\ldots,2}_{N/2},
\underbrace{0,\ldots,0}_{N/2}\}.}
\]

In particular `rank(C_x)=N/2`, `||C_x||_2=2`, and `||C_x||_F²=2N`.

For any matrix `T` satisfying `[T,Pi_x]=0`,

\[
\|C_x\|_2=\|[U_N-T,\Pi_x]\|_2\le 2\|U_N-T\|_2.
\]

The identical Frobenius inequality gives

\[
\boxed{\|U_N-T\|_2\ge1,\qquad
\frac{\|U_N-T\|_F}{\sqrt N}\ge\frac1{\sqrt2}.}
\]

Both bounds are **sharp over the full commutant**: the zero matrix attains the operator-norm bound; the orthogonal projection `(U_N+Pi_x U_N Pi_x)/2` attains the Frobenius bound. Sharpness for the broad commutant does *not* mean a finite Green-sum network realizes those particular matrices.

## 3. Stronger full-rank commutators for the other half-shifts

**Theorem (full-rank Weyl mismatch).** For either `Pi_y` or `Pi_xy`, the Fourier commutator `C=U_N Pi-Pi U_N` has precisely two singular values:

\[
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{2+\sqrt2} &\quad (N/2\text{ copies}),\\
\sqrt{2-\sqrt2} &\quad (N/2\text{ copies}).
\end{aligned}}
\]

Thus `C` has **rank N**, `||C||_2=sqrt(2+sqrt(2))`, and `||C||_F²=2N`. In particular, `T Pi=Pi T` implies

\[
\boxed{\|U_N-T\|_2\ge \tfrac12\sqrt{2+\sqrt2}
=\cos(\pi/8)\approx0.9238795325.}
\]

This operator bound is also attained within the unrestricted commutant by `(U_N+Pi U_N Pi)/2`.

### Exact proof of the two-singular-value statement

Let `F_s` denote the normalized size-s DFT, let `Z=diag((-1)^a)`, and let `Q` shift by `h`. Then `F_s Q=Z F_s` and `Q F_s=F_s Z`. With `S` swapping the two registers and

\[
D=\operatorname{diag}_{a,b}\big(\zeta_{s^2}^{ab}\big),
\quad\zeta_m=e^{-2\pi i/m},
\]

the two-digit Fourier factorization is an **exact matrix identity**:

\[
U_{s^2}=S(I\otimes F_s)D(F_s\otimes I).
\]

For `Pi_y=I\otimes Q`, conjugation by the outer unitary factors reduces its commutator to `A-B`, with

\[
A=D(I\otimes Q),\qquad B=(Q\otimes I)D.
\]

Both are unitary. The monomial unitary `W=A^*B` maps `|a,b>` to `|a+h,b+h>` (indices modulo s), multiplied by a phase

\[
\rho(a,b)=\frac{\zeta_{s^2}^{ab}}{\zeta_{s^2}^{\bar a b}},
\quad \bar a=a+h\pmod s.
\]

Applying it twice returns to `|a,b>` with phase

\[
\rho(a,b)\rho(\bar a,\bar b)
=\zeta_{s^2}^{(a-\bar a)(b-\bar b)}\in\{i,-i\},
\]

because each coordinate difference is `±s/2`. Thus `W^4=-I`. The eigenvalues of W are eighth roots of unity with odd exponents, so

\[
(A-B)^*(A-B)=2I-W-W^*
\]

has eigenvalues `2±sqrt(2)`. The permutation part of W shifts both registers without fixed points; hence `tr(W)=0`, forcing equal multiplicities `N/2`.

For `Pi_xy=Q\otimes Q`, the same unitary conjugation yields `A=D(Z\otimes Q)`, `B=(Q\otimes Z)D`. Its W has an additional product of parity signs that cancels over a two-step orbit, again producing `W^4=-I` and `tr(W)=0`. The same singular-value theorem follows. **No numerical approximation is needed for this proof.**

Equivalently, for `H=C^*C`, the exact polynomial identities are

\[
\begin{cases}
H(H-4I)=0,&\Pi=\Pi_x,\\
H^2-4H+2I=0,&\Pi\in\{\Pi_y,\Pi_{xy}\},
\end{cases}
\quad \operatorname{tr}(H)=2N.
\]

The project independently checks these identities over `GF(65537)`, using modular Fourier coefficients, for `s=2,4,8,16`.

## 4. The fourfold-invariant Frobenius barrier

When **both** `Pi_x` and `Pi_y` commute with T, so does their product. The four matrices

\[
\{U_N,\Pi_xU_N\Pi_x,\Pi_yU_N\Pi_y,\Pi_{xy}U_N\Pi_{xy}\}
\]

are mutually orthogonal in Hilbert–Schmidt inner product: after cyclic rearrangement, every off-diagonal inner product equals `tr(U_N^*Pi U_N Pi)=0` for some nonidentity Pi in the group, as established by the above commutator Frobenius identity.

The group average

\[
\mathcal P(U)=\tfrac14\sum_{g\in\{I,\Pi_x,\Pi_y,\Pi_{xy}\}} gUg
\]

is the orthogonal projection onto the simultaneous commutant. It has `||P(U)||_F²=N/4`, leaving a residual squared norm `3N/4`. Therefore the exact *minimum* relative Frobenius error over all operators commuting with both involutions is

\[
\boxed{\min_{[T,\Pi_x]=[T,\Pi_y]=0}
\frac{\|U_N-T\|_F}{\sqrt N}=\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\approx0.8660254.}
\]

The minimal **operator** error remains exactly 1: the `Pi_x` theorem supplies the lower bound, and `T=0` attains it. On isotropic Gaussian inputs, this entails expected squared transform error at least `3N/4` for every fixed simultaneously invariant T.

## 5. Quantitative necessary symmetry breaking

The obstruction is robust to small weight changes. Let `G=(I-P)^(-1)` for a weighted permutation P whose *topology* commutes with Pi. Write

\[
\delta(P,\Pi)=\max_i|w_i-w_{\Pi(i)}|.
\]

The inverse commutator identity is

\[
[G,\Pi]=G[P,\Pi]G,
\]

and `||[P,Pi]||_2=delta(P,Pi)` exactly, because the permutation topology commutes with Pi and the difference is a weighted permutation. Hence

\[
\boxed{\|[G,\Pi]\|_2\le\|G\|_2^2\,\delta(P,\Pi).}
\]

For a layer `L_j=sum_t c_{jt} G_{jt}`, define the upper bounds

\[
A_j=\sum_t |c_{jt}|\|G_{jt}\|_2,
\quad B_j=\sum_t|c_{jt}|\|G_{jt}\|_2^2\delta(P_{jt},\Pi).
\]

For a product `T=L_L...L_1`, the commutator product rule gives

\[
\boxed{\|[T,\Pi]\|_2\le
\sum_j B_j\prod_{m\ne j}A_m=:B(T,\Pi).}
\]

Thus the **operator approximation lower bounds** include

\[
\boxed{\|U_N-T\|_2\ge
\begin{cases}
\max(0,1-\tfrac12B(T,\Pi_x)),\\
\max(0,\cos(\pi/8)-\tfrac12B(T,\Pi_y)).
\end{cases}}
\]

This quantifies the strength of translation-asymmetric weights necessary for approximation under bounded resolvent norms. Very ill-conditioned resolvents can weaken this numerical bound; the next theorem avoids that caveat.

## 6. Exact rank-k symmetry-breaking barrier: independent of conditioning

**Theorem (low-rank change of a Green circuit).** Fix graph permutations and scalar coefficients. Let T0 be **any** finite product of scalar-weighted sums of invertible Green resolvents with Pi-invariant vertex weights. Construct T by changing a total of `k` individual vertex-weight entries across its resolvents, keeping the graph permutations, coefficients and order unchanged. Assume both the reference and modified resolvents exist. Then

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}(T-T_0)\le k,\qquad
\operatorname{rank}([T,\Pi])\le 2k.}
\]

**Proof.** Changing `k_j` individual weights in a fixed weighted permutation P changes P by rank at most `k_j`. The resolvent identity `G'-G=G'(P'-P)G` bounds `rank(G'-G)<=k_j`. Stage sums preserve sum-of-ranks bounds. Telescoping a product of changed versus unchanged stages bounds the final matrix-difference rank by the total `k`. Since `[T0,Pi]=0`, the defect is `[T-T0,Pi]`, a sum of two matrices each of rank at most `k`. QED. This proof does **not** rely on condition numbers or the sizes of the weight changes.

The Fourier commutator ranks from Sections 2–3 now give an exact **counting obstruction**:

\[
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
T=U_N&\implies k\ge N/4 &&(\Pi_x\text{ invariant reference}),\\
T=U_N&\implies k\ge N/2 &&(\Pi_y\text{ or }\Pi_{xy}\text{ invariant reference}).
\end{aligned}}
\]

For arbitrary complex approximations, Eckart–Young applied to the Fourier commutators gives

\[
\boxed{k<N/4\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \|U_N-T\|_2\ge1}
\]

when T0 is `Pi_x`-invariant, and, for a `Pi_y`-invariant reference,

\[
\boxed{\|U_N-T\|_2\ge
\begin{cases}
\cos(\pi/8),&k<N/4,\\
\sin(\pi/8),&N/4\le k<N/2.
\end{cases}}
\]

Thus any spectral-norm approximation with error below `sin(pi/8)` requires at least `N/2` individually changed weights relative to such a valid `Pi_y`-invariant reference. This is an *absolute number of entries counted across all resolvents*, not the fraction of their total parameter budget.

A corresponding Frobenius inequality from `Pi_x` is

\[
\boxed{\frac{\|U_N-T\|_F}{\sqrt N}\ge
\sqrt{\max(\tfrac12-\tfrac{2k}N,0)}.}
\]

### Scope and limitations of the perturbation theorem

- **Reference existence matters:** both the symmetric baseline Green inverses and the modified ones must exist. An arbitrary weight array may not admit a low-change invertible symmetric comparison; the theorem cannot simply count unequal pairs without checking this.
- Scalar stage coefficients and graph topology must stay fixed. Changing a scalar multiplying a dense Green solver is **not** counted as one vertex-weight edit.
- Other global permutations, non-Green stages, or input-dependent nonlinear decisions must have their own symmetry-breaking or rank costs; they are not automatically covered.
- The theorem imposes a necessary weight-variation budget, **not** an operation-count lower bound. `Omega(N)` weight degrees of freedom are consistent with a hypothetical `O(N)` algorithm.
- `s` must be even. When `s` is odd, `2a=2b=0 mod s` has only the trivial solution; these particular central translations do not exist.

## 8. Position within the literature and next research target

The discrete Fourier shift–modulation intertwining principle is classical Weyl/Heisenberg mathematics, not novel in itself. Graph expanders, resolvent identities, commutator bounds, group averaging, and low-rank perturbation inequalities also have established histories. The particular combination tailored to this mixed-axis Green network is derived and audited here, but **no claim of independent mathematical novelty** is made absent a dedicated prior-art examination.
