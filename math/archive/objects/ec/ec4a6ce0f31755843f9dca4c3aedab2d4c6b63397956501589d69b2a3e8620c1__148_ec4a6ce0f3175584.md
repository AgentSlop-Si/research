# A precise limitation of the coordinate-sampled estimator

This is a lower bound for the particular empirical signed-atom estimator, not for every approximate Fourier algorithm or an input-aware corrected decoder.

Take even N and x_j=(-1)^j. Then L_box=N and the zero-frequency coefficient of the unitary Fourier transform is zero. A sample from the signed-atom mass distribution contributes an independent fair sign xi_l in {−1,+1} at that bin. For k samples the estimator's error is

    Z = (sqrt(N)/k) sum_(l=1)^k xi_l.

Consequently

    E Z^2 = N/k,
    E Z^4 = (N^2/k^4)(3k^2−2k) <= 3(N/k)^2.

The elementary second-moment inequality applied to W=Z^2 gives

    Pr[ Z^2 >= (1/2) N/k ] >= 1/12.

For completeness, this follows from E[W 1_(W>=theta E W)] >= (1−theta) E W and Cauchy–Schwarz, giving Pr[W>=theta E W] >= (1−theta)^2 (E W)^2/E W^2; take theta=1/2.

Thus whenever k < N/(2 a^2), the estimator has absolute error exceeding a at this one bin with probability at least 1/12. To guarantee a fixed O(1) absolute error with failure probability below 1/12 uniformly over fresh inputs, this estimator needs k=Omega(N). Polynomially small failure probabilities eventually fall below 1/12.

This makes the v3 note's variance warning rigorous. Variance alone would not generally prove a high-probability lower bound; the fourth moment supplies the missing step here.

Scope matters: an input-aware algorithm can compute this particular zero-frequency coefficient directly in O(N), so this is not a lower bound on all O(N) algorithms. A different estimator, additional input statistics, or a correction mechanism must be analyzed on its own. Also, for flat-magnitude input, ||x||_2=√N. A tolerance epsilon||x||_2 is still epsilon√N; to match typical O(1) coefficient magnitudes one would need a scale such as epsilon||x||_2/√N, a relative coefficient guarantee, or another explicitly defined stronger target.
