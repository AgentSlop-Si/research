# Composite root-of-unity squarefreeness: bounded research result

## Verdict

This investigation did **not** establish an all-composite proof or a characteristic-zero counterexample to

    Disc_z H_m(z; ζ_N) ≠ 0,
    N ≡ 1 (mod 4), N composite, m = (N−1)/2.

The accepted classification therefore remains partial. No change is warranted to its classification through 1001, prime-order theorem, or uniform resonance theorem. Those theorems were not reproved here.

The useful additions are:

1. An exact counterexample to extending the accepted sector/congruence argument from nonresonance to algebraic simplicity, even under its same strict phase bounds.
2. A fixed-characteristic sufficient condition for a uniform proof, with fresh exact characteristic-2 tests at all 62 composite target orders through 401. This is a possible route, not an established uniform claim or an expansion of the accepted finite range.
3. More directly relevant primary literature and a precise boundary on what was actually inspected. In particular, root-of-unity orthogonality with Gauss-sum weights predates this project. Publication priority of the present half-degree specialization remains unverified.

## 1. Exact obstruction to a sector-based simplicity inference

Let

    ρ = exp(−iπ/4) = (1−i)/√2,
    u₁ = ρ(15−8i)/17,
    u₂ = 4ρ/17,
    u₃ = ρ(15+8i)/17.

Let J be the symmetric 4×4 tridiagonal matrix with zero diagonal and off-diagonal entries c_j satisfying c_j²=u_j, with the phases specified below. Its characteristic polynomial follows from the three-term determinant recurrence:

    det(xI−J) = x⁴ − (u₁+u₂+u₃)x² + u₁u₃
              = x⁴ − 2ρx² + ρ²
              = (x²−ρ)².

Its two nonreal eigenvalues are both algebraically double. Every off-diagonal entry is nonzero, so the eigenspaces are one-dimensional; this does not repair the algebraic multiplicities.

Nevertheless J has precisely the qualitative congruence properties used in the accepted nonresonance proof. Put α=arg(15+8i), so 0<α<π/6, and β=π/4. Choose

    arg c_j = −β/2 + (j−2)α/2,  j=1,2,3,
    θ_j = −β/4 + (j−3/2)α/4,  j=0,1,2,3,
    D = diag(exp(iθ_j)).

Then θ_(j−1)+θ_j=arg c_j. Thus J=D R D, where R is real symmetric with positive Jacobi edges

    1, 2/√17, 1.

Since α<π/6, every θ_j lies strictly between −π/8 and 0. Consequently B=D^(−2) has strictly positive real and imaginary parts as Hermitian diagonal matrices. All the strict sector conditions hold, but J is not diagonalizable.

This is **not** a counterexample for u_j=1−ζ_N^j. It proves that any proposed proof using only the positivity of R and the stated bounds on D necessarily needs an additional, genuinely chirp-specific ingredient. In particular, geometric simplicity of an irreducible tridiagonal matrix cannot silently be substituted for algebraic simplicity.

`independent_symbolic_controls.py` verifies the displayed characteristic polynomial and zero ordinary discriminant exactly.

## 2. Fixed-characteristic reduction: a sufficient target

Write

    D_m(q) = Disc_z H_m(z;q) ∈ Z[q].

Since N is odd, the cyclotomic polynomial Φ_N is squarefree modulo 2. Each of its irreducible factors f gives a homomorphism

    Z[ζ_N] → F₂[q]/(f),  ζ_N ↦ q mod f,

and q has exact order N there. Therefore the following sufficient condition would finish the characteristic-zero theorem:

    For every target N, at least one irreducible factor f of Φ_N mod 2
    has gcd_z(H_m(z;q mod f), ∂H_m/∂z(z;q mod f)) = 1.

Equivalently, a sufficient uniform assertion is

    Φ_N(q) does not divide D_m(q) in F₂[q].

This equivalence concerns the existence of at least one good factor, not goodness at every prime above 2. If D_m(ζ_N)=0 in characteristic zero, every reduction would be zero, so one good reduction suffices. Conversely, bad characteristic-2 reductions would not by themselves be characteristic-zero counterexamples.

Fresh exact code tests all composite N≡1 mod 4 with 5≤N≤401. There are 62 such N. For every case the first selected irreducible factor already gives gcd degree zero. The largest tested composite is 393; 397 and 401 are prime and are excluded from this composite-only experiment. The output saves each factor as a binary-coefficient hexadecimal integer, its extension degree, and the exact gcd degree. Multiplication and Euclidean division over the extension field are implemented directly using integer bit polynomials. Gaussian coefficients are constructed using q-Pascal. Exact multiplicative order is separately asserted. SymPy supplies integer prime factorization, cyclotomic polynomials, and their factorization over F₂.

These tests support the proposed sufficient condition only at their stated finite indices. They do not prove it uniformly. They do not improve the accepted finite classification through 1001, which is stronger as a range claim and remains the correct public finite bound.

A second symbolic experiment computes the ordinary discriminant over Z[q] for degrees 2 through 12, factors it, and records its exact cyclotomic factors. No cyclotomic factor at order 2n+1 appears. This is again only a bounded check. Numerical root locations of unrelated noncyclotomic discriminant factors are not used as evidence of a theorem.

## 3. Why the other quick routes remain insufficient

### Hermite degeneration

The accepted identity

    D_n(q)=(q−1)^(n(n−1)/2) E_n(q),
    E_n(1)=∏_(j=1)^n j^j

proves the stated result when n<p at a primitive p^a-th root. At n=(p^a−1)/2 with a>1, the value at 1 already contains p, and no contradiction follows. For N with at least two distinct prime divisors, Φ_N(1)=1. This investigation found no valid strengthening of that argument that settles the requested composite family.

### A q-discriminant is a different invariant

There is an especially small exact warning within the Rogers–Szegő family. At q=−3,

    H₂(z;−3)=(z−1)²,
    D_q H₂(z;−3)=−2z−2,
    Res(H₂,D_q H₂)=16,
    Disc_z H₂=0.

Thus a nonzero q-difference resultant cannot generally establish ordinary squarefreeness, even for this named family. This example lies outside the requested root-of-unity specialization; it refutes the general implication only. It is checked exactly in the fresh symbolic controls.

### Positive-measure theorems

The root-of-unity q-Hermite recurrence has genuinely complex coefficients. A finite orthogonality relation with complex weights does not supply the real positive-measure simple-zero theorem. The sector counterexample above shows that the available substitute for positivity is still insufficient for simplicity.

## 4. Primary literature and priority boundary

The following sources were retrieved through public web research. The summaries below distinguish full mathematical text from metadata or abstract-only inspection.

### Spiridonov and Zhedanov (1997)

V. Spiridonov and A. Zhedanov, *Zeros and orthogonality of the Askey–Wilson polynomials for q a root of unity*, Duke Mathematical Journal 89 (1997), 283–305. [Full author text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/q-alg/9605034).

The full text was inspected. Section 1 explicitly warns that complex recurrence coefficients do not guarantee simple zeros. Section 2 gives the roots and simplicity condition of the degree-N polynomial for q of order N. Section 5 includes the complex q-Hermite weight, equation (5.15). The positive-weight subfamilies impose additional parameters. None of those displayed results is a theorem on ordinary squarefreeness of the present degree-(N−1)/2 Rogers–Szegő polynomial. The degree-N zero formula must not be relabeled as a half-degree result.

### Zhedanov (1997), via an inspected primary successor

A. S. Zhedanov, *Gauss sums and orthogonal polynomials*, International Journal of Modern Physics A 12 (1997), 289–294, DOI 10.1142/S0217751X97000438.

Its full text was not successfully retrieved. The inspected primary paper by M. K. Atakishiyeva, N. M. Atakishiyev and T. H. Koornwinder, *q-Extension of Mehta’s eigenvectors of the finite Fourier transform for q a root of unity*, explicitly attributes finite q-Hermite orthogonality with complex weights to Zhedanov and supplies the bibliographic entry as reference [28]. [Full successor text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0811.4100).

The successor’s Section 2 displays degree-N reduction to Chebyshev and the higher-degree block factorization; it does not supply a lower-degree simple-zero theorem. This is strong evidence that the broad root-of-unity/Gauss-sum/orthogonal-polynomial connection is established prior work. It is not enough to determine priority for the present exact squarefreeness specialization without inspecting Zhedanov’s original paper.

### Lubinsky and Saff (1987)

D. S. Lubinsky and E. B. Saff, *Convergence of Padé approximants of partial theta functions and the Rogers–Szegö polynomials*, Constructive Approximation 3 (1987), 331–361. [Publisher record and abstract](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF01890574).

The publisher abstract and author bibliography were inspected; the linked full PDF could not be fetched. The paper studies unit-modulus q, partial-theta Padé approximants, and zero distributions of Rogers–Szegő polynomials. This is a relevant priority lead for the chirp-moment/Padé aspect. Absence of the target theorem from the retrieved abstract is not evidence of its absence from the full paper. A full-text priority review of this paper remains necessary.

### Zhedanov (1999)

A. Zhedanov, *On the polynomials orthogonal on regular polygons*, Journal of Approximation Theory 97 (1999), 1–14. [Publisher abstract](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0021904596932578).

The publisher abstract was inspected. It treats the root-of-unity specialization of Pastro–Al-Salam–Ismail polynomials and parameter regimes yielding positive discrete weights on regular N-gons. This is relevant surrounding prior work, but abstract-only inspection does not establish or exclude the target half-degree theorem.

### Ismail and Witte (2001)

M. E. H. Ismail and N. S. Witte, *Discriminants and Functional Equations for Polynomials Orthogonal on the Unit Circle*. [Full author text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0012259).

The full text’s Section 5 defines a generalized operator discriminant; equations (5.16)–(5.18) evaluate the q-difference version for Rogers–Szegő polynomials. Those formulas do not evaluate the ordinary derivative discriminant required here. The exact degree-2 example above independently illustrates the distinction.

### Responsible attribution

The polynomial family, root-of-unity orthogonality, Gauss-sum weights, and partial-theta/Padé connection all have substantial prior literature. The accepted scalar-resolvent application and its exact half-degree guard should be described as a project-specific specialization unless a separate priority review establishes more. This investigation does not claim that the remaining composite statement is open in the literature. It only records that no proof was established from the inspected material and that relevant original full texts remain uninspected.
