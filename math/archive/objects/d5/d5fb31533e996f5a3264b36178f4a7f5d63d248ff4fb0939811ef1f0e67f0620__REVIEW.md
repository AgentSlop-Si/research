# Independent review: conjugated-involution feedback

Date: 2026-10-09 UTC. Verdict: the equivalence, separate cost statements, commutator obstruction, and scoped noncommuting control are valid. No Fourier-compatible admissible factors are constructed. A longer supplied composition must not be called mathematically outside the two-Green class unless non-membership is independently proved.

## 1. Exact equivalence and orientation

Let J^2=I, with characteristic not two; let A,C be invertible; let lambda!=0 and lambda^2!=1. If BA=lambda CJC^(-1), then necessarily

B=lambda CJC^(-1)A^(-1).

Writing Delta=(lambda J+lambda^2 I)/(1-lambda^2), one obtains

(I-BA)^(-1)B=C Delta C^(-1)A^(-1).

This follows by conjugating I-BA, and the displayed order is correct. No unproved commutation of A^(-1) with C or Delta is used. The loop minimal polynomial divides z^2-lambda^2 and is exactly quadratic if J is nonscalar. For scalar J it has degree one; the independent scalar control verifies this boundary case.

If J is a supplied diagonal sign matrix, Delta is diagonal with values lambda/(1-lambda) and -lambda/(1+lambda), both nonzero. For arbitrary J it is merely the displayed affine function of J. A proof that J is abstractly diagonalizable does not provide a cheap change of basis. The reviewer explicitly tested a non-diagonal involution for which Delta is non-diagonal.

For any specified invertible target F, the equations T=F, F A=C Delta C^(-1), and A=F^(-1)C Delta C^(-1) are equivalent. The last equality is not a parameter-construction method unless its computation is paid for; inserting F_N into it would hide the target transform.

Conversely, an actual supplied factorization F=C Delta C^(-1)R gives A=R^(-1) and B=lambda CJC^(-1)R, and these do give the prescribed feedback transfer. Exact algebra does not by itself make any of these factors cheap or members of the requested Green class.

## 2. Cost statements that must remain separate

The original degree-two evaluator needs only one A application, two B applications, and O(N) vector work. It does not need C,C^(-1),A^(-1) during application when A,B plans and the closure proof are already supplied.

Direct evaluation of the equivalent constant product needs A^(-1), C^(-1), Delta, and C. It is O(N) only if every action and its setup have uniformly O(N) implementations. Cheap forward A alone proves no such fact about A^(-1).

Likewise the formula defining B can be used as a cheap construction only if its inverse and conjugation actions are cheap, or a separate simplification proves B cheap. Even a bounded composition of cheap scans is not automatically a sum of two allowed Green plans. The constructor supplies no general conversion into that architectural class.

Thus the equivalence relocates the same missing synthesis obligation into an explicit constant-factor Fourier product. It neither solves that obligation nor proves it impossible. A uniform solution would be a constructive result on its own merits.

## 3. Direct independent-Cayley ansatz

Suppose W,V share the parity involution J, so JWJ=-W and JVJ=-V. With legal Cayley factors U=C_a(W), Z=C_b(V), one has JUJ=U^(-1) and JZJ=Z^(-1).

The direct ansatz A=U, B=lambda ZJ therefore has

K=lambda ZU^(-1)J,
K^2=lambda^2 ZU^(-1)Z^(-1)U.

Consequently K^2=lambda^2 I if and only if U and Z commute. The orientation of the group commutator is correct. Noncommuting orders cannot simply be substituted into the original common-cycle construction while retaining its annihilator.

When a,b are nonzero and the field has characteristic not two,

W=a^(-1)(U-I)(U+I)^(-1),
V=b^(-1)(Z-I)(Z+I)^(-1).

The denominators are invertible because U+I=2(I-aW)^(-1), and similarly for Z. Thus U,Z commute exactly when W,V do. This conclusion is correctly restricted to nonzero parameters and legal denominators; a zero Cayley parameter could collapse a factor to I.

## 4. Genuine noncommuting closure control

For every even N>=6 in characteristic zero, let P be the natural row shift, let sigma swap coordinates 2 and 4, and put Q=sigma P sigma. Both shifts alternate the natural parity J. They do not commute: row zero of PQ selects coordinate 4, while row zero of QP selects coordinate 2.

Set U=C_2(P), Z=C_3(Q), C=UZ, A=U, and B=2 CJC^(-1)U^(-1). All positive and negative Cayley denominators are nonzero for even N in characteristic zero. The maps and inverse actions are bounded products of uniformly generated cyclic scans; no Fourier data occur in their definitions. The conjugation forces BA=2 CJC^(-1) and genuine quadratic minimal polynomial z^2-4 even though U,Z do not commute.

This is a legitimate cheap noncommuting low-degree control. Its supplied B implementation uses five Cayley actions and a parity diagonal. The correct architectural statement is that no sum-of-two-Green implementation of B is provided. The number of factors in one representation does not prove that some other admissible representation cannot exist. The constructor was notified to replace the initial section heading “outside the four-plan class” with qualified wording.

## 5. Cut-rank Fourier rejection

Take I to be the first N/2 coordinates and J0 its complement. When N>=10, sigma preserves both halves. Each of P,Q crosses the cut once in either direction. Hence the relevant crossing block of each Green denominator has rank at most one.

For any invertible matrix M, rank((M^(-1))_(I,J0))=rank(M_(I,J0)). One direct proof considers the coordinate subspace V_J0. The right-block rank of M is dim(V_J0)-dim(M V_J0 intersect V_J0), and the corresponding inverse rank is dim(V_J0)-dim(M^(-1)V_J0 intersect V_J0); M maps the latter intersection bijectively to the former. Thus no assumption about an invertible principal subblock is required.

A Cayley action or inverse is twice such a Green inverse minus I, so its off-diagonal cut rank is at most one. For two products, rank((AB)_(I,J0)) is at most rank(A_(I,J0))+rank(B_(I,J0)); this follows by splitting the intermediate coordinates into the two halves.

The transfer has five Cayley actions U,Z,Z^(-1),U^(-1),U^(-1), with a diagonal Delta between them. Its cut rank is at most five. The Fourier crossing block with consecutive rows and distinct columns is Vandermonde and has rank N/2. Therefore this explicit control cannot be Fourier for every even N>=12.

At N=10, the same cut bound is five and the Fourier cut rank is five, so this particular argument provides no exclusion there. The reviewer retains N=10 as a threshold control rather than extending the theorem incorrectly.
