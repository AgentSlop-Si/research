## Verdict

1. The repeated-bit-reversal, order-one, three-stage asymptotic candidate is excluded at every dyadic N>=128 under the endpoint hypotheses below. The first exact witness is a 43-by-43 boundary block at N=128. The stronger formula is r1+r2+r3>=ceil(log2(N)/2). See BIT_REVERSAL_EXCLUSION.md.
2. The paired constructor's extension to arbitrary independent internal permutations is valid: the necessary grid discrepancy bound is at most 2r1+r2+r3 for the output-adjacent permutation. Schmidt's classical planar discrepancy theorem forces this quantity to grow as Omega(log N). Therefore no fixed-order three-stage quasiseparable family gives the exact Fourier map for all sufficiently large N in this scope. This is a factorization obstruction, not a scalar-operation lower bound.
3. Dense inverse support alone does not escape this obstruction. A stage can have a dense inverse and still retain bounded quasiseparable order.
4. A finite exact F8 construction, if supplied, would be compatible with both results. A floating residual alone would not establish it. The proposed rigorously bounded interval-Newton certificate remains a separate finite-size task, not an all-length evaluator.

## A. Independent reconstruction of the cyclic-window extension

Let M=S3 P2 S2 P1 S1, with quasiseparable orders r3,r2,r1, and use the permutation convention P e_j=e_(p(j)). Fix I=[0,W), and let J_s={s,s+1,...,s+W-1} modulo N be one of the N cyclic windows of width 1<=W<N.

The exterior block S3[I,Ic] has rank at most r3. If J_s does not wrap, S1[J_s^c,J_s] is a vertical combination of at most two offdiagonal cut blocks, so its rank is at most 2r1. If J_s wraps, its complement is one interior interval and its columns form a prefix plus suffix: the same exterior matrix is a horizontal combination of a lower-left and an upper-right offdiagonal block. Its rank is again at most 2r1. This verifies the wrapped-window step without treating a wrapping set as a single natural interval.

Split each outside restriction into its part supported on its own window plus its exterior error. Group the two error contributions as in BIT_REVERSAL_EXCLUSION.md. This yields

rank M[I,J_s] <= 2r1+r3+rank S2[R,C_s],
R=p2^{-1}(I), C_s=p1(J_s).

For a middle-coordinate threshold0<=t<=N, put a=|R intersect[0,t)| and b_s=|C_s intersect[0,t)|. Deleting W-a rows plus b_s columns leaves upper-right separation; deleting a rows plus W-b_s columns leaves lower-left separation. Choose the better orientation. Hence

rank S2[R,C_s] <= r2+W-|a-b_s|.

If the target block has rank W, necessarily |a-b_s|<=K with K=2r1+r2+r3. Every original input coordinate belongs to exactly W of the N cyclic windows. Since p1 is a permutation, exactly t original coordinates map below t, so

sum_(s=0)^(N-1) b_s = Wt.

Averaging the inequalities gives |a-Wt/N|<=K. This exact cyclic averaging is essential. Uniformly averaging only nonwrapping windows would not assign equal weights to coordinates and would not justify this centering.

Thus the permutation q=p2^{-1} must satisfy

max_(0<=W,t<=N) |#{i<W:q(i)<t}-Wt/N| <= 2r1+r2+r3.

At W=0 or N, the discrepancy is exactly zero, so omitting those window cases causes no gap. Transposition gives the corresponding bound r1+r2+2r3 for the other internal permutation.

## B. Passing to planar star discrepancy

Use the N-point set {(i/N,q(i)/N):0<=i<N}. For any half-open anchored rectangle[0,x)×[0,y), choose W=ceil(Nx), t=ceil(Ny), capped at N; the counting term is exactly the corresponding grid count. Because0<=W/N-x<1/N and0<=t/N-y<1/N (with the obvious equality conventions), the difference between Nxy and Wt/N has absolute value at most 2. Consequently

||D_N||_infinity <= K+2.

Schmidt's planar star-discrepancy theorem gives an absolute c>0 such that ||D_N||_infinity>=c log N for every N-point set. Therefore

2r1+r2+r3 >= c log N-2,
and in particular r1+r2+r3 >= (c log N-2)/2.

No explicit numerical constant or universal finite cutoff has been derived here. The separate bit-reversal theorem supplies its explicit N=128 threshold without appealing to discrepancy literature.

## C. Endpoint and field scope

For natural Fourier output ordering, the first W rows restricted to ANY W distinct input columns form a Vandermonde matrix. Thus this proof permits an arbitrary input endpoint permutation, not merely an affine one. Independent invertible diagonal endpoint scales preserve block rank. Affine output indexing i→a i+b with gcd(a,N)=1 changes the primitive root and column phases, so it also preserves the argument. Transposition covers affine input indexing with arbitrary output permutation.

Accordingly the theorem covers either one affine-coprime Fourier endpoint and an arbitrary permutation at the other endpoint. Two arbitrary non-affine endpoint permutations are not covered by this Vandermonde argument. At composite N, selected Fourier blocks can be singular. A separate full-spark hypothesis could broaden the scope, but it must not be assumed over arbitrary finite fields.

All purely algebraic rank statements hold over any field containing a primitive Nth root. The planar discrepancy theorem concerns only the integer permutation layout, independent of coefficient field. Stage singularities, generator zeros and zero cuts are harmless in the proof. No conditioning or bit-precision conclusion is obtained.

## D. Complete algebraic compatibility formulation

An invertible matrix and its inverse have equal ranks at each corresponding strict offdiagonal complementary cut. A proof avoids invertible principal-block assumptions: for A with inverse B, vectors z in ker A[I,Ic] correspond bijectively to x in ker B[I,Ic] by A(0,z)=(0,x). Thus the two nullities, and hence ranks, coincide; the reverse orientation is identical. Therefore inversion preserves quasiseparable order even when principal pivots vanish.

For repeated bit reversal P=P^{-1}, introduce arbitrary matrices X,Y and a scalar tau, set H=P X F_N Y P, and impose:

- every strictly separated2-by-2 minor of X,Y,H is zero;
- tau det(X)det(Y)=1.

There are2*binomial(N,4) distinct separated minors for each matrix: for a<b<c<d, use rows(a,b)/columns(c,d) and rows(c,d)/columns(a,b). These conditions are precisely quasiseparable order<=1, including all zero/degenerate branches. X,Y minors are quadratic; H minors are quartic in their entries. The determinant equation removes only singular outer factors, which cannot occur in an actual square Fourier factorization. If the system is solved, take S3=X^{-1}, S1=Y^{-1}, S2=H; the identity follows directly. Conversely every admissible factorization supplies a solution.

This is an exact necessary-and-sufficient finite compatibility system, not efficient synthesis. It has2N²+1 scalar unknowns, O(N⁴) constraints, dense matrix construction/verification costs and cyclotomic coefficient requirements. A finite-field infeasibility observation alone does not prove complex infeasibility; legitimate lifting requires a separately justified exact elimination/certificate argument. A numerical solution alone does not prove exact existence.

The ordinary separated-generator subclass is incomplete. The invertible matrix [[1,0,0],[1,1,0],[0,1,1]] is quasiseparable order one, but entries(2,1)=1,(3,2)=1,(3,1)=0 cannot all have the form a_i b_j. General transition generators or cut minors are needed. The complete minor formulation avoids every normalization chart issue.

## E. Ranking text: claims2,5,6

Claim2 correctly described only survival of previously tested widths6 and8. It should now be revised: repeated bit reversal is excluded asymptotically at rank one, and the classical discrepancy bridge excludes every fixed-order three-stage internal-permutation family under the above endpoint scope. Small-size nonlinear compatibility remains meaningful, but it cannot supply that asymptotic family.

Claim5 correctly notes that dense inverse operators can evade inverse-row-support growth. It must not equate dense support with escape from low quasiseparable order: invertible quasiseparable matrices retain their cut ranks under inversion. Operators outside a common quasiseparable representation, higher ranks, more stages, or other endpoint structures require separate analysis. Arbitrary non-affine Green sums are not automatically in the quasiseparable class, so no exclusion is transferred to them solely from a scan label.

Claim6 understates the already verified correlation-aware result. The master has a proof for actual first-Bessel-channel forcings, not just independently materialized channels: moving a selected nonconstant output weight inside one fixed Green scan with the same diagonal extension needs repair rank at least max(0,K−1−(Q−N)), hence K−2 at N=Q−1. That closes one particular bounded-rank repair even before arbitrary-channel independence is assumed. Other input-side weights, high-rank structured repair, cancellation across all channels and a genuinely joint evaluator remain open.
