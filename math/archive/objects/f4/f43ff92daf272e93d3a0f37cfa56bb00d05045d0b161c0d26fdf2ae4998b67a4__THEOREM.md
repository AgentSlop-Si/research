# Phase 34 — A sparse-inverse / low-rank obstruction for non-additive Green networks

**Research date:** 10 October 2026.  
**Scope:** Restricted but exact algebraic theorem for products of sums of closely related Green resolvents, including common outer diagonal gauges, limited independently modified phase gauges, arbitrary interstage monomial gates, and arbitrarily poor conditioning. This is **not** a new general-input sub-\(N\log N\) FFT or a universal Green-circuit lower bound.

## 1. Full-spark Fourier-versus-row-sparse rigidity lemma

Let \(F_N=(\zeta^{ij})_{0\le i,j<N}\), with \(\zeta\) a primitive \(N\)-th root of unity over a field containing such a root (in particular \(\mathbb C\)). Let \(P\) be an arbitrary \(N\times N\) matrix with **at most \(k\) nonzero entries in every row**. Then

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}(F_N-P)\ge \left\lfloor\frac{N}{k+1}\right\rfloor.}\tag{34.1}
\]

This statement also holds for \(F_N^{-1}\) or any nonzero scalar multiple of it.

**Proof.** Set \(m=\lfloor N/(k+1)\rfloor\). Restrict to the first \(m\) rows of \(P\). Their union of nonzero column positions has size at most \(mk\), leaving \(N-mk\ge m\) columns on which every selected row of \(P\) vanishes. Choose any \(m\) such columns \(j_1,\ldots,j_m\). On the resulting \(m\times m\) block, \(F_N-P\) equals the Fourier minor

\[
V_{ab}=(\zeta^{j_b})^a,\qquad 0\le a<m.
\]

This Vandermonde determinant is \(\prod_{a<b}(\zeta^{j_b}-\zeta^{j_a})\ne0\), since the columns are distinct modulo \(N\). Therefore \(\operatorname{rank}(F_N-P)\ge m\). For \(F_N^{-1}\), use its conjugate root and a nonzero normalization. □

The lemma is a restricted **per-row support** statement. It is not contradicted by the known general non-rigidity of Fourier matrices under a more flexible total-entry-change budget.

## 2. Main theorem: product of Green sums near a sparse reference

Consider \(t\) invertible stages over \(\mathbb C\):

\[
S_j=\sum_{s=0}^{q_j-1}c_{js}L_{js}B_{js}^{-1}R_{js},
\]

where every \(B_{js}\) is invertible, each \(L_{js}\) and \(R_{js}\) is an invertible diagonal matrix, and the chosen *reference* denominator \(B_{j0}\) has at most \(d_j\) nonzero entries per row. The denominators need not commute; they can have independent coordinate orderings, nonnormal coefficients, and arbitrarily small singular values. Let \(c_j=\sum_s c_{js}\ne0\), and define the comparison budget

\[
 r_j=\sum_{s=1:c_{js}\ne0}^{q_j-1}
 \bigl[\operatorname{rank}(B_{js}-B_{j0})+
       \operatorname{rank}(L_{js}-L_{j0})+
       \operatorname{rank}(R_{js}-R_{j0})\bigr].\tag{34.2}
\]

Let \(M\) be the product of these \(t\) stages, with **arbitrary invertible monomial matrices** (diagonal scales, complex phase masks, permutations) inserted between the stages and at the two ends.

**Theorem 34A.** If \(M\) equals the normalized or unnormalized length-\(N\) DFT, then

\[
\boxed{\sum_{j=1}^t r_j\ \ge\
\left\lfloor\frac{N}{1+\prod_{j=1}^t d_j}\right\rfloor.}\tag{34.3}
\]

**Proof.** Put \(H_{js}=L_{js}B_{js}^{-1}R_{js}\). The resolvent identity

\[
B_{js}^{-1}-B_{j0}^{-1}
 =B_{j0}^{-1}(B_{j0}-B_{js})B_{js}^{-1}
\]

shows \(\operatorname{rank}(B_{js}^{-1}-B_{j0}^{-1})\le \operatorname{rank}(B_{js}-B_{j0})\). Expanding

\[
H_{js}-H_{j0}=(L_{js}-L_{j0})B_{js}^{-1}R_{js}
 +L_{j0}(B_{js}^{-1}-B_{j0}^{-1})R_{js}
 +L_{j0}B_{j0}^{-1}(R_{js}-R_{j0})
\]

then gives \(S_j=c_jH_{j0}+E_j\), with \(\operatorname{rank}(E_j)\le r_j\). Since both \(S_j\) and \(c_jH_{j0}\) are invertible,

\[
S_j^{-1}-(c_jH_{j0})^{-1}
 =-(c_jH_{j0})^{-1}E_j S_j^{-1},
\]

so the difference of the inverses has rank at most \(r_j\). The reference inverse is

\[
(c_jH_{j0})^{-1}=c_j^{-1}R_{j0}^{-1}B_{j0}L_{j0}^{-1},
\]

which has row support at most \(d_j\). Invert the full circuit and expand the product via the telescoping identity

\[
A_t\cdots A_1-B_t\cdots B_1
=\sum_{j=1}^t A_t\cdots A_{j+1}(A_j-B_j)B_{j-1}\cdots B_1.
\]

Hence \(M^{-1}=P+E\), where \(\operatorname{rank}(E)\le\sum_j r_j\) and each row of \(P\) has at most \(\prod_j d_j\) nonzeros. Interstage monomial matrices preserve the per-row support bound. If \(M\) is the DFT, then apply (34.1) to \(F_N^{-1}-P=E\). □

If \(c_j=0\) but \(r_j<N\), the same expansion makes \(S_j\) singular; thus this case cannot yield an invertible Fourier circuit. Otherwise, we make no claim about a zero-sum stage.

### Consequences

For cyclic bidiagonal resolvents \(B=I-\alpha W\), we can take \(d_j=2\). For uniformly bounded \(r_j\le r=O(1)\),

\[
rt\ge\left\lfloor \frac{N}{2^t+1}\right\rfloor,
\qquad
\boxed{t\ge \log_2N-O(\log\log N)}.\tag{34.4}
\]

This is a **depth lower bound for this restricted circuit architecture**. If each stage is evaluated separately using a constant number of linear-work Green recurrences, it prevents an improvement by simply shortening that network to sublogarithmic depth. It is **not** a lower bound against algorithms that share the internal computations across stages in some other way.

For general fixed per-row sparsity \(d>1\), the same argument yields \(t\ge\log_dN-O(\log\log N)\) when all \(r_j\) stay bounded.

## 3. Exact rank-distance identity for two noncommuting cycle Green solves

Let \(W_0,W_1\) be arbitrary permutation matrices, \(\alpha\ne0\), \(G_a=(I-\alpha W_a)^{-1}\), and suppose \(S=G_0+G_1\) is invertible (over characteristic not two). Then

\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{rank}\bigl(S^{-1}-\tfrac12(I-\alpha W_0)\bigr)
 &=\operatorname{rank}(G_1-G_0)\\
 &=\operatorname{rank}(W_1-W_0)\\
 &=N-c(W_0^{-1}W_1),
\end{aligned}\tag{34.5}
\]

where \(c(P)\) is the number of cycles of the permutation \(P\), including fixed points.

The first equality follows from the inverse-difference identity and invertibility of its left/right factors. The second follows from the resolvent identity. The third follows because the kernel of \(I-P\) consists of vectors constant on each permutation cycle. Thus (34.5) is valid in exact characteristic zero and in the audited finite fields, without a numerical matrix-rank computation.

This gives an \(O(N)\)-time **screening tool** for selecting genuinely rank-distant Green orderings. For a pair differing by a single label transposition, the rank can be only 2. For independent Hamiltonian cycles it is typically \(N-O(\log N)\); that empirical scale is not needed for the theorem.

## 4. Verified exact experiments

All arithmetic is performed over \(\mathbb F_{998244353}\), with a primitive root for the checked power-of-two transform sizes. The nonzero-rank conclusions are consistent with and independently entailed by the characteristic-zero theorems above.

| Experiment | Exact result | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Two stages, one label transposition in each reference cycle, \(N=64\) | rank of inverse correction 4; Fourier-vs-sparse rank floor 12 | All such fixed-budget variants excluded |
| Two stages, each with one transposition plus one changed entry in each left/right diagonal gauge, \(N=64\) | correction rank 8; Fourier rank floor 12 | Sparse complex phase patches still insufficient |
| Three noncommuting two-Green stages with arbitrary intervening nonzero diagonals, \(N=64\) | correction rank 6; Fourier rank floor 7 | Uniform all-parameter obstruction |
| Independent cycle pair, \(N=64\) | cycle-rank defect 62 and inverse correction rank 62 | Escapes the low-rank-correction obstruction |
| Independent four-cycle product, \(N=32,64,256\) | Both Weyl equations rejected on every sampled output coordinate | Candidate not a Fourier transform; not a universal impossibility theorem |

The positive controls include the ordinary Fourier matrix passing both Weyl equations, all computed Green inverses multiplying their denominators to identity, and an explicitly constructed sparse-plus-low-rank decomposition whose rank respects the inequality.

## 5. Scope and unresolved problem

1. The theorem does **not** apply to far-apart independently ordered Green denominators when their differences have rank \(\Theta(N)\). These are our intended Phase 35 target.
2. It does **not** apply when independent diagonal masks on different summands vary at \(\Theta(N)\) coordinates each. That variation can provide the required correction rank, though it doesn't guarantee correct Fourier phases.
3. It is an exact-rank statement, **not a finite-precision stability theorem**. No lower bound on forward approximation error is claimed without additional conditioning assumptions.
4. Nonzero finite-field rejection of a concrete candidate does not alone imply a universal complex-number rejection. The all-parameter obstructions in this phase follow from symbolic rank identities and Fourier Vandermonde minors.
5. Matrix non-rigidity results for Fourier transforms under unrestricted sparse changes are compatible with the particular row-wise restriction in (34.1).
