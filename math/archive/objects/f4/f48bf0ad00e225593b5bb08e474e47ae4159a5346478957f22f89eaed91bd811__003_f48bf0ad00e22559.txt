OVERSAMPLED BOSTAN–MORI SUBTREE FUSION: EXACT FORMULAS AND COST
2026-10-09

BOTTOM LINE
-----------
The proposed algebraic fusion is valid. Divide by the common denominator
on a pole-free grid, and all numerator/denominator BM multipliers cancel.
An h-level subtree is exactly a collection of radix-2 inverse-DFT
butterflies, with S=2^h L samples at every level and O(S h) arithmetic.

However, raw rational samples encode cyclically aliased amplitudes. There
is an exact, cheaply describable correction, including a precise solution
at resonant poles. Applying the correction lets the leaves use ordinary
L-point inverse DFTs instead of rational-series Toeplitz decoding. This is
a real simplification, not an invalid cancellation argument.

With standard exact transforms, initial oversampled numerator evaluation
costs O(S log L), fusion costs O(S h), and leaf decoding costs O(S log L),
where L=Theta(k). The resulting bound is O(S(log k+h)) plus charged
numerator/support preparation. Ordinary h-level BM subtree processing is
already O(S log k), since its node counts grow geometrically. Thus this
particular fusion proves no o(N log k) all-support algorithm when
k=Theta(log N). It is not a lower bound on better joint processing of the
constrained numerator family.

1. SETUP AND THE EXACT RATIONAL BUTTERFLY
----------------------------------------
At the root of the subtree write

  F(t)=P(t)/Q(t)=sum_s b_s/(1-w_s t),
  Q(t)=product_s(1-w_s t), deg P<k, Q(0)=1.

For a node at global depth ell and residue r, one can take
w_s=z_s^(2^ell), b_s=a_s z_s^r. Coincident w_s can be combined; keeping
unreduced repeated factors also gives valid rational identities.

Let B=2^h, S=B L. Let omega_S be a primitive S-th root and let

  t_j=lambda omega_S^j,   c=lambda^S.

Assume first that 1-c w_s^S is nonzero for every s. This implies no pole
on the initial grid or any of its squared descendant grids.

The true rational even/odd subsequences satisfy

  F_even(t^2)=(F(t)+F(-t))/2,
  F_odd(t^2) =(F(t)-F(-t))/(2t).

These follow either directly from partial fractions or by dividing the
standard BM numerator formulas by Q(t)Q(-t). Consequently division before
branching genuinely eliminates the common Q multipliers. No FFT-doubling
is needed while the existing grid halves.

At depth d, each of the 2^d nodes has S/2^d samples. An ordinary butterfly
implementation therefore uses Theta(S) scalar work per level, O(S h)
in total. This is a cost count for this implementation, not an arithmetic
lower bound for the constrained input family.

More explicitly, let x_j=t_j^B=lambda^B omega_L^j, 0<=j<L. The depth-h
leaf for residue 0<=r<B has values

  F_r(x_j) = t_j^(-r)/B * sum_{q=0}^{B-1}
                  omega_B^(-q r) F(t_j omega_B^q)
           = sum_s b_s w_s^r/(1-w_s^B x_j).

Thus the whole subtree consists of L length-B inverse DFTs plus twists.
This finite rational identity does not require analytic convergence.

The common denominator can also be propagated cheaply:

  Q_h(x_j)=product_{q=0}^{B-1} Q(t_j omega_B^q).

Across successive levels the shared denominator-grid lengths sum to less
than 2S, so denominator propagation is O(S), not O(S h). Multiplying the
leaf rational samples by Q_h yields all leaf numerator spectra in O(S).

2. WHAT RAW P/Q GRID VALUES ACTUALLY INTERPOLATE
------------------------------------------------
For 0<=m<S define the normalized, untwisted inverse DFT

  g_m=lambda^(-m)/S * sum_{j=0}^{S-1}
                           F(t_j) omega_S^(-j m).

The exact answer is

  g_m=sum_s b_s w_s^m/(1-c w_s^S),

rather than f_m=sum_s b_s w_s^m. A finite proof uses

  1/(1-w_s t_j)
    = [sum_{m=0}^{S-1}(w_s t_j)^m]/(1-c w_s^S).

There is no appeal here to summing an infinite geometric series at the
unit circle. In the quotient algebra K[t]/(t^S-c), raw sampling computes
P times the inverse of Q. Its coefficient vector is the unique cyclic
recurrence solution with these boundary conditions; it need not be the
first S formal-series coefficients of P/Q.

The same denominator 1-c w_s^S survives at every leaf. The raw leaf at
residue r, after its L-point inverse DFT, produces

  sum_s b_s w_s^(r+B m)/(1-c w_s^S),   0<=m<L.

The partial FFT has therefore only reorganized the aliasing; it has not
removed it.

3. EXACT AMPLITUDE AND NUMERATOR CORRECTIONS
-------------------------------------------
Define the desired finite polynomial

  A_S(t)=sum_{m=0}^{S-1} f_m t^m
        =sum_s b_s [1-(w_s t)^S]/(1-w_s t).

On t^S=c, at nonpoles,

  A_S(t_j)=sum_s b_s(1-c w_s^S)/(1-w_s t_j).

Therefore preweight the amplitudes by

  b'_s=b_s(1-c w_s^S).

Corrected rational sampling, the h-level rational FFT network, and the L
inverse DFTs at the leaves produce exactly f_{r+B m}. Interleaving these
coefficients gives f_0,...,f_{S-1}. The lambda^B twists at the leaves must
be undone. The correction is the same for every leaf, because
(w_s^B)^L=w_s^S and (lambda^B)^L=c.

If the support powers w_s^S are available, amplitude reweighting itself
is O(k). Computing those powers by repeated squaring costs O(k log S)
and belongs to support setup. Constructing the corrected numerator is a
separate task: it is not made free by calling it a shared linear map.
A product/sum tree constructs P and Q, or the corrected numerator H, in
O(M(k) log k) standard arithmetic.

There is also an exact polynomial-space correction when P is already
available and recovering modal amplitudes is inconvenient. Put

  R_S(t)=t^(-S) mod Q(t),
  P_shift(t)=[R_S(t) P(t)] mod Q(t),
  H(t)=[(1-c R_S(t))P(t)] mod Q(t)
      =P(t)-c P_shift(t).

Since Q(0)=1, t is invertible modulo Q. In particular, if
Q=1+q_1 t+...+q_k t^k, then

  t^(-1) mod Q=-(q_1+q_2 t+...+q_k t^(k-1)).

Modular binary powering computes R_S in O(M(k) log S) with ordinary fast
polynomial modular arithmetic. It is shared by all nodes having the same
Q and S. Applying the correction to a new P costs O(M(k)), with reduction
setup shared. This is not an O(k) per-numerator operation by default.

For distinct support roots, evaluation at t=1/w_s immediately gives
P_shift=sum_s b_s w_s^S Q/(1-w_s t). More generally, the coefficient-shift
operator on a rational numerator is

  T(P)=[P-P(0)Q]/t=t^(-1)P mod Q,

so P_shift=T^S(P) and the formula holds for general recurrences as well.

The finite polynomial identity behind all versions is

  Q(t) A_S(t)=P(t)-t^S P_shift(t).

The corrected rational quotient H/Q agrees with A_S on the grid when Q
is invertible there. Off the grid these are different functions.

4. RESONANT POLES: EXACTLY WHAT MUST BE RESTORED
------------------------------------------------
Suppose c w_s^S=1. Then there is exactly one grid point t_j=1/w_s.
The contribution of this mode to the finite polynomial's grid values is

  b_s * sum_{m=0}^{S-1}(w_s t_j)^m
    = S b_s,  if t_j=1/w_s,
    = 0,      otherwise.

Thus a resonant mode is a sparse grid impulse of height S b_s. Combine
amplitudes first if several modes have the same w_s.

CRUCIAL LIMIT DISTINCTION. After substituting the fixed scalar c, the
rational summand b_s(1-c w_s^S)/(1-w_s t) is identically zero. Its ordinary
removable limit is zero, not S b_s. The value S b_s comes from the finite
geometric polynomial [1-(w_s t)^S]/(1-w_s t), before imposing t^S=c.
Consequently canceling H/Q, or evaluating H'(t_j)/Q'(t_j), recovers only
the nonresonant contribution and still needs the impulse S b_s added.

A correct unshifted-grid algorithm is:
  - form the corrected rational function from the nonresonant modes;
  - evaluate its reduced, pole-free quotient on the grid;
  - add S times each resonant combined amplitude at its matching point;
  - perform the FFT network and leaf transforms.

After impulses are inserted, these are finite-polynomial grid samples,
not evaluations of the fixed-c rational function H/Q. The same butterfly
operations still perform their exact coefficient decimation.

For simple Q roots the reduced value at a resonant point can equivalently
be obtained from H'/Q'; for a root of multiplicity mu with full
cancellation, use H^(mu)/Q^(mu). These give the nonresonant value only.
Keeping the resonant amplitudes is necessary: H alone has discarded them.
Locating the matching grid point should also be charged; the original
Fourier support indices make this immediate in a fully aligned grid,
whereas a general roots-only representation should not assume a free
discrete logarithm. A shifted grid avoids this bookkeeping entirely.

Algebraically, if Q and t^S-c share roots, the equation

  Q A_S = H mod (t^S-c)

has a kernel. The impulses are exactly the missing kernel coordinates.
This is information loss from this representation, not a runtime lower
bound.

Over C with all |w_s|=1, choosing any nonzero lambda with |lambda|!=1
avoids every pole. Lambda=2 is a simple exact choice. One must still
charge obtaining the FFT roots and powers, and numerical conditioning or
bit complexity is a separate issue from unit-cost exact field arithmetic.
Over a finite field a suitable coset need not exist in the base field;
an extension field or the impulse correction may be needed.

5. FULL-PERIOD ALIGNMENT EXPOSES THE ORIGINAL SPARSE DFT
-------------------------------------------------------
If w_s^S=1 for every mode and c=1, then H is identically zero. The finite
polynomial is nevertheless nonzero:

  A_S(t)=(1-t^S)F(t),
  A_S(omega_S^j)=S b_s at omega_S^j=1/w_s,

with zero values away from the support and sums at coincident supports.
This grid vector is precisely the original sparse Fourier input, up to
sign/order and normalization. Applying the inverse DFT to it is exactly
the original sparse expansion problem. A rational cancellation that
keeps H=0 but drops the impulses would erase the entire answer.

If the same full-period problem uses a pole-free coset instead, then
b'_s=(1-c)b_s is just scalar reweighting, and the Cauchy samples are dense.
This is another exact representation of the same transform.

For the original N-periodic sequence, S=N is full-period alignment.
When the proposed S must be a power of two, that special case does not
cover arbitrary odd N. For odd prime N and a power-of-two S, only the
constant mode can be resonant on the unshifted grid. Thus full alignment
cannot provide a uniform all-N shortcut by itself.

6. CHARGING INITIAL DATA, FUSION, AND LEAF DECODING
--------------------------------------------------
Let L be a power of two with k<=L=Theta(k); S=B L and B=2^h. M(k) denotes
standard degree-k polynomial multiplication cost, normally O(k log k).

Initial polynomial evaluation is not O(S) merely because deg P<k.
There is a particularly explicit pruned-FFT construction. For
0<=u<B and 0<=v<L,

  P(lambda omega_S^(u+Bv))
   =sum_{n=0}^{L-1} [p_n lambda^n omega_S^(u n)] omega_L^(v n).

Thus all S values require B twisted L-point FFTs, O(S log L) arithmetic,
including O(S) twists. Recovering p_n from an existing L spectrum first
costs O(L log L). Degree-L denominator evaluation has the same bound,
with its leading term folded into the constant term separately for each
coset. Its values are shared across all nodes at a given global depth.
Direct rational summation would instead cost O(S k), which is worse.

For a terminal corrected subtree the charged steps are:

  Support/corrected-numerator setup: as in section 3.
  Evaluate H on the S grid:         O(S log L).
  Evaluate/shared Q grid:           O(S log L) once per common Q/grid.
  Divide pointwise:                 O(S).
  h rational butterfly levels:      O(S h).
  B inverse L-point leaf DFTs:       O(S log L).
  Undo twists/interleave:           O(S).

The butterfly levels and leaf DFTs together are an ordinary length-S
inverse DFT, with cost O(S log S)=O(S(h+log L)). The correction removes
the extra terminal rational-series Toeplitz multiplication but does not
remove the leaf transforms themselves.

For a nonterminal subtree, skip amplitude correction, propagate raw
rational samples, and multiply by Q_h at its B leaves. That yields true
leaf numerator spectra. Its standard cost is still O(S(log L+h)),
including the initial oversampled numerator evaluations.

Ordinary BM on h levels has 1+2+...+2^(h-1)=B-1 internal nodes, each
costing O(k log k) with standard arithmetic. Its aggregate bound is
O(B k log k)=O(S log k), not O(S h log k). This geometric node count is
important when comparing the two constructions.

Tiling a whole BM tree into h-level groups preserves the same conclusion:
the materialized sizes at group boundaries grow geometrically, giving
O(N(log k+h)) aggregate standard work, plus support preprocessing and
terminal decoding as appropriate. Choosing h=O(log k) matches O(N log k);
h substantially larger worsens this specific implementation. Fusing the
whole depth at once uses S=Theta(N) and h=Theta(log(N/k)), giving
O(N log N) via the literal FFT network, rather than O(N log k).

7. ACTUAL SAVINGS, AND THE PRECISE OPEN POSSIBILITY
--------------------------------------------------
There are genuine local simplifications:
  - Q cancels exactly in the rational butterflies.
  - Shared denominator propagation needs O(S) arithmetic.
  - Corrected leaf decoding needs plain DFTs, with no additional Toeplitz
    rational-series multiplication.
  - Pruning initial polynomial evaluation saves log(S/L) relative to a
    generic full S-point transform.
  - If initial oversampled rational samples are already available for
    another reason, h levels cost O(S h). For h=o(log k), this is a local
    improvement over standard numerator processing, conditional on not
    charging their construction to this task.
  - At full-period resonance the grid input is sparse; ordinary sparse-
    input/pruned FFT processing can recover O(S log k), rather than a
    literal unpruned O(S log S). That is the established sparse transform,
    not a new bound obtained from rational division.

These do not establish an end-to-end little-o improvement. A genuinely
new algorithm could still exploit the k-dimensional family jointly,
fuse the initial evaluation through the reciprocal-Q diagonal and the
inverse transforms, or exploit special support collisions. Nothing here
rules that out. The exact factorization identifies the work such an
algorithm must actually accelerate rather than proving it impossible.

For k=Theta(log N), all polynomial(k,log N) support setup is negligible
relative to N log k, but a fresh O(S log k) transform per materialized
subtree is not. Declaring support-dependent matrices precomputed pays
for their entries, not for their amplitude-dependent applications.

  540 pole-free cases;
  540 modular numerator-correction checks;
  7,230 raw parity-leaf values checked against partial fractions;
  7,230 corrected decoded coefficients checked against direct expansion.

The separate check_cyclic_rational_fusion.py independent test
covers 960 finite-field cases, including 200 resonant cases and 384
resonant grid values. The uncorrected alias formula passes all 760
pole-free cases; 758 differ from the desired unaliased vector. It also
checks the modular numerator correction in all 960 cases. Results:
cyclic_rational_checks.json. These are identity checks with direct small
polynomial arithmetic/DFTs, not runtime benchmarks or complexity proofs.

The O(S log L) pruned-transform construction is explicitly derived above.
As an independent primary implementation reference, FFTW's authors
explain O(N log K) pruning for K nonzero inputs, including the duality
with keeping the first K outputs:
https://fftw.org/pruned.html

The rational fusion, correction, and resonance identities here are direct
finite algebraic derivations. No novelty or general FFT lower-bound claim
is being made.
