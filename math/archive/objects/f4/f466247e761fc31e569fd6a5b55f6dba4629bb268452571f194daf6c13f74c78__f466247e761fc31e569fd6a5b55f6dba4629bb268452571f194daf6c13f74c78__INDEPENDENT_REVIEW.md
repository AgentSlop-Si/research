# Independent review: bounded-fragmentation middle permutations collapse four stages

Date: 2026-10-09 UTC. Result proposed by the paired constructor and independently proved here. No uploaded program was executed. This report is separate from all previously sealed milestones.

## Verdict

The reduction is valid for the full quasiseparable class, including zero transition coefficients, rank-deficient cut blocks, and singular individual stages. A four-stage family with a middle multiplier-by-three permutation and uniformly bounded stage orders cannot represent all sufficiently large Fourier transforms in the endpoint scope below. The general argument gives an asymptotic obstruction. The accompanying `EXPLICIT_CUTOFF_AND_GRAY_REVIEW.md` independently verifies the constructor's subsequent concrete cutoff: the four-QS1 `R-A3-R` word fails at every dyadic length `N>=2^27`.

Use permutation matrices with `Q e_j = e_{q(j)}` and write the original word as

\[
M=S_4P_3S_3Q S_2P_1S_1.
\]

Here `S_i` has quasiseparable order at most `r_i`: both strictly off-diagonal blocks across every natural prefix/suffix cut have rank at most `r_i`. For `q(j)=3j mod N`, with `gcd(3,N)=1`, define

\[
T=S_3(QS_2Q^{-1}).
\]

Then `T` has order at most `r_3+5r_2`, and the exact identity is

\[
M=S_4P_3 T(QP_1)S_1.
\]

Thus the existing three-stage discrepancy theorem applies. In its strongest directly used form, the grid star discrepancy of the permutation `p_3^{-1}` is at most

\[
2r_1+r_3+5r_2+r_4.
\]

Its transpose form similarly bounds the other outside permutation with `r_1+r_3+5r_2+2r_4`. The classical two-dimensional discrepancy lower bound forces these weighted order budgets to grow at least proportionally to `log N`. If all four orders are bounded by `r`, the first displayed budget is at most `9r`.

## 1. Exact boundary-count lemma

For a subset `K` of `{0,...,N-1}`, let

\[
\gamma(K)=\#\{j:0\le j<N-1,\quad 1_K(j)\ne 1_K(j+1)\}.
\]

If `S` is quasiseparable of order `r`, then both `S[K,K^c]` and `S[K^c,K]` have rank at most `gamma(K) r`.

Proof: partition `K` into its nonempty maximal natural intervals. For an interval `[a,b)`, the columns in `K^c` to its left form a submatrix of the lower off-diagonal block at `a`, and those to its right form a submatrix of the upper off-diagonal block at `b`. Each has rank at most `r`; a side is absent when the interval touches the corresponding endpoint. Horizontal concatenation adds at most the two ranks, then vertical concatenation over all intervals adds their bounds. The total number of present sides is exactly `gamma(K)`. To bound the reverse block, apply the same argument to `S^T`, which has the same quasiseparable order. No invertibility assumption occurs.

In particular, if `K` consists of `b` intervals,

\[
\gamma(K)=2b-1_{0\in K}-1_{N-1\in K}.
\]

The often-used sharpened bound `(2b-1)r` therefore requires at least one endpoint in `K`. An arbitrary `b`-interval set has only the safe bound `2br`.

## 2. Conjugation and multiplication

Across a prefix `I=[0,t)`, a conjugated cut of `QSQ^{-1}` is, up to row and column ordering, `S[K,K^c]` for `K=q^{-1}(I)`. Therefore

\[
\operatorname{qs}(QSQ^{-1})\le\Gamma(q)r,
\qquad
\Gamma(q)=\max_{1\le t<N}\gamma(q^{-1}([0,t))).
\]

This is a permutation-only condition. Bounded prefix fragmentation gives bounded `Gamma`, even when the permutation is not affine.

For the unshifted positive multiplier `q(j)=a j mod N`, `1<=a<N`, the preimage of a nonempty proper prefix is the union of at most `a` natural intervals. This follows by grouping integers `j` according to `floor(a j/N)` and solving `ell N <= a j < ell N+t` in each group. It contains index zero, so `Gamma(q)<=2a-1`. In particular `a=3` gives `Gamma<=5`. Dyadic lengths always satisfy `gcd(3,N)=1`.

Finally, quasiseparable orders are subadditive under multiplication: across `I,I^c`,

\[
(AB)[I,I^c]=A[I,I]B[I,I^c]+A[I,I^c]B[I^c,I^c],
\]

whose rank is at most `qs(B)+qs(A)`; the lower block is identical in form. This proves the claimed order of `T` and the four-to-three collapse.

## 3. Fourier endpoint scope and exclusions

The invoked discrepancy theorem needs every block formed from an output prefix and a cyclic input interval of the same width to have full rank. Natural Fourier indexing satisfies this by the Vandermonde determinant. Either one endpoint can instead be affine with a multiplier coprime to `N`, while the opposite endpoint can be an arbitrary permutation: consecutive affine Fourier powers evaluated at distinct roots still give a full-rank Vandermonde matrix. Transposition handles the opposite endpoint choice. Nonsingular diagonal endpoint scalings preserve all ranks.

At complex prime lengths the full-spark theorem allows both endpoint permutations arbitrary. For composite or dyadic lengths with both endpoint permutations arbitrary, this report makes no such extension. The matrix word uses no outside dense mixers. It concerns a fixed number of quasiseparable stages, not unrestricted arithmetic circuits or an arithmetic lower bound for the FFT.

For the proposed `R-A3-R` word, finite cut-capacity survival through previously tested lengths was only a necessary condition. This algebraic collapse now rules out that fixed-order word as an all-dyadic construction. It does not assert that every individual size fails.

## 4. Independent exact controls

`check_collapse.py` is newly authored standard-library Python. It constructs scalar-transition quasiseparable matrices over `F_1009`, including zero transitions and orders 0, 1, and 2. It verifies the boundary-count lemma on every nontrivial subset for `N=2,...,8`, multiplier conjugation at lengths 8, 16, 32, 40, and 64, and nine full four-stage versus collapsed-three-stage matrix identities with independently shuffled outside permutations. All checks pass.

The controls include a quasiseparable-order-one matrix whose `A3` conjugate has order exactly five. Thus replacing five by a smaller universal constant would require extra hypotheses. These finite controls support the proof and do not replace it.

## 5. Relation to the approved discrepancy proof

The underlying three-stage derivation is in the separately sealed `review_three_stage/THREE_STAGE_REVIEW.md`. For completeness, write a three-stage word as `A P B Q C`, with stage orders `a,b,c`. Fix an output prefix `I` of width `W` and any cyclic input window `J` of width `W`. Expanding first along `I,I^c` at the outside left stage, and then along `J,J^c` at the outside right stage, shows

\[
\operatorname{rank}M[I,J]\le a+2c+\operatorname{rank}B[p^{-1}(I),q(J)].
\]

The cyclic-window exterior block of `C` has rank at most `2c`, including a wrapped window: its prefix and suffix pieces each cross one natural cut. Put `x=|p^{-1}(I) intersect [0,t)|` and `y=|q(J) intersect [0,t)|`. Deleting `W-|x-y|` rows and columns in total leaves an upper or lower strictly separated block of `B`, so its original rank is at most `b+W-|x-y|`. Full Fourier rank `W` then implies `|x-y|<=a+b+2c`.

Average over all `N` cyclic windows `J`. Every input coordinate occurs in exactly `W` of them, so the mean of `y` is exactly `Wt/N`. Hence `|x-Wt/N|<=a+b+2c`. This proves the first discrepancy inequality used here; transposition gives the second. Averaging only nonwrapping windows would not be valid.

The primary source for the unnormalized discrepancy lower bound is Bilyk, Lacey, Parissis and Vagharshakyan, [arXiv:0810.5544](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0810.5544), page 2, equation (1.2), quoting Schmidt's planar theorem. Grid and continuous discrepancy differ by at most two. No explicit numerical Schmidt constant is used here.
