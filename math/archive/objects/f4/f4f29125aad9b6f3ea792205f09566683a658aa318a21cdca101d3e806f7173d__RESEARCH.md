# Hero #17, Phase 5 — Gauge-invariant audit of square-length orbit union

**Date:** October 10, 2026  
**Status:** Exact finite-field and complex-analytic certificates. **No new uniform sub-FFT upper bound.**  
**Objective:** Test whether the rank-one-displacement orbit matrices from Phase 4 can be shared across *all* frequency channels to reduce the full arbitrary-input DFT to \(O(N)\) sequential arithmetic, with coefficient recovery and precomputation charged.

## Executive verdict

The canonical square orbit basis has an exact multiscale transition-mask rank law, and its optimal spanning-tree **mask-rank budget** can be computed exactly for every \(M\). However, the same arbitrary-input DFT admits gauges whose transition-mask rank can be **any integer from 1 to \(M\)**, without changing the end-to-end transform. Therefore, low transition-mask rank is not a gauge-invariant measure of arithmetic speedup. In one natural universal gauge, the coefficient-recovery matrices are extremely ill-conditioned over the complex numbers. In a stable Fourier gauge, the intermediate factors collapse algebraically to the classical FFT stages. These constitute mathematically scoped rejection tests, not a lower bound for unrestricted exact Fourier circuits.

All experiments include the original raw vector; no orbit coefficients are supplied gratis. The reference transforms and phase ranks use exact prime-field arithmetic with \(M^2\mid p-1\). Numerical stability is separately audited at high precision over \(\mathbb C\).

## 1. Complete arbitrary-input contract

Let \(N=M^2\), \(M\ge2\), \(\omega\) a primitive \(M^2\)-th root of unity and \(\eta=\omega^M\), which has order \(M\). Assume a field of characteristic not dividing \(M\). Index raw data as

\[
y_t[u]=x_{t+Mu},\quad 0\le t,u<M.\]

Define fully charged fiber spectra

\[
C_t[h]=\sum_{u=0}^{M-1} x_{t+Mu}\eta^{-hu}.
\]

The full unnormalized DFT is, for \(k=h+sM\),

\[
X[h+sM]=\sum_{t=0}^{M-1}\eta^{-st}\omega^{-ht}C_t[h].
\]

Put \(A_h[s,t]=\eta^{-st}\omega^{-ht}\). A complete algorithm must read \(x\), produce \(C\), and compute the entire collection \(A_h C[:,h]\), returning \(M^2\) explicit outputs. It must charge any auxiliary basis or plans.

## 2. Universal gauge-freedom theorem (exact)

Choose *arbitrary* integer shears \(a_0,\ldots,a_{M-1}\), each modulo \(M\), and let

\[
\mu_j=\omega^j,\qquad
\lambda_j(h)=\mu_j\eta^{-h a_j}=\omega^{j-Mha_j},\qquad
V_h[t,j]=\lambda_j(h)^t.
\]

**Invertibility for every gauge and every channel:** If \(\lambda_j(h)=\lambda_{j'}(h)\), the primitive-root property implies

\[
j-j'-Mh(a_j-a_{j'})\equiv0\pmod{M^2}.
\]

Reducing modulo \(M\) gives \(j\equiv j'\pmod M\), and since both lie in \(\{0,\ldots,M-1\}\), they are equal. Therefore the nodes are distinct and each square Vandermonde \(V_h\) is invertible.

Set \(B_h=V_h^{-1} C[:,h]\). The orbit-output geometric matrix is

\[
K_h[s,j]=\sum_{t=0}^{M-1}\bigl(\omega^{-h}\eta^{-s}\lambda_j(h)\bigr)^t.
\]

Multiplying out shows \(K_h=A_hV_h\), whence

\[
\boxed{K_h B_h=K_hV_h^{-1}C[:,h]=A_hC[:,h].}
\]

**Every shear choice computes the identical full-input DFT.** Changing the algebraic presentation, even to lower the mask rank, is not automatically a faster algorithm.

For the same entire class of gauges \(\mu_j=\omega^j\), the geometric routing condition is \(j=h\). Every channel has one orbit column with exactly one nonzero row, and \(M-1\) entirely nonzero columns. Thus each channel has \(M^2-M+1\) nonzero entries in \(K_h\), and all channels together have \(M^3-M^2+M\) nonzeros, independent of \(a_j\). This is only an edge count, not an arithmetic-circuit lower bound.

## 3. Exact Schur-transition-rank theorem

For any \(\Delta\pmod M\), consecutive channel representations satisfy

\[
V_{h+\Delta}=V_h\odot E_\Delta,
\quad E_\Delta[t,j]=\eta^{-\Delta t a_j}.
\]

Let \(R_\Delta=\{\Delta a_j\bmod M:0\le j<M\}\). Columns associated with the same residue are identical. Columns associated with distinct residues are linearly independent: they are distinct columns of the invertible size-\(M\) Fourier matrix. Therefore

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{rank} E_\Delta=|R_\Delta|.}
\]

The proof is constructive. Define \(L[t,r]=\eta^{-tr}\), for \(r\in R_\Delta\), and \(R[r,j]=\mathbf1\{\Delta a_j\equiv r\pmod M\}\). Then \(E_\Delta=LR\), with rank \(|R_\Delta|\). The matrix rank is the minimum number of **separable row-column masks** required for an exact Schur decomposition. It does not measure all arithmetic circuits.

**Specialization to Phase 4:** If \(a_j=j\), multiplication by \(\Delta\) on the cyclic group has an image of size \(M/\gcd(M,\Delta)\). Thus

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{rank} E_\Delta=\frac{M}{\gcd(M,\Delta)}}
\]

(including rank 1 for \(\Delta=0\)). If \(\gcd(\Delta,M)=1\), by choosing the shear list to use exactly \(r\) distinct residues for any \(1\le r\le M\), we can make \(\operatorname{rank} E_\Delta=r\), **without sacrificing universality**. Hence the mask-rank metric is gauge-dependent and cannot alone certify a speedup.

## 4. Minimum spanning-tree mask budget — all dimensions

For the canonical \(a_j=j\) representation, assign to an edge between channel labels \(h\ne h'\) the weight

\[
w_M(h,h')=M/\gcd(M,h-h').
\]

This is a well-defined **proxy**: the minimum number of separable Schur masks on that edge. Define the proxy cost of a spanning tree by the sum of edge weights. We solve this *restricted combinatorial optimization* exactly.

Sort divisors \(d_1<\dots<d_z\) of \(M\) greater than 1. Define \(L_0=1\), \(L_i=\operatorname{lcm}(L_{i-1},d_i)\). At threshold \(d_i\), the graph containing all edges of weight at most \(d_i\) connects precisely the cosets of the unique subgroup of \(\mathbb Z_M\) of order \(L_i\). It thus has \(M/L_i\) components.

Kruskal's theorem yields the exact optimal budget

\[
\boxed{W(M)=\sum_{i=1}^{z}d_i\left(\frac{M}{L_{i-1}}-\frac{M}{L_i}\right).}
\]

If \(M=2^m\), each power-of-two level costs precisely \(M\), hence

\[
\boxed{W(2^m)=M\log_2 M.}
\]

For prime \(M\), every nontrivial edge costs \(M\) and \(W(M)=M(M-1)\). For non-prime-power \(M\), the lcm formula captures interactions among several divisor chains; for instance \(W(6)=12\), \(W(12)=28\).

**Scope restriction:** \(W\) minimizes the *sum of per-edge Schur ranks* along a spanning tree, with independently factored transitions. It is **not** a lower bound on the field operations needed for the arbitrary-input FFT, since circuits can bypass those masks entirely. More decisively, the all-zero-shear universal gauge has transition-mask rank 1 on every edge, making its own tree proxy only \(M-1\), while the full DFT map remains unchanged.

## 5. Complex numerical-stability gate

The \(\mu_j=\omega^j\) universal family has \(V_0[t,j]=\omega^{tj}\), *independent of every shear \(a_j\)*. Over \(\mathbb C\), take \(\omega=\exp(2\pi i/M^2)\).

Consider the binomial coefficient vector \(v_j=(-1)^j\binom{M-1}{j}\). Then

\[
(V_0v)_t=(1-\omega^t)^{M-1},\qquad
\|v\|_2^2=\binom{2M-2}{M-1}.
\]

For \(0\le t<M\), \(|1-\omega^t|\le2\pi/M\). So

\[
\sigma_{\min}(V_0)\le\frac{\sqrt M(2\pi/M)^{M-1}}{\sqrt{\binom{2M-2}{M-1}}}.
\]

Every column of \(V_0\) has norm \(\sqrt M\), giving \(\sigma_{\max}(V_0)\ge\sqrt M\). Combining,

\[
\boxed{\kappa_2(V_0)\ge\sqrt{\binom{2M-2}{M-1}}\left(\frac{M}{2\pi}\right)^{M-1}.}
\]

This bound becomes extremely large as \(M\to\infty\). Since all shear lists share the identical \(V_0\), optimizing their transition-mask ranks **cannot remove this conditioning problem**. The finite-field calculations remain exact, but floating-point implementations require a separate error analysis.

## 6. Orthogonal gauge positive control (and why it does not improve work)

Instead choose \(\mu_j=\eta^j\) and a common shear \(a_j=a\). Then

\[
V_h[t,j]=\eta^{-aht}\eta^{jt}
\]

is a row-scaled Fourier matrix. In complex arithmetic, its condition number is 1 for *all* \(h\). The transition masks have rank 1. But coefficient recovery and application still contain the full temporal Fourier transformation.

For \(a=0\), the inverse is explicitly \(V_h^{-1}=M^{-1}F_M\), where \(F_M[s,t]=\eta^{-st}\). The output matrix is

\[
K_h=F_M\operatorname{diag}(\omega^{-ht})M F_M^{-1},
\]

which is circulant. Hence

\[
K_hV_h^{-1}=F_M\operatorname{diag}(\omega^{-ht}),
\]

and cancellation recovers precisely the standard second Fourier stage. At \(h=0\), \(K_0=M I\) (all-orbit routing); for all \(h\ne0\), every entry of \(K_h\) is nonzero. This is not linear-time work from fresh input.

**Prime-index rigidity:** For prime \(M\) and \(\mu_j=\eta^j\), requiring all \(V_h\) invertible for all \(h\in\mathbb Z_M\) forces all \(a_j\) to be equal. Indeed, if \(a_j\ne a_k\), then \(h=(j-k)/(a_j-a_k)\) yields coincident nodes \(\eta^{j-h a_j}=\eta^{k-h a_k}\). Conversely constant shear preserves distinct Fourier nodes. This prohibits nontrivial independent shears in this specific, well-conditioned Fourier-root gauge when \(M\) is prime; it is not an impossibility theorem for other bases or algorithms.

## 7. Cost accounting and what this phase does not establish

The demonstrator in `phase5.py` performs raw fiber transforms, full \(V_h\) inversion via Gaussian elimination, and **direct** evaluation of \(K_hB_h\), explicitly retaining all costs. It is intentionally a slow correctness oracle, not a faster FFT. Direct matrix-vector multiplications with precomputed bases would cost \(\Theta(M^3)=\Theta(N^{3/2})\) for all channels; a naive inversion for every \(h\) can cost \(\Theta(M^4)\) total preprocessing. Classical fast Fourier execution of the raw expression costs \(O(N\log N)\). Faster exact all-input algorithms exist in the unrestricted exact-complex-arithmetic model, but none of these gauge constructions yields \(O(N)\) work.

The \(O(N)\) structured-input engines from previous phases remain correct within their stated families; this phase tests the attempt to extend the shear-orbit representation to arbitrary fresh inputs. No new universal FFT or arbitrary-integer-multiplication bound was found.

## 9. Specific next research gate

Find a **basis-invariant** algorithmic compression of the composed map from raw \(x\) to all \(X[k]\), not merely a low-rank parameterization of the hidden orbit tensors. A successful candidate must:

1. Specify a sequential arithmetic model and an explicit family of uniform circuits over all \(N\).
2. Include construction/inversion of coordinate changes, coefficient recovery, all channel calculations, roots, indexing, and output materialization.
3. Demonstrate a fully charged sub-baseline recurrence for arbitrary **fresh** input, not just a low-rank surrogate for a chosen gauge.
4. Survive the present arbitrary-rank mask-gauge counterexample and the orthogonal-gauge cancellation control.
5. Address finite precision if promising practical stable FFT acceleration.
