# Independent proof review: natural/bit-reversed paired Green layers

Date: 2026-10-09 UTC. This theorem was proposed by the paired constructor during the recursive-bank investigation. The proof below was independently reconstructed, and the accompanying checker was independently authored. It is distinct from the externally supplied three-QS1 bit-reversal cut.

## Accepted theorem

Let `N=2^k`, `m=2^floor(k/2)`, and `q=N/m`. Partition coordinates into `q` consecutive blocks of length `m`. Let `Z` be the natural cycle and `R` bit reversal. Each of `L` layers is a sum of two weighted Green terms whose cycle permutations are `Z` and `RZR`. Each term is allowed independent nonsingular monomial endpoints that preserve the bank blocks. Its defining cycle inverse must exist; edge weights may be zero.

Insert any number of matrices that are block diagonal on those same bank blocks anywhere among the layers. They may include a partially or fully active bank of length-m Fourier transforms, or other arbitrary internal block computations. Then representing the natural Fourier matrix requires

\[
L(q/2+m)\ge N/2,
\qquad
L\ge\left\lceil\frac{N}{q+2m}\right\rceil.
\]

Consequently three paired layers fail for every dyadic `N>=128`, and four paired layers fail for every dyadic `N>=256`. Uniform fixed depth fails in this specified cycle/endpoint class; the required layer count grows proportionally to `sqrt(N)`. This is a representation restriction, not an unrestricted arithmetic-circuit lower bound.

## 1. Full rank of the alternating-block Fourier rectangle

Let `I` be the union of even-numbered bank blocks, and let `I^c` be the odd blocks. Both have size `N/2`. Put `t=q/2`; in the balanced dyadic case `t` divides `m`. Index rows and columns as

\[
i=2ma+b,\qquad j=2mc+m+d,
\]

where `0<=a,c<t` and `0<=b,d<m`. With a primitive Nth root `omega`, the terms containing `4m²ac` and `2m²a` in `ij` are multiples of N, because `m/t` is integral. Thus

\[
\omega^{ij}=\zeta^{ad+bc}\omega^{b(m+d)},\qquad \zeta=\omega^{2m}.
\]

Apply invertible inverse t-point Fourier transforms in the coarse row coordinate `a` and coarse column coordinate `c`. Their orthogonality gives zero unless `e=d mod t` and `f=b mod t`. On the remaining entries the two inverse normalizations cancel the two sums, leaving `omega^{b(m+d)}`.

Group rows and columns by `(e,f)`. Write `b=f+t beta`, `d=e+t delta`, and `ell=m/t`. Each resulting `ell`-by-`ell` block differs by nonzero row and column scales from

\[
[\eta^{\beta\delta}]_{0\le\beta,\delta<\ell},
\qquad \eta=\omega^{t^2}.
\]

Since `N=2 ell t²`, the root `eta` has order `2 ell`; its first `ell` powers are distinct, and the block is an invertible Vandermonde matrix. There are `t²` such blocks, totaling dimension `ell t²=N/2`. Therefore `rank F_N[I,I^c]=N/2`.

The argument works in any field containing a primitive Nth root, with characteristic not dividing N. It does not use complex prime full-spark results. The balanced dyadic values have `ell=2` for even k and `ell=1` for odd k. More generally the Fourier rank proof works whenever `N=2mt` and `t|m`.

## 2. Exact cycle geometry and rank budget

For any invertible matrix A, the complementary off-diagonal ranks of A and its inverse are equal. For `A=I-D_w P_pi`, the cross-cut block is a weighted partial matching. Its rank is the number of nonzero cycle weights entering `I` from `I^c`, at most the unweighted incoming-edge count. This remains valid with zero weights or singular principal blocks. Endpoint monomials preserving the cut only apply invertible internal row/column operations.

For an elementary proof of the inverse identity, a vector v in the kernel of `A[I,I^c]` gives `A(0,v)=(0,w)`. Invertibility makes `v -> w` an isomorphism to the kernel of `A^{-1}[I,I^c]`, whose inverse is obtained by applying `A^{-1}`. The two blocks have the same column count and equal nullity, hence equal rank. This does not divide by a principal subblock.

The natural cycle has one incoming edge at the beginning of every even block, hence `q/2` incoming edges. For `RZR`, membership in `I` tests the binary digit at position `log2(m)`. Conjugation by R changes the tested digit to position `log2(q)-1`. In natural cyclic order, that digit alternates in runs of length `q/2`; there are `N/q=m` zero runs, hence m incoming edges. A paired sum therefore has cross rank at most `q/2+m`.

For arbitrary square matrices A,B, the cross block of their product is

\[
A[I,I]B[I,I^c]+A[I,I^c]B[I^c,I^c],
\]

so its rank is at most the sum of their cross ranks. Every inserted same-bank block-diagonal matrix has zero cross rank. This proves the L-layer cap and the theorem.

The proof actually permits endpoint monomials that preserve `I` and `I^c` even if they permute individual blocks within the two parity classes. The stated block-preserving hypothesis is a convenient sufficient condition. Arbitrary endpoint permutations that mix the striped classes are not covered, nor are arbitrary independent cycle orders.

## 3. Thresholds and non-excluded boundary

For even k, `q=m` and `L>=ceil(m/3)`. For odd k, `q=2m` and `L>=ceil(m/2)`. Therefore the first dyadic three-layer contradiction is N128, and the first four-layer contradiction supplied by this witness is N256. Combining the two exponent parities gives the all-larger-dyadic statements above.

At N128, `m=8,q=16`, the four-layer cap is exactly 64, equal to the target block rank. This witness gives no contradiction there. At N256, `m=q=16`, the four-layer cap is 96 while the target rank is 128. Neither threshold is asserted to be optimal among all possible witnesses.

## 4. Independent controls and transfer to the construction track

`independent_checks.py` computes exact incoming-edge counts and Fourier cross-block ranks at every dyadic N4 through N512 over `F_65537`, and checks the small residual Vandermonde blocks. The results verify the boundary equality at N128 and strict four-layer exclusion from N256. The all-N conclusion follows from the proof, not from finite maxflow survival or sampled ranks.

The natural-plus-bit-reversal construction track has independently identified its current diagonal-mask/weighted-wrap subclass as using precisely these two cycle orders. The theorem therefore closes that fixed geometry, including a full recursive bank. A replacement must change the relevant cross-cut geometry, for example by a sufficiently different cycle order or endpoint routing that mixes the striped classes, and must still meet all coefficient and cost conditions. Simply passing this one cut would not establish a construction.

Explicitly, the current root-free summed family `T_N(s,c)+u R T_N(r,h) R^{-1}`, where T has the natural weighted-cycle Green representation, is covered with arbitrary diagonal masks between three such sums. At N128 each pair has rank budget `8+8=16`, so three pairs have budget 48 against the Fourier block rank 64. The exclusion is independent of any tensor reshape rank of the masks. Zero wrap edges can only reduce these ranks. This specialization retains the fixed two cycle orders and does not exclude a redesigned order or a permutation that mixes the striped classes.
