# Hero #1 Phase 17 — Sparse-residual noncommuting Green FFT

**Date:** October 10, 2026. **Status:** Exact structural results and executable finite-field algorithms; proven arithmetic upper bounds in a suitable field-arithmetic model; no general arbitrary-input sub-`N log N` FFT and no precision-certified complex floating implementation.

## Conventions

Let `N >= 2` and let `omega` be a primitive `N`-th root of unity in a field `E` of characteristic not dividing `N`. Let `F[k,j] = omega^(kj)` denote the **unnormalized** DFT over `E`, `Sx[j]=x[j-1 mod N]`, and `Dx[j]=omega^j x[j]`. Over the complex numbers the unitary version is `mathcal F = F / sqrt(N)`.

Fix

    L = 7I + 2S + D,
    P = 7I + S + 2D^{-1}.

The intertwining identities `S F = F D^{-1}` and `D F = F S` imply, **exactly**,

    L F = F P.                                           (1)

Over the complex numbers, `||2S+D||_2 <= 3`, so `sigma_min(L)>=4`, `||L||_2<=10`, and `cond_2(L)<=5/2`. Similarly `P=F^{-1} L F` is unitarily similar to `L`, with the same condition bounds. Both `L` and `P` are cyclic diagonal-plus-shift operators, so their inversion costs `O(N)` field operations using one affine recurrence and one cycle-closure correction (Phase16 theorem). Over a finite field, invertibility requires that the relevant denominators and closure determinants be nonzero; this is explicitly checked in the code for every tested length. The complex inequality is *not* transferred to finite-field absolute values.

## Theorem 1 — Exact Green recovery from sparse residual, all transform lengths

For arbitrary input `x` define the **observable residual** `r = P x`. Suppose `K = |supp(r)|`. If `K >= 1`, set `T = supp(r)` and `lambda_j = omega^j`. The exact Fourier forcing is

    z_k = (F r)_k = sum_{j in T} r_j lambda_j^k,  k=0,...,N-1.  (2)

The ordinary generating function of this *entire forcing vector*, considered as the first `N` terms of an infinite sequence, is

                  A(t)                 sum_j r_j prod_{h != j}(1-lambda_h t)
    Z(t) = -----------------------  = --------------------------------------,
           B(t) = prod_j(1-lambda_j t)     prod_j(1-lambda_j t)

with `deg(B)=K`, `B(0)=1`, `deg(A)<K`. In particular, `z_k` obeys a linear recurrence of order `K` with characteristic roots `lambda_j`.

**An exact executable evaluator:**

1. Build `A,B` by a balanced sum-of-fractions tree. At an internal node combine `(A1,B1)` and `(A2,B2)` as `(A1*B2+A2*B1, B1*B2)`. Fast polynomial convolution yields `O(K log^2(K+1))` field operations, including the whole construction; small naive products are allowed.
2. Let `H=2^ceil(log2 K)` and compute `U=B^{-1} mod t^H` by Newton inversion in `O(H log H)` field operations.
3. Emit the first `H` coefficients `z_0,...,z_{H-1}` from `A U mod t^H`.
4. For every subsequent block beginning at `s>=H` (multiples of `H`), let `h_i=z_{s-K+i}`, `i=0,...,K-1`, and define `H_s(t)=sum_{i=0}^{K-1} h_i t^i`. Let `c_j=[t^(K+j)]B(t)H_s(t)` for `j=0,...,K-1`, and zero for `j>=K`. Since `B(t)Z(t)=A(t)` and `s>=K>deg(A)`, the new block obeys `B(t) Z_s(t) = -sum_{j=0}^{H-1} c_j t^j mod t^H`. Obtain the next `H` coefficients using **one convolution with the cached `U`**, following **one convolution with cached `B`**. Repeat until `N` coefficients exist.
5. Output `y=L^{-1} z`. Identity (1) shows `y=F x` exactly for *every* `x` having that residual, not merely in expectation.

Each emitted block performs two convolutions on arrays of length `O(H)=O(K)`. Precomputing the short NTT transforms of `B` and `U` once, as well as short twiddle tables, and **charging that setup** permits exact kernel reuse across all blocks; each new block still pays for its varying forward transforms, pointwise products, inverse transforms, writes, and routing. Therefore its total arithmetic bound is

    T_sparse(N,K) = O(N log(K+1) + K log^2(K+1))          (3)

for `K>=1`, plus `O(N)` preprocessing, root enumeration, Green setup and inversion. For `K=0`, invertibility of `P` gives `x=0`, and all outputs can be emitted in `O(N)` time. Convolution uses short roots of unity of order `2^ceil(log2 O(K))` with a field supporting those roots; `N` itself need *not* be a power of two. Over an algebraically closed characteristic-zero field such as `C`, those roots exist, but treating exact root operations as unit cost is an algebraic model choice, not a bit-complexity claim.

**The model is exact scalar/field arithmetic** with roots made available or generated and every scalar operation charged. This is an *input-parameterized* upper bound. In a growing finite field, source prime/root construction and bit complexity of field elements require additional analysis. The Python implementation verifies finite values of `N` in concrete NTT primes; it does not establish an efficient number-theoretic prime family or complex floating precision bound for all `N`.

For `K=O(1)`, (3) is `O(N)`. For `K <= (log N)^c` at fixed `c`, it is `O(N log log N)` for large `N`. The data `x` need not be sparse.

## Theorem 2 — The input can be dense in every coordinate

Over `C`, let `r=e_j` (one nonzero impulse), and set `x=P^{-1}e_j`. This is well-defined since `P` is strictly diagonally dominant: its diagonal entries are `d_h=7+2 omega^{-h}`, with `|d_h|>=5`, and its cyclic predecessor coefficient is one.

For all indices `h!=j`, the recurrence is

    d_h x_h + x_{h-1} = 0.

All `d_h` are nonzero. If any `x_h` vanished, the zero would propagate along the entire predecessor recursion until all entries vanished, contradicting `Px=e_j`. Hence **all `N` coordinates of `x` are nonzero**, yet `Px=e_j` has `K=1`. Its full Fourier vector is exactly obtainable in `O(N)` field operations by (2) and `L^{-1}`, including input reading/output writing. The construction works for any `j` and every `N` over complex exact arithmetic. The finite-field tests explicitly verify all coordinates nonzero for the selected moduli and lengths.

This is an exact result for a nontrivial family of *dense* structured signals; the set `{P^-1 e_j}` has `N` examples, and their K-sparse linear combinations form larger structured families. It **does not** cover all dense vectors.

## Theorem 3 — Whole-vector deterministic approximation certificate (complex exact arithmetic)

For arbitrary complex `x`, let `r=Px`, and choose a retained residual `r_T` supported on any `K` coordinates. Define the approximate normalized transform

    yhat = L^{-1} mathcal F r_T,   y = mathcal F x.

Then by (1), `y-yhat=L^{-1} mathcal F(r-r_T)`. Unitarity and `4<=sigma_min(L)<=sigma_max(L)<=10` imply the two-sided *deterministic* norm certificate

    (1/10) ||r-r_T||_2 <= ||y-yhat||_2 <= (1/4) ||r-r_T||_2.   (4)

The `K` largest-magnitude entries of `r` minimize `||r-r_T||_2` over all such coordinate-supported truncations. Equation (4) avoids calculating a reference Fourier transform to certify its error envelope. It is an exact-arithmetic norm statement: rounding within the Green solve, FFT or rational expansion contributes additional error not included here. The finite-field implementation does exact recovery; the NumPy test separately verifies (4) at small complex lengths.

## Theorem 4 — Why this does not beat the general FFT

`P` is an invertible matrix. For any input distribution absolutely continuous on `C^N`, each nonzero linear coordinate functional `(Px)_j` vanishes on a proper hyperplane of measure zero. Therefore `K=N` with probability one; no sparsification occurs for generic fresh inputs.

Over `F_p` with uniform random `x`, invertibility makes `r=Px` uniform on `F_p^N`, with independent coordinates. Thus `K` follows `Binomial(N,1-1/p)`; for a large NTT prime, `K` is essentially `N`. In either case the sparse-source bound is no better than ordinary FFT asymptotics. An ordinary Bluestein (short convolutions of size `Theta(N)`) fallback computes generic inputs in `O(N log N)` whenever suitable NTT roots are available. It is implemented and independently checked.

The method proves **no** exact `o(N log N)` FFT for arbitrary inputs. The Phase16 forcing-FFT equivalence has not been broken: only the **restricted residual-sparse input class** admits the stronger bound. No model-independent FFT lower bound is claimed either.

## Theorem 5 — Support-dependent precision obstruction

The polynomial denominator `B(t)=prod_{j in T}(1-omega^j t)` may have intermediate coefficients of magnitude approaching `binom(K,floor(K/2))` while the actual Fourier forcing has `|z_k|<=sum_j |r_j|` (equal to `K` for unit coefficients). Indeed, for `T={0,...,K-1}` and `N -> infinity` with fixed `K`, all `omega^j -> 1`, hence `B(t)->(1-t)^K` coefficientwise. Its central coefficient is `binom(K,floor(K/2)) ~ 2^K/sqrt(pi K/2)`. This yields a potentially exponential-in-`K` intermediate-coefficient-to-output ratio.

A near-clustered `N=131072,K=32` test produces a denominator coefficient of roughly `6.010799038e8`; the analogous evenly spaced `K=32` support gives a denominator `1-t^32`, with largest coefficient one. The comparison is an *observed coefficient growth*, not an automatically established rounding-error lower bound. It warns that the final `cond(L)<=2.5` alone is **insufficient** to certify a stable floating implementation of the rational branch. Exact finite-field execution avoids this particular rounding issue; Word-RAM/Turing claims must price growing intermediate precision, modular primes and reconstruction.
