## A constructive orientation lemma for a total circuit

The supplied material properly warns that commutative **rank** duality cannot
be used without checking its model. There is a simpler constant-overhead
transfer if the available premise is already a **total arithmetic circuit**.
The following elementary derivation avoids a rank-duality assumption.

Assume a uniform straight-line program of `T(n,p)` scalar gates computes

\[
       X Y,\qquad X\in K^{n\times n},\quad Y\in K^{n\times p},
\]

over a commutative field. Allowed gates are addition, subtraction,
multiplication, and division by specified nonzero constants. Count all
constant preparation and the construction of the program in its premise.
Then an `n × p` matrix `U` and a `p × n` matrix `W` can be multiplied with
at most `5T(n,p)` scalar gates plus linear-size bookkeeping and output.

Set `Y=Wᵀ`, run the given program with `X=0`, and retain every primal gate
value. Seed the output adjoints with `U`; reverse the gate list. For example,

\[
 z=a+b:\ (\bar a,\bar b)\mathrel{+}=(\bar z,\bar z),
 \qquad
 z=ab:\ (\bar a,\bar b)\mathrel{+}=(b\bar z,a\bar z).
\]

Subtraction changes the appropriate accumulation to subtraction. Division
by a fixed constant uses that same constant in its reverse gate. Thus an
addition gate needs at most two reverse scalar operations, and a
multiplication gate at most two reverse multiplications and two reverse
additions. Including the forward pass gives the bound `5T`.

The resulting adjoint at the entries of `X` is

\[
 \bar X_{ij}
   =\frac{\partial}{\partial X_{ij}}
       \sum_{r,a} U_{ra}(XY)_{ra}
   =\sum_a U_{ia}Y_{ja}
   =(UW)_{ij}.
\]

No scalar objective needs to be formed: its derivative is implemented by
seeding the output adjoints. Formal differentiation is exact; it is not a
finite-difference or approximate argument. The evaluation point `X=0` is
legal because there are no input-dependent denominators. Reading/transposing
the two thin inputs, initializing adjoints, constructing reverse instructions,
and writing the dense answer cost `O(T+n²+np)` word operations and storage.
If the original program is uniform with `T=O(n²)` and `p=O(log n)`, so is
the transferred program, with a uniform constant.

This proves a conditional transfer of **total cost** between the two cited
orientations. It does not manufacture a circuit from its rank, recover the
missing Brockett–Dobkin program, or justify evaluating a rational program
with input-dependent divisions at zero. The hypotheses of the unavailable
construction must still be checked. No novelty is claimed for reverse
differentiation.

## Conditional exponential-sum consequence, with its costs retained

Suppose a uniform, exact algorithm over the intended field multiplies
`n × p` by `p × n` matrices in `O(n²)` total operations for
`p ≤ c log₂ n`, with fixed positive `c`. Let

\[
 f_m=\sum_{s=1}^{k} a_s z_s^m,\quad 0\le m<N,
 \qquad k\le C\log N.
\]

Take `n=ceil(sqrt(N))`, set `U[r,s]=z_s^r` and
`W[s,b]=a_s(z_s^n)^b`, and compute their product in a constant number of
inner-dimension groups. Its `(r,b)` entry is exactly `f_(r+nb)`.

Consecutive power tables, including `z_s^n`, take `O(kn)` field operations.
If the roots are given as powers of a supplied root of unity, binary
exponentiation takes another `O(k log N)` operations. Group sums and the
full output use `O(N)`. Padding gives `n²< N+2sqrt(N)+1`, so total work is
`O_C(N)` under the stated thin-circuit premise. No distinct-root assumption,
fixed support, preprocessing amortization, or finite-alphabet restriction is
needed for this reduction.

The reduction is already in the supplied research and is restated here to
retain its exact dependency. This pass does not promote its conditional
conclusion to an independently implemented theorem.

## Remaining construction checks

The minimum evidence needed to close this branch is the corrected five-page
1976 construction, or another explicit primary construction establishing
the same total bound. The audit must then identify:

1. The actual parameter range, including whether its constant is uniform as
   `p` grows with `log n`.
2. Every encoding and decoding linear form, its generation, and an addition
   recurrence, not only the number of products.
3. The scalar coefficient set and how coefficients are prepared; fixed
   powers of two and their divisions must be charged rather than declared
   free.
4. Whether any input-dependent division, branching, nonuniform advice, or
   growing-alphabet table occurs, and the precise valid coefficient fields.
5. A symbolic verification using the corrected signs, followed by an exact
   operation ledger for a scalable implementation.
