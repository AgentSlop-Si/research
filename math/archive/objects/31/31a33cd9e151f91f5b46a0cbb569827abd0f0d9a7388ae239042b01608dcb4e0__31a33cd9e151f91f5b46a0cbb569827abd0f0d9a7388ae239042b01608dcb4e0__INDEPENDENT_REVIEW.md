# Independent Gray-layout compatibility review

Date: 2026-10-09 UTC. This is a separate continuation of the sealed affine-middle review.

## Result

The opposite-side collapse does not restore a bounded-crossing obstruction for the Gray candidate: both the Gray permutation and its inverse have maximum natural-path threshold crossing count exactly `m` at `N=2^m`. The complete inverse-variable compatibility system is algebraically correct and includes all zero/degenerate QS1 generators. A small exact four-point factorization provides a positive control; it has no claimed extension to larger sizes.

The separate `GENERIC_DIMENSION_LIMIT.md` proves an additional exact result: at N16 the four-QS1 `R-G-R` factorization image has dimension 246, hence codimension ten. Its proof accounts for internal diagonal and first-pair GL2 gauges and supplies matching nonzero modular Jacobian minors with a characteristic-zero lift. This prevents a full-256-row inverse-Jacobian certificate, but does not exclude the Fourier target.

## 1. The inverse permutation also needs checking

For the four-stage word

\[
M=S_4P_3S_3Q S_2P_1S_1,
\]

the already reviewed collapse conjugates `S2` by `Q`. There is also an exact alternative:

\[
M=S_4(P_3Q)\big[(Q^{-1}S_3Q)S_2\big]P_1S_1.
\]

Its middle order is at most `kappa(Q^{-1})r3+r2`, where `kappa` is the maximum number of adjacent membership changes in a prefix preimage. Thus a proposed middle permutation needs growth in both directions to avoid these two bounded-crossing obstructions.

Let `B_m` be the sequence of inverse-Gray values, so `B_m(G_m(j))=j`. Writing `H=2^(m-1)`, its recursion is

\[
B_m=(B_{m-1},\;2H-1-B_{m-1}).
\]

This follows directly from Gray decoding: when the most significant Gray bit is zero the lower binary bits decode normally; when it is one the lower binary bits are all complemented.

Let `w_m(t)` count adjacent threshold crossings in `B_m`. For `1<=u<H`, the second half and the joining edge give

\[
w_m(H+u)=w_{m-1}(H-u)+1.
\]

The first half contributes no crossing of an upper-half threshold. Reflection of second-half values turns threshold `H+u` into the complementary threshold `H-u`; complementing membership does not change the crossing count. The joining edge always crosses because its first endpoint is below `H` and its second endpoint is `2H-1`.

For lower thresholds, `w_m(u)=w_{m-1}(u)+epsilon`, with `epsilon` either zero or one depending on the join; at `t=H` the count is one. Consequently the maximum increases by exactly one from the base one, proving

\[
\kappa(G_m^{-1})=m.
\]

A maximizing threshold is `(2N+(-1)^m)/3`, by the recursion `t_m=N-t_(m-1)`. Combined with the separately proved `kappa(G_m)=m`, this checks both collapse directions. It only removes this particular obstruction. The unknown compatible stage coefficients remain the substantive issue.

## 2. Complete inverse-variable equations

For the target word

\[
F_N=S_4R S_3G S_2R S_1,
\]

take unknown full matrices `U,V,W`, and put

\[
Z=G^{-1}W R U F_N V R.
\]

Require all strictly separated 2-by-2 minors of `U,V,W,Z` to vanish, and impose `tau det(U)det(V)det(W)=1`. This is equivalent to the desired full-QS1 factorization. In the forward direction `U=S4^{-1}`, `V=S1^{-1}`, `W=S3^{-1}`, and `Z=S2`; in the converse these same assignments reconstruct the word. The inverse cut-rank identity does not require invertible principal blocks, and `Z` is automatically invertible when the target is Fourier.

No division by a generator or nonzero-transition condition is used. The equations for `Z` are degree at most six, those for each of `U,V,W` degree at most two; the determinant saturation has degree `3N+1`. The system is an exact representation criterion, not a coefficient compiler or an arithmetic upper bound.

The newly authored checker generates 36 invertible full scalar-transition QS1 quadruples over `F_1009`, at lengths 4, 8 and 16. It includes zero transitions and a diagonal middle stage. Using each resulting exact target product, it verifies the inverse substitutions, all separated minors of `U,V,W,Z`, and exact recovery of the original target. These arbitrary-product controls validate the equations and zero-case coverage; they do not assert that a Fourier target solves them.

## 3. Actual Fourier control at N=4

Use positive-sign unnormalized `F4`, with entries `i^(jk)`. Set

\[
R=[0,2,1,3],\qquad G=[0,1,3,2],
\]

\[
B=\operatorname{diag}(H_2,H_2),\quad
H_2=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&-1\end{pmatrix},\quad
D=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,1,i).
\]

Choose `S1=I`, `S2=G^{-1}B`, `S3=D`, `S4=RB`. The exact identity is

\[
S_4R S_3G S_2R S_1=RBRDBR=F_4.
\]

All four factors are QS1: `S2` is block diagonal, `S3` is diagonal, and the only potentially nontrivial upper/lower cut minors of the four-by-four `S4` both vanish. The checker verifies the product and minors using Gaussian integer pairs, with no floating point.

Importantly, `S4[0,1]` and `S4[1,2]` are nonzero while `S4[0,2]=0`. These entries are incompatible with the upper transition-free separated form `u_i v_j` for `i<j`. Thus searching only that narrower chart can miss even this exact small Fourier realization. Full transition parameters or the complete inverse-variable system are needed for a completeness claim.

This control is a reformulation of ordinary radix-two FFT at four points. It supplies no uniform construction, no solution at untested sizes, and no improvement to generic FFT cost. Preparation and field access remain part of any eventual cost claim.
