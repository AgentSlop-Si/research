# Independent audit: independently routed inverse-cycle branches

Audit date: 2026-10-10 (UTC).

## Verdict

**ACCEPT.** The proposed obstruction for `N > r(r+2)^r` is valid. In fact, its
own structural lemma proves the stronger necessary condition

\[
\boxed{N\le r(r+1)^r}
\]

for every representation of a Fourier matrix as a sum of `r >= 1` independently
endpoint-monomial-routed inverse diagonal-plus-permutation branches. In particular,
one branch is impossible for `N >= 3`, and two branches are impossible for
`N >= 19`.

There is no counterexample or unfilled proof step in the audited field model.
Zero entries require no genericity assumption or limiting argument. This is a
branch-count obstruction, **not** an unrestricted arithmetic lower bound.

## Precise model

Let K be a field and let omega in K have multiplicative order exactly N. Set

\[
F_{a,b}=\omega^{ab},\qquad 0\le a,b<N.
\]

Suppose

\[
F=\sum_{i=1}^{r}L_iQ_i^{-1}R_i,
\qquad Q_i=D_i+E_iP_i,
\]

where each Q_i is invertible, D_i and E_i are arbitrary diagonal matrices,
P_i is any permutation matrix, and L_i,R_i have support contained in a
permutation matrix. The nonzero locations and all weights may differ between
branches. Weights, including the endpoint weights, may be zero. Equivalently,
each endpoint matrix is a diagonal matrix times a permutation matrix; its
diagonal need not be invertible. No bound on coefficient size or description
length is imposed.

The zero-branch case r=0 cannot represent F for any N>=1. For r>=N the displayed
necessary bound is automatic, so the proof below only needs r<N.

## Lemma: deleting inverse-row constraints gives k rank-one column classes

Let Q=D+EP be invertible and let S be any set of k distinct row indices, with
1<=k<=N. Then the nonzero columns of A=Q^{-1}[S,:] have **exactly k distinct
projective directions**. Representatives of these k directions are linearly
independent. Some columns of A may be zero.

### 1. Fix the permutation convention

Write P e_j=e_{pi(j)}, using e_j here for the standard basis vector. Write the
diagonal entries of D and E as d_j and epsilon_j. Column j of Q is

\[
q_j=d_j e_j+\epsilon_{\pi(j)}e_{\pi(j)}.
\]

For a fixed point the two contributions combine at the same coordinate.
Reversing the convention for permutation matrices only replaces pi by its
inverse and does not affect the argument.

### 2. Establish the correct annihilating constraints

Let T be the complement of S and B=Q[:,T]. Since Q^{-1}Q=I,

\[
A B=0.
\]

All N-k columns of B are independent, because they are a subset of the columns
of the invertible matrix Q. Therefore the left nullspace

\[
W=\{x\in K^{1\times N}:xB=0\}
\]

has dimension k. A has k independent rows, so its rowspace equals W.

Notice the index orientation: selecting rows S of Q^{-1} means **removing
constraint columns S of Q**, not removing rows or vertices from Q.

### 3. Use the permutation-cycle superpattern, not the actual nonzero graph

Draw vertex j for each coordinate, and draw one labeled edge j with endpoints
j and pi(j). The edge set is a disjoint union of permutation cycles:

- a fixed point contributes one loop on one vertex;
- a 2-cycle contributes two distinct parallel edges on two vertices;
- a longer permutation cycle contributes the ordinary cycle.

Keep every superpattern edge even when one or both of its formal coefficients
vanish. Delete the k edges with labels in S. Every unaffected permutation cycle
stays a cycle. Deleting t>0 edges of an original cycle produces t path
components, where a single isolated vertex is a path with zero edges. This
also handles deletion of a loop and the two possible deletions of a 2-cycle.
Consequently the resulting graph has exactly k path components, together
with any untouched cycles.

Each retained column of B has all its support in the component containing
its labeled edge. A component's retained columns remain independent when
restricted to its vertex coordinates: a dependence after restriction would
be exactly the same dependence globally, since these columns vanish outside
that component.

An untouched cycle with v vertices has v independent constraint columns, so
its local left nullspace is zero. A path with v vertices has v-1 independent
constraint columns, so its local left nullspace is one-dimensional. This
dimension calculation uses rank-nullity, not division by an edge coefficient.
It remains valid when a generator of the one-dimensional kernel has zeros.

### 4. Obtain projective directions

Choose one nonzero local left-null vector h_t for each of the k paths, extending
it by zero off that path. These vectors have disjoint supports and form a basis
of W. Put them into the rows of a k-by-N matrix H. Since A and H are bases of
the same rowspace, A=C H for an invertible k-by-k matrix C.

If vertex j is in path t, then

\[
A_{:,j}=(h_t)_j C_{:,t}.
\]

If j lies in an untouched cycle, its A column is zero. Each h_t has a nonzero
coordinate, so every direction C_{:,t} occurs. These k vectors are independent
because C is invertible. This proves the stated stronger lemma.

## Endpoint routing and the zero-column improvement

Fix any k output rows of a routed branch LQ^{-1}R. Its left endpoint selects,
scales and reorders at most k distinct rows of Q^{-1}, inserting zero rows
where necessary. If there are ell>0 active underlying rows, their restriction
has ell projective directions by the lemma. Applying the fixed embedding and
row scalings maps those ell lines to at most ell lines. Right endpoint routing
only permutes and scales columns, and cannot create a new line. An identically
zero restriction causes no difficulty.

Thus the N restricted columns can always be partitioned into **at most k
classes, each spanning a space of dimension at most one**. Put zero columns
into any existing class. If every column is zero, use one class. The zero
vector is not a projective point, but it belongs to every linear line and
does not increase the rank of a class. This is why the proposed extra zero
category is unnecessary.

This step permits entirely independent endpoint permutations in different
branches. No common cycle order, common output routing, or common input
routing is used.

## Fourier obstruction

Assume r<N and put k=r+1. Select Fourier output rows 0,1,...,r. For each
branch, partition the N column indices into at most k rank-at-most-one
classes as above. Their common refinement has at most k^r cells.

On any cell C, every branch has rank at most one. Therefore

\[
\operatorname{rank}F[0:r,C]\le r.
\]

Any k distinct Fourier columns restricted to rows 0,...,k-1 form a Vandermonde
matrix. If their indices are b_1,...,b_k, its determinant, up to column order,
is

\[
\prod_{u<v}(\omega^{b_v}-\omega^{b_u}),
\]

which is nonzero because omega has exact order N and K is a field. Thus no
cell can contain k or more columns. Every cell has at most r columns, and

\[
N\le r k^r=r(r+1)^r.
\]

The same determinant argument works for any consecutive output rows,
including a cyclic wrap, by scaling each column by a nonzero power of omega.
It works for composite N and positive characteristic whenever the stipulated
primitive root exists. It does **not** assert that arbitrary row selections
of a composite-order Fourier matrix are full spark.

The proposed weaker inequality follows immediately. The optional weighted
zero-category count N<=r(r+1)(r+2)^(r-1) is also valid, but unnecessary and
weaker than absorbing zeros.

## Growth and computational scope

Taking logarithms gives log N<=log r+r log(r+1). Hence any such representation
family has r=Omega(log N/log log N). This is an exact algebraic obstruction
to a specified **sum representation**, not an arithmetic-circuit complexity
theorem. It makes no claim about products of branches, nonmonomial endpoint
mixing, nonlinear intermediate representations, cancellations across a shared
evaluation, preprocessing, approximation, or all algorithms for Fourier.

Independent evaluation admits O(Nr) arithmetic work: each cycle system can
be solved in linear time, and endpoint monomial maps and summation also take
linear work per branch. This is an upper bound for that evaluation method.
No Omega(Nr) lower bound on all possible shared implementations follows.

For completeness, degeneracies do not obstruct the linear solve assertion.
On a nontrivial permutation cycle, its determinant is the sum, with a sign,
of the product of diagonal coefficients and the product of cyclic-edge
coefficients. Invertibility therefore ensures at least one of these two
products is nonzero. Choose that full nonzero coefficient family as the
pivots and solve the resulting first-order cyclic recurrence, whose closure
coefficient is nonzero by invertibility. Loops are scalar solves.

## Sharpness cautions and examples

- For r=1, the improved cutoff is sharp: when N=2 and a primitive second root
  exists, F_2^{-1} is an arbitrary admissible 2-by-2 matrix for the 2-cycle
  pattern, so F_2 itself is one branch.
- The bounds for r>=2 are not claimed sharp or attainable at equality.
- A zero direction is genuinely possible. For the 3-cycle pi=(0 1 2) and
  Q=P, selecting rows 0 and 1 of Q^{-1} gives columns 0,(1,0),(0,1).
  It would be incorrect to assume all path-generator coordinates are nonzero.
- A retained-edge support graph may split further when coefficients vanish.
  Replacing the original superpattern by that actual support graph without
  redoing the rank count is unnecessary and potentially misleading.

## Independent verification status

The accompanying `fresh_exact_checks.py` was independently authored for this
audit. No supplied program was executed, imported, compiled, or AST-parsed.
It imports only Python standard-library modules. All finite-field calculations
are exact. The assertions passed, as recorded in `exact_results.json` and
`exact_checks.log`.

- Exhaustive all permutations and all actual allowed entry assignments over
  GF(2) for 1<=N<=5 and GF(3) for 1<=N<=4: 161,817 pattern-matrix cases,
  33,167 invertible cases, and 567,819 nonempty row restrictions. Fixed-point
  coefficients are enumerated by their arbitrary sum; this covers every
  possible D+EP without redundantly listing all loop decompositions.
- Every exhaustive restriction has exactly k distinct nonzero directions.
  Of them, 407,644 restrictions also contain a zero column.
- Random exact tests in GF(2), GF(3), GF(5), GF(7), GF(17), N<=14: 11,160
  retained-graph and routed-restriction checks. These include 5,139 untouched
  cycle components and 1,239 identically zero endpoint-routed restrictions.
- 400 independent-routing multi-branch tests, with N=40, r=1,2,3,4, GF(101),
  verify rank-one classes after zero absorption and rank<=r on every common
  refinement cell.
- 21,217 consecutive-row Vandermonde minors checked over prime fields, at
  N=3,4,6,8,12; this includes composite orders and wrapping consecutive rows.

These tests are supplementary evidence. They do not replace the field-general
proof, nor do they search all Fourier decompositions or establish novelty.

## Literature status

`PRIMARY_SOURCE_COMPARISON.md` records the primary papers checked. Zaslavsky's
*Biased Graphs IV*, Section 2.1, supplies a direct classical antecedent of the
componentwise projective-column mechanism through its contraction map. The
inverse low-rank mechanism also belongs to the established nullity/semiseparable
literature; the consecutive-row Fourier Vandermonde property is established. The
targeted review did not locate the precise independently routed branch-sum
obstruction with this counting bound. That bounded negative search result is
**not a novelty certification**.
