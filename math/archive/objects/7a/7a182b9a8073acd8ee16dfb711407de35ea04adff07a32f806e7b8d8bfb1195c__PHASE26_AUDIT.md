# Phase 26 independent audit of simultaneous Green circuit cuts

9 October 2026. Bounded mathematical and implementation audit of the Library-discovered Phase 26 research archive. This is independent checking, not peer review or a claim of literature priority. Submitted programs were read as text and were never imported or executed. The executable supplement was independently authored.

## Main finding

The central Phase 26 result is valid: a common balanced character cut bounds the rank of products of weighted cyclic Green sums, including arbitrary intermediate diagonal gates and affine coordinate permutations. For a fixed bound on the number of affine step magnitudes and exceptional edges per term, exact normalized Fourier representation requires `Q = Omega(log N / log log N)` Green-term occurrences. The same conclusion holds for a fixed complex operator-norm error strictly below `1/sqrt(2)`.

The source's crossing estimate can be strengthened by a factor of two. Hamiltonian cycles cannot use both orientations of an undirected edge, and a cycle has equal crossing counts in the two directions. With exact integer rounding, the certified rank is at most

    min(N/2, T floor((N-1)/r) + sum_q floor(b_q/2)),
    r = floor((N-1)^(1/s)),  T = sum_q d_q <= d Q.

Here `s` counts distinct nonzero step magnitudes after moving all affine interstage permutations to the boundary; `d_q` and `b_q` describe the q-th transformed cycle. This improves the submitted `2 d Q N/r + B` estimate without changing the restricted architecture or asymptotic order.

One small, explicit implementation defect needs repair: the submitted `simultaneous_cut` accepts a floating-tolerance witness that need not satisfy its advertised strict inequality. At `N=16`, strides `[1,2,8]`, it accepts `h=1` even though the largest shift is `8=N/r`. Exact acceptance must use `r*abs(t)<N`. The theorem is unaffected; the stronger integer certificate must use a genuinely strict witness. This supplement supplies an exact pigeonhole implementation.

This project package establishes no general sub-`N log N` Fourier algorithm, unrestricted FFT lower bound, or new integer-multiplication result.

## 2 Model and quantifiers

Take `N=2^k>=8`. Each Green term is

    G_q = (I - diag(gamma_q) W_q)^(-1),

where `W_q e_j=e_(successor_q(j))` is one Hamiltonian cycle. The denominator is invertible exactly when `1-product_j gamma_q[j]` is nonzero. Zero weights are allowed. No conditioning assumption enters the exact rank argument.

After omitting at most `b_q` directed exceptional edges, every remaining edge has displacement `+a` or `-a` for a member of a set `S_q` of at most `d_q` nonzero magnitudes. Equal magnitudes, including `a` and `-a`, can be merged. The `S_q` used in the theorem are those of the conjugated cycles after all intermediate affine permutations have been moved to an outer boundary.

The permitted expression is a product of sums of diagonal-gauged Green terms, with arbitrary additional diagonal gates and affine intermediate permutations `j -> u j+c`, `gcd(u,N)=1`. `Q` counts term occurrences across the stages. Rank induction also works for an explicitly given sum/product expression tree when occurrences are counted; it is not a complexity accounting theorem for a shared-state algorithm or a DAG with free reuse.

For the common-cut construction, assume `s=|union_q S_q|>=1`. The empty-generator case is separate: every edge is exceptional, and any balanced cut has at most `floor(b_q/2)` incoming crossings per cycle. Thus rank is bounded by `sum_q floor(b_q/2)` without defining an s-th root. With fixed `b` and `N>b`, a Hamiltonian cycle whose every edge is exceptional is impossible. The `d=0` asymptotic family is consequently empty once `N>b`; it must not be handled by division by `d`.

If `Q=0`, the remaining diagonals and permutations give a monomial matrix, which cannot equal the dense Fourier matrix for `N>=2`. Nontrivial term-count formulas below use `Q>=1` and `d>=1`.

## 3 Exact geometry of the character cut

Let `a_1,...,a_s` be the distinct nonzero magnitudes and set `r=floor((N-1)^(1/s))`. Put the `N` points

    ((j a_1 mod N)/N, ..., (j a_s mod N)/N),  0<=j<N,

in `r^s<=N-1` half-open boxes. Two points are in one box. Subtract the smaller index from the larger to obtain `0<h<N`. The ordinary differences of every pair of box coordinates have absolute value below `1/r`, so the least circular residues `t_i` satisfy `|t_i|<N/r`. Repeated points cause no difficulty and can give zero shifts.

Every quantity can be computed exactly: the box label is

    tuple(floor(r*(j a_i mod N)/N)).

Consequently the implementation in this supplement has no floating threshold. Since shifts are integers,

    |t_i| <= ceil(N/r)-1 = floor((N-1)/r).

Define `I={j:(h j mod N)<N/2}` and `J=I^c`. Let `g=gcd(h,N)`. Since `N` is a power of two and `0<h<N`, `g` divides `N/2`. The character image is the multiples of `g`, each attained `g` times; exactly half are in each semicircle. Hence `|I|=|J|=N/2`, even when `h` is not invertible modulo `N`.

For translation by `a`, put `t` equal to a centered representative of `ha`. It is a multiple of `g`. Translation of the half-circle causes exactly `|t|/g` image points to enter it, each with `g` preimages. The directed crossing count is therefore exactly `|t|`. At the tie `|t|=N/2`, either centered sign gives the same count. For `t=0`, the count is zero.

The power-of-two hypothesis matters. For a general even modulus, a nonzero character can have odd-order image and the same threshold need not be balanced; e.g. `N=6,h=2` produces a 4-to-2 split. The proof does not silently extend to arbitrary composite lengths.

## 4 Stronger Hamiltonian crossing budget

For one magnitude `a`, the undirected graph with edges `{j,j+a}` has at most `2|t|` edges across the character cut. For `a` not equal to `-a`, this is exactly the sum of incoming and outgoing crossing counts of the directed translation, each `|t|`. For the order-two magnitude `a=N/2`, the translation lists each undirected edge twice, so the bound remains valid and may be loose.

A directed Hamiltonian cycle of length greater than two cannot contain both `u->v` and `v->u`: those edges would form a directed two-cycle. Its selected nonexceptional crossing edges therefore inject into the undirected boundary of the union of its signed-step graphs. There are at most

    2 sum_(a in S_q) |h a mod N|_centered + b_q

selected directed crossings in both directions combined. Every directed cycle enters and leaves a subset equally often. Its incoming crossing count is thus at most

    c_q(h) <= sum_(a in S_q) |h a mod N|_centered + floor(b_q/2).

Summing and using the strict pigeonhole rounding proves

    sum_q c_q(h)
      <= T floor((N-1)/r) + sum_q floor(b_q/2)
      <= d Q floor((N-1)/r) + Q floor(b/2).

If actual edge counts are available, `sum_q c_q(h)` is stronger still and should be used directly. The improvement above is a uniform certificate from the structural parameters; it does not replace an exact edge count by a purported equality.

The no-two-cycle condition is essential to this particular improvement. At `N=8`, the permutation `[7,2,1,4,3,6,5,0]` consists of adjacent transpositions and uses only the signed magnitude one. Across `I={0,1,2,3}` it has two incoming edges, exceeding `|t|=1`. It is not a Hamiltonian cycle. The source's coarser signed union bound would still hold.

## 5 Weighted inverses and product rank

For `A=I-diag(gamma)W`, a proper principal subgraph of the Hamiltonian cycle contains only paths. Its weighted adjacency is nilpotent, even with zero weights, so `A[J,J]` is invertible. If `A` is invertible, block inversion gives

    A^(-1)[I,J] = -S^(-1) A[I,J] A[J,J]^(-1),

where the Schur complement `S` is invertible. Therefore

    rank(A^(-1)[I,J]) = rank(A[I,J]).

The nonzeros of `A[I,J]` lie in distinct rows and columns. Its rank equals the number of crossing edges whose destination weights are nonzero, and is at most the total crossing count. Zero weights can reduce the rank; equality with the unweighted edge count requires those crossing weights to be nonzero. No determinant-one assumption on gauges or lower bound on weight magnitudes is needed.

In fact, the complementary off-diagonal rank identity holds for any invertible `A`, even if a principal block is singular. The map `A` is an isomorphism between the vectors supported in `J` that `A` maps into `J` and the vectors supported in `J` that `A^(-1)` maps into `J`. These are the kernels of the two off-diagonal blocks, and their domains have the same dimension. This observation clarifies why the invariant is robust; no broader algorithmic claim is needed here.

For a fixed complementary cut,

    (AB)[I,J] = A[I,I]B[I,J] + A[I,J]B[J,J].

Rank subadditivity gives `rank((AB)[I,J])<=rank(A[I,J])+rank(B[I,J])`. A diagonal has zero off-diagonal block, and diagonal multiplication cannot increase a block's rank. Applying these facts to the sums and then all product stages proves the source theorem and the strengthened certificate. Individual sums and the whole candidate need not be invertible.

The supplied weighted recurrence is algebraically correct. It solves `y[successor(j)]-gamma[successor(j)]y[j]=x[successor(j)]`, matching the declared successor convention. Its operation count is linear in `N` in an exact field-arithmetic model. The denominator product and the single nonzero scalar inverse are included. This does not give a floating-point stability or bit-complexity guarantee near resonance.

## 6 Intermediate and outer permutations

For an affine permutation matrix `P`, moving it through a factor uses

    P A = (P A P^(-1)) P.

Conjugating a diagonal keeps it diagonal. Conjugating a weighted cyclic denominator permutes its weights and maps its cycle to the affine image. An edge displacement `a` becomes `u a`; the translation offset cancels. The count of magnitudes and exceptional edges does not increase. Multiple intervening affine maps compose to another affine map. Consequently all such maps can be moved to one boundary before choosing a single cut for the entire normalized product.

The transformed step sets must be computed before selecting the character. Selecting a cut from the original strides and then treating an arbitrary affine interstage permutation as invisible would be wrong. Arbitrary non-affine intermediate permutations cannot be absorbed while preserving bounded step complexity.

Independent outer row and column permutations relocate the certified balanced block. Its new row and column sets need not be complements; this is harmless for the Fourier comparison. Invertible outer diagonal scalings preserve rank. Singular outer diagonals can only lower rank, so an upper-bound obstruction still holds. The source's phrase "without changing rank" should be read with invertible monomial factors; with unrestricted possibly singular scalings, "without increasing rank" is precise.

All approximation statements compare the final candidate, including its outer factors, directly with the fixed normalized Fourier matrix. They do not claim that arbitrary rescaling of the target preserves an absolute spectral-error floor.

## 7 Fourier comparison and approximation scope

For any balanced row set `R` and column set `C`, `H=Fhat_N[R,C]` has `N^2/4` entries of squared modulus `1/N`, so `||H||_F^2=N/4`. Coordinate restriction of a unitary matrix has operator norm at most one. Thus its rank is at least `N/4`.

If the corresponding candidate block has rank at most an integer `rho<N/4`, its best possible spectral error is at least the `(rho+1)`-st singular value of `H`. At most `rho` singular values can contribute one each to the squared Frobenius norm, whence

    (N/2-rho) sigma_(rho+1)^2 >= N/4-rho.

Restriction cannot enlarge operator norm, giving the claimed bound

    ||M-Fhat_N||_2 >= sqrt((N/4-rho)/(N/2-rho)).

A noninteger upper bound can first be rounded down to an integer. If the certified rank is already at least `N/4`, this particular universal argument gives no positive error floor. For `rho=o(N)`, the bound tends to `1/sqrt(2)`.

The limiting constant cannot be improved using arbitrary balanced-submatrix singular values alone. Even rows and the first `N/2` columns give exactly `Fhat_(N/2)/sqrt(2)`. Every singular value is `1/sqrt(2)`. This is a limitation of the universal block information, not proof that the complete restricted Green architecture achieves that approximation error.

For a fixed `0<=epsilon<1/sqrt(2)`, define

    c_epsilon = (1-2 epsilon^2)/(4(1-epsilon^2)) > 0.

Any candidate with `||M-Fhat_N||_2<=epsilon` must have rank at least `c_epsilon N` on every balanced block. This follows by solving the displayed bound when the rank is below `N/4`; ranks above it already satisfy the condition. The term-count deduction is therefore valid at every fixed such epsilon. It is not uniform when epsilon approaches `1/sqrt(2)` with `N`.

These norm arguments are over the complex numbers. Exact modular rank checks verify algebraic examples, not singular values, conditioning, or the complex norm theorem.

## 8 Term count and finite length qualifications

For `d>=1`, `Q>=1`, `s>=1`, put `beta=floor(b/2)`. Since `s<=dQ`, a necessary condition for exact Fourier representation is

    N/4 <= d Q floor((N-1)/r_0) + beta Q,
    r_0 = floor((N-1)^(1/(dQ))).

This is an integer, finite-`N` necessary condition. It is not sufficient to build a circuit.

A simple branch form is also useful. If `beta Q>N/8`, the budget is already linear in `N` when beta is a fixed positive constant. Otherwise the strict rounding gives `r<8dQ`; because `r` is an integer,

    log(N-1) < d Q log(8dQ).

Thus the strengthened finite statement is the disjunction

    beta Q > N/8  OR  log(N-1) < d Q log(8dQ).

If `b=0` or `b=1`, beta is zero and only the logarithmic branch is needed; there is no division by zero. The `s=0` and `d=0` cases were separated in Section 2.

The source's equation (3), with `16dQ+1`, was derived in its `bQ<=N/8` branch. For general `d,b`, it should retain that branch condition or be stated as a disjunction with `bQ>N/8`; the displayed implication alone does not account for the large-bridge branch at every finite length. For fixed `d,b` this presentational gap does not affect the asymptotic conclusion. Eventually the linear branch is much stronger than the logarithmic one.

For approximation, either `beta Q>c_epsilon N/2`, or

    log(N-1) < d Q log(2dQ/c_epsilon + 1).

Consequently, for fixed `d,b` and fixed permissible epsilon, both exact and approximate representation require

    Q >= (1-o(1)) log N / (d log log N).

This more explicit leading-constant form follows directly from the logarithmic inequality: a budget at most `(1-delta) log N/(d log log N)` makes its right side at most `(1-delta+o(1)) log N`, a contradiction for every fixed positive delta. It does not establish optimality.

For `d=1,b=2`, the first budgets not excluded by the source's coarse integer implementation are `6,11,18,32` at `N=2^32,2^64,2^128,2^256`. The stronger certificate changes these to `7,12,21,36`, respectively. These numbers are bounds on what the certificate fails to rule out, not constructions, proven minimum representations, or counts of FFT stages.

## 9 Concrete certificate and the next research screen

For the cut with `h=2`, the three zigzags with magnitudes `2,N/2+2,N/2+6` and the ordinary shift have incoming crossing counts `[3,3,11,2]`. In fact, these counts hold for every power-of-two `N>=32`, which is slightly broader than the theorem's conservative `N>=128` range.

To see the counts directly, write the zigzag magnitude as `a=c` or `a=N/2+c`, with `c=2` or `6`. On this character cut, its even `+a` internal run and odd `-a` internal run each inherit `c` incoming translation crossings when `N>=4c`. The last even edge would cross into zero, but is replaced by a noncrossing bridge. The last odd edge and both bridges add no incoming crossing. The total is `2c-1`. The ordinary shift contributes two. This proves the all-size counts independently of the program.

One four-term stage therefore has rank at most 19; two such stages, with arbitrary allowed diagonal gauges and an intervening diagonal, have rank at most 38. The source-seed fully weighted example at `N=256` was independently reconstructed, and its actual block rank is 38 over `F_998244353`. Independent arbitrary outer permutations and nonzero diagonal scalings preserve that rank on the correctly relocated block.

The alternating-quarter Fourier block is invertible for every dyadic `N>=8` over every field of characteristic not two containing a primitive N-th root. Pair columns `v` and `v+N/2`. Their sum and difference separate even and odd rows. In each sector, the `N/4` row indices `2t+p` yield distinct nodes `(omega^2)^t`, and the surviving columns have `N/4` consecutive exponents. Nonzero diagonal scaling reduces the sector to a square Vandermonde matrix. Both sectors are invertible. Normalization is irrelevant to this rank statement.

At `N=256` the block rank is therefore 128, while the universal balanced-block complex rank bound is 64. The source's permutation-robust complex error floor `sqrt(26/90)=0.5374838498865699...` is correct. The special full-rank fact must not be used as though every arbitrarily permuted Fourier block had full rank.

The source's non-affine sample diagnostics were independently reproduced: the minimum character-cut crossing sums of two seeded random cycles plus the shift are `63,124,248,501` at `N=128,256,512,1024`. Independently implemented Fourier controls satisfy both Weyl identities, while the sampled two-stage Green candidates fail at every tested coordinate. These are exact tests of those particular samples, not impossibility results for tunable non-affine circuits.

The correct next structural gate is to search for a **common low total cut budget across all factors**. It is unnecessary to require every individual factor to expand every balanced cut; different factors can have different small cuts. Nor does checking all character cuts check all balanced cuts. A tiny exact control using two seeded random cycles plus the shift at `N=16` has minimum character score 9 but actual minimum balanced-cut score 5. Passing either screen still does not establish Fourier phase covariance or an efficient full transform.

Code findings requiring care:

1. `simultaneous_cut` uses `n/r` and a tolerance. The strict-witness counterexample above is small and exact. Replace the predicate with integer multiplication. The source tests also use a tolerance and therefore do not detect this defect. For very large integers, float conversion can additionally overflow or lose meaningful integer precision.
2. `exact_coarse_cut_bound` implements the one-magnitude-per-term case, with numerator `2 Q N`. Its general-sounding docstring must not be used to certify a term with several distinct magnitudes unless the missing incidence factor is supplied. All source numerical threshold calls use the intended `d=1` case.
3. Its ceiling is conservative for the submitted real-valued bound; exploiting the strict witness gives the sharper integer floor derived here. The original thresholds were independently reproduced, so their interpretation rather than their arithmetic is the issue.
4. The test file's `unittest.main()` guard occurs before later test classes. The documented discovery command sees all 15 test methods, but direct execution of the file stops before defining the later classes. Move the guard to the end for consistent direct-script behavior. No claimed test failure is inferred from this layout alone.
5. Functions that silently remove zero strides or assume a positive stride count are utility interfaces, not proofs of the `s=0` boundary case. That case is handled analytically in Section 2.

## 11 Literature and research boundaries

The author-hosted bibliography verifies the Mans and Shparlinski paper, *Random walks, bisections and gossiping in circulant graphs*, Algorithmica 70 (2014), 301–325. It is relevant background on circulant bisections; this audit does not identify the complete Phase 26 statement with an exact theorem from that paper or claim novelty beyond the project. [Author bibliography](https://web.maths.unsw.edu.au/~igorshparlinski/Publ.html), [publisher DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00453-013-9810-3).

The Boito and Eidelman paper, *Computation of quasiseparable representations of Green matrices*, LAA 721 (2025), 47–80, concerns inverse rank structure and linear-complexity quasiseparable representation algorithms. Its publisher record and author preprint support treating Green inverse rank structure as established background. [Publisher record](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0024379524001976), [author preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.02701).

No priority conclusion is drawn from these two references or this bounded literature check. The audit's proofs of the stated common-cut specialization and its factor-two sharpening are self-contained.

The restriction to bounded affine increments is substantial. Arbitrary non-affine interstage permutations, general sparse or expander-like denominators, branching/shared-state algorithms, oracle-style preprocessing, and unrestricted Fourier algorithms are outside the conclusion. The finite field `F_998244353` has dyadic roots only through length `2^23`; the much larger length thresholds are symbolic integer inequalities, not finite-field transforms evaluated at those lengths. Arithmetic, storage, field/root preparation, preprocessing, and precision must be charged in any actual algorithmic claim.

The useful result to retain is a valid multistage restricted representation obstruction, with an exact witness repair and a stronger Hamiltonian cut constant. It closes a specific affine-run Green approach without resolving general Fourier complexity.
