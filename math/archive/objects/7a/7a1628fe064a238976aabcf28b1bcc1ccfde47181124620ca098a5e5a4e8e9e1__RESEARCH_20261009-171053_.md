# All-length dyadic lift for the unit-step chirp Fourier sketch

**Checkpoint:** 10 October 2026  
**Status:** Reproducible theorem candidate, exact-arithmetic proof derivation, independent numerical checks. **Randomized and approximate; not an exact FFT, not a fixed-binary64 asymptotic theorem, and not a novelty claim.**

## 1. Result at a glance

The v9 unit-step binomial-chirp evaluator gives a sharp full-output approximation for dyadic lengths, but its direct theorem requires a useful factorization of the transform length. The remaining obstruction was a prime or otherwise rough length `N`.

The new bridge is:

1. lift an arbitrary `N` to the next dyadic length

   \[
   Q=2^{\lceil\log_2N\rceil},\qquad N\le Q<2N;
   \]

2. map every target frequency `k/N` to its nearest `Q`-grid frequency `j_k/Q`;
3. approximate only the residual phase by a stable separable Jacobi--Anger/Chebyshev expansion;
4. replace every required dyadic `Q`-DFT by the same v9 randomized chirp row;
5. aggregate the **final arbitrary-length outputs** online with the three-grid majority sketch.

The separable rank needed for polynomially shrinking error is

\[
R=\Theta\!\left(\frac{\log N}{\log\log N}\right).
\]

Consequently, for **every integer length** `N`:

### Fixed-error all-length theorem

For every fixed `epsilon>0`, three randomized rows suffice asymptotically for

\[
\boxed{T(N)=O_\epsilon(N),\qquad S(N)=O(N)}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\|\widetilde X-U_Nx\|_\infty\le \epsilon\|x\|_2
}
\]

with failure probability

\[
O\!\left(\frac{(\log N)^2}{\epsilon^4N}\right).
\]

This is a coarse energy-relative approximation, not FFT-level accuracy.

### Polynomially shrinking all-length theorem

Fix `a>=0` and an integer `s>1+a`. With `2s-1` rows, there is an explicit algorithm with

\[
\boxed{
T(N)=O_{a,s}\!\left(\frac{N\log N}{\log\log N}\right),
\qquad S(N)=O(N)
}
\]

such that, with probability at least `1-N^{-a}`,

\[
\boxed{
\|\widetilde X-U_Nx\|_\infty
=O_{a,s}\!\left(
\sqrt{\log N}\,
N^{-1/2+(1+a)/(2s)}\|x\|_2
\right).
}
\]

Thus the v9 prime-power result now extends to arbitrary lengths with a strict `o(N log N)` cost, at the price of the dyadic-lift rank.

## 2. Nearest dyadic grid

For `0<=k<N`, define

\[
j_k=\operatorname{round}\!\left(\frac{kQ}{N}\right)
\]

and

\[
\theta_k=\frac{kQ}{N}-j_k.
\]

Because `Q>=N`, the indices `j_k` are distinct. By nearest rounding,

\[
|\theta_k|\le\frac12.
\]

For a time index `0<=n<N`, put

\[
s_n=\frac nQ.
\]

Then the exact kernel factors as

\[
\exp\!\left(-\frac{2\pi i kn}{N}\right)
=
\exp\!\left(-\frac{2\pi i j_kn}{Q}\right)
\exp(-2\pi i s_n\theta_k).
\tag{2.1}
\]

The first factor is on the dyadic `Q`-grid. Only the second, slowly varying factor must be approximated.

---

## 3. Stable rank-`R` Jacobi--Anger lift

For

\[
z=\pi s,\qquad y=2\theta,
\]

we have `0<=z<=pi` and `-1<=y<=1`. The Jacobi--Anger expansion gives

\[
\exp(-izy)
=
J_0(z)
+2\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}(-i)^rJ_r(z)T_r(y),
\tag{3.1}
\]

where `J_r` is the Bessel function of the first kind and `T_r` is the Chebyshev polynomial.

Define the truncation

\[
p_R(s,\theta)
=
J_0(\pi s)
+2\sum_{r=1}^{R-1}(-i)^rJ_r(\pi s)T_r(2\theta).
\tag{3.2}
\]

This basis is preferable to the monomial Taylor form for implementation:

- `|T_r(2theta)|<=1`;
- the Bessel weights decay rapidly;
- no powers `s^r` and `theta^r` with strongly unbalanced scales are required.

### Uniform tail

From the Bessel power series,

\[
|J_r(z)|
\le
\frac{(z/2)^r}{r!}
\exp\!\left(\frac{z^2}{4(r+1)}\right).
\tag{3.3}
\]

Let `b=pi/2`. For `R+1>b`, summing a geometric majorant gives

\[
\boxed{
\tau_R
=
2\exp\!\left(\frac{\pi^2}{4(R+1)}\right)
\frac{b^R}{R!}
\frac1{1-b/(R+1)}
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

and

\[
\sup_{0\le s\le1,\ |\theta|\le1/2}
|e^{-2\pi is\theta}-p_R(s,\theta)|
\le\tau_R.
\tag{3.5}
\]

In particular,

\[
|p_R(s,\theta)|\le1+\tau_R.
\tag{3.6}
\]

Stirling's formula implies that for every fixed `alpha>0`, achieving

\[
\tau_R\le N^{-\alpha}
\]

requires

\[
R=(\alpha+o(1))\frac{\log N}{\log\log N}.
\tag{3.7}
\]

## 4. Deterministic all-length approximation

For every expansion index `r`, form a zero-padded vector of length `Q`:

\[
y^{(0)}_n=x_nJ_0(\pi n/Q),
\]

and for `r>=1`,

\[
y^{(r)}_n=x_nJ_r(\pi n/Q),
\qquad 0\le n<N,
\]

with zeros for `N<=n<Q`.

Let

\[
F^{(r)}=U_Qy^{(r)}.
\]

The rank-`R` lifted transform is

\[
\overline X_k
=
\sqrt{\frac QN}
\left[
F^{(0)}_{j_k}
+2\sum_{r=1}^{R-1}
(-i)^rT_r(2\theta_k)F^{(r)}_{j_k}
\right].
\tag{4.1}
\]

Substituting (3.2) into (2.1) verifies (4.1) directly.

The deterministic approximation error is

\[
\begin{aligned}
|X_k-\overline X_k|
&\le
\frac{\tau_R}{\sqrt N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}|x_n|\\
&\le \tau_R\|x\|_2.
\end{aligned}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\|\overline X-U_Nx\|_\infty
\le\tau_R\|x\|_2.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

This part is deterministic and applies to every integer `N`.

## 5. Substituting the v9 chirp row

Choose the v9 dyadic block size

\[
M=2^{\lfloor\log_2\log_2Q\rfloor}
=\Theta(\log Q),
\]

reduced if necessary so `M^2<=Q`.

For one v9 random state `omega=(a,beta)`, let `P_omega` be its implicit block projection. **Use the same state for every expansion term.** Replace `F^(r)` in (4.1) by

\[
\widehat F^{(r)}=U_QP_\omega y^{(r)}.
\]

For a fixed target output `k`, linearity combines all terms into

\[
\widehat X_k^{(\omega)}
=
\sqrt{\frac QN}
\left[U_QP_\omega z^{(k)}\right]_{j_k},
\tag{5.1}
\]

where

\[
z^{(k)}_n=x_np_R(n/Q,\theta_k)
\]

for `n<N`, with zero padding afterward.

This identity is the crucial point: the rank-`R` sum is **one v9 covariance problem**, not `R` independent error terms.

Since the v9 row is unbiased,

\[
\mathbb E\widehat X_k^{(\omega)}=\overline X_k.
\tag{5.2}
\]

The exact dyadic covariance theorem gives

\[
\mathbb E
|[U_Q(P_\omega-I)z]_{j}|^2
\le
\frac MQ\|z\|_2^2.
\]

After multiplying by `Q/N` from (5.1) and using (3.6),

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E
|\widehat X_k^{(\omega)}-\overline X_k|^2
\le
\frac MN(1+\tau_R)^2\|x\|_2^2.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

The denominator is the **original arbitrary length `N`**, not the lifted length `Q`.

## 6. Streaming robust aggregation

Set

\[
\sigma_R=(1+\tau_R)\sqrt{\frac MN}\|x\|_2,
\qquad
\lambda=g\sigma_R,
\qquad g>\sqrt2.
\]

Use the existing three shifted grids and Boyer--Moore candidate/counter states separately for real and imaginary components. With `L` independent rows,

\[
\Pr\left[
\|\widetilde X-\overline X\|_\infty
>
\frac5{\sqrt2}g\sigma_R
\right]
\le
2N\exp\left[-L D\left(\frac12\middle\|\frac1{g^2}\right)\right].
\tag{6.1}
\]

For a fixed odd row count `L=2s-1`, the elementary binomial bound is

\[
\Pr[\text{failure}]
\le
2N{2s-1\choose s}g^{-2s}.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Combining (4.2) and (6.1),

\[
\boxed{
\|\widetilde X-U_Nx\|_\infty
\le
\left[
\tau_R
+
\frac5{\sqrt2}g(1+\tau_R)\sqrt{\frac MN}
\right]\|x\|_2.
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

## 7. Fully charged complexity

One separable term requires:

- `O(Q)` weighted-input construction;
- one v9 dyadic row: `O(Q+(Q/M)log(Q/M))=O(Q)`;
- `O(N)` gathering and accumulation.

Thus one lifted row costs

\[
\boxed{O(RQ)=O(RN).}
\tag{7.1}
\]

The Bessel weights and Chebyshev output factors can be generated term by term. The implementation stores only:

- one weighted length-`Q` vector;
- one active dyadic row;
- one accumulated arbitrary-length row;
- six majority candidate/counter states per final complex output.

Therefore

\[
\boxed{S(N)=O(N)}
\tag{7.2}
\]

independent of the expansion rank and randomized row count.

All Bessel-weight generation, Chebyshev recurrence work, grid mapping, term accumulation, and final output writes are charged.

## 8. The two all-length regimes

### 8.1 Fixed error in linear time

Fix `epsilon>0`. Choose a constant rank `R_epsilon` satisfying

\[
\tau_{R_\epsilon}\le\epsilon/2.
\]

Use three randomized rows and set

\[
g=
\frac{\epsilon}{5\sqrt2(1+\tau_R)}
\sqrt{\frac NM}.
\tag{8.1}
\]

Then the statistical radius in (6.3) is `epsilon||x||_2/2`, so the total error is at most `epsilon||x||_2`.

For three rows, (6.2) gives

\[
\Pr[\text{failure}]
\le
\frac{15000(1+\tau_R)^4M^2}{\epsilon^4N}
=
O\!\left(\frac{(\log N)^2}{\epsilon^4N}\right).
\tag{8.2}
\]

Since `R_epsilon` is independent of `N`,

\[
\boxed{T(N)=O_\epsilon(N),\qquad S(N)=O(N).}
\tag{8.3}
\]

### 8.2 Polynomially shrinking error below `N log N`

Fix `a>=0` and integer `s>1+a`. Let

\[
L=2s-1,
\qquad
\delta=N^{-a},
\]

and choose

\[
g=
\left(
\frac{2N{2s-1\choose s}}{\delta}
\right)^{1/(2s)}.
\tag{8.4}
\]

Define

\[
\alpha
=
\frac12-\frac{1+a}{2s}>0.
\]

Choose `R` so that

\[
\tau_R\le N^{-\alpha}.
\]

Equation (3.7) gives

\[
R=O_{a,s}\!\left(\frac{\log N}{\log\log N}\right).
\]

Then, with probability at least `1-N^{-a}`,

\[
\boxed{
\|\widetilde X-U_Nx\|_\infty
=
O_{a,s}\!\left(
\sqrt{\log N}\,N^{-\alpha}\|x\|_2
\right).
}
\tag{8.5}
\]

The charged resources are

\[
\boxed{
T(N)=O_{a,s}\!\left(\frac{N\log N}{\log\log N}\right)
=o(N\log N),
\qquad
S(N)=O(N).
}
\tag{8.6}
\]

Example with five rows (`s=3`) and constant failure probability:

\[
\|\widetilde X-U_Nx\|_\infty
=
O\!\left(\sqrt{\log N}\,N^{-1/3}\|x\|_2\right).
\]

## 9. Verification

The saved prototype verifies four independent layers.

### Kernel identity

The rank-`R` lifted formula using `R` separate dyadic FFTs was compared with an independent `O(N^2)` materialization of the truncated kernel for lengths

`7, 10, 15, 17, 31, 45`.

Maximum discrepancy:

\[
3.685313445256025\times10^{-14}.
\]

### Deterministic truncation

Across flat, Gaussian, random-phase, spike, and chirp inputs at prime and composite lengths, the maximum observed normalized truncation error was less than

\[
0.04648\,\tau_R.
\]

### One-row variance

Fifteen arbitrary-length experiments used 1,200 random rows each. The largest ratio

\[
\frac{\max_k\widehat{\operatorname{Var}}(\widehat X_k)}
{(M/N)(1+\tau_R)^2\|x\|_2^2}
\]

was

\[
0.945968.
\]

For `N=7`, all dyadic v9 states were enumerated exactly at floating-point precision. The mean equaled the deterministic lifted center, and the full per-output variance stayed below (5.3).

### Robust end to end

Twenty seeded end-to-end cases at rough lengths `31,61,97,127` had maximum

\[
\frac{\text{observed error}}{\text{proved radius}}=0.512386.
\]

All **12 automated tests pass**.

## 10. Prior-art boundary

The arbitrary-length bridge is not being presented as an isolated new NUFFT principle.

- Anderson and Dahleh (1996), *Rapid Computation of the Discrete Fourier Transform*, treated unrestricted/nonequispaced transforms with local Taylor expansions and FFTs.
- Ruiz-Antolin and Townsend (2018), *A Nonuniform Fast Fourier Transform Based on Low Rank Approximation*, developed low-rank corrections of a nearby DFT grid; their paper explicitly discusses Taylor and stable Chebyshev variants.
- The Jacobi--Anger identity and its Bessel/Chebyshev expansion are classical.

The project-specific result is the **composition theorem**:

1. use a stable near-grid separable lift to dyadic length;
2. replace every dyadic FFT term by the v9 unit-step chirp row;
3. reuse one random projection across all terms so their errors recombine into a single exact v9 covariance problem;
4. retain the all-output streaming majority and `O(N)` memory accounting.

A limited search did not identify this exact composition, but that is not a novelty proof.

---

## 11. Honest boundary

This checkpoint does **not** establish:

- an exact sub-`N log N` DFT;
- coefficientwise relative accuracy;
- ordinary machine-precision FFT accuracy in `O(N)`;
- a fixed-binary64 asymptotic theorem;
- a practical speedup over FFTW;
- a new low-rank NUFFT principle;
- peer review or established novelty.

The fixed-error `O(N)` theorem permits absolute error `epsilon||x||_2`, which can be much larger than an individual cancellation-heavy Fourier coefficient. Polynomially shrinking error costs `O(N log N/log log N)` rather than `O(N)`.

The Bessel/Chebyshev basis avoids the most obvious monomial instability, but a complete end-to-end floating-point theorem—including Bessel table generation and cancellation in the `R`-term sum—remains open.

## 12. New frontier

For the randomized full-output **approximate** problem, the missing all-length asymptotic bridge is now closed.

The next meaningful targets are:

1. prove a certified finite-precision bound for the Bessel/Chebyshev lift plus v9 scan;
2. fuse the `R` dyadic chirp rows so the rank factor is reduced in memory traffic and wall time;
3. replace the classical low-rank grid correction with a project-originated direct rough-length evaluator;
4. compare against FFTW/FINUFFT at equal output error, not merely equal transform length;
5. formalize the v9 projector theorem and the v10 composition in a proof assistant.
