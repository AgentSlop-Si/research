# Independent review: exact multi-parent product reuse

**Verdict:** PASS within the stated fixed-degree, trusted-cache, exact-integer
scope. The construction supplies a valid structured-batch improvement over
the previous single-parent sparse-correction family. It is not an arbitrary-
input multiplication improvement, and differing seed backends cannot be
compared by dividing their scalar counters.

Reviewed `multiparent.py`, `audit.py`, and the final README. Additional targeted
checks and final source hashes are saved in `independent_review_checks.json`.

## Exact identities and mixed terms

In secant mode every B is explicitly checked against the common operand.
The computed residual R_i=A_i-2A_(i-1)+A_(i-2) therefore gives

    A_i B = 2(A_(i-1)B) - A_(i-2)B + R_i B.

Either the coordinate residual or its signed canonical numeric representation
is valid after evaluation at base 256. The latter is necessary when long
digit-carry chains obscure a small integer residual. Both representations
are constructed and checked before choosing the smaller support. Dense
residuals trigger a paid seed; discovery is still charged.

In polynomial mode all applicable finite-difference tests are performed
before accepting an operand degree. Vanishing differences give a rational
Newton polynomial agreeing with every supplied complete integer. Multiplying
those two polynomials gives degree at most d_A+d_B and the stated recurrence
of order d_A+d_B+1. This proof includes the mixed term when both operands
vary. For short batches a degree can be accepted vacuously, but the required
seed prefix then covers all available queries, so no unsupported extrapolation
occurs.

An independent both-affine control used A_i=255+3i and B_i=256-2i. The correct
product recurrence used three seeds; the incorrect two-seed secant prediction
missed the next output by -12=2(3)(-2). A cubic-by-cubic control verified the
maximum supported product degree six and its seven-seed recurrence.

## Signed carries and widths

Discovery tests have difference order at most four. Their total coefficient
absolute weight is at most 16, so an extra operand byte safely accommodates
the signed represented difference. A negative carry is converted using the
base-256 complement construction. Values at either excluded signed boundary
are rejected.

Product recurrences have order at most seven and sum of absolute non-current
weights at most 127. Canonical product digits are at most 255, so recurrence
accumulators and their carries stay within a constant arithmetic width. The
extra product guard byte and final carry are checked before accepting the
2n output digits. Secant patches may contain an extra high digit, but their
convolution still fits the allocated 2n+1 coefficient buffer.

Review found that the helper `signed_patch` could silently truncate an
oversized coefficient buffer, even when its represented integer fit the
claimed range. For example, [-255,1] represents 1 at base 256. The author
added an explicit length guard; the independent negative control now rejects
this unsupported buffer instead of returning -255. Core schedule buffers
already obeyed the width condition.

The requested long-carry family around 256^(n-1), with offsets
[-2,-1,0,1,3,4,5,7] and dense B, passes for n=3 and n=64. The verified second-
difference supports are [0,0,1,1,0,1], including a negative residual, and only
two seed multiplications are needed. All resulting full products match the
independent integer oracle.

## Cost, provenance, and scope

Seed multiplication is explicitly a multiplication of at most 8n-bit operands,
charged as M(8n). Input packing, output conversion, validation, cache copies,
and full output writes are recorded separately. A zero in the baseline's
additional-word-arithmetic column does not make its multiplication free.

For fixed degree limit three, degree discovery and recurrence arithmetic have
O(Qn) bounded-width work. Secant mode uses ns digit products for a support-s
residual; accumulators can grow to O(log(s+2)) bits, so a growing patch limit
must not be interpreted as constant-bit arithmetic. With a fixed patch limit,
the non-seed arithmetic is linear in Qn. Index-word operations and interpreter
traffic remain separate from an arithmetic-bit count, as the final README
now explains. Selected logical counters are not an exhaustive machine trace.

Cached prefixes use complete operand keys and canonical product digits. Valid
partial prefixes pay every missing seed; an independent one-seed cache control
required two further calls for a three-seed trajectory, restoring the cold
total. Creation, validation, copying and output rematerialization can repeat
linear work. Product digits with correct format remain trusted internal
results, not independently authenticated multiplication certificates.

The dense-first-difference/zero-second-difference family genuinely separates
the two supplied reuse models. The lower bound on one-parent correction work
applies to the specified direct sparse-digit kernel, not every possible
single-parent algorithm. The same-target comparison against the earlier
NTT graph proves a structural change in required fresh evaluations; it does
not prove a wall-clock ratio between different seed backends.

Independent targeted verification passes 47 additional complete product
comparisons, 21 signed-width boundary controls, the oversized-buffer rejection,
mixed-term control, and partial-cache accounting. No unresolved correctness
issue was found under the declared assumptions.
