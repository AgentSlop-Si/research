# Independent audit: half-cut and low-rank-update controls

9 October 2026. Uploaded source files were read only as mathematical text. No uploaded Python file was run or imported. The checker accompanying this report was written independently using only Python's standard library. No literature-priority claim is made.

## Findings first

1. **The half-cut theorem is valid.** Its stated odd-length bound and its two separate one-endpoint relabeling extensions are valid. Arbitrary relabeling of both endpoints is outside the proof and must remain excluded.
2. **Correct the rank-two all-length threshold.** The proved bound excludes two rank-two stages for all `N >= 18`; for even lengths it excludes `N > 16`. It does not itself exclude `N = 17`. The first excluded power of two remains `N = 32`.
3. **The low-rank-update control can use the stronger, nonunitary bound `sum rank(R_i) >= floor(N/2)`.** This is a direct application of the project's existing sparse-rigidity/Vandermonde gate at sparsity one, not a new project discovery or novelty claim. It also allows arbitrary nonzero monomial gains. The source's weaker unitary trace proof is correct.
4. **Inverse/complementary ranks validate the Green premise without pivot assumptions.** An invertible matrix and its inverse have exactly the same upper and lower cut ranks. Thus an invertible tridiagonal matrix has a one-quasiseparable inverse even when some principal blocks are singular.
5. The old square-root minor theorem and the three-stage proof-technique counterexample are valid. Neither gives a general FFT lower bound or resolves the endpoint-permuted, two-stage question.

## 1. Exact half-cut proof, including odd lengths

Write `N = 2m` or `2m+1`, with `m = floor(N/2)`. Let `I=L={0,...,m-1}` and `R={N-m,...,N-1}`. For odd `N`, the central coordinate is in neither `L` nor `R`.

For `M=A P B`, write `(PB)[k,j]=d_k B[pi(k),j]`, with `pi` a permutation and `d_k != 0`. Let `a` be the upper cut rank of `A` at cut `m`, let `b_lower` bound the lower cut rank of `B` at cut `m`, and let `b_upper` bound the upper cut rank of `B` at cut `N-m`. Set

    c_L = |{k in I : pi(k) in L}|,
    c_R = |{k in I : pi(k) in R}|.

The contribution through middle indices outside `I` has rank at most `a` for either output block. Among the indices inside `I`, the rows not originating in `L` have their left restriction in `B[L^c,L]`; the rows not originating in `R` have their right restriction in `B[R^c,R]`. Consequently,

    rank M[I,L] <= c_L + a + b_lower,
    rank M[I,R] <= c_R + a + b_upper.

Since `c_L+c_R <= m`, two full-rank target blocks imply the slightly more directional statement

    2a + b_lower + b_upper >= m.

In particular, `r_A+r_B >= m/2` under the source's uniform bounds. For even `N`, `c_L+c_R=m`; for odd `N`, it may be smaller. The central row is already included in the relevant complementary off-diagonal block. This is the precise justification for the source's phrase “proof is unchanged.”

The Fourier target blocks have rank `m`: the rows are consecutive powers of the distinct nodes associated with the selected frequencies. The same two blocks for `V[i,j]=alpha_i^j` are a Vandermonde matrix and a nonsingular row scaling of one, since the nodes are distinct and nonzero.

### Threshold correction

If both stages have rank at most two, their rank sum is at most four. Contradiction requires `floor(N/2)/2 > 4`, which is exactly `N >= 18`. At `N=17`, the necessary condition is `4 >= 4`, so this certificate leaves the case unresolved. This is a limitation of this bound, not evidence that a factorization at 17 exists.

For equal integer cut rank `r`, the all-length necessary condition is

    r >= ceil(floor(N/2)/4).

For even lengths this equals `ceil(N/8)`, as used in the table.

### Invertibility wording

The rank estimates are valid without assuming either stage invertible. However, an exact identity `F_N=A P B` forces both square stages invertible because `F_N` and `P` are invertible. Replace “the proof allows ... singular individual stages” with “the rank estimates require no separate stage-invertibility assumption.” There is no singular-stage loophole in an exact factorization.

## 2. Endpoint permutation scope

**Output endpoint alone, for Vandermonde matrices:** after arbitrary output-row permutation, the first `m` output rows still correspond to `m` distinct nonzero nodes. Each of the two consecutive-column blocks is nonsingular. Arbitrary nonzero row gains only scale rows.

**Input endpoint alone, for Fourier matrices:** after arbitrary input-column permutation, the first `m` natural output rows are powers `0,...,m-1` of any selected `m` distinct Fourier nodes. Thus both required blocks are nonsingular. Arbitrary nonzero column gains do not change rank.

**Both endpoints:** the same premise fails. Already for `F_4`, the rows `{0,2}` and columns `{0,2}` give an all-ones `2 x 2` block of rank one. More importantly, the project's previously verified `F_16` construction has two rank-one bidirectional stages with independently permuted endpoints; it would contradict a false extension of the bound to both endpoints. No such extension should be inferred from this theorem.

The input-only extension is special to Fourier. For example, a general Vandermonde matrix with nodes including `1` and `-1` has dependent restrictions on these two rows when the selected powers are both even; arbitrary input-column selection need not retain the needed full minor.

## 3. Inverse/complementary ranks and Green stages

For an invertible `N x N` matrix `X` and arbitrary row/column sets `I,J`, the complementary-rank identity is

    rank(X^{-1}[I,J])
      = rank(X[J^c,I^c]) + |I| + |J| - N.

For complementary prefix/suffix sets `L,R`, this gives

    rank(X^{-1}[L,R]) = rank(X[L,R]),
    rank(X^{-1}[R,L]) = rank(X[R,L]).

For a direct proof of the first equality, identify `R` with its coordinate subspace. The kernel dimension of `X[L,R]` is `dim(R intersect X^{-1}R)`. The kernel dimension of `X^{-1}[L,R]` is `dim(R intersect XR)`. Multiplication by `X` is a bijection from the first intersection to the second, so these nullities, and hence ranks, agree. The other direction is identical.

An invertible tridiagonal `T` has upper and lower off-diagonal cut rank at most one, so `T^{-1}` does too. Therefore, whenever `T` and `T+I` are invertible, `T^{-1}+(T+I)^{-1}` has both cut ranks at most two. This does not require positive definiteness or invertibility of every leading principal block. Taking an inverse preserves the directional cut ranks; it does not by itself strengthen the coefficient `1/4` in the half-cut theorem.

## 4. Stronger low-rank-update control, using the existing rigidity gate

Let `M` be any monomial matrix, with arbitrary nonzero complex gains, and let `m=floor(N/2)`. Among the first `m` rows, `M` uses exactly `m` columns. Choose `m` columns outside that support; there are at least `N-m >= m` available. On the resulting square minor, `F_N-M` is precisely a Fourier Vandermonde minor with consecutive rows, hence has rank `m`. Thus

    rank(F_N-M) >= floor(N/2).

The same statement holds for the normalized Fourier matrix and for arbitrary external monomial changes at both endpoints: rank is invariant under invertible left/right multiplication, and monomial matrices stay monomial under those changes. More generally, a Vandermonde target with distinct nodes also obeys this bound: take its first `m` columns and choose `m` rows outside the monomial support of those columns.

For a circuit consisting of stages `I+R_i`, interspersed with arbitrary invertible monomial matrices, let `M` be the product with all updates replaced by identities. Telescoping the product and using rank subadditivity gives

    rank(T-M) <= sum_i rank(R_i).

Every summand in the telescoping expansion has one factor `R_i`; arbitrary left/right products cannot increase its rank. Therefore an exact Fourier realization requires

    sum_i rank(R_i) >= floor(N/2),
    t*r >= floor(N/2)  when every rank(R_i) <= r.

This improves the source's bound `(N-sqrt(N))/2`, removes its unitary-monomial restriction, and applies despite arbitrarily large gains. It remains exclusively a low-rank-update-to-monomial obstruction. Dense Green stages can have large distance from every monomial, so this is not a Green-stage depth lower bound.

The Householder example in the source is correct: its vector is unit, `H` is orthogonal, the first-column absolute sum is `sqrt(N-1)`, `kappa_1(H) >= N-1`, and `kappa_2(H)=1`. The trace/Frobenius estimate is also correct within its stated unitary scope.

## 5. Other source claims

The square-root theorem's disjoint-interval counting, two off-diagonal contributions from `B`, first-prefix contribution from `A`, and Fourier Vandermonde witness are valid. Its transposed inequality is valid because the unnormalized Fourier matrix is symmetric. The stated rank-two exclusion at `N>=64` is correct, although superseded in its fixed-endpoint scope by the half-cut bound.

For the three-stage control, a block diagonal matrix with `[[1,1],[1,2]]` blocks has cut rank at most one, while all four even/odd half-submatrices are full rank. This correctly defeats an attempted proof step that assumes arbitrary permuted half-submatrices of the central stage have low rank. It establishes neither existence nor impossibility of a three-stage Fourier factorization.

## 6. Independent checks and files

`independent_checker.py` uses exact modular Gaussian elimination, with independently selected primes and verified primitive roots for lengths 2 through 17 and 32. It checks all permutations at lengths up to 6 and samples larger permutations with nonzero gains. It tests the directional half-cut inequalities, all four one-endpoint minor claims, low-rank-distance witness minors, inverse cut-rank identities, tridiagonal-inverse cut ranks, and the three-stage even/odd blocks. It separately confirms the simultaneous-endpoint minor failure at length four and the unresolved threshold arithmetic at 17.

All checks passed: 1,232 half-cut product checks; 4,928 one-endpoint minor checks; 1,232 monomial-distance witnesses; 1,002 general inverse-cut identities; 1,002 tridiagonal-inverse cut checks; and 36 three-stage-control block checks. Results are in `independent_results.json`; captured standard output is in `checker_stdout.json`. Finite tests are audit support. The universal conclusions are justified by the proofs above.
