# Prior art and originality boundaries

The project builds on classical DFT Fourier orthogonality and cyclic convolution, Prony/Berlekamp–Massey reconstruction of short exponential sums, Sylvester resultants, Hankel rank identities, Dickson/Chebyshev polynomial trace tests, and the Cooley–Tukey/Bluestein FFTs. The Phase 21/22 theorem for reciprocal-resultant compression is inherited and is not asserted here as independently peer-reviewed or formally verified.

The new Phase 23 work is limited to a specific **redundant-probe elimination** (`4s -> 2s+2`), a shared streaming prefix implementation, a joint adjacent-Hankel compiler, and preserving exactness when the previous unique-prefix early exit is no longer applicable. No broad novelty, worldwide priority or asymptotic generic FFT speedup is claimed. Similar overlap-exploitation and transposed summation techniques are commonplace in computational algebra; a literature search is insufficient to prove these exact optimizations are novel.

Classical restricted FFT lower bounds are relevant barriers but should **not** be overstated as proving the unrestricted problem impossible:
- J. Morgenstern, “Note on a Lower Bound on the Linear Complexity of the Fast Fourier Transform,” *JACM* 20(2), 1973: https://doi.org/10.1145/321752.321761 (bounded-coefficient setting).
- N. Ailon, “An n log n Lower Bound for Fourier Transform Computation in the Well Conditioned Model,” arXiv:1403.1307, https://arxiv.org/abs/1403.1307 (conditioning assumptions).

CrocSwap/integer-mult-bounds is an unrelated conditional integer-multiplication framework. Phase 23 does not use its exponent-construction mechanism. The transferable principle is only **charge all operations and verify complete physical/exact identities**, not to copy CrocSwap's proof path.
