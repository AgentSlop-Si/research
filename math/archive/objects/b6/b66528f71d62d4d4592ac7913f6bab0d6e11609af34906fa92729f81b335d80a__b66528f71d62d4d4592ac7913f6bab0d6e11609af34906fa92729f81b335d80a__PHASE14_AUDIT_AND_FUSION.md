# Phase 14 audit and fusion with the Haar/whole-word gates

Independent audit, 9 October 2026. No uploaded executable was imported or run.

## Executive conclusion

The phase-14 algebraic results are correct in their stated models:

- An arbitrary single diagonal mask between projectively `K_B = Q(ζ_B)` invertible factors exists precisely when `N ≤ B²`, for powers of two `8 ≤ B < N`.
- A single integer-polynomial root-phase mask cannot work, even in that feasible range.
- The fixed-strength two-stage Green/Haar architecture is impossible at every `N ≥ 16` by the last stage's concentration.
- The Galois rank budget is a phase-support restriction, not an arithmetic-time lower bound.
- The two-mask constructions at `N = 128, 256, 512` are genuine positive controls with arbitrary `K_8` mixers. They do not establish a Green/Haar realization or an asymptotic improvement.

There is one harmless normalization repair: the explicit phase-only sparse formula in §8, and the uploaded modular controls, produce the unnormalized matrix `[ω^(kj)]`. To match the normalized Fourier matrix used at the beginning, multiply the residual by `1/√N ∈ K_B`. This changes neither the field, sparsity, number of nonbase masks, nor the asymptotic accounting.

Two useful consequences emerge when the proofs are fused:

1. **Exact entropy allocation in Galois descent.** The orbit factorization can be normalized to `F = U Q V` with both `U,V` unitary over `K_B`. Its two factors have entropies
   
   `Φ(U) = N(log₂(N/B) − 1) + B`,
   
   `Φ(V) = N(log₂ B + 1) − B`.
   
   Thus the growing-conductor residual itself requires `Ω(N log B)` two-coordinate unitary gates. When `B ≍ √N`, an `O(N)` residual executor is impossible in this model, not merely unproved. The unrestricted arithmetic/fusion question remains open.
2. **Conductor-resolved phase budgets.** In a `K_8` circuit the rank test applies at every larger dyadic base conductor `B′`, not just 8. At the top level, any root-phase masks must satisfy
   
   `Σ_masks min(number of even exponents, number of odd exponents) ≥ N/4`.
   
   A few isolated primitive-`N` phase patches cannot supply the missing highest-conductor component, even if the original `σ₉` budget passes.

## 1. Independent proof audit

### 1.1 Cyclotomic and permutation facts

Let `N=2^m`, `B=2^b`, `3 ≤ b < m`, `L=N/B`, and `a=1+B`. The automorphism `σ_a(ω)=ω^a` generates `Gal(Q(ζ_N)/Q(ζ_B))`, since

`v₂((1+B)^(2^s) − 1) = b+s`.

The normalization `N^(-1/2)` belongs to the base field, so `σ_a(F)=P_a F` also holds for normalized Fourier.

For multiplication by `a` on residue indices, the cycle histogram is:

- `B` cycles of length 1;
- `B/2` cycles of each length `2,4,…,L`.

Therefore

`c(N,B)=B+(B/2)log₂ L`.

Every primitive `L`-th eigenvalue has multiplicity `B/2`. The maximum eigenvalue multiplicity is exactly `c(N,B)`, attained by the eigenvalue 1. Consequently the projective Galois deviation of Fourier is exactly, not just bounded below by,

`min_(c≠0) rank(σ_a(F)−cF) = N−c(N,B)`.

### 1.2 Arbitrary one-mask threshold

Absorb the projective scalars and coordinate permutation into base-field factors to obtain

`F=B₂ Q′ B₁`, with `B₁,B₂ ∈ GL_N(K_B)`.

Because `Q′=B₂^(-1) F B₁^(-1)`, its diagonal entries automatically lie in `E=Q(ζ_N)`, even when the original supplied mask had arbitrary complex entries. Thus no unstated algebraicity assumption is needed. Applying Galois gives

`P_a = B₂ [σ_a(Q′) Q′^(-1)] B₂^(-1)`.

The displayed diagonal must itself lie in `K_B`, since it equals `B₂^(-1)P_aB₂`. Hence `P_a` is diagonalizable over `K_B`. Its primitive `L`-th eigenvalues belong to `K_B` exactly when `L ≤ B`. This proves necessity of `N ≤ B²`.

For sufficiency, diagonalize each permutation cycle by its character basis over `K_B`, choose `Q` with Galois ratios equal to the corresponding eigenvalues, and descend `Q^(-1)B₂^(-1)F` to the fixed field. This argument is valid and does not assume a cheap executor for either base-field factor.

### 1.3 Polynomial-phase exclusion

For `Q_jj=ω^(f(j))` with `f∈Z[x]`, the Galois-ratio eigenvalue is `ζ_L^(f(j))`. The residues `f(j) mod L` repeat every `L` coordinates, so each eigenvalue multiplicity is divisible by `B`. The primitive-root eigenvalue multiplicity of `P_a` is `B/2`, a contradiction.

A permutation of mask coordinates preserves these multiplicities. Nonzero base-field diagonal factors cancel out of the Galois ratio. Projective scalar factors multiply all eigenvalues by the same scalar, preserving the multiplicities. Thus the stated variants are covered. This does not exclude arbitrary non-polynomial schedules, as the small-size construction demonstrates.

### 1.4 Fixed-strength Green/Haar theorem

For a path Cayley with adjacency norm at most 2 and coupling `1/32`, `‖C−I‖₂ ≤ 1/8`. For the normalized involution rotation at strength `1/512`, `‖V−I‖₂ ≤ 1/512`. Unitary product telescoping gives

`‖S₂−I‖₂ ≤ 129/512`.

The same bound holds for the inverse. In column 1, the diagonal probability weight therefore exceeds `1/2`. In the base-field expansion of `(S₂^(-1)DS₂)[1,1]`, the coefficient of `ω` has one contribution of magnitude greater than `1/2`, while all other contributions together have magnitude below `1/2`; it cannot vanish.

One detail worth making explicit: if `S₂=gB` is only projectively base-field-valued and unitary, the weights still belong to `K`. Indeed

`|g|² = 1 / Σ_k |B_k1|² ∈ K`,

because the cyclotomic base field is stable under complex conjugation. Hence `|S₂[k,1]|² ∈ K`, as the proof needs.

Monomial similarity of `S₁ Z S₁^(-1)` would require the diagonal entries in question to be in `K`, yielding the contradiction. This excludes all exact invertible Fourier intertwiners in the stated fixed-strength architecture. Strong retunings do not satisfy this norm hypothesis; the older Haar entropy gate, rather than this concentration estimate, handles arbitrary real strengths.

### 1.5 Phase-support telescoping

For a common automorphism and an invertible diagonal `Q`,

`r_σ(Q)=min_(c≠0) rank(σ(Q)−cQ)`.

Product telescoping and rank subadditivity bound the corresponding projective deviation of a word by the sum of the deviations of its nonbase masks. Projectively base-field factors contribute zero. For Fourier the exact minimum is `N−c(N,B)`, proving the budget.

For supplied algebraic root phases this is directly operational: replace each exponent by `(1+B)` times itself. For an arbitrary complex circuit, an automorphism extension requires the field-theoretic setup stated in phase 14; no numerical operation or continuous phase deformation is implied. The budget concerns exact algebraic equality and is not a robust approximation theorem.

### 1.6 Covariance checks and finite controls

The recurrence `AZ=DA` determines all columns from `Ae₀`. Consequently `AZ=DA` plus `Ae₀=ones/√N` is sufficient for exact Fourier. Galois covariance is an additional necessary screen; Weyl and Galois covariance alone do not fix the seed. For example, suitable Fourier row-phase modifications still pass both while differing from `F`.

The twelve failed three-stage examples remain only sampled failures. The positive two-mask identity is a valid Cooley–Tukey identity with descended bounded-size factors; it prevents extrapolating those samples to a general two-mask or three-mixer impossibility.

Finite-field tests must be interpreted carefully. A characteristic-zero symbolic identity can be checked by an exact exponent proof or field argument; a successful single modular evaluation alone does not prove it. Conversely a nonzero, well-defined reduction certifies failure of the corresponding characteristic-zero identity. Phase 14 supplies the needed proofs in addition to tests.

## 2. New lemma: no entropy discount in the explicit descent

### Statement

Assume `8 ≤ B < N ≤ B²`, all powers of two. Let the explicit phase-14 orbit construction use cycles `C` of sizes `d`. There is a normalized factorization

`F_N = U Q V`,

where `U,V ∈ U(N)∩M_N(K_B)` and `Q` is a unit-modulus root-phase diagonal. Its entropies have the exact values

`Φ(U)=N(log₂(N/B)−1)+B`,

`Φ(V)=N(log₂ B+1)−B`.

Here `Φ(X)=−Σ_ij |X_ij|² log₂|X_ij|²` for unitary `X`.

### Proof

For a cycle `k_t=k₀(1+B)^t`, `0≤t<d`, label its character columns by `e`, set `r=eL/d`, and write

`U[k_t,(C,e)] = d^(-1/2) ζ_d^(et)`.

The blocks are normalized Fourier matrices, so `U` is unitary over `K_B`. Set `Q_(C,e)=ω^r`.

The corresponding residual row is

`V[(C,e),j] = √(d/N) ζ_B^((k₀j−r)/L)` if `k₀j≡r (mod L)`, and zero otherwise.

The normalization factors belong to `K_B` because `d,N` are powers of two and `√2∈K_B`. The phase congruence proof yields `F=UQV`. Since `F,U,Q` are unitary, `V=Q^*U^*F` is unitary as well.

Each row of the `d`-cycle block of `U` has `d` nonzeros of squared magnitude `1/d`, giving entropy `log₂d` per row. For `V`, the congruence has exactly `N/d` solutions in `j`, because `gcd(k₀,L)=L/d`. Each solution has squared magnitude `d/N`; the row entropy is `log₂(N/d)`. The cycle contributes

`d log₂d` to `Φ(U)` and `d log₂(N/d)` to `Φ(V)`.

Their sum over all cycles is `N log₂N`. Using the cycle histogram, and `ℓ=log₂L`,

`Φ(U)=(B/2)Σ_(s=1)^ℓ s2^s = B[(ℓ−1)2^ℓ+1] = N(ℓ−1)+B`.

Subtracting from `N log₂N` gives the residual formula. This proves the lemma.

### Consequences for executors

A two-coordinate unitary changes `Φ` by at most two bits; unitary diagonals and coordinate permutations change it by zero. Therefore any such exact executor for this particular normalized residual satisfies

`G_V ≥ [N(log₂B+1)−B]/2`.

Likewise

`G_U ≥ [N(log₂(N/B)−1)+B]/2`.

These are individual-factor lower bounds, not just the lower bound on the final Fourier product. Separate unitary implementations cannot make the residual entropy disappear. For growing `B≥√N`, `G_V=Ω(N log N)`. The direct sparse executor's `Θ(NB(1+log(N/B)))` work is not proved optimal; an `O(N log N)` structured implementation may still improve on that naive executor.

If the normalized residual is implemented by `t` of the previously audited Haar–Green stages or inverse stages, interleaved with arbitrary unitary monomials, the gate decomposition there yields the more specific restriction

`t ≥ [N(log₂B+1)−B]/(11N−12)`.

The primitive gate coefficients in this comparison model are unrestricted complex unitaries; the Givens decomposition is not claimed to preserve the cyclotomic field.

This closes the proposed constant-stage/O(N)-unitary-gate residual strategy when `B` grows. It does not rule out general nonunitary arithmetic, a different nonlocal primitive, or an algebraically fused whole-word circuit outside this comparison model. In particular, the lower bound must not be exported to a nonunitary executor merely because its final matrix is unitary.

## 3. New lemma: a full conductor profile of phase support

### Statement

Let a word use projectively `K_B`-valued invertible stages, permutations, and root-phase masks

`Q_ell[j,j]=ω^(f_ell(j))`,

where `f_ell` are arbitrary integer schedules, not necessarily polynomials. Suppose it computes normalized Fourier at length `N`. For every dyadic `B′` with `B ≤ B′ < N`,

`Σ_ell [N − max_(r mod N/B′) #{j : f_ell(j)≡r (mod N/B′)}]`

`≥ N − B′ − (B′/2) log₂(N/B′)`.

### Proof

The automorphism `σ_(1+B′)` fixes `K_B`, as `B` divides `B′`. Its permutation has `B′+(B′/2)log₂(N/B′)` cycles. The mask's Galois ratio is `ζ_(N/B′)^(f_ell(j))`, so its projective deviation rank is exactly `N` minus the modal residue population. Apply the same rank-telescoping proof independently at every `B′`. Projective scalar multipliers cancel after taking the minimum over scalars.

### Highest-conductor consequence

At `B′=N/2`, the residue modulus is 2, so

`Σ_ell min(n_even(ell), n_odd(ell)) ≥ N/4`.

This is invariant under a common phase scalar on each mask: adding an odd constant merely swaps the even and odd counts. It measures genuine coordinate-dependent use of the highest conductor, rather than a globally irrelevant primitive-root scalar.

### A screen missed by the original single-automorphism budget

At `N=128`, use two schedules

`f₁(j)=2j`,

`f₂(j)=2j+1` for `j<3`, and `f₂(j)=2j` otherwise.

For `B′=8`, their deviation ranks are `112` and `112`, easily passing the required total `104`. At `B′=16,32` they pass as well. But at `B′=64`, the ranks are `0` and `3`, below the required `32`. Thus no choice of surrounding `K_8` stages can make these masks compute Fourier. Unlike the trivial case of all-even exponents, the circuit contains primitive-128 phases; they are simply too sparsely distributed.

All these remain support restrictions. A dense phase mask costs only `O(N)` scalar multiplications, and passing every conductor budget is not sufficient for Fourier.

## 4. Precise relation to the previous two gates

### Haar entropy: phase freedom does not erase mixing work

The earlier Haar result permits arbitrary strengths, path weights, coordinate orders, and arbitrary unit-modulus diagonal masks or permutations between stages. A phase mask has zero matrix-entropy change irrespective of its conductor. Therefore phase 14's higher-conductor masks do not evade

`t ≥ N log₂N/(11N−12)`

for exact Fourier made from that Haar–Green stage family. Galois restrictions and entropy restrictions measure different resources and should be imposed together. The former survives arbitrary invertible base-field mixers, while the latter allows arbitrary scalar fields but requires the specified unitary gate realization.

The two-mask positive control does not conflict: its non-diagonal factors are arbitrary `K_8` matrices, not established single Haar–Green stages. Its finite sizes also do not form an unbounded one-mask regime.

### Whole-word rational and single-solve gates

If a candidate fusion stays in a common-base scalar rational function of a matrix with quasiseparable order `h`, the previously proved condition remains

`h · max(deg p,deg q) ≥ floor(N²/4)/N`.

If it becomes one low-cut-rank solve with local scalar ports, the same prior whole-word rank obstruction applies, including arbitrarily many auxiliary variables under those local-port assumptions. Field descent does not alter these exact matrix-rank facts.

Conversely, a new high-conductor diagonal inserted inside a word need not commute with its common rational base. It can destroy the common-base rational representation. Arbitrary internal permutations can destroy a shared low-quasiseparable ordering. The rational theorem cannot be applied after such changes unless its structural hypothesis is re-established.

There is one simple extension already implicit in the prior cut-rank product lemma: if all stages are measured in one common order, then arbitrary diagonal masks have quasiseparable order zero, so

`qsep(total word) ≤ Σ_j qsep(stage_j)`.

Hence `Σ_j qsep(stage_j) ≥ floor(N²/4)/N` is necessary for Fourier up to nonsingular endpoint monomials. This does not require the stages to commute or share a scalar rational function. Changing internal permutations requires charging their cut ranks or proving a compatible order; it is not free under this assertion.

The old quadratic-chirp/common-cycle positive control is also compatible with phase 14. It uses two endpoint chirps and a convolution whose coefficients already involve high-conductor roots, so it is not a single polynomial mask sandwiched between two base-field factors.

### What remains open

No result here proves a general exact Fourier arithmetic lower bound, a sub-FFT algorithm, or practical numerical acceleration. A genuine further algorithm must both pass the algebraic/covariance gates and provide a completely charged executor outside every applicable obstruction. Priority of the new combinations has not been certified by a literature search.
