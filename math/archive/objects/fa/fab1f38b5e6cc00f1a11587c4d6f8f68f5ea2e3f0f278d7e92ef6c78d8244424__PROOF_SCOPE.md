## P1. Induced-condition budgets and the Haar escape

as does every column of its inverse. Thus kappa_1(U)=N. Submultiplicativity gives
N <= product_i kappa_1(S_i) for invertible factors. Unit phases, permutations,
and nonzero global scalars have condition number one. This proves the stated
logarithmic stage bound for a uniformly bounded-condition family. A stage bound
becomes a time bound only when each displayed full-array pass is separately paid.

For T=3I-A_path, the convergent nonnegative Neumann series gives ||T^-1||_1<=1,
||T||_1<=5. With U=T+I, ||U^-1||_1<=1/2. For S=T^-1+U^-1,
S^-1=T(T+I)(2T+I)^-1=T/2+I/4-(2T+I)^-1/4. Since
||(2T+I)^-1||_1<=1/3, the inverse norm is at most 17/6 and kappa_1(S)<=17/4.
These arguments hold for every path length, including endpoints and N=1.
Sixteen newly executed exact rational instances corroborate the identities.

For M=G+epsilon PGP^T, epsilon=1/100, factoring out G and applying the Neumann
inverse bound yields ||M||_1<=101/100, ||M^-1||_1<=100/19, hence kappa_1<=101/19.
Because both terms are positive definite and each lies spectrally between I/5
and I, their sum has condition number at most five.

The Haar transform uses N-1 butterflies. Its uniform-average row implies
||W^-1||_1=sqrt(N); its columns have geometrically summable absolute magnitudes,
so 1<=||W||_1<=constant. Consequently kappa_1(W)=Theta(sqrt(N)) while
kappa_2(W)=1. The inequality kappa_1(W)<=kappa_1(WM)kappa_1(M) proves the claimed
growing one-condition lower bound for WM. It proves neither Fourier covariance
nor inheritance of every cut-rank property by left multiplication with W.

## P2. Low-rank perturbations and forward-cycle spectral gates

If rank(U-G)<=r<N, restrict U-G to any r+1 coordinate columns; it has a nonzero
kernel vector x supported there. Then Gx=Ux. Flatness gives
||Ux||_infinity<=sqrt(r+1)||x||_2/sqrt(N), and unitarity plus
||y||_2^2<=||y||_1||y||_infinity gives
||Ux||_1>=sqrt(N)||x||_2/sqrt(r+1). Dividing by ||x||_1 proves
||G||_1>=sqrt(N)/(r+1). Inverse-difference rank is preserved, so the same proof
applies to G^-1. Telescoping deletion of B rank-one factors changes rank by at
most B. With the stated bounded Green factors this proves 7^t(B+1)^2>=N.
Since (B+1)^2<=7^B for integer B>=0, t+B>=ceil(log_7 N). Arbitrary free unitaries
cannot be included in the zero-K branch: Fourier itself would then be free.

For a unitary full weighted cycle M with M^N=-I, K=(2I-M)(I-M)^-1 has squared

## P3. Sandwich support, cut movement, and covariance

For A=L^-1 T R^-1 with bidiagonal L,R and contiguous invertible T components of
length <=b, a row of R T^-1 meets at most two neighboring components. Right
multiplication by L extends their interval by at most one position. Therefore
each inverse row has at most 2b+1 nonzeros. Row-support sizes multiply under
matrix products, and monomials preserve them, proving product_j(2b_j+1)>=N
for Fourier factors. This requires genuinely separated contiguous components.
The source's displayed preparation/application sums independently simplify to
17N-19 and 9N-8. They do not certify the hidden generator code or N=1 support.

At each cut, (AB)_12=A_11 B_12+A_12 B_22 yields rank subadditivity. A permutation
cross-cut rank counts one-direction crossings; summing gives half its absolute
coordinate displacement. A complex Fourier s-by-(N-s) block has Frobenius norm
squared s(N-s) and operator norm squared <=N, hence rank >=s(N-s)/N. Summing over
s=1..N-1 yields (N^2-1)/6. This is a displacement condition, not a RAM time bound.

If a finite candidate A has full-rank defect AZ-DA, any additive correction R
making covariance exact must obey N<=rank(RZ-DR)<=2rank(R). This gives rank(R)>=N/2
for that candidate only. For a two-stage product S2 Q P S1, rearrangement makes

diagonal entries and P permutes them, proving the necessary multiset test.
It does not exclude other stage choices or longer products.

## P4. Sampling probability and guarded-word arithmetic

For signed real/imaginary atoms sampled with exact integer masses, each complex
summand has real and imaginary coordinates in [-1,1], with mean equal to the
mass-normalized Fourier coefficient. Applying Hoeffding at component error
a/sqrt(2), and union-bounding over two components and N outputs, gives
4N exp(-k a^2/4). Rejection from a power-of-two ticket range succeeds with
probability greater than one half (or one in the exact power-of-two case).
R=ceil(log2(k/delta_cap)) attempts per sample bounds total cap failure by
delta_cap. Successful tickets remain uniform; independent sample attempts
retain independence after conditioning on success. Sampling costs
O(k(R+log N))=O(log^2 N), in addition to O(N) scanning, for fixed accuracy and
the stated polynomial failure regime. A pseudorandom seed is not this theorem.

Bias a lane from [-U,U] into [0,2U], choose h=ceil(log2(2UG+1)), and pack
r=floor(w/h) complete lanes in each word. Every partial lane sum is <2^h, so
no cross-lane carry occurs. The maximum complete word is <=2^(rh)-1<2^w.
Unbiasing recovers the exact integer sum. New exhaustive small controls checked
593 packed words across 2,089 lanes using physical 16-bit bounds.

For B=Theta(log N), g=Theta(log N/loglog N), G=Theta(loglog N), the packed-word
count per row is O(1+logloglog N). Accumulation across output blocks is o(N),
while descriptors and explicit writes are O(N). This establishes the lane
primitive and its stated asymptotic ledger, conditional on the companion
descriptor/table/quantization construction. It does not alone establish the
complete approximation theorem or useful coefficient-relative accuracy.

## P7. Structured fresh DFT and bilinear-count scope

If Q(z)A(z)=R(z), degree(Q)=d, and R has s nonzero coefficients, evaluate Q and R
on each of the N roots at O((d+s)N) cost. Divide where Q is nonzero. At the at
most d exceptional roots, directly evaluate A for O(dN) additional work. This
justifies O((d+s+1)N) field arithmetic when the filter and valid roots are given
and preparation is included as claimed. Boundary terms must be kept in R.
Finding a useful filter for arbitrary inputs is not supplied by this identity.

Length-n polynomial multiplication has 2n-1 independent output coefficient
bilinear forms, giving the flattening rank lower bound 2n-1. Evaluation and
interpolation achieve that variable-product count over a field with enough
evaluation points. Transforming to/from those points still costs arithmetic;
the simple dense algorithm is quadratic. The finite prime and NTT length
restrictions cannot be dropped when moving from this count to software.

## P9. Joint measurement stability and symbolic outputs

For N=2m and binary x=(u,1-u), all even frequencies are independent of u. The
2^m choices have odd measurements in an r-complex-dimensional box whose real
side lengths are at most N. Partition each real dimension into
M=2^floor(N/(4r))-1 intervals. Since M^(2r)<2^m, two inputs share a cell.
Their measured complex sup-distance is at most sqrt(2)N/M. Their full Fourier
sup-distance is at least sqrt(2), by Parseval and input difference squared
norm at least two. Therefore a full-spectrum decoder using only those measured
coordinates needs L>=M/N. Polynomial L forces r=Omega(N/log N).
The bound does not apply to an evaluator also using the original input.

An O(N) cyclotomic coefficient descriptor remains a symbolic representation;
conjugate output values require a charged evaluator. Likewise, a smooth
factorization of the complex DFT through w complex scalars has Jacobian rank
at most w, so w>=N. These width/interface results do not prove NlogN work.
Binary-only polynomial identities cannot be silently differentiated as
identities on an open complex domain.
