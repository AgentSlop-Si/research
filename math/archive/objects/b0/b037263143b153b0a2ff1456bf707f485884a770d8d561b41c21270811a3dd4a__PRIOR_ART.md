# Prior art and contribution boundary

Dickson/Chebyshev polynomials and the identity `D_N(w+w^-1)=w^N+w^-N` are classical. The use of polynomial gcd modulo a short polynomial to test algebraic common roots is likewise established computer algebra. Phase 22 does not claim either foundational tool as new.

Useful references:

- Encyclopedia of Mathematics, **Dickson polynomial**, https://encyclopediaofmath.org/wiki/Dickson_polynomial — recurrence, roots, composition and Chebyshev relationship.
- Antonia W. Bluher, *Permutation properties of Dickson and Chebyshev polynomials and connections to number theory*, https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.06877 — finite-field setting and classical parametrizations.
- P. G. Spain, *Tracking poles, representing Hankel operators, and the Nehari problem*, Linear Algebra and its Applications (1995), https://doi.org/10.1016/0024-3795%2894%2900106-N — pole/zero recovery and Hankel methods from root-of-unity samples.
- Classical Prony reconstruction, sparse rational interpolation, cyclic convolution, Tonelli–Shanks modular roots, baby-step/giant-step discrete logarithms, and Cooley–Tukey/Bluestein FFTs all predate this project.

**Incremental research result:** In our precise Phase 21 reciprocal Hankel-resultant representation, the trace/Dickson gcd gives a deterministic, always-sound all-shift rejection certificate and a complete low-degree candidate extraction route without constructing a degree-N polynomial. It is experimentally faster for sufficiently large N in the tested finite-field field-op model. The new combined statement has not been formally peer-reviewed, and historical novelty/priority is unverified.

**Model limitations:** F_p root supply and exact scalar field primitives are assumed. No breakthrough in generic arbitrary-input FFT, integer multiplication, numerically stable approximate FFT, or quantum FFT gate count is claimed.
