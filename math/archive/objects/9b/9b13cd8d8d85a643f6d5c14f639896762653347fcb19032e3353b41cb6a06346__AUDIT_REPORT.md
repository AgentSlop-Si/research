# Delta-Graph and Spectral Newton Union: independent audit

Audit date: 9 October 2026 UTC. Source filenames retain their supplied 10 October label; this is not an independently established publication date.

## Verdict

Accept the **selected-factor, fixed-order, exact finite-field Newton theorem**, including raw-input recognition, arbitrary dense initial fibers, globally budgeted spectral differences and all-N output. Accept the single-anchor Delta theorem and the separate sparse-variation convolution reconstruction, under their charged assumptions. They strictly broaden the earlier sparse-per-fiber family. They do not transform arbitrary rejected inputs.

Two source coverage qualifications matter:

1. The budgeted multiple-anchor recognizer is greedy and is **not complete for the stated union over all placements of B anchors**. A 16-coordinate counterexample and an exact, linearly charged repair are provided.
2. The default chooses a single divisor. It does not recognize the union over all balanced divisors or all difference orders. Moreover, `fused_fft.py` remains byte-identical to the old bundle and never calls the new Delta/Newton branches. Its inherited factorial chirp eliminant is also unchanged.

The new graph-delta path and the prior output-rank fallback are genuinely complementary. Independently proved improvements combine them, remove an unnecessary second balance condition from convolution reconstruction, and extend the output variation gate to fixed higher differences. These are scoped project extensions, not functionality credited to the uploaded program or publication-priority claims.

## 2. Model, normalization and exact identity

Write N=QM, work in a supplied prime field F_p with p>N and p=N^{O(1)}, and supply a primitive Nth root omega. Put eta=omega^Q. The root table checks exact order in O(N) modular multiplications, but does not certify primality or construct a field/root from N. Those are premises.

Let y_t[u]=x[t+Qu], and define the unnormalized short spectrum

    C_t[h] = sum_u y_t[u] eta^{-hu}.

Fix 1<=d<=Q. The first d raw rows may be arbitrary. For t>=d, suppose the dth backward difference has character amplitudes

    nabla^d y_t[u] = sum_h e_t[h] eta^{hu},
    R_actual = sum_{t=d}^{Q-1} nnz(e_t) <= R_budget.

Thus nabla^d C_t[h]=M e_t[h]. The factor M is essential: the event amplitudes are normalized Fourier coefficients, whereas the anchor spectra are unnormalized.

Let A_j[h]=sum_{i=0}^j (-1)^{j-i} binom(j,i) C_i[h]. Applying nabla^d to both sides and checking the first d rows proves

    C_t[h] = sum_{j=0}^{d-1} binom(t,j) A_j[h]
             + M sum_{a=d}^t e_a[h] binom(t-a+d-1,d-1).

For k=h+sM, z=omega^{-k}, character orthogonality gives X[k]=sum_t z^t C_t[h]. Consequently

    X[k] = sum_{j=0}^{d-1} A_j[h] z^j B_{j+1,Q-j}(z)
           + M sum_{a=d}^{Q-1} e_a[h] z^a B_{d,Q-a}(z),

where B_{a,L}(z)=sum_{v=0}^{L-1} binom(v+a-1,a-1)z^v. For z!=1,

    B_{a,L}(z) = (1-z^L sum_{j=0}^{a-1} binom(L+j-1,j)(1-z)^j)/(1-z)^a.

At z=1 it is binom(L+a-1,a); at L=0 it is zero. Multiplying the finite sum by (1-z)^a, or induction on a, proves the identity over the integers, hence after reduction in F_p. No approximation or division by a possibly zero factorial in the field is needed.

The source obtains z^L, z^a and other phases from the charged global powers table. All nonzero 1-omega^{-k} reciprocals are prepared with O(N) products and **one scalar inversion**. No per-output exponentiation in N is hidden. Binomial evaluation is O_d(1) for fixed d; no uniform growing-order bound is attributed to the literal code.

## 3. Raw recognition: row counts, rejection and inversions

For a length-M difference row with k true nonzero character modes, the source tries geometrically increasing caps up to min(budget_remaining, floor(M/3)). At a cap r, bounded BM costs O(Mr), root scanning costs O(Mr), moment recovery O(r²), and full sample verification O(Mr). For M>=3r the quadratic term is absorbed. Distinct admissible modes have minimal recurrence degree k and a nonsingular Vandermonde/Hankel factorization, so recovery succeeds once r>=k. Full verification prevents false acceptance of an accidental short recurrence.

The BM caches the reciprocal of the last degree-increasing discrepancy: at most r inversions per cap. The exact moment solver uses at most r additional inversions. Geometric caps sum to O(k) on successful sparse rows. If that route fails and the remaining budget is at least floor(M/3), a full M² short transform is allowed. For M>=3, floor(M/3)>=M/5, so this costs O(M*budget_remaining). M=1,2 are constant-size direct cases. The normalized direct transform uses one scalar inverse; zero rows are detected before any inverse.

Crucially, **there are Q-d rows to scan even when R_actual=0**. The correct aggregate accepted recognition ledger is

    O_d(N + d M² + M((Q-d)+R_actual)) arithmetic,
    O(R_actual) scalar inversions.

The first term includes complete raw reads, differences, supplied-root validation and anchor transforms. On the first failed row, doubling/direct work and inversions are charged to the unspent declared budget. Thus all-input recognition/rejection is bounded by

    O_d(N + d M² + M((Q-d)+R_budget)) arithmetic,
    O(R_budget) scalar inversions.

The executor adds O_d(N+Q R_actual) field work, one batch inversion and explicit N output writes. Live storage is O(N+R_budget+dM) in general, and O(N) under the stated fixed-order O(Q) budget. The source certificate executors trust caller-created certificate fields; the raw recognizers are the membership boundary.

With Q²,M²<=C N for fixed C and R_budget<=cQ for fixed c,d, every raw input is recognized or rejected in O_{c,d}(N), and accepted inputs receive all N exact outputs in the same bound. With O(log p) word work per scalar inversion, the extra O((R_budget+1)log p) is absorbed for balanced sizes and polynomial-size p. This assumes bounded-word exact arithmetic, addressing and modular operations. It is not O(N) bit complexity, and it proves neither stable floating-point recognition nor arbitrary integer multiplication.

For the original fixed-per-row Delta wrapper, resetting precisely when a row difference exceeds its cap is complete: every such transition must be an anchor. With at most B anchors, include the rejected sparse attempts at those resets, B dense M² anchor transforms, B N anchor-output terms and Q E event-output terms. Fixed B,r and balanced dimensions preserve O(N).

For the budgeted multiple-anchor wrapper, failed attempts at reset rows can cost up to O(M²) and O(M) inversions each. An honest bound is O(N+B M²+M(Q+R)+B N+Q R), with O(R+B M+1) inversions, before treating fixed B and balanced dimensions as constants. Merely counting the accepted events understates failed-attempt work, although it does not invalidate the fixed-parameter linear theorem.

## 4. Multiple-anchor completeness counterexample

Use N=16, Q=M=4, F_17, omega=3, total_modes=4, max_anchors=2. The raw rows are

    y_0 = [0,0,0,0]
    y_1 = [4,0,0,0]
    y_2 = [5,1,1,1]
    y_3 = [6,2,2,2].

Equivalently the interleaved raw vector is

    [0,4,5,6, 0,0,1,2, 0,0,1,2, 0,0,1,2].

Since eta=13, the three consecutive differences have Fourier support sizes 4,1,1. The first difference has all four character amplitudes equal to 1; the next two are the h=0 character.

The shipped budgeted loop consumes all four modes at t=1, resets at t=2 when no modes remain, then rejects at t=3 because its second anchor was already used. A valid representation places the second anchor at t=1 and spends only two events afterward. This proves missed membership without executing the source. Every accepted source output remains sound.

The general completeness repair is especially simple because the path differences are fixed independently of anchor placements. Let k_t be the exact support of y_t-y_{t-1}. A reset removes the cost of that one transition. Therefore the minimum event total with at most B anchors is

    sum_t k_t minus the sum of the largest min(B-1,Q-1) values k_t.

Recognize each transition with cap R_budget, recording exact k_t when it fits and the marker R_budget+1 otherwise. Maintain the B-1 largest recorded costs; reject as soon as the sum of all other costs exceeds R_budget. The marker is sufficient, since every transition with cost greater than the whole budget must be reset. At completion choose those largest transitions as resets, discard their events, and obtain the source's interval representation from the remaining discovered events plus raw anchor rows.

Before the first rejecting row, retained costs sum to at most R_budget; at most B-1 discarded rows and the final tested row each have at most M real modes or cost O(M²) to recognize. Hence total discovery work is O(MQ+M R_budget+B M²), with O(R_budget+B M) inversions. For fixed B one can maintain the largest costs in O(BQ) work and defer anchor transforms until acceptance. Keep discovered modes for the current largest B-1 exact-cost rows too: a later larger row can displace one of them back into the retained set. Their total storage is at most (B-1)M; retained rows use at most R_budget events before rejection, and the current row uses at most M. Rows marked greater than the whole budget need no stored decomposition because they must be resets. Thus live storage is O(N+R_budget+B M), which is O(N) for fixed B and R_budget=O(Q). Full transform cost adds B M²+B N+Q R_budget and one batch inversion.

This is a complete charged algorithm for the promised reset union. It is an independently derived repair, not a patch applied to the supplied file. The accompanying checker exhaustively verifies the cost-selection rule against all reset subsets in 46,860 finite cases.

## 5. Default selector and implementation coverage

For the single-anchor first-difference family at c=1, take N=12, F_13, omega=2 and

    x=[9,4,3,5,11,2,7,6,2,7,10,0].

At Q=2, M=6, the sole difference has two modes, so R_budget=Q=2 accepts. Both balance inequalities hold with C=4. The default largest divisor not exceeding sqrt(12) is Q=3, M=4; its two differences each have four modes, so the default R_budget=3 rejects. This is another selected-divisor limitation, not an output error.

The same qualification applies to order: a d=2 family may have dense first differences and fail d=1. Trying a fixed finite public list of eligible divisors and orders is valid if every attempt is charged; an unbounded all-divisor/all-order search is not free.

The fixed-order entry points raise ValueError when their chosen Q is smaller than the requested order. A fused dispatcher must explicitly skip that ineligible order, or handle the bounded small-size case; it should not confuse this with mathematical nonmembership. With fixed d and balance, Q<d implies N<=C d², so a charged direct small transform is a legitimate constant-size option.

The unchanged fused_fft.py dispatches only tensor, per-fiber and coset/common-chirp branches. It does not implement Delta, budgeted Delta, second differences, Newton or graph-output rank fusion. The polynomial Toeplitz eliminant from the prior master is not substituted in its inherited chirp engine. Separate parameters and explicit branch integration remain work to be implemented.

## 6. Supported family is exactly countable and remains proper

For a specified Q,M,d, the map from raw x to its first d raw rows and all later order-d differences is invertible triangular. Each short Fourier transform is also invertible. Therefore, writing U=(Q-d)M and clipping R to U, the number of supported raw vectors is **exactly**

    p^{dM} sum_{e=0}^{min(R,U)} binom(U,e)(p-1)^e.

The source's upper bound can be strengthened to equality for this specified-order, no-reset family. Full ordinary fiber-matrix rank and dense individual row spectra are consistent with this small family size.

For balanced large Q,M, fixed d, R=O(Q), p>=N and polynomial-size p, the fraction of all p^N raw inputs is at most p^{-N+O(sqrt N)}. A fixed union of branches changes only the bounded prefactor/exponent constants. For d=Q the coordinate count becomes p^N, but then d grows with Q except at bounded lengths; the fixed-order asymptotic theorem cannot be applied as if its constants were uniform.

Unrestricted order-d differences have R=Theta(N). The literal event executor then performs Theta(QR) updates. This identifies the still-unproved joint-evaluation problem for this mechanism; it is not an unrestricted DFT lower bound.
