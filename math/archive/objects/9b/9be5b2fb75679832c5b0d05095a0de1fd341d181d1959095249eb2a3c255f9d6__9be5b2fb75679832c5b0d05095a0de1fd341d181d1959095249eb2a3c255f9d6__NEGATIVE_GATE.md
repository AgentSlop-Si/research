# Phase19 falsification controls and why generic FFT remains unresolved

**1. Noncoprime graph cycles.** `r=(1,1,-1,-1)` over GF(17) is a nowhere-zero eigenvector of `S^2` with `(I+S^2)r=0`, while the coprime K<=1 detector has no certificate. No unsupported extension of the two-adjacent-ratio theorem to noncoprime zero-defect input is permitted. The full FFT remains correct.

**2. Resonant two-defect class collisions.** N=128, delta=63, alpha=omega^9, `r[t*delta mod N]=alpha^t*(3 if t<17 else 5)`. It has exactly two nonzero graph defects, but `alpha^N=1` merges wrap and nonwrap ratio classes. The multi-defect detector fails and falls back; this is an explicit limitation of the wrap-separation theorem, not a bug hidden by testing.

**3. Full two-unknown-factor input defeats ratio classification.** N=128,384,4096 examples with `Q1Q2 r=e_j` have respectively 128,384,4096 distinct ordinary adjacent ratios, although both factors and their union are graph-sparse as operators. Consequently, blindly searching only small ratio alphabets cannot solve even the one-impulse two-factor family. Sparse-circular-inverse discovery does recover it, by a different structural invariant.

**4. Short spectral probes are not certificates.** A 4-tap inverse candidate was generated from the first eight Fourier samples of a valid r. Add `N^(-1)omega^(-8j)` to every time coordinate: the first eight samples are unchanged but the ninth differs. The candidate remains identical; all-coordinate cyclic convolution verification rejects it, and Bluestein returns the correct vector.

**5. Generic inverse-sparsity measure zero.** For fixed N and t<N, sparse C supported on t positions has t free coefficients. Rational inversion to `r=C(S)^(-1)e0` preserves dimension <=t. Unknown support is only a finite union at fixed N, and invertible Green P preserves dimension. Hence the inverse-sparse fast family does not contain generic complex signals. No general FFT lower bound is inferred.

**6. Unknown multi-impulse union remains difficult.** With unknown Q of s sparse taps and arbitrary K-sparse h (K>1), the Fourier data satisfy `R_k = H(omega^k)/Q(omega^k)`. The inverse samples `1/R_k = Q(omega^k)/H(omega^k)` are typically NOT s-term exponential sums. Blind sparse circular inversion may fail, despite an O(N) *known-Q* Phase18 evaluator. There is no certified O(N) recovery algorithm for this full class in Phase19.

**7. Bit/precision boundary.** A root-rich exact finite field may vary with N; field operations aren't unit bit operations. Complex Prony/Vandermonde systems become ill conditioned when spectral nodes cluster, and exact zero tests are not trustworthy under rounding. No numerical stability theorem is claimed.

**8. Prior-art distinction.** Prony/Berlekamp-Massey, spectral sparse interpolation and cyclic convolution algebra predate this checkpoint. We assert new verified integration relative to the Phase18 codebase, not an independent historical first.
