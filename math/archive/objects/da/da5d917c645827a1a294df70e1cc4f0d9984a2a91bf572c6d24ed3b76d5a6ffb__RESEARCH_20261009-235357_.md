# Research report: moving partitions instead of one invariant middle bit

**Date:** October 10, 2026.  
**Goal:** Exact arbitrary-input Fourier calculation with better counted arithmetic than classic FFT, using globally overlapping nonunitary linear-work scans, rather than cached prior input products or multithreading.

## Prior checkpoint

The previous `fft_four_stage_central_bit_no_go_2026-10-10.zip` established that four order-one quasiseparable scans with repeated bit reversal cannot factor F_N for powers of two N>=128. It also gave selected N=256 failures for bit-reversal shuffles modified by a middle rotation or XOR. That theorem depended on a fixed partition preserved or swapped by every shuffle.

## This round's main result

The new moving-partition rank theorem, proved in `PROOF_CERTIFICATE.md`, tracks an input group through each arbitrary intermediate permutation. Each order-r quasiseparable stage transmits at most r times the number of natural-order boundaries of the current group. The crossing ranks add over stages. A Fourier submatrix selected by consecutive input columns remains Vandermonde-full-rank for any output row subset.

This produces exact obstructions without solving for generator coefficients or requiring a common invariant partition. For binary linear shuffles, all 2^k-1 nonzero parity masks can be tested efficiently using an exact popcount/trailing-ones boundary formula.

### Stronger infinite-family theorems

- **Local binary mixing:** If each shuffle is a banded binary affine map with at most c positions of downward bit dependence, then for rank-r quasiseparable stages, `N/2 <= r*(2*(2^(ct)-1)/(2^c-1)-t)`. For constant c,r, depth is Omega(log N). With c=r=1, depth >= log2 N-1.
- **Successive cyclic bit rotations:** although each has a global wraparound so the locality theorem does not directly apply, following the ancestral top bit gives the exact channel costs `1,3,7,15,...`. Rank-one depth must be at least `log2 N-1`.
- **Repeated rotated bit reversal:** reflection of bit positions preserves a different central parity mask. The resulting Fourier block is exactly full rank, with a 2-by-2 block proof for even log2 N and a 4-by-4 Vandermonde proof for odd log2 N. Necessary rank-one depths:

| k=log2 N | N | Necessary depth |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 5 | 32 | 4 |
| 6 | 64 | 5 |
| 7 | 128 | 8 |
| 8 | 256 | 9 |
| 9 | 512 | 16 |
| 10 | 1,024 | 17 |

This gives Omega(sqrt N) stages in that particular dynamic shuffle family, not in every possible global layout.

### Experiments and verification

- Eight new test groups pass. Includes **200** randomly generated exact product/submatrix checks with independently shuffled layouts and rank-one/rank-two stages, and every nonzero parity mask through N=1024.
- Nine lengths N=4..1024 (powers of two) pass the rotated-reversal invariant-mask identities and Fourier full-rank checks over **two** primes.
- **13 separate four-stage characteristic-zero nonvanishing Fourier certificates** are serialized and validated.
- Six representative mincut checks at N=128,256 exactly matched the dynamic-boundary counts.
- Exhaustive interval searches: 2,079 nonempty proper intervals at N=64, 8,255 at N=128, and 32,895 at N=256, per layout.
- The preceding eight-test research checkpoint and four recorded independent graph-cut certificates were rerun successfully.

### A rank-feasible but unconstructed candidate

A fixed seeded set of three dense GF(2)-linear interstage shuffles (`mixing_survivor.make(8,20261016)`) at N=256 passed all 255 nonzero parity-mask tests and 32,895 consecutive-interval tests. Eight selected half-size input/output subsets each had **full network mincut 128**. That shows the current rank tests cannot reject this particular layout in these tests.

**It is not a Fourier factorization.** There are no coefficients realizing the Fourier matrix, no exact transform implementation, and no new operation-count improvement. The next valuable experiment is Fourier-specific nonlinear compatibility of this candidate, including singular generator charts and all stage multiplications.

## Next gate

1. Attack coefficient feasibility for mixing networks that pass both full-network cuts and moving-partition constraints; use exact finite-field Jacobian or elimination with *all generator branches*.
2. Automate certified selection of input/output subsets using a Fourier-rank oracle, rather than only parity masks or consecutive intervals.
3. Seek a genuinely scalable exact formula for the Fourier matrix with **fully charged arithmetic work** lower than NlogN—not just a compact matrix representation, unchanged stage counts, or stages with large hidden coefficients.
4. Keep previous proof-limited failures as negative controls. Avoid claiming an unrestricted FFT lower bound from these structured families.

## Scientific context

These rank and FFT-submatrix themes have antecedents in structured-matrix and Fourier-submatrix literature, including Delvaux & Van Barel (2008, DOI 10.1016/j.laa.2008.04.043) and learned butterfly factorization (Dao et al., 2019, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6879380/). The results here are new **within this project**, without an assertion of publication-level priority.

### Independent network-model audit

`test_network_audit.py` rebuilds four-stage scalar scan networks and calculates **exact integer maximum-flow capacities**. Six selected moving-partition witnesses at N=128/256 returned exactly their analytic boundary sums. For the deliberately mixed but unconstructed control, 8 representative half-by-half input/output tests at **each** of N=64/128/256 returned the full capacity N/2. None of these controls proves coefficient compatibility or a Fourier factorization.
