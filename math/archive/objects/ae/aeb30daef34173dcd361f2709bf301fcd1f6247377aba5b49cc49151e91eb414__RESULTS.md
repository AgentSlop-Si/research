# Reproducible v13 experiment summary

**Date:** 2026-10-10. Scope: independent exact-arithmetic theorem plus floating-point diagnostics, not an optimized native benchmark and not a proof of exact sub-`N log N` computation.

## Verification summary

- 38 Python unit tests passed (`check_v13`, the preserved v12 test suite, the preserved v11 test suite).
- Six finite-field exhaustive **all-basis** controls across N=24,40,48,80,96,160: 45,696 exact DFT entries passed; 1,664 exact local projector entries; all six deliberately corrupted phase-bank strides rejected.
- 10 single-root twiddle precision-stress diagnostics passed under their stated conservative error model.
- Global exact one-row squared error expectation `(M-1)||x||_2^2` agreed with the empirical mean over 80 independent rows, ratios approximately 1.013, 0.990, 0.996, and 0.995 for N=96,384,768,1280 respectively. Empirical tests do not replace the exact proof.

## Most important limitation

The one-row linear map is `A=F_N P`, `P^2=M P`, `rank P=N/M`; its spectral error is exactly `M-1`. With M chosen near `log G` for near-linear work, this diverges as G grows. The result concerns per-coordinate variance, not globally small `l2` error or exact inputs. Averaging R independent rows reduces *global expected squared error* by 1/R and needs `R=Omega(M)` for constant relative `l2` accuracy, restoring Omega(N log G) total work in the favorable fixed-m case. Similarly, robust simultaneous all-coordinate failure probability entails Theta(log N) rows.

## Public context

Classical Cooley–Tukey identity is long established; the project-specific result is applying the saved unit-step chirp sketch with **independent** mixed-radix columns and proving the exact error/resource accounting. Randomized approximate and sparse Fourier algorithms also have substantial prior literature; no priority claim is made.
