# Mathematical proof certificates — spectral determinant gate

**Date:** 2026-10-09. Everything in this document is a claim about a **specified low-rank matrix family**. No statement establishes an unconditional complexity lower bound for FFTs.

## 0. The exact model and the charts

Let `z` be a primitive eighth root of unity, `F8[i,k]=z^(i*k)` and `P=[7,0,6,3,4,1,2,5]`, interpreted by `B[:,j]=(F8 C)[:,P[j]]` where `F8=B P A` and `C=A^-1`. Both `C` and `B` are required to belong to the **globally separated-generator rank-one semiseparable** class:

`X[i,j]=l_i v_j` for `i>j`; `X[i,j]=u_i w_j` for `i<j`, with unrestricted diagonal (normalized to one for `C`). This is narrower than arbitrary rank-one quasiseparable matrices with nonseparable local generators. In particular, classical block-diagonal Cooley–Tukey factors need not belong to this chart.

The rational eight-variable construction has parameters

`(t,h0,h1,h2,h6,beta,gamma1,gamma2)`;

its reciprocal quantities include nonzero `t,h0,z-1`, row sums `S1[k]`, coupling pivots `Delta[k]`, and a boundary 2x2 determinant. The stage `C` must be invertible. Some elimination steps assume nonzero Fourier corner pivots and nonzero coefficient of `beta`. The code stores and tests these restrictions. **Exceptional branches cannot be discarded** in any impossibility proof.

## 1. Four-by-four determinant Q: exactly 45 terms, degree <= 4

Let `xi=z^-1`. By the *first lower-output-column* support condition, for `k=1..6`, the first-stage lower generator has

`L_k=(a+b*xi^k+beta*H_k)/t`,

`b=(t-1+beta*(h0-1))/(z-1)` and `a=1-beta*h0-b`; set `L7=1`. The first two independent lower/upper Fourier-support constraints imply `H0..H5` follows a three-frequency recurrence, while `H6=h6` remains free. Define the 4x4 matrix `M`, indexed by Fourier rows `i=4,5,6,7`, with columns

1. `sum_{k=0}^2 z^(i*k)*H_k`
2. `sum_{k=4}^7 z^(i*k)*L_k`
3. `z^(3i)`
4. `sum_{k=0}^7 z^(i*k)*C[k,7]` (equivalently the `H` last-column transform)

The **lower rank-one constraint for output column j=3** forces the four Fourier-row values of `C[:,P[3]]` to be proportional to the values of `C[:,P[0]]`. Eliminating their unknown ratio and the two remaining generator coefficients implies `det(M)=0`. Clearing the factor `t(z-1)` gives polynomial `Q(t,h0,h1,h2,h6,beta)=0`. This is necessary on the chart; the derivation does not assert it is sufficient. Its full coefficients and computationally independent determinant verifier are in `exact_spectral_minor.json`, `exact_minor.py`, `column_minor.py`.

Coefficient extraction over `Z[z]/(z^4+1)` finds `deg Q=4`, `45` nonzero terms, and no monomial containing both `t` and `beta`; consequently

`Q=t*A(h)+beta*B(h)+C(h)`, with 12, 21 and 12 terms in `A,B,C`. Where `B(h)!=0`, `beta=-(t*A+C)/B`. **When `B(h)=0`, do not divide**.

## 2. The upper-stage polynomial constraints and rank certificate

Eliminate the row-dependent ratios from upper rank-one conditions as described in `upper_minor.py` and `exact_upper.py`. The resulting necessary polynomials `R1,R2,R5` have degree 7 and term counts `192,300,330` over `Z[z]/(z^4+1)`. Each is affine separately in `gamma1,gamma2` and has no products `gamma1*gamma2`.

The 4x8 Jacobian matrix of `(Q,R1,R2,R5)` with respect to the eight chart variables has a 4x4 minor using columns `(t,h0,h1,h2)` nonzero modulo `p=12289`, namely `10570`, at a parameter vector in `independence_witness.json`. Nonvanishing of this exact polynomial minor mod `p` proves nonvanishing in characteristic zero, hence algebraic independence of the four polynomials in `Q(z)[t,h0,h1,h2,h6,beta,gamma1,gamma2]`. **Algebraic independence does not imply that their common zero locus is empty.**

Writing `Rk = E[k,0]+gamma1*E[k,1]+gamma2*E[k,2]`, three necessary equations imply

`G=det_{k=1,2,5;j=0,1,2}(E[k,j])=0`.

Coefficient polynomials `E[k,j]` are quadratic in `beta`. Let `m=-(t*A+C)` and define `Ehat[k,j]=B^2*E0+B*m*E1+m^2*E2`. The five-variable necessary condition

`H(t,h)=det(Ehat)=B(h)^6*G(t,h,m/B)=0`

is valid **on B(h)!=0**. The arithmetic circuit, not a vast expanded polynomial, is exported in `five_parameter_circuit.json`. Modular nonzero witness: `p=12289,z=8246`, inputs `[10931,3273,7557,2279,5478]` give `B=12110,m=11930,H=4730`.

**Warning:** when the adjacent-column constraint is additionally enforced, an upper gamma-pivot previously used to solve for gamma can become singular. Thus a solver based on dividing this gamma determinant is **not** a nonexistence proof. Our certificate keeps that singular branch.

## 3. Adjacent-column lemma: a necessary three-mode tail

For the fixed P8, the columns `j=5,6` of `B` correspond to original columns `k=1,2` of `C`. Above the diagonal, the global rank-one form of `B` implies the restrictions of these two Fourier-transformed columns to frequencies `i=0..4` are proportional (rank <= 1). Denote them by `u=C[:,1]` and `v=C[:,2]`.

In the separated-generator chart, for time-coordinate positions `k=3..7` the two vectors share the same tail `L_k`: specifically `u_k=V1*L_k`, `v_k=V2*L_k`. Because `C` is invertible, `u,v` are linearly independent. The 5x3 Fourier matrix with rows `i=0..4`, columns `k=0..2`, has full column rank (Vandermonde with distinct roots); therefore `V1,V2` cannot both be zero. Set `w=V2*u - V1*v`. It has support only in `k=0,1,2` and is nonzero. Its restricted Fourier image lies in the (at most) one-dimensional span of `Fu,Fv`, hence that span belongs to the image of the first 3 time-coordinate basis vectors. Subtracting the head parts of `u` or `v`, the restricted Fourier image of the common tail also lies in that 3-dimensional image.

Thus there exist `a0,a1,a2` with

`sum_{k=3}^7 z^(i*k) L_k = sum_{k=0}^2 a_k z^(i*k)` for `i=0,1,2,3,4`.

After extending `L_k` by zero for `k=0,1,2`, the length-eight DFT of the difference vanishes on frequencies `i=0..4`. The inverse Fourier transform gives that the tail samples `L_3..L_7` are in the span of the three characters `z^(-5k),z^(-6k),z^(-7k)`; since `z^8=1`, these are `z^(3k),z^(2k),z^k`. As a result, the characteristic polynomial `(X-z)(X-z^2)(X-z^3)` yields two linear equations

`E3=L6-s1*L5+s2*L4-s3*L3=0`,
`E4=L7-s1*L6+s2*L5-s3*L4=0`,

`s1=z+z^2+z^3, s2=z^3+z^4+z^5, s3=z^6`.

Notice these equations use **no S1 or Delta denominators** in their derivation. They do rely on the separated global generators, invertibility of `C`, and the stated upper B rank-one constraint. The calculation is independently verified over two exact finite fields in `test_new_gate.py`.

## 4. The cubic W: 31 terms in four variables

Substitute the known generator formula for `L3..L7` into `E3,E4`. Multiply by the common nonzero factor `t(z-1)` to obtain two polynomial equations affine in `t,beta`. `Q` is also affine in `t,beta`. Necessarily the 3x3 matrix of their coefficients, in order `[coefficient(t),coefficient(beta),constant]`, has determinant zero:

`W(h0,h1,h2,h6)=det( coefficients[E3,E4,Q] )=0`.

The complete polynomial, exported in `adjacent_tail_elimination.json`, has exactly 31 nonzero terms and total degree three in four generator parameters. The nonzero finite-field witness `p=12289,z=8246, (h0,h1,h2,h6)=(2,3,5,7)` gives `W=9966 mod p`. This proves `W` is not the zero polynomial over the complex cyclotomic field. **It does not exclude solutions W=0**, even with nonzero determinants and all other compatibility equations.

One may solve E3,E4 for `(t,beta)` *only where their 2x2 coefficient determinant is nonzero*; that is an additional open chart, not an innocuous algebraic simplification. The zero-determinant branch remains unresolved.

## 5. Exhaustive GF(17) branch search is not a complex theorem

With `p=17,z=9`, enumerate `h0!=0` and all `h1,h2,h6 in GF(17)`, giving `16*17^3=78,608` quadruples. Where the E3,E4 determinant is nonzero, uniquely solve `t,beta`; reject `t=0` and values with `Q!=0`. Solve the three necessary upper equations for the two gamma ratios over GF(17), then reconstruct C using the saved rational eight-variable parameterization, rejecting its zero denominators. Test C invertibility and the **entire B-stage rank-one set**, not only the polynomial witnesses.

Counters from `exhaustive_GF17_chart.json`:

- 78,608 h-tuples visited.
- 4,624 have singular E3,E4 coefficient determinant and are excluded, **not ruled out**.
- 4,352 yield t=0 and are excluded.
- 4,596 meet the additional lower quartic Q after allowed eliminations.
- 17,829 upper-gamma pairs meet the chosen necessary upper constraints.
- 12,358 fail an eight-variable chart denominator and are excluded, **not ruled out**.
- 2,546 full-rank C matrices survive the displayed necessary filters (others have smaller rank).
- **0** of the 2,546 candidates satisfy every required B-stage rank-one polynomial over GF(17).

This is a **finite-field, open-chart exclusion only**. It does not preclude characteristic-zero solutions (a complex solution need not reduce to a GF(17) point), omitted singular branches, or entirely different Fourier factorizations. It is also not a cost improvement.

## 6. All-length defect formula — an independent identity, not a factorization

For `n=8,16,32,64`, first three lower and upper spectral-support constraints impose an `n`-length recurrence for the common upper generator `H`. Let `h*_k` be the 3-mode continuation of the first three values. In the first Fourier column of `B`, for frequencies `i>=3`, the nonzero spectral tails are completely determined by the two boundary defects:

`B[i,0] = (h_(n-2)-h*_(n-2))*z^(-2i) + (1-h*_(n-1))*z^(-i)`.

This follows by subtracting the n-sample three-mode sequence from C's last column and taking the DFT: its first three Fourier modes vanish beyond i=2, leaving only n-2 and n-1 delta contributions. Finite-field reproduction at n=8,16,32,64 appears in `two_defect_tail.py`/`.json`. This identity describes the *candidate-stage Fourier image*, not an exact two-stage factorization.

## Final gate

The unsolved problem is to prove an ideal is empty **after proper saturation by all required nonzero determinants**, or provide an exact invertible solution of the complete two-stage polynomial equations (including exceptional branches). Even a resolved fixed-P8 impossibility would not imply an `Omega(N log N)` lower bound for arbitrary FFTs.
