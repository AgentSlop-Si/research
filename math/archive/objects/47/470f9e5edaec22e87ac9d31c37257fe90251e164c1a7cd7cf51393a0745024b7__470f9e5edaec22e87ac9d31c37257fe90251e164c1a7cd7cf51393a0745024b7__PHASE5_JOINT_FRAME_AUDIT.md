# HERO0 Phase 5: joint-frame audit and bounded fusion

Audit date: 9 October 2026. The source's 10 October date is its research label. No uploaded program was executed, imported, or compiled. No master or Library file was modified by this audit.

## Verdict

The complete-frame identities, unitary local transfer, finite-population moments, and measurement-only Gaussian obstruction are valid with their explicit domains. The independent exact and numerical checks pass. There is no new exact faster FFT.

Most importantly, eliminating the complete bank's scans and recovering Cooley–Tukey was already proved in the master at `additions/1717/astra_exact_bridge/EXACT_BRIDGES.md`, section 1. Phase 5 provides a useful standalone implementation and cross-lineage revalidation of that endpoint, not a new identity. Its new material relative to that proof is the explicit local transfer formulation, exact sampling-without-replacement moments, and stronger probabilistic information-loss gate. These are additions to this project, not claims of publication novelty.

Two scope corrections must accompany the source:

1. The local transfer is unitary after stripping its displayed prefactor and choosing the stated summary coordinates. The physical map from one input block to the selected M Fourier outputs has singular values 1/sqrt(K), not one. Neither statement lower-bounds general Fourier arithmetic.
2. The determinant bound depends on the actual input coordinates and their normalization. Formula (15) is valid for independently supplied raw Q coordinates and the specified unnormalized intermediate outputs. It is not automatically the remaining-work bound when unnormalized summaries D are free, nor the same bound for the N-dimensional zero-padded domain.

The strongest warranted fusion remains: a complete stable information basis plus exact correctness gates and scoped no-go results. A genuinely faster fresh-input global contraction is still absent.

## 2. Exact identity and scope of the local gate

Write Q=KM and omega_d=exp(-2 pi i/d). Let s_(b,t) have unit modulus, and define

D_(b,r) = sum_t s_(b,t) x_(bM+t) omega_M^(-rt).

Then

x_(bM+t) = conjugate(s_(b,t)) M^(-1) sum_r D_(b,r) omega_M^(rt).

This is simply a blockwise invertible Fourier-coordinate change. It proves the complete-frame resolution and algebraic independence of all Q summary coordinates only when x itself ranges over all C^Q. With an N-dimensional padded input, the summaries range over an N-dimensional subspace. Nonzero but non-unit s would require reciprocals rather than conjugates.

Substitution into the full Fourier sum gives

(U_Q x)_(ell+jK) = Q^(-1/2) sum_t omega_Q^(ell t) omega_M^(jt) sum_b x_(bM+t) omega_K^(ell b).

This is exactly the pre-existing Cooley–Tukey endpoint. For given D, the supplied evaluator first recovers x locally, then performs the length-K bank, twiddles, and length-M output bank. It removes all separate Q-long Green scans by returning to an ordinary transform of the recovered input.

The source's local summary-to-output matrix is

T_(ell,b) = U_M diag(conjugate(s_(b,t)) omega_Q^(ell t)) U_M^T.

Every factor is unitary, so every singular value is one. Consequently the best rank-r spectral approximation error is one for r<M. But the actual map from D_b to those M outputs includes 1/sqrt(Q), and D_b has norm sqrt(M)||x_b||. The physical map from raw x_b has all singular values 1/sqrt(K). This is why the local normalized obstruction cannot be added across blocks to manufacture a global algorithmic lower bound.

A clean global statement is available without normalization ambiguity: put z=D/sqrt(M). Then x↔z is unitary, and the complete synthesis z→U_Qx is unitary. Every rank-deficient approximation to that entire linear operator has spectral error at least one. This is the same basic information obstruction as the complete-frame and stationary proper-projection gates, not a scalar-operation lower bound.

The algebraic identities themselves need only positive K,M and Q=KM. The uploaded chirp helper imposes additional K>=M and unit-slope restrictions for its Green lineage. The modular checker also validates the broader identities at small K<M, without claiming the source accepts those helper inputs.

## 3. Exact finite-population refinement

For M>=2, define E_r=P_r/M. These are mutually orthogonal projections with sum E_r=I. For a uniformly chosen size-s subset S, 1<=s<=M,

Pbar_S = (1/s) sum_(r in S) P_r = (M/s) E_S,

where E_S=sum_(r in S) E_r is an orthogonal projection. Thus

E||U_Q(Pbar_S-I)x||² = (M-s)/s ||x||².

The with-replacement variance is (M-1)||x||²/s. The reduction factor is exactly (M-s)/(M-1). For M=1,s=1 the error is zero, and the ratio containing M-1 is unnecessary.

The source only states a fixed-subset spectral floor of one. The exact value is

||Pbar_S-I|| = max(1,M/s-1), for s<M; it is zero for s=M.

Indeed, on range E_S the error eigenvalue is M/s-1, and on its nonzero orthogonal complement it is -1. In particular, sampling few phases and rescaling can have large worst-case operator error, even though it is unbiased.

There is also an exact input-dependent deterministic error identity:

||U_Q(Pbar_S-I)x||²
= ||x||² + [(M/s-1)²-1] sum_(r in S)||E_r x||².

This compares directly with HERO1 Phase14's unrescaled orthogonal projection: E_S is contractive, while (M/s)E_S is unbiased under uniform subset selection but can amplify selected components. Neither dominates the other for every task.

### Fusion through Gaussian weights and a fixed linear output map

Let y_r=L P_r z, where L is any fixed linear postprocessing map and z may already contain deterministic Gaussian input weights. Put mu=Lz and

C_pop = (1/M) sum_r (y_r-mu)(y_r-mu)*.

A direct expansion of the indicators of membership in a uniform s-subset gives the full covariance identity

E[(s^(-1) sum_(r in S)y_r-mu)(...) *]
= (M-s)/(s(M-1)) C_pop.

This includes the source's per-frequency variance as a diagonal entry, and remains valid after the Gaussian filter or an exact deweighting map. It is conditional on the chosen base chirp; the complete-bank mean mu does not depend on that chirp, so averaging an independently sampled base adds no between-base covariance.

To compare to the audited Phase 4 random row, choose the base slope independently and uniformly among units and beta_0 uniformly modulo K; then beta=beta_0+rK with uniform r is uniform modulo Q. The grouped estimator therefore inherits the exact finite-population variance reduction. A fixed beta_0 alone is insufficient for importing the previous all-Q-beta covariance certificate; the extra beta_0 randomization is essential to that comparison. Deterministic Gaussian truncation/rounding bias remains separately charged. Even at s=M, the exact group mean is Lz, so the stochastic error vanishes while finite-filter and rounding bias relative to U_Nx can remain. Since ||Pbar_S||=M/s, the earlier deterministic Gaussian-remainder estimate for the whole group improves its factor M to M/s; the phase-rounding bound is unchanged. Median amplification needs independent groups, not a fiction that phases within one group are independent. With fixed s this changes constants only; it does not improve the alpha<1/2 exponent or turn the approximate theorem into an exact FFT. Forming all D just to choose a few phases would itself cost O(Q log M); direct selected summaries avoid that extra unnecessary preprocessing for fixed s.

## 4. Measurement-only Gaussian obstruction

The source's argument is valid for a fixed nonadaptive sketch A, or an input-independent random sketch conditioned on its random state. Every fully occupied M-cell block has at least M-s unobserved complex directions when at most s<M complex-linear rows are retained. If B such blocks exist, d=N-rank(A)>=B(M-s). For centered consecutive input support, B>=floor(N/M)-2 is a conservative valid bound.

For x~CN(0,I_N), conditioning on Ax leaves an independent standard complex Gaussian on ker(A). An arbitrary measurable nonlinear or randomized decoder cannot remove that component. Under the normalized U_N transform its covariance trace is d, so some output coordinate has variance at least d/N. The success event plus ||x||<=2sqrt(N) is included in a disk of radius 2C N^(1/2-alpha). Bounding its Gaussian density and adding the norm tail proves

Pr[all-bin error <= C N^(-alpha)||x||]
<= min(1,4 C² N^(2-2alpha)/d + exp(-(3-log 4)N)), for d>0.

The norm cutoff is an event inclusion; one must not condition the posterior law on the norm bound and continue calling it Gaussian. Decoder random coins may be conditioned on as independent auxiliary randomness.

For M=Theta(log N), s<=M-1, and N/M growing, d=Omega(N/M). Thus success tends to zero for every fixed alpha>1/2. Averaging over independent sketch states and then over Gaussian x contradicts a high-probability guarantee for every fixed input. It does not assert one universal adversarial x working against every possible algorithm or every N.

The independent probability review provides a stronger d-dimensional Gaussian-ball version and carefully bounded transcript-adaptive extension; see `GAUSSIAN_MEASUREMENT_BARRIER_AUDIT.md`. Those are supplementary refinements, not needed to accept the source's nonadaptive theorem.

The obstruction does not apply after arbitrary raw-input-dependent state selection, to extra input reads, to unconstrained nonlinear measurements, or merely because some sketch has one missing dimension. It neither refutes the separately reviewed alpha<1/2 approximation nor settles the alpha=1/2 boundary. The required rank deficit is quantitative. The Phase 4 positive theorem still needs fresh states, independent median groups, the sampler/abort contract, and its supplied-constant ideal arithmetic/RAM model.

## 5. Determinant gate: exact stage coordinates

All determinant magnitudes below are for Q independent complex input coordinates. Rearrangements of coordinates and unit-modulus diagonal gauges do not change magnitudes. F_d denotes the unnormalized length-d Fourier matrix, with determinant magnitude d^(d/2).

Let H denote the local complete summary transform x→D. It consists of K length-M unnormalized transforms and unit diagonal phases, so

|det H| = M^(Q/2), while H/sqrt(M) is unitary.

There are two different length-K banks, which must not be conflated:

- **The Phase 5 Cooley–Tukey intermediate Z.** Recover x=H^(-1)D, then Fourier-transform the K block index separately for each t. Write Z=Cx, where |det C|=K^(Q/2). Thus |det(D→Z)|=|det(CH^(-1))|=(K/M)^(Q/2). Starting from independent raw x gives the source's K^(Q/2) volume; starting from free unnormalized D gives the smaller ratio. Starting from normalized z=D/sqrt(M) gives K^(Q/2) again.
- **The old per-phase Green decoder's K-FFT summaries W.** Here W=R D, where R applies an unnormalized K-transform separately for each phase r. Then |det R|=K^(Q/2), and |det(x→W)|=|det(RH)|=(KM)^(Q/2)=Q^(Q/2). This is a different intermediate bank from Z; the later Green decoder is different too.

Completing Phase 5's Cooley–Tukey map Z→Y adds twiddles, K unnormalized M-transforms, and global normalization 1/sqrt(Q). Its determinant magnitude is M^(Q/2)Q^(-Q/2)=K^(-Q/2), cancelling |det C|. Therefore raw x→Y=U_Qx has determinant magnitude one; free D→Y has magnitude M^(-Q/2). A determinant argument on an explicitly required intermediate does not become a determinant lower bound on the normalized output-only problem.

Under the declared binary linear-gate convention y=a u+b v with |a|,|b|<=1, a maximal-volume determinant potential grows by at most two per gate. Therefore explicitly producing Cx from independent raw Q inputs requires at least (Q/2)log_2 K gates. Free D→Z instead yields only max(0,(Q/2)log_2(K/M)) from that final determinant. Invertibility alone does not preserve a bounded-coefficient circuit model under a free change of input basis.

The zero-padded N-input domain requires a rectangular argument. For an isometric padding E:C^N→C^Q, (CE)*(CE)=K I_N. Cauchy–Binet supplies an N-output minor with magnitude at least K^(N/2)/sqrt(binomial(Q,N)), giving

g >= (N/2)log_2 K - (1/2)log_2 binomial(Q,N) >= (N/2)log_2 K-Q/2.

At Q≈4N this is still Omega(N log K) for that explicitly produced unnormalized intermediate, but it is not formula (15)'s Q/2 coefficient. Nonunit Gaussian preweights require their additional determinant factor and cannot be silently called an isometry. See `accuracy_review.md` for the explicit model and primary-source check. None of these gates applies to arbitrary/unbounded coefficients, freely normalized intermediate targets, or a circuit that avoids the specified intermediate bank.

## 6. Charged complexity, precision, and inherited gates

The supplied joint evaluator costs O(Q log M) to invert D, O(Q log K) for the long-axis bank, O(Q) twiddles, and O(Q log M) for the final local bank. Producing D from fresh raw input adds O(Q log M). It stores O(Q) scalar words and writes Q outputs. This is an ordinary O(Q log Q) scalar-arithmetic implementation; if raw x is already present, creating then undoing D is unnecessary. These are charges for this implementation, not proof that each bank is unavoidable in every circuit.

For general complex lengths, the FFT subroutines require suitable supplied roots/constants and a standard all-length implementation. The small-field checks only establish algebra in the selected fields, not a uniform fast FFT over every finite field or a root-generation theorem. Reading arbitrary supplied phase tables costs O(Q); generating structured phases requires a declared coefficient model. The exact unitary formulas do not make transcendental constants finite field-operation constructions.

No certified working-precision bound is supplied. The uploaded prototype uses complex128, floating twiddle arguments, NumPy FFTs, and int64 phase products. Large integer products can overflow or lose low phase bits before reduction. The source correctly restricts practical claims; its saved tests do not certify all sizes. Unitarity shows excellent exact conditioning for normalized analysis/synthesis, but does not bound errors from root generation, repeated multiplication, the FFT implementation, finite arithmetic, or the Gaussian pre/postweights. The Gaussian finite-precision gap remains open.

Fusion with the other approved reports is deliberately limited:

- **Stationary Gram:** the orthogonal bank is exactly conditioned; the different stationary full frame has its independently proved condition bound. Neither yields a faster dense coefficient/FFT-bank evaluator.
- **Complete Weyl fingerprints:** they can certify a concrete proposed full operator or compatibility of a middle monomial. They cannot discover a cheaper contraction or waive coefficient construction work.
- **Correlated forcing:** the actual rank obstruction for moving a nonconstant output weight through one scan remains intact. Constant complete-bank averaging does not contradict it, and the commutator result cannot be added over bank rows to forbid simultaneous cancellation.
- **Gaussian approximation:** finite-population grouping sharpens variance constants while retaining the separate bias, independence, model, and precision conditions.
