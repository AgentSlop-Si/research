# Research findings and implications

## Starting checkpoint

The preceding work investigated `F_8=B P A`, `P=(7,0,6,3,4,1,2,5)`, `C=A^{-1}`, and two globally separated generator stages. It derived a 31-term cubic, an 11x9 matrix compatibility gate, and a specific 250-term degree-eight polynomial gate `Dhat=-D_B D_H`. Earlier partial complex exclusions did **not** settle the full feasibility problem.

## Result 1: why the old rank gate has no hidden extra minors

The adjacent-column recurrence forces the first five Fourier images of the lower tail to lie in the span of three low Fourier characters. Thus the 11x9 gate's top row block has rank <=3. Exact sparse-polynomial verification checks 30 vanishing 4x4 determinants, 10 Plücker identities and every one of the 55 maximal minors; only 10 can be nonzero, and all are constant multiples of the previously selected `Dhat`.

The old rank gate is useful, but exhausting further 9x9 minors **cannot** improve the exclusion. A genuinely different compatibility test is needed.

The exceptional rank-drop factor `D_B` has a degree-two absolutely irreducible zero set: its homogenized symmetric matrix has a nonzero 4x4 determinant, witnessed by 11251 modulo 12289 at the eighth root 8246.

## Result 2: two-sided Fourier tail-rank theorem

For a two-stage QS representation `B=F_N C[:,perm]`, with invertible `C`, let the last `t` output columns of `B` correspond to the set `J` of columns of `C`. Let `ell=N-1-max(J)`. Then

`min(t,ell) <= rank_lower(C) + rank_upper(B)`.

Dually, for the first `t` output columns with `a=min(J)`,

`min(t,a) <= rank_upper(C) + rank_lower(B)`.

This is proved by rank-nullity and the full Vandermonde rank of Fourier characters restricted to consecutive head/tail coordinates. It does not assume globally separated generator charts; *any* quasiseparable operators satisfying these rank bounds are covered.

If both relevant ranks are one, `perm[-3:]` must contain at least one input index >= `N-3`, and `perm[:3]` must contain at least one input index <=2. In particular, `perm[-3:]=(1,2,5)` works at N=8 only in the sense that the theorem does not exclude it; it fails for every N>=9.

### Quantitative permutation screening

Applying the theorem to *every* prefix and suffix gives a low-cost necessary-condition filter. The exhaustive 8-point scan found 28,584 of all 40,320 permutations passing the rank-sum-two necessary test; 11,736 were eliminated. The supporting script also produces deterministic seeded random screening statistics for larger lengths. These counts concern this particular necessary test, **not** FFT algorithms constructed or ruled out by other methods.

## Validation

- All 55 gate maximal minors checked as exact polynomial identities over `Z[zeta_8]`.
- The rank-drop irreducibility witness is a 4x4 determinant nonzero modulo 12289.
- The all-length rank theorem was independently checked against Fourier kernel Vandermonde ranks and arbitrary full-column-rank test matrices for lengths 8, 16, 32, 64 over GF(12289) and GF(65537).
- The 6x6 determinant factorization (boundary matching auxiliary identity) was checked at N=16,32,64 in two prime fields, and the N=8 failure mode was explicitly tested; see `BOUNDARY_LOCK_TESTS.json`.

## Next research gate

Focus on permutations **passing both tail-rank screens** and on intersections of independent nonlinear compatibility equations, keeping singular/zero-generator branches. For a proposed growth strategy, screen the permutation first: if its required rank sum grows, it cannot realize two bounded-rank stages. Do not confuse stage count or dense intermediate coordinates with actual arithmetic savings. The arithmetic objective remains an exact transform of arbitrary fresh inputs with total cost below the current reference comparison.
