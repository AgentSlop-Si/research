# Phase 26 — simultaneous modular cuts for multi-stage cyclic Green circuits

**Date:** October 10, 2026  
**Status:** Self-contained restricted-model proofs, exact-field implementations and regression checks. No worst-case `o(N log N)` FFT or integer-multiplication improvement. **Not independently peer reviewed.**

## 0. The problem and the precise model

Let `N=2^k>=8`, `Fhat_N[j,l] = exp(-2π i j l/N)/sqrt(N)` and let `W` be a permutation matrix encoding one Hamiltonian cycle (a cyclic successor on all `N` coordinates). The allowed Green operation is a **weighted cyclic inverse**

```
G = (I - diag(gamma_j) W)^(-1).
```

The product of weights around the cycle must be different from one; the weights can otherwise be any complex numbers, can vary with `j` and `N`, and can be near-resonant. An application of `G` costs `O(N)` arithmetic using the explicit recurrence in `phase26_cuts.py`.

A circuit is an arbitrary product of sums of these Green operations with arbitrary left/right diagonal gauges, arbitrary intervening diagonal gates (not necessarily unitary), and optionally **intermediate affine coordinate permutations** of the form `j -> u*j+b mod N` with `gcd(u,N)=1`. Arbitrary outer coordinate permutations and diagonal scalings are also allowed. **Unstructured internal permutations are excluded.**

An individual Green cycle is called *d-affine-run* here if, after removing at most `b` exceptional directed edges (bridges), every remaining edge is a translation by `+a` or `-a`, for one of at most `d` step magnitudes `a` associated with that cycle. The Phase25 zigzags are `d=1,b=2`. A Hamiltonian cycle represented by `d` affine arithmetic runs has `d` internal step values and at most `d` bridges; allowing signs and merging equal step magnitudes fits this model.

Let `Q` be the **total count of Green terms**, across all sums and all product stages. Let `s` be the number of distinct step magnitudes across all terms *after moving affine intermediate permutations to one end*. Let `B` be the total exceptional-edge count. We always have `s <= d Q` if each term uses at most `d` magnitudes.

## 1. Simultaneous-character balanced cut

**Lemma 1 (pigeonhole).** For `s>=1` and any nonzero residues `a_1,...,a_s mod N`, put

```
r = floor((N-1)^(1/s)).
```

There exists an integer `1 <= h < N` such that, simultaneously for every `a_i`, the least-absolute representative `t_i` of `h*a_i mod N` satisfies `|t_i| < N/r`.

**Proof.** Consider the `N` vectors `({ja_1/N},...,{ja_s/N})` for `j=0,...,N-1`. Partition `[0,1)^s` into `r^s <= N-1` half-open cubes of side `1/r`. Two vectors lie in the same cube, and subtracting their indices produces nonzero `h` with all circular distances below `1/r`. QED.

For this `h`, define

```
I = {j : (h*j mod N) < N/2},  J = complement(I).
```

Because `N` is a power of two and `1<=h<N`, `gcd(h,N)` divides `N/2`. Hence **both sets have exactly N/2 elements**. For the translation `j -> j+a_i mod N`, exactly `|t_i|` directed edges go from `J` into `I`. This follows because the phase `hj mod N` is uniform on the multiples of its gcd with `N`, and a signed translation by `t_i` crosses an arc endpoint in exactly `|t_i|` positions (counted with multiplicity). This identity is exhaustively tested for small `N` and every `h,a`.

A cycle with at most `d` step magnitudes and `b` exceptional edges therefore satisfies

```
#(J -> I edges) <= 2*d*N/r + b,
```

where the factor 2 allows both signs of each step magnitude and is conservative for one-sided steps. With one magnitude per cycle, the bound is `2*N/r + b`.

## 2. Exact Green inverse rank across complementary cuts

**Lemma 2.** Let `A=I-diag(gamma_j)W` be an invertible weighted cyclic denominator, and let `I,J` be any nonempty complementary subsets. Then

```
rank( (A^(-1))[I,J] ) = rank( A[I,J] )
                         <= #(cycle edges from J to I).
```

**Proof.** The restriction of one Hamiltonian directed cycle to the proper subset `J` consists of directed paths, never a cycle. Hence `A[J,J]` is invertible: its off-identity part is nilpotent in a topological ordering. The block inverse formula gives

```
A^(-1)[I,J] = - (A[I,I]-A[I,J] A[J,J]^-1 A[J,I])^-1
                A[I,J] A[J,J]^-1.
```

All outside factors are invertible, because `A` and `A[J,J]` are. Hence equality of ranks. Each entry in `A[I,J]` comes from a directed cycle edge crossing `J -> I`, so its rank cannot exceed the edge count. The argument holds over any field for which the denominator is invertible and **requires no condition-number bound**. QED.

Diagonal row/column gauges do not increase that rank. Summing matrices adds their block-rank bounds.

## 3. Products do not repair a common small cut quickly

**Lemma 3 (elementary block identity).** For a fixed complementary partition `I,J`,

```
(AB)[I,J] = A[I,I] B[I,J] + A[I,J] B[J,J],
```

thus

```
rank((AB)[I,J]) <= rank(A[I,J]) + rank(B[I,J]).
```

A diagonal intermediate operation has zero cross-cut rank. Induct over every sum and product in the circuit. **The number of stages is irrelevant**; only the total count of weighted Green terms matters.

**Theorem 4 (main rank barrier).** In the above model *without unstructured internal permutations*, a balanced complementary cut `I,J` exists such that

```
rank(M[I,J]) <= 2*d*Q*N / floor((N-1)^(1/s)) + B.             (1)
```

If every Green term is a Phase25-type two-run affine cycle, set `d=1`, and the bound reduces to `2*Q*N/r+B`.

This remains valid under intermediate affine permutations. Such permutations conjugate each Green term into a Green term whose steps are multiplied by an invertible residue `u`, preserving the number of affine runs and bridges; moving the intermediate affine permutations through the product leaves only a final outer permutation, while all diagonal factors remain diagonal. The theorem applies to the resulting collection of conjugated cycles. Any arbitrary external **monomial** (permutation-plus-diagonal) factors move the certified low-rank block to some other balanced row/column subsets, without changing its rank.

### A Fourier-rank and spectral-distance consequence

For any balanced row and column subsets `R,C`, the normalized Fourier submatrix `H=Fhat_N[R,C]` obeys

```
||H||_F^2 = N/4,     ||H||_2 <= 1,     rank(H) >= N/4.
```

The rank bound follows from the squared Frobenius norm and the upper bound of one for every singular value. Consequently, **if the right-hand side of (1) is below `N/4`, exact Fourier factorization is impossible** even with arbitrary external monomial transformations.

More quantitatively, if any `N/2` by `N/2` block of `M` has rank at most `rho < N/4`, then Eckart–Young and the same singular-value accounting give

```
||M-Fhat_N||_2 >= sqrt((N/4-rho)/(N/2-rho)).                (2)
```

This is valid for arbitrary complex coefficients, numerical conditioning and external coordinate reordering. If `d,s` are fixed, `Q=o(N^(1/s))`, and `B=O(Q)`, then `rho=o(N)` and the right-hand side tends to `1/sqrt(2)`. This is an *approximation obstruction*, not just an exact identity obstruction.

**The `1/sqrt(2)` limiting constant cannot be raised using only universal balanced-submatrix information:** choose `R` to be the even row indices and `C={0,...,N/2-1}`. Then `Fhat_N[R,C]=Fhat_(N/2)/sqrt(2)`, so all its singular values are exactly `1/sqrt(2)`. Numerical SVD checks passed for `N=16,32,64,128`.

## 4. A new Green-term-count lower bound, even with arbitrary affine interstage permutations

Suppose each Green term uses at most `d` affine step magnitudes, at most `b` bridge edges, and `d,b` are constants independent of `N`. There are at most `s<=dQ` distinct transformed magnitudes, and `B<=bQ`.

Exact Fourier equality requires

```
N/4 <= 2*d*Q*N/r + b*Q.
```

If `Q > N/(8b)` (for `b>=1`), the claimed logarithmic bound is immediate. Otherwise `bQ<=N/8`, and hence `r<=16*d*Q`. Since `s<=dQ`,

```
(N-1)^(1/(dQ)) < r+1 <= 16*d*Q+1.
```

Therefore we have the explicit **necessary** inequality

```
log(N-1) < d*Q*log(16*d*Q+1).                              (3)
```

Thus, for fixed `d,b`, every exact circuit in this model requires

```
Q = Omega(log N / log log N).
```

The same term-budget asymptotic is necessary for any fixed operator-norm approximation error strictly smaller than `1/sqrt(2)`, with a different constant inside the logarithm. This is a representation-complexity statement; it is **not** an `Omega(N log N)` arithmetic lower bound because Green evaluations may share work and lookup/preprocessing models may differ.

For the two-run `d=1,b=2` family, the coarse bound's first *nonexcluded* `Q` (not a sufficient number of terms!) is `6` at `N=2^32`, `11` at `2^64`, `18` at `2^128`, and `32` at `2^256`. These values are from the explicit coarse rank inequality, not from finding a Fourier factorization.

## 5. Concrete two-stage certificate

Choose the four cyclic orderings at power-of-two `N>=128`:

```
W_2, W_(N/2+2), W_(N/2+6), Z,
```

where `W_a` is the Phase25 two-run zigzag with steps `+/-a` plus two bridges, and `Z` is the ordinary shift. For the character `h=2`, the cut is the alternating-quarter partition

```
I = [0,N/4) union [N/2,3N/4),
J = [N/4,N/2) union [3N/4,N).
```

The **exact directed edge counts** from `J` to `I` are `[3,3,11,2]`, independently of `N` for `N>=128`. One stage with one weighted Green inverse from each ordering has cross-cut rank at most `19`, regardless of its edge weights and diagonal gauges. Therefore **a product of two such four-term sums, with an arbitrary diagonal gate between them, has rank at most 38** on this common cut.

At `N=256`, a deterministic random-weight example was checked over `F_998244353`:

```
actual candidate minor rank:     38
certified candidate rank upper:  38
N/4 Fourier universal minimum:  64
actual Fourier minor rank:      128
complex operator-error lower:   sqrt(26/90) = 0.5374838499...
```

The 128-rank statement is more than a numerical observation: the `N/2` by `N/2` Fourier block on this particular alternating-quarter cut is **invertible for every power-of-two `N>=8`**, and over every field of characteristic not 2 containing a primitive `N`th root.

**Proof of Fourier minor invertibility.** Pair each column index `v` in `[N/4,N/2)` with `v+N/2`. The two columns agree on even rows and are negatives on odd rows. The invertible column sum/difference change separates even and odd rows. In either parity sector, the row indices are `i=2t+p` with `N/4` distinct values of `t modulo N/2`. The remaining column indices are `N/4` **consecutive** integers `v`. After nonzero diagonal scalings, each sector is a Vandermonde matrix with row nodes `(omega^2)^t` all distinct. Hence both sectors are invertible. QED.

This full-rank statement is specific to the unpermuted alternating-quarter block. The **weaker rank-at-least-`N/4` bound**, not the special full-rank property, is what makes the impossibility robust under *arbitrary* external permutations.

## 6. The first constructive escape screen

A genuinely non-affine Hamiltonian cycle may use `Theta(N)` different circular edge increments. Then the fixed-stride simultaneous-cut theorem does not apply. `audit.py` compares two seeded random Hamiltonian cycles plus the natural shift against the affine examples. Their best cuts among character-balanced partitions have `63,124,248,501` directed crossing edges for `N=128,256,512,1024`, rather than `19` for the fixed four-term affine stage. **These are only character-cut diagnostics, not a proof of all-cut expansion**.

Two independently ordered weighted Green stages built from the random cycles were tested against both exact Weyl equations using full-length random vectors over `F_998244353`. Their reference FFT controls have zero defects; the Green candidates have nonzero defects at every tested coordinate. Thus escaping the affine separator barrier alone remains insufficient for correct Fourier phases.
