# Ramified root clusters: a conditional theorem and three infinite composite families

## Result and scope

This pass proves a new-to-this-project sufficient criterion for characteristic-zero squarefreeness at `N=dp`, where `p` is an odd prime occurring exactly once in `N`. The criterion is a finite, explicit calculation at the lower order `d`, rather than a test of the degree `(dp−1)/2` polynomial.

It gives the following infinite-family extension of the accepted classification:

> For every prime `p ≡ 1 (mod 4)`, each of the orders `N=5p`, `N=9p`, and `N=13p` has squarefree `H_((N−1)/2)(z;ζ_N)` over characteristic zero. Hence its legal scalar-resolvent minimum with one free diagonal is `(N−1)/2`.

The ramified proof covers `p∤d`. The two exceptions to that hypothesis within the displayed families, `N=25` and `N=169`, use the already accepted finite certificates; those two certificates have been independently rechecked as data by fresh code here. For `d=9`, a prime `p≡1 (mod 4)` cannot divide `d`.

This is **not** an all-composite theorem. In particular, it does not prove every `N=5r²`, nor supply a new result for arbitrary prime powers. No target-order census was repeated or extended. The accepted uniform resonance theorem and finite range through 1001 remain valid; the proved families now also extend beyond that range.

The core additions are:

1. A Hermite leading model for each separated repeated cluster in the q-Lucas degeneration at an arbitrary odd root order `d`.
2. An exact leading formula for the **ordinary derivative discriminant**, and exact cyclotomic division before reduction modulo `p`.
3. A lower-degree integer-content criterion whose verified values at `d=5,9,13` establish the three families.
4. Explicit bad-prime and overlap examples showing why the hypotheses cannot be silently discarded.

“New” here means an addition to these project packages. Literature priority of this particular cluster formula and its corollaries has not been established.

## 1. Definitions and known q-Lucas factorization

Let

    H_n(z;q) = Σ_(k=0)^n [n choose k]_q z^k,
    D_n(q) = Disc_z H_n(z;q) ∈ Z[q].

Set `D_0=D_1=1`. These are ordinary discriminants, not operator or q-difference discriminants. The recurrence is

    H_0=1, H_1=1+z,
    H_n=(1+z)H_(n−1)+(q^(n−1)−1)zH_(n−2).

Fix an odd integer `d≥1`, a primitive complex `d`th root `ω`, and integers `A≥1`, `0≤b<d`. Put `n=Ad+b`. The established q-Lucas theorem gives

    H_n(z;ω) = (1+z^d)^A H_b(z;ω).                         (1)

Indeed, `[Ad+b choose cd+e]_ω = binom(A,c)[b choose e]_ω`; summing proves (1). A primary treatment is Bruce Sagan, *Congruence properties of q-analogs*, Advances in Mathematics 95 (1992), Theorem 2.2, pp. 131–132: https://users.math.msu.edu/users/bsagan/Papers/Old/cpq-pub.pdf . This known identity is not claimed as a new result.

Write

    F(z)=1+z^d,   G(z)=H_b(z;ω),
    Δ=Disc G,   R=Res_z(F,G),
    P_A=∏_(j=1)^A j^j,   K=dA(A−1)/2,
    Q_d(q)=(q^d−1)/Φ_d(q).

For the separated-cluster statements below assume `ΔR≠0`: `G` is squarefree and none of its roots is a root of `F`. This is a genuine hypothesis. We do not infer its characteristic-p analogue from characteristic-zero squarefreeness.

## 2. Separated-cluster Hermite lemma

Introduce a formal parameter `ε` and set

    q(ε)=ω(1+ε²)^(1/d),

where the binomial series has constant term 1. Thus `q(ε)^d−1=ε²` exactly. If `α^d=−1`, then

    H_n(α+εx;q(ε))
      = ε^A (−1)^A d^(A/2) G(α)
          He_A(√d x/α) + O(ε^(A+1)).                    (2)

Here `He_A` is the monic probabilists' Hermite polynomial, with recurrence

    He_0=1, He_1=x, He_A=x He_(A−1)−(A−1)He_(A−2).

The apparent square roots cancel in (2), so its coefficients lie in `Q(ω,α)[x]`. The formula itself remains an identity if `G(α)=0`, but then its leading coefficient vanishes and it does **not** describe that overlapping cluster.

### Proof of (2)

Use the formal generating function

    Σ_(n≥0) H_n(z;q)t^n/(q;q)_n
      = exp(Σ_(k≥1) (1+z^k)t^k/[k(1−q^k)]).             (3)

For completeness, the left side is the product of `Σ t^j/(q;q)_j` and `Σ (zt)^j/(q;q)_j` by the Gaussian coefficient formula. If `E(t)=Σ t^j/(q;q)_j`, its coefficient recurrence gives `E(qt)=(1−t)E(t)`. Taking the formal logarithm gives `log E(t)=Σ t^k/[k(1−q^k)]`, proving (3). Thus no analytic convergence or exchange of infinite numerical sums is required.

Split the exponent into terms with `d|k` and `d∤k`. The latter define a series `B(t;z,q)` analytic in `ε`; the coefficient of `t^b` at `ε=0` is `G(α)/(ω;ω)_b` because `b<d`.

For the multiple-of-d part put `u=t^d`. At `z=α+εx` the coefficients of `u` and `u²` in its logarithm are respectively

    x/(α ε)+O(1),    −1/(2d ε²)+O(ε^(−1)).

For `u^l` with `l≥3` the pole order is at most 2, strictly smaller than `l`. Consequently, the coefficient of `u^a` in the exponential has pole order at most `a`, and its coefficient of `ε^(−a)` is

    d^(−a/2) He_a(√d x/α)/a!.

This follows by retaining the first two displayed logarithm terms, which give the Hermite generating function. Every term involving `u^l`, `l≥3`, has strictly smaller pole order than its total u-degree. To form the coefficient of `t^(Ad+b)`, the terms involving coefficients `t^(b+jd)` of `B` with `j>0` have pole order at most `A−j`, and therefore do not affect the leading term.

Finally,

    (q(ε);q(ε))_(Ad+b)
      = (−1)^A A! d^A (ω;ω)_b ε^(2A)(1+O(ε²)).

The multiples of d contribute `∏_(j=1)^A(−jε²)`; every full block of the other factors contributes `∏_(j=1)^(d−1)(1−ω^j)=d`. Multiplying the leading coefficients proves (2).

## 3. Exact leading ordinary discriminant

Under the separated-cluster hypotheses `ΔR≠0`,

    D_n(q(ε)) = ε^(2K) [C(ω)+O(ε²)],                   (4)

where

    C(ω)= (−1)^(A(d−1)/2)
          d^(dA(A+1)/2) P_A^d Δ R^(2A).                (5)

In particular the order at `q=ω` is **exactly** `K`.

### Proof

The Hermite discriminant is `Disc He_A=P_A`, by `He_A'=A He_(A−1)` and the three-term recurrence. It is nonzero in characteristic zero. Formula (2) and formal implicit lifting therefore give exactly A distinct branches near each α,

    z_(α,j)=α+ε(α/√d)h_j+O(ε²),

where the `h_j` are the distinct Hermite zeros. The other b roots lift the simple roots of G and remain separated from these clusters.

Within one cluster, the product of squared root differences contributes

    ε^(A(A−1)) (α²/d)^(A(A−1)/2) P_A.

Multiplying over the d roots α removes their product factor because `A(A−1)` is even. This gives `ε^(2K)d^(−dA(A−1)/2)P_A^d`.

The cross-cluster factors contribute `(Disc F)^(A²)`, where

    Disc(z^d+1)=(−1)^((d−1)/2)d^d.

The factors from G contribute `Δ`, and those between G and all clusters contribute `R^(2A)`. Their product is (5). Since `D_n(q(ε))` is an even series in ε, the normalized remainder in (4) starts at ε². This also verifies the sign and normalization, not merely nonvanishing.

## 4. The exact cyclotomic bridge to a ramified order

This step is essential: a first nonzero term of an arbitrary p-adic Taylor series would not alone be enough, especially when its order exceeds the ramification index. We instead divide an integer polynomial by an exact cyclotomic power **before** reducing.

The exact order K from Section 3 holds at every primitive dth root by Galois conjugacy. Hence

    D_n(q)=Φ_d(q)^K E_n,d(q),    E_n,d ∈ Z[q].           (6)

Monic division gives integrality. Since `q^d−1=Φ_d(q)Q_d(q)`, (4) implies the exact identity in `Q(ω)`

    E_n,d(ω)=Q_d(ω)^K C(ω).                             (7)

Let p be an odd prime with `p∤d`, and now specialize

    A=(p−1)/2,  b=(d−1)/2,  n=(dp−1)/2.

Suppose there is a prime `𝔭` of `Z[ω]` above p such that the reductions of `Δ` and `R` are both nonzero. Then their characteristic-zero values are also nonzero, so Sections 2–3 apply. Because `A<p`, `P_A` is a p-unit. Also d and `Q_d(ω)` are units modulo `𝔭`; the latter follows since `q^d−1` is squarefree modulo p and `Φ_d` is one of its factors. Equations (5)–(7) show

    E_n,d(ω) is nonzero modulo 𝔭.                       (8)

If `D_n(ζ_(dp))=0`, irreducibility and Gauss's lemma imply `Φ_(dp)|D_n` in `Z[q]`. Since `Φ_(dp)` and `Φ_d` are distinct irreducibles, (6) implies `Φ_(dp)|E_n,d`. But the standard identity

    Φ_(dp)(q)=Φ_d(q^p)/Φ_d(q),   p∤d,

reduces to

    Φ_(dp)(q) ≡ Φ_d(q)^(p−1)  (mod p).                  (9)

This would force `E_n,d(ω)=0` modulo every prime above p, contradicting (8). Therefore

    D_((dp−1)/2)(ζ_(dp)) ≠ 0.                           (10)

This proof does not require `K<p−1`, does not neglect p-divisible lower terms, and does not assume that a characteristic-zero induction supplies a good prime. The needed good prime is an explicit hypothesis checked below.

The cyclotomic ramification interpretation is standard: for `N=p^a d` with `p∤d`, p has ramification index `φ(p^a)` in `Q(ζ_N)`, and the residue field retains roots of exact order d. See Romyar Sharifi's primary lecture notes, *Algebraic Number Theory*, Proposition 3.1.21: https://math.ucla.edu/~sharifi/notes/algnum-ch03.html . The proof above only needs the elementary polynomial identity (9), not a hidden p-adic convergence assertion.

## 5. A finite lower-order content criterion

For an odd d define the integer polynomial of degree below `φ(d)`

    B_d(q) = rem_(Φ_d(q)) [
        D_((d−1)/2)(q)
        Res_z(z^d+1,H_((d−1)/2)(z;q)) ].                 (11)

If it is nonzero, let `c_d>0` be the gcd of its integer coefficients. If it is zero, put `c_d=0` and regard the criterion as inapplicable.

For `p∤d`, `Φ_d mod p` is squarefree. Thus there is a primitive dth root ω in some finite extension of `F_p` for which both factors in (11) are nonzero if and only if

    B_d mod p is not identically zero,

which is equivalent to `p∤c_d` when `c_d≠0`. This is an existence condition on **one** good residue factor. It is not a claim that all primes above p are good.

Combining this observation with Section 4 proves:

> **Ramified sufficient criterion.** Let d be odd and p an odd prime with `p∤d`. If `c_d≠0` and `p∤c_d`, then `H_((dp−1)/2)(z;ζ_(dp))` is squarefree over characteristic zero, for every primitive choice of `ζ_(dp)`.

This also shows: whenever the lower characteristic-zero polynomial is squarefree and separated from `z^d+1`, all but finitely many odd primes p give squarefreeness at order dp. The excluded primes are explicitly bounded by the divisors of `d c_d`; a failure of this sufficient criterion is not a counterexample to squarefreeness.

For `d≡3 (mod 4)`, the lower degree b is odd and `H_b(−1;q)=0`, so `B_d=0`. The present separated-cluster criterion deliberately does not cover that overlap case.

## 6. Exact fixed-base computations and infinite families

Fresh exact symbolic arithmetic gives the following complete remainders and their contents. These are finite calculations on lower degrees 2, 4 and 6, not samples of an asserted infinite theorem.

### Base d=5

Here `b=2`,

    H_2=z²+(1+q)z+1,
    Δ=q²+2q−3,
    R mod Φ_5=5(2q²+2q+1),
    B_5=5(4q³−3q²−6q−5),
    c_5=5.

The primitive part has coefficient gcd 1. Consequently **every odd prime `p≠5`** passes the criterion. In particular p=61 passes, even though one residue factor there is bad; see Section 7.

### Base d=9

Here `b=4`,

    B_9=3^8(2674q⁵+387q⁴−2088q³−900q²−3017q−3710),
    c_9=3^8.

The displayed primitive part has coefficient gcd 1. Every odd prime `p≠3` passes.

### Base d=13

Here `b=6`,

    B_13=13^4(
       38059673422q¹¹−325103706677q¹⁰−1006293241861q⁹
       −1849456455979q⁸−2661435680273q⁷−3256214747883q⁶
       −3497539014697q⁵−3330121488092q⁴−2792318310715q³
       −2007330772573q²−1154992956792q−430562494655),
    c_13=13^4.

Again the primitive coefficient gcd is 1. Every odd prime `p≠13` passes.

All three statements hold for odd p without a congruence restriction as squarefreeness assertions. For the user's smaller legal branch count, take `p≡1 (mod 4)`, since all three bases are 1 modulo 4. The accepted zero-node and resonance guards then complete the scalar-bank criterion. Including the separately verified existing certificates at N=25 and N=169 gives the infinite-family statement at the beginning of this report.

## 7. Exact obstructions to dropping the hypotheses

### A lower characteristic-zero discriminant can be bad at a specific prime

At `d=5,p=61`, the primitive fifth roots in `F_61` are `9,20,34,58`. The lower discriminant takes values `35,10,1,0` respectively. In particular `ω=58=−3` gives

    H_2(z;58)=(z−1)²  in F_61[z].

Thus characteristic-zero squarefreeness at d=5 does not make every prime above61 good. The other three roots pass both guards, which is why the existential criterion still proves N=305.

### Cluster separation can fail modulo p even when the residual polynomial stays squarefree

At `d=13,p=5`, exact arithmetic gives

    gcd_q(Φ_13, D_6) = 1  in F_5[q],

but

    gcd_q(Φ_13, Res_z(z^13+1,H_6))
      =q⁴−2q³−2q+1.

This quartic is one of the three irreducible quartic factors of `Φ_13 mod5`. At that prime the residual H_6 is squarefree but collides with one or more repeated q-Lucas clusters. Other factors remain good, so N=65 still passes the existential criterion. The two unit conditions in Section 4 are distinct.

### An intrinsic overlap changes the discriminant order

At `d=3,b=1`, `G=z+1` shares `α=−1` with `F=z³+1`. Exact characteristic-zero controls show

    ord_(Φ_3) D_7 = 5   for A=2,
    ord_(Φ_3) D_10 =12  for A=3,

where the inapplicable separated-cluster exponents would be 3 and 9. Formula (5) has zero leading coefficient in these cases. It is therefore invalid to reuse that formula as a nonzero first coefficient for `d≡3 (mod4)`.

### Higher ramification does not automatically finish prime powers

For `N=p^a d`, `a≥2`, q-Lucas gives `A=(p^a−1)/2`. Then `A≥p`, so `P_A=∏j^j` is divisible by p. Even when the lower residual factors are units, the first Hermite-cluster discriminant coefficient in (5) is zero modulo p. Exact division still exists, but the nonzero-unit bridge (8) has failed. Further multiscale splitting or a different invariant would be needed. Nothing here promotes a vanished leading residue into a characteristic-zero multiple root.

## 9. Stopping boundary and next mathematical step

The proved delta is the separated-cluster Hermite lemma, the exact ordinary-discriminant coefficient, the cyclotomic-division bridge, and the resulting three infinite families. This is a genuine partial extension beyond the former finite composite classification.

For a uniform all-composite proof, the remaining gaps include intrinsic overlap when every usable complementary order d is 3 modulo4, repeated prime factors where the first Hermite coefficient vanishes modulo p, and a uniform assertion about the lower-order contents c_d. No general theorem that c_d has prime factors only dividing d has been proved. The observed values at d=5,9,13 are not evidence sufficient to assert that.

The q-Lucas theorem and cyclotomic ramification are established prior mathematics. The full target half-degree squarefreeness question is still unresolved within this project; this report does not assert that it is an open problem in the mathematical literature or claim publication priority.
