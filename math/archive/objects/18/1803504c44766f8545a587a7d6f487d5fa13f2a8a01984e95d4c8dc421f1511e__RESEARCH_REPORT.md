# Ramified contents: a uniform valuation theorem and a smaller exact obstruction

## Verdict and proved delta

The assertion that the prime support of `c_d` is confined to the primes dividing `d`, for every `d≡1 (mod4)`, remains **unproved here**. No counterexample was found in the three specifically permitted checks `d=17,21,25`. Those checks cannot justify extrapolation.

The useful structural results are:

1. **Exact conditional ramified-content valuation.** Let `s≡1 (mod4)`, let `p≡1 (mod4)` be prime, and suppose `c_s≠0` and `p∤s c_s`. Then

       v_p(c_(sp)) = floor(3s(p−1)/8).                   (A)

   This is a uniform theorem, proved below from the accepted separated-cluster lemma and exact cyclotomic division. In particular, for every prime `ℓ=4r+1`,

       v_ℓ(c_ℓ)=floor(3r/2)=floor(3(ℓ−1)/8).            (B)

   It determines the forced ramified prime exponent exactly. It does **not** exclude other prime divisors of `c_ℓ` or `c_(sp)`.

2. **Classical reciprocal and Gaussian identities reduce the unramified obstruction.** For `d=4r+1`, the original degree-`2r` discriminant and degree-`d` cluster resultant can be replaced, for prime-support purposes away from `d`, by one `r×r` multiplication determinant. Two cluster factors and the alternating Gaussian value are uniformly units away from `d`. The precise surviving determinant appears in §3.

3. **Fixed-base corollaries.** Fresh exact calculations give

       c_17=17^6,   c_21=3^11·7^9,   c_25=5^17.

   Therefore the already accepted ramified sufficient criterion proves squarefreeness at `N=17p,21p,25p` for every odd prime `p∤d` in the respective family. This excludes `p=17`, `p∈{3,7}`, and `p=5`, respectively. No excluded case is silently supplied. These are finite-base corollaries of the existing theorem, not a proof of the general content assertion.

No asymptotically faster FFT, unrestricted resolvent lower bound, publication novelty, or all-composite squarefreeness is claimed. This package supplies a mathematical extension and a sharper proof target, not an FFT algorithm.

## 1. Definitions

Write

    H_n(z;q)=Σ_(k=0)^n [n choose k]_q z^k,
    D_n(q)=Disc_z H_n(z;q).

These are ordinary derivative discriminants. For odd `d`, let `b=(d−1)/2` and

    R_d(q)=Res_z(z^d+1,H_b(z;q)),
    B_d=rem_(Φ_d)(D_b R_d),
    c_d=positive gcd of the coefficients of B_d.

Set `c_d=0` if `B_d=0`, and use `D_0=1`, so `c_1=1`. Let `O_d=Z[ζ_d]`. The canonical remainder basis `1,ζ_d,…,ζ_d^(φ(d)−1)` is an integral basis. Consequently

    v_p(c_d)=max{k: B_d(ζ_d)∈p^k O_d}                 (1)

when `B_d≠0`.

The accepted theorem says that `c_s≠0` and `p∤s c_s` guarantee one prime of `O_s` above `p` at which both the lower ordinary discriminant and lower cluster resultant are nonzero. This existential condition is crucial; it does not make every residue factor good.

## 2. A short exact reciprocal identity

Let `d=4r+1` and `H=H_(2r)`. Reciprocity gives a unique monic polynomial `P=P_r(u;q)∈Z[q,u]` of degree `r` such that

    H(z;q)=z^r P(z+z^−1;q).

Define the monic integer polynomial `C_d(u)` of degree `2r` by

    (z^d+1)/(z+1)=z^(2r) C_d(z+z^−1).                 (2)

For a primitive `d`th root `q`, its roots are

    η_j=−q^j−q^(−j),  1≤j≤2r.

They are distinct in every characteristic not dividing `d`. The standard factorization into reciprocal quadratics gives

    Disc_z H=P(2)P(−2)(Disc_u P)^2,                   (3)
    Res_z(z^d+1,H)=H(−1) Res_u(C_d,P)^2.              (4)

For (3), factor `P=∏(u−a_i)` and then `H=∏(z²−a_i z+1)`. The quadratic discriminants give `∏(a_i²−4)=P(2)P(−2)`, and the pairwise quadratic resultants give `∏_(i<j)(a_i−a_j)^4`. The resulting polynomial identity holds integrally, including after specialization or reduction. For (4), pair the roots `−q^j` and `−q^(−j)` of `z^d+1`; their powers of `z^r` cancel. The remaining root is `−1`.

Since `P(2)=H(1)` and `P(−2)=(−1)^r H(−1)`, the exact product identity is

    D_(2r) R_d
      = (−1)^r H(1) [H(−1) Disc(P) Res(C_d,P)]²       (5)

at `q=ζ_d`. No ordinary discriminant has been replaced with a q-difference discriminant.

### The first known unit

Gauss's alternating identity is

    H_(2r)(−1;q)=∏_(j=1)^r(1−q^(2j−1)).              (6)

For every rational prime `p∤d`, a primitive `d`th root remains of exact order `d` in each residue field. Since `0<2j−1<d`, all factors in (6) are nonzero at every such residue factor. Thus `H(−1)` is a unit in `O_d[1/d]`.

This removes the apparent endpoint obstruction `P(−2)` uniformly. It does not remove `P(2)` or `Disc(P)`.

## 3. Two more Gaussian units and the single determinant

Two established special-value identities are

    H_(2r)(−q;q)=H_(2r)(−1;q),
    H_n(t;t²)=∏_(j=1)^n(1+t^j).                       (7)

They appear in Vinroot's primary paper, equations (2.6) and (2.5), respectively. They can also be verified directly from the three-term recurrence. Their role here is algebraic; no analytic Gaussian integral or positivity claim is needed.

The first identity makes `P(η_1)` a unit in `O_d[1/d]`. For the second, put

    t=−q^(2r+1),   t²=q.

Then `t` is the cluster root of index `2r+1`, reciprocal to index `2r`. Every factor `1+t^j`, `1≤j≤2r`, is nonzero in every residue field of characteristic `p∤d`:

- in odd characteristic, `t` has order `2d`, so `t^j=−1` would require `j≡d (mod2d)`;
- in characteristic two, `t` has order `d`, so `t^j=1` would require `d|j`.

Neither is possible in the displayed range. Reciprocity therefore makes `P(η_(2r))` a unit as well.

Define, in `O_d[u]`,

    C_d^*(u)=C_d(u)/[(u−η_1)(u−η_(2r))].              (8)

This is monic of degree `2r−2`; for `d=5` it is `1`. Exact division is integral because the two factors are monic and are factors of the complete root factorization of `C_d`.

Now form

    M_d=Res_u(P,(u−2)P'(u)C_d^*(u)) ∈ O_d.            (9)

Up to the explicit sign `(-1)^(r+r(r−1)/2)`, this is

    H(1) Disc(P) Res(C_d^*,P).

It is also the determinant of multiplication by `(u−2)P'C_d^*` on the free rank-`r` module `O_d[u]/(P)`.

### Exact unramified prime-support reduction

For every rational prime `p∤d`,

    p|c_d  ⇔  M_d∈p O_d.                             (10)

To prove this, reduce modulo `p`. The ring `O_d/pO_d` is a product of fields. In each component, (5) has the same zero set as `H(1)Disc(P)Res(C_d,P)`: squaring does not alter a zero set in a field. The removed three factors are units in every component, so this has exactly the same zero set as `M_d`. The product in (5) is zero in the entire product ring if and only if `M_d` is zero there. Canonical coefficient divisibility gives (10).

This remains valid at `p=2`; no division by two was used in the reduction argument. It is an equivalence for divisibility by an unramified rational prime, not an assertion that all corresponding prime-ideal valuations agree.

### The precise next lemma

The unresolved assertion can now be targeted as follows:

> For every `d=4r+1≥5` and rational prime `p∤d`, there is a primitive `d`th root `ω` over `F_p` for which the rank-`r` multiplication operator in (9) is invertible.

Equivalently, its canonical determinant has no rational prime in its content away from `d`. A stronger constructive way to prove it would be a uniform identity

    Σ_(a∈(Z/dZ)^×) A_a · σ_a(M_d)=d^L,

with `A_a∈O_d` and `L≥0`, where `σ_a(q)=q^a`. Such a Galois-orbit Bézout identity would exclude an unramified rational prime dividing every coefficient. No such identity has been produced here.

The real remaining factors are `P(2)`, `Disc(P)`, and the residual cluster resultant against `C_d^*`. Classical Gaussian identities remove known factors, but do not force this surviving determinant to be nonzero on a whole primitive-root orbit. The previously verified bad factors at `(d,p)=(5,61)` and `(13,5)` prohibit pretending that every residue factor is good.

## 4. Exact conditional ramified-content valuation

### Theorem

Let `s≡1 (mod4)`, let `p≡1 (mod4)` be prime, and suppose `c_s≠0` and `p∤s c_s`. Put

    d=sp, A=(p−1)/2, b=(s−1)/2,
    n=(d−1)/2=As+b,
    K=sA(A−1)/2, L=3sA²/2.

Then `c_d≠0` and

    v_p(c_d)=floor(L/(p−1))=floor(3s(p−1)/8).

### Proof

Let `ω` be primitive of order `s`, put `G=H_b(z;ω)`, and write

    Δ=Disc G, R=Res_z(z^s+1,G),
    Q_s(q)=(q^s−1)/Φ_s(q), P_A=∏_(j=1)^A j^j.

The hypothesis supplies a prime of `O_s` above `p` at which `ΔR` is nonzero. In particular `ΔR≠0` in characteristic zero, so the accepted separated-cluster lemma and discriminant formula apply.

Introduce `q=ω(1+ε²)^(1/s)`. At each `α^s=−1`, that lemma says

    H_n(α+εx;q)
      = ε^A (−1)^A s^(A/2) G(α)
          He_A(√s x/α)+O(ε^(A+1)).                    (11)

The exact ordinary-discriminant exponent is `K`, with quotient evaluation

    [D_n/Φ_s^K](ω)=Q_s(ω)^K C,
    C=(−1)^(A(s−1)/2)s^(sA(A+1)/2)P_A^s ΔR^(2A).    (12)

All factors on the right of (12) are units at the chosen prime above `p`: `p∤s`, `A<p`, and `ΔR` is nonzero there.

For `0≤j<d`, set

    V_j(q)=H_n(−q^j;q) ∈ Z[q],
    J_d(q)=∏_(j=0)^(d−1)V_j(q).

Along the displayed parameterization, `−q^j=−ω^j+O(ε²)`. Because `A` is even, evaluating (11) with `x=O(ε)` gives

    V_j(q)= (q^s−1)^(A/2) κ G(−ω^j)
              +O((q^s−1)^(A/2+1)),
    κ=(−s)^(A/2)(A−1)!!.                             (13)

The stronger integral-order remainder follows because the left side is an even series in `ε`. The leading coefficient is nonzero. At every primitive `s`th root, `V_j` consequently has exact order `A/2`; therefore `Φ_s^(A/2)` divides `V_j` over `Z[q]`, by monic exact division.

Multiplying (13), and noting that each of the `s` roots is represented `p` times among `j=0,…,sp−1`, yields

    [J_d/Φ_s^(dA/2)](ω)
       =Q_s(ω)^(dA/2) κ^d R^p.                       (14)

This is again a unit at the chosen prime: `p∤s`, `A<p`, and `R` is a unit there.

Combining (12) and (14) gives an exact integer-polynomial factorization

    D_n(q)J_d(q)=Φ_s(q)^L E(q), E∈Z[q],
    L=K+dA/2=3sA²/2,                                (15)

where `E(ω)` is a unit at at least one prime above `p`.

Now evaluate at a primitive `d`th root `ζ_d`. The roots `−ζ_d^j` are exactly the roots of `z^d+1`, so

    J_d(ζ_d)=R_d(ζ_d),
    D_n(ζ_d)J_d(ζ_d)=B_d(ζ_d).                       (16)

At every prime of `O_d` above `p`, normalized with uniformizer valuation one, `ζ_d^s−1` has valuation one and `Q_s(ζ_d)` is a unit. Hence `v(Φ_s(ζ_d))=1`. Equation (15) gives valuation at least `L` at every prime above `p`. At a prime lying over the chosen good lower residue factor, `E(ζ_d)` reduces to the nonzero `E(ω)`, so its valuation is zero and the valuation of (16) is exactly `L`.

The ramification index of `p` in `O_d` is `p−1`. Thus the largest rational power `p^k` dividing the element in (16) has

    k=floor(min_(𝔓|p) v_𝔓(B_d(ζ_d))/(p−1))
      =floor(L/(p−1)).

Equation (1) identifies this with `v_p(c_d)`. The finite exact valuation also proves `B_d≠0`. This completes the proof.

### Why the restrictions matter

`p≡1 (mod4)` makes `A` even, so `He_A(0)≠0` and the diagonal-evaluation leading term in (13) is nonzero. For odd `A`, that constant vanishes and this proof does not give the same exponent. The conditions `p∤s` and `p∤c_s` ensure the required ramification index and one unit lower residue factor. No statement for `p²|d` follows from this theorem.

## 5. Prime-base specialization and an elementary local proof

Take `s=1`, so `c_1=1`, and let `ℓ=4r+1` be prime. Formula (A) gives (B). There is also a direct calculation that makes the origin of the exponent transparent.

Write `t=q−1`, `y=z+1`, and assign weights `wt(y)=1`, `wt(t)=2`. From

    H_n=(1+z)H_(n−1)+(q^(n−1)−1)z H_(n−2),

the lowest-weight part of `H_n(−1+y;1+t)` is the homogenized Hermite polynomial, with recurrence

    L_n=yL_(n−1)−(n−1)tL_(n−2).

Its constant-in-`y` term at even degree `2r` is

    (−1)^r(2r−1)!! t^r.

Because `−q^j+1=O(t)`, for every nonnegative integer `j`,

    H_(2r)(−q^j;q)
      =(−1)^r(2r−1)!! (q−1)^r+O((q−1)^(r+1)).        (17)

The ordinary Hermite discriminant limit gives

    D_(2r)(q)=(q−1)^(r(2r−1)) E_(2r)(q),
    E_(2r)(1)=∏_(k=1)^(2r) k^k.                     (18)

Both leading coefficients are `ℓ`-units because `2r<ℓ`. Multiplying (17) over `ℓ` indices with (18) gives exact valuation

    r(2r−1)+ℓr=6r²

at `λ=ζ_ℓ−1`. Since `v_λ(ℓ)=ℓ−1=4r`, the rational content exponent is `floor(6r²/(4r))` as asserted.

The same calculation separately gives

    v_ℓ(content(D_(2r) mod Φ_ℓ))=floor((ℓ−3)/8),
    v_ℓ(content(R_ℓ mod Φ_ℓ))=r.

Their floors need not add to the floor for the product: at `ℓ=17`, the separate content exponents are `1` and `4`, while their product has exponent `6`. Fractional ramified valuations account for the extra factor; it is not an arithmetic inconsistency.

## 6. Strategically limited computations

Only the permitted new bases `17,21,25` were evaluated. Each was chosen to test one form of the prime-support claim: a new prime base, a squarefree composite base with two primes both `3 mod4`, and a repeated-prime base. Complete exact coefficient arrays are in `base_17.json`, `base_21.json`, and `base_25.json`.

The first fresh program used cyclotomic-field arithmetic, a three-term construction cross-checked against q-Pascal, reciprocal contraction for the ordinary discriminant, and the product over all cluster nodes for the resultant. Its reported per-base times were approximately `0.17`, `0.28`, and `0.84` seconds in this environment.

A separately written alternate-route program constructed Gaussian coefficients multiplicatively and computed the **direct ordinary discriminant and direct resultant**. It agreed with every saved coefficient of both factors and their product. It also verified the complete identity (5) and Gauss's alternating identity. Per-base times were approximately `1.16`, `1.19`, and `30.27` seconds. These are implementation timings for tiny fixed cases, not asymptotic complexity claims or performance comparisons with an FFT.

Further exact controls check (7)–(9), including the single-resultant sign and exact division defining `C_d^*`. An initial check reproduced a known odd-degree argument-swap sign problem in the installed SymPy resultant interface; the final check uses an explicit multiplication-matrix determinant and a reduced-degree resultant cross-check. `RESULTANT_SIGN_CONTROL.md` records the minimal countercontrol and why the previously saved contents and factor arrays are unaffected. A formal calculation with symbolic index `j` verifies all lower coefficients vanish and the leading coefficient in (17) for `r=1,…,5`. These controls guard the derivations; the theorem is proved symbolically, not inferred from them.

An exploratory characteristic-two calculation in degrees `2,4,…,14` found noncyclotomic factors in contracted discriminants, so a tempting claim that all such discriminants factor solely into short-order cyclotomic factors was not adopted. It is recorded as an unsuccessful route, not evidence for the global content conjecture. No target-order range was scanned, no target-order modular squarefreeness census was repeated, and no larger bases were added.

### New finite-base family corollaries

Applying the accepted criterion to the three exact contents proves:

- `H_((17p−1)/2)(z;ζ_(17p))` is squarefree for every odd prime `p≠17`.
- `H_((21p−1)/2)(z;ζ_(21p))` is squarefree for every odd prime `p∉{3,7}`.
- `H_((25p−1)/2)(z;ζ_(25p))` is squarefree for every odd prime `p≠5`.

For `p≡1 (mod4)`, the accepted resonance theorem and existing restricted common-cycle scalar-resolvent model therefore give its half-degree minimum `(N−1)/2`. This remains a statement in that previously defined model. It does not lower the arithmetic cost of applying the entire transform and does not establish a sub-`N log N` FFT.

## 8. Stopping boundary

The promised general prime-support statement has not been proved or refuted. The precise remaining lemma is the invertibility assertion after (10). A proof must control the remaining ordinary-discriminant and residual-cluster factors over a complete primitive-root orbit; positivity in one complex embedding and one-good-factor finite examples do not do that.

The new proved result is the exact conditional ramified valuation (A), including its all-prime specialization (B). The structural reduction (9) identifies a smaller and cleaner unramified obstruction. Further base enumeration would add corollaries but would not close the missing proof, so this pass stops rather than expanding a census.
