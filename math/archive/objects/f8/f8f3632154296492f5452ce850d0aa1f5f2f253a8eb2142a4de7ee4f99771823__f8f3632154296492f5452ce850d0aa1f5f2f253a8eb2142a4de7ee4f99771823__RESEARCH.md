# Frequency-shift median Fourier sketch

**Checkpoint:** 9 October 2026  
**Status:** Algebraically verified randomized full-output approximation for factorizable lengths, with an exact per-frequency variance identity, a rigorous median-amplified all-output bound, a verified balanced-bucket generalization, reproducible prototypes, and a charged arithmetic-operation count.  
**Not claimed:** an exact FFT, machine-precision replacement, all-length theorem, bit-complexity result, or established novel theorem.

## 1. Why this branch was opened

The preceding `fft_wordram_shrinking_error_v4` checkpoint produced an approximate full-output transform by sampling individual time coordinates and evaluating their phasor trajectories with packed lookup tables. Its error guarantee shrank with `N`, but its natural scale remained tied to

\[
L_{\rm box}(x)=\sum_j(|\Re x_j|+|\Im x_j|).
\]

That scale can be much larger than a cancellation-heavy Fourier coefficient. The v4 lower-bound calculation also showed that an unbiased **one-coordinate-at-a-time** estimator has variance at least `||x||_1^2/N` on flat inputs. Therefore another coordinate sampler was not a credible route to coefficient-scale accuracy.

The present branch changes the measurement itself. It combines many coordinates before evaluation, while choosing the mixing so that the result is still evaluable by two separable families of smaller FFTs.

The key idea is:

> Randomly shift the `K`-frequency spectrum of each of `M` interleaved columns, add the shifted columns with random fourth-root phases, and then decode every one of the `N=KM` Fourier outputs with `K` short length-`M` FFTs.

This is structurally CountSketch-like in the intermediate frequency coordinates, but the hash is implemented by time-domain modulation and remains compatible with a Cooley–Tukey factorization.

## 2. Model and notation

Assume

\[
N=KM,
\]

and reshape the input as

\[
x_{r,t}=x_{rM+t},\qquad 0\le r<K,\quad 0\le t<M.
\]

Use the unitary DFT convention

\[
(U_Nx)_m=\frac1{\sqrt N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}x_n e^{-2\pi i nm/N}.
\]

Every output index has a unique decomposition

\[
m=p+Kv,
\qquad 0\le p<K,\quad 0\le v<M.
\]

Define the length-`K` column transforms

\[
A_t[p]
=\frac1{\sqrt K}\sum_{r=0}^{K-1}x_{r,t}e^{-2\pi i rp/K}.
\]

A direct Cooley–Tukey rearrangement gives

\[
(U_Nx)_{p+Kv}
=\frac1{\sqrt M}\sum_{t=0}^{M-1}
A_t[p]e^{-2\pi i(p+Kv)t/N}.
\tag{2.1}
\]

The research prototype and proofs below count complex arithmetic operations. Roots of unity are treated as available constants, as in the ordinary algebraic FFT model. Finite-precision roundoff and bit complexity are not yet included in the theorem.

## 3. One frequency-shift sketch

Independently draw, for every column `t`,

\[
q_t\sim \operatorname{Unif}(\mathbb Z_K)
\]

and

\[
\xi_t\sim\operatorname{Unif}\{1,-1,i,-i\}.
\]

The fourth-root choice is useful because

\[
\mathbb E\xi_t=0,
\qquad
\mathbb E\xi_t^2=0,
\qquad
|\xi_t|=1.
\]

### 3.1 Sketch construction

Form

\[
z_r
=\sum_{t=0}^{M-1}
\overline{\xi_t}\,e^{-2\pi i r q_t/K}x_{r,t}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Then compute one unitary length-`K` FFT

\[
Z=U_Kz.
\]

By the modulation/shift identity,

\[
Z[s]
=\sum_{u=0}^{M-1}\overline{\xi_u}\,A_u[s+q_u].
\tag{3.2}
\]

All `K`-indices in this document are taken modulo `K`.

### 3.2 Full-output decoder

For every `p in Z_K`, form the length-`M` vector

\[
b_t^{(p)}
=\xi_t Z[p-q_t]e^{-2\pi i pt/N}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Compute its unitary length-`M` FFT. Place its `v`-th value at output index `p+Kv`:

\[
\widehat X_{p+Kv}
=\frac1{\sqrt M}\sum_{t=0}^{M-1}
\xi_t Z[p-q_t]e^{-2\pi i(p+Kv)t/N}.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Thus one randomized estimate explicitly returns all `N` frequencies.

---

## 4. Exact expansion and unbiasedness

Substituting (3.2) into (3.4) gives

\[
\widehat X_{p+Kv}
=\frac1{\sqrt M}
\sum_{t,u}
\xi_t\overline{\xi_u}
A_u[p+q_u-q_t]
 e^{-2\pi i(p+Kv)t/N}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

The diagonal `u=t` is exactly (2.1), because `q_u-q_t=0` and `xi_t conjugate(xi_t)=1`. Therefore

\[
\widehat X_m=X_m+E_m,
\]

where

\[
E_m
=\frac1{\sqrt M}
\sum_{t\ne u}
 e^{-2\pi i mt/N}
\xi_t\overline{\xi_u}
A_u[p+q_u-q_t],
\qquad m=p+Kv.
\tag{4.2}
\]

For every off-diagonal pair `t != u`,

\[
\mathbb E_{\xi}[\xi_t\overline{\xi_u}]=0.
\]

Hence the estimator is unbiased, even conditional on all frequency shifts:

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E[\widehat X_m\mid(q_t)_t]=X_m
}
\qquad\text{and therefore}\qquad
\boxed{
\mathbb E\widehat X_m=X_m.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

---

## 5. Exact per-output variance

This is the strongest clean result of the branch.

Take the squared magnitude of (4.2). Because the `xi_t` are independent fourth roots, all cross terms vanish. The only surviving pairing is the identical ordered pair `(t,u)`. The reversed pairing `(u,t)` contains `E[xi_t^2] E[conjugate(xi_u)^2]=0`.

Therefore, conditional on the shifts,

\[
\mathbb E_\xi|E_m|^2
=\frac1M\sum_{t\ne u}
\left|A_u[p+q_u-q_t]\right|^2.
\tag{5.1}
\]

For fixed `t != u`, the difference `q_u-q_t` is uniform on `Z_K`. Consequently

\[
\mathbb E_q
\left|A_u[p+q_u-q_t]\right|^2
=\frac1K\sum_{s=0}^{K-1}|A_u[s]|^2
=\frac1K\sum_{r=0}^{K-1}|x_{r,u}|^2,
\tag{5.2}
\]

where the last equality is Parseval for the unitary length-`K` DFT.

Each `u` occurs with `M-1` choices of `t`. Summing (5.1) and (5.2) yields, for **every** frequency `m`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E|\widehat X_m-X_m|^2
=\frac{M-1}{N}\,\|x\|_2^2.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

Important consequences:

1. The guarantee is **energy-aware**, depending on `||x||_2` rather than `L_box(x)` or `||x||_1`.
2. It holds for arbitrary fresh dense complex inputs.
3. It is the same at every output frequency.
4. The normalized one-sketch RMS error is exactly

   \[
   \frac{\sqrt{\mathbb E|E_m|^2}}{\|x\|_2/\sqrt N}
   =\sqrt{M-1}.
   \]

The sketch is not accurate enough by itself for a simultaneous maximum-error guarantee. Independent repetition and a median provide the amplification.

## 6. Coordinatewise median amplification

Take

\[
R=2t-1
\]

independent sketches. For each output coordinate, take the median of the `R` real parts and separately the median of the `R` imaginary parts. Denote the resulting complex vector by `X_tilde`.

Let

\[
C_t={2t-1\choose t},
\qquad
\sigma^2=\frac{M-1}{N}\|x\|_2^2.
\]

A general median-moment theorem states that if `Y` is the median of `2t-1` iid real variables `Y_j`, then

\[
\mathbb E|Y-\mathbb EY_1|^{2t}
\le C_t\left(\mathbb E|Y_1-\mathbb EY_1|^2\right)^t.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Apply (6.1) separately to the real and imaginary parts. Each component has second moment at most `sigma^2`. Markov's inequality and a union bound over `2N` real components give the following theorem.

### Theorem 6.1 — all-output bound

For every `0 < delta < 1`, with probability at least `1-delta`,

\[
\boxed{
\|\widetilde X-U_Nx\|_\infty
\le
\sqrt2
\left(\frac{2NC_t}{\delta}\right)^{1/(2t)}
\sqrt{\frac{M-1}{N}}\,\|x\|_2.
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

Since `C_t <= 2^(2t-1)`, a looser asymptotic form is

\[
\|\widetilde X-U_Nx\|_\infty
=
O\!\left(
\sqrt M\,
N^{-1/2+1/(2t)}
\delta^{-1/(2t)}
\|x\|_2
\right).
\tag{6.3}
\]

For polynomial failure `delta=N^{-a}` and fixed `t`,

\[
\|\widetilde X-U_Nx\|_\infty
=
O\!\left(
\sqrt M\,
N^{-1/2+(1+a)/(2t)}
\|x\|_2
\right).
\tag{6.4}
\]

Thus the energy-relative maximum error vanishes polynomially whenever

\[
t>1+a.
\]

Examples for constant failure probability:

| independent sketches | `t` | proved energy-relative exponent |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 3 | 2 | `O(sqrt(M) N^(-1/4))` |
| 5 | 3 | `O(sqrt(M) N^(-1/3))` |
| 9 | 5 | `O(sqrt(M) N^(-2/5))` |

These are worst-case simultaneous bounds. The measured medians are much smaller, but the implementation results are not substituted for the proof.

## 7. Fully charged arithmetic complexity

For one sketch:

1. Build `z`: `O(N)`.
2. Compute one length-`K=N/M` FFT: `O(K log K)`.
3. Build all decoder vectors: `O(N)`.
4. Compute `K` length-`M` FFTs: `O(K M log M)=O(N log M)`.
5. Write all `N` outputs: `O(N)`.

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
T_1(N,M)
=O\!\left(
N\log M+\frac{N}{M}\log\frac NM+N
\right).
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

For `R=2t-1` independent sketches and the coordinatewise median,

\[
\boxed{
T_R(N,M)
=O\!\left(
R\left[N\log M+\frac{N}{M}\log\frac NM+N\right]
\right).
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

The `O(RN)` median/output work is already included. For constant `R`, memory is `O(N)` if the rows are processed in blocks or retained as a constant-size stack.

### 7.1 Balanced-bucket extension

The most obvious next modification is to partition the `M` columns into `d` equal deterministic buckets. Let

\[
c=M/d.
\]

Construct one vector `z^(b)` per bucket, so a column collides only with the other `c-1` columns in its own bucket. Compute `d` length-`K` FFTs, and in the short decoder use the transformed bucket belonging to column `t`. The same expansion and fourth-root cancellation proof gives

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E|\widehat X_m-X_m|^2
=\frac{c-1}{N}\|x\|_2^2.
}
\tag{7.3}
\]

The charged one-row cost becomes

\[
\boxed{
T_{1,\mathrm{bucket}}
=O\!\left(
N\log M+\frac{N}{c}\log\frac NM+N
\right).
}
\tag{7.4}
\]

The prototype verifies (7.3) for bucket counts `1,2,4,8,16`; with `d=M` we have `c=1`, zero variance, and the construction becomes an exact Cooley–Tukey transform.

This extension is useful as an implementation/factorization knob, but it does **not** create a new asymptotic frontier. For a fixed collision size `c>1`, the normalized cost term

\[
\log M+\frac{\log(N/M)}c
\]

is increasing in `M` for `M>=c`. Its minimum is attained at `M=c`, which is exactly the original one-bucket construction with a smaller short-transform length. Thus balanced buckets recover the same time–variance tradeoff already available by varying `M`; they do not beat it. This closes the most immediate extension rather than leaving it as an uncharged conjecture.

### When is this sub-`N log N`?

For constant `R`, it is enough that

\[
M\to\infty
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\log M=o(\log N).
\tag{7.5}
\]

Then both `log M / log N` and `1/M` vanish, so

\[
T_R(N,M)=o(N\log N).
\]

This is not an exact transform; the speedup is purchased by randomized approximation.

## 8. Useful parameter regimes

The theorem is stated for any factorization `N=KM`. For the cleanest infinite family take `N=2^L` and choose `M` as a power of two.

### 8.1 Fastest point of this evaluator

The one-sketch cost proxy

\[
\log M+\frac{\log(N/M)}M
\]

is minimized near

\[
M=\Theta(\log N).
\]

Then, for constant rows,

\[
\boxed{
T=O(N\log\log N)
}
\tag{8.1}
\]

and the all-output theorem gives

\[
\boxed{
\|\widetilde X-U_Nx\|_\infty
=O\!\left(
\sqrt{\log N}
N^{-1/2+1/(2t)}
\delta^{-1/(2t)}
\|x\|_2
\right).
}
\tag{8.2}
\]

With five sketches (`t=3`) and constant failure probability, this is

\[
O\!\left(\sqrt{\log N}\,N^{-1/3}\|x\|_2\right).
\]

### 8.2 Slower but more accurate sub-FFT point

Choose

\[
M=\Theta(\log\log N).
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
T=O\!\left(\frac{N\log N}{\log\log N}
ight)
=o(N\log N)
}
\tag{8.3}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\|\widetilde X-U_Nx\|_\infty
=O\!\left(
\sqrt{\log\log N}
N^{-1/2+1/(2t)}
\delta^{-1/(2t)}
\|x\|_2
\right).
}
\tag{8.4}
\]

More generally, any arbitrarily slowly growing valid divisor `M` yields a correspondingly slow asymptotic speedup and a smaller variance factor.

### 8.3 Growing number of rows

At the fastest point `M=Theta(log N)`, let

\[
t=\frac{\log N}{(\log\log N)^2}.
\]

Then

\[
T=O\!\left(\frac{N\log N}{\log\log N}\right)
=o(N\log N),
\]

while (6.2) becomes

\[
\|\widetilde X-U_Nx\|_\infty
\le
\frac{\|x\|_2}{\sqrt N}\,N^{o(1)}.
\tag{8.5}
\]

This is near the natural coefficient-energy scale up to a subpolynomial factor. It is **not** a machine-precision guarantee, and the present median-moment proof does not reach a merely polylogarithmic factor while retaining a strict little-`o(N log N)` runtime.

That last sentence is a boundary of the current proof/algorithm, not a general lower bound.

### 9.1 Independent algebra check

The fast separable evaluator was compared against a second implementation that explicitly materializes all column transforms `A_t[p]` and evaluates the double sum (4.1).

Across four different factorizations, the maximum discrepancy was

\[
2.1321\times10^{-14}.
\]

This checks the decoder identity independently of the fast indexing path.

### 9.2 Variance identity

Twenty-four base-sketch experiments covered:

- `M in {8,16,32}`;
- flat signals;
- random phases;
- complex Gaussian data;
- a tone concentrated in one local column;
- repeated local patterns;
- one time-domain spike.

The ratio

\[
\frac{\text{empirical per-bin MSE}}
{((M-1)/N)\|x\|_2^2}
\]

ranged from

\[
0.97857\quad\text{to}\quad1.03175,
\]

with median exactly `1.0` to reported precision.

A further thirty balanced-bucket checks used all divisors `d in {1,2,4,8,16}` of `M=16` across the same signal families. They confirmed the replacement of `M-1` by the collision factor `c-1`. At `d=M` (`c=1`), the measured error was numerical roundoff only, as predicted by the exact zero-variance limit.

### 9.3 Median experiments

Seventy-two checks used `1,3,5,9` independent rows. As expected, one-sketch normalized RMS closely tracks `sqrt(M-1)`. More rows reduce typical RMS and maximum error substantially. On the deliberately pathological local-spike/tone signal at `N=2048, M=16`, the median normalized RMS values were approximately:

| rows | normalized RMS median |
|---:|---:|
| 1 | 3.8730 |
| 3 | 1.1838 |
| 5 | 0.4846 |
| 9 | `2.9e-14` in the median trial |

The near-zero nine-row result is an empirical collision-elimination event for that particular structured signal, not a universal exactness claim.

Seven automated tests pass:

## 10. What this result does and does not establish

### Established inside the stated model

- All `N` approximate outputs are explicitly produced.
- The input may be arbitrary, fresh, dense, and complex.
- One row is unbiased.
- Every bin has the exact variance `(M-1)||x||_2^2/N`.
- Independent coordinatewise medians give the rigorous all-output probability bound (6.2).
- Every construction, FFT, gather, median, and output write is charged in (7.2).
- For factorable lengths and suitable `M`, the arithmetic count is `o(N log N)`.

### Not established

- Exact DFT computation below `N log N`.
- Relative error for every Fourier coefficient.
- Ordinary FFT-level floating-point accuracy.
- A uniform result for prime or arbitrary lengths without a new reduction analysis.
- Fixed-precision Word-RAM or bit complexity.
- Numerical-stability bounds for the nested FFT implementation.
- Novelty in the research literature.
- A wall-clock win over FFTW, MKL, pocketfft, cuFFT, or other optimized libraries.

For a flat cancellation-heavy input with `||x||_2=Theta(sqrt(N))`, (8.2) may still permit absolute error larger than an individual `O(1)` coefficient. The theorem is cancellation-aware in the global energy sense, but it is not yet a coefficientwise relative-error result.

## 11. Prior-art boundary

The ingredients have substantial prior art:

1. **CountSketch and median amplification.** CountSketch uses random hashing/signs, and the general high-moment inequality for medians used in Section 6 appears in Larsen, Pagh, and Tětek, *CountSketches, Feature Hashing and the Median of Three*, arXiv:2102.02193.
2. **Cooley–Tukey factorization and modulation/frequency shifts.** These are classical Fourier identities.
3. **Sparse Fourier transforms.** Random shifts, hashing, and aliasing are standard tools in sparse Fourier recovery. Those algorithms typically seek a sparse or best-`s`-term spectrum and do not solve the same full-`N`-output dense approximation problem.
4. **FFT lower bounds.** Known `Omega(N log N)` results depend on restricted linear models such as unitary two-by-two gates or uniformly well-conditioned computation. They do not prohibit this randomized approximate algorithm.

A targeted arXiv/web search did not reveal this exact combination—column-dependent random frequency shifts, fourth-root mixing, a full separable decoder, the exact variance identity (5.3), and full-output median amplification. That search is not exhaustive and is not evidence sufficient for a novelty claim.

Primary references inspected:

- K. G. Larsen, R. Pagh, J. Tětek, *CountSketches, Feature Hashing and the Median of Three*, arXiv:2102.02193.
- N. Ailon, *An Omega((n log n)/R) Lower Bound for Fourier Transform Computation in the R-Well Conditioned Model*, arXiv:1403.1307.
- S. Merhi, R. Zhang, M. A. Iwen, A. Christlieb, *A New Class of Fully Discrete Sparse Fourier Transforms*, arXiv:1706.02740.

## 12. Exact next research step

The remaining frontier is no longer “find any cancellation-aware sketch.” This branch has one.

The balanced-bucket extension has now been derived and experimentally checked. It lowers the variance factor from `M-1` to `c-1`, but after charging all `d=M/c` long FFTs it is asymptotically equivalent to choosing the original short-transform length `M=c`. It is therefore closed as a separate breakthrough path.

The next precise questions are:

1. **Sharper distribution analysis.** The theorem uses only one-row variance plus a general median moment inequality. Determine the exact or bounded higher moments of the special fourth-root quadratic chaos, including its collision atoms. This is the clearest route to reducing the gap between measured median error and (6.2).
2. **Alternative robust aggregator.** Compare coordinatewise median, trimmed mean, median-of-means, and collision-aware rejection without assuming a tail bound that has not been proved. Any aggregator must retain a charged `o(N log N)` runtime.
3. **Finite-precision stability.** Charge phase generation, rounding, and nested FFT errors in a `Theta(log N)`-bit Word-RAM or floating-point model.
4. **Arbitrary lengths.** Determine whether Bluestein/Rader/mixed-radix reductions preserve the approximation theorem and strict sub-`N log N` cost.
5. **Native benchmark.** Implement the batched short FFTs in C++ with FFTW/pocketfft and compare identical output/error targets against a full optimized FFT.
6. **Independent prior-art audit.** Search sketching, randomized numerical linear algebra, approximate DFT, coded FFT, and sparse FFT literature before any novelty statement.

The strongest immediate mathematical target is item 1: prove distribution-specific median moments rather than relying only on the generic second-moment theorem.
