# Two-Column Fourier Phase Pencil and the Four-Green Separation Witness

**October 10, 2026 — exact-arithmetic research checkpoint**  
**Status:** Proven elementary reduction, an independently checked exact finite-field certificate, and a characteristic-zero existence consequence. **Not a new all-input FFT executor; no global novelty claim.**

## 1. Motivation and research provenance

The research target is a uniform exact DFT on **fresh arbitrary inputs** with total sequential cost `o(N log N)`, ideally `O(N)`. The most specific surviving architecture in the project is a product of two sums of independently ordered, dressed cyclic Green inverses:

\[
F_N \stackrel{?}{=} L\,(G_1+G_2)\,D\,(G_3+G_4),
\]

where `L` and `D` are invertible diagonals and each `G_j` has an `O(N)`-work evaluation recurrence (when its coefficients are provided). The intermediate diagonal can be absorbed into the individual stage-branch endpoint gauges and does not add a qualitatively new class of circuits. Every successful exact factorization must include a **proof of generating the cycles, weights, gauges, and roots in O(N) work**. None exists in these archives.

**Important prior work correction.** The user's **Phase 18** already derives the necessary monomial similarity condition using `A^{-1} Δ A` and `B S B^{-1}` for the full Weyl covariance. **Phase 19** already implements simultaneous diagonal/monomial similarity using a graph cocycle for a pair of Harper/Weyl-related operators, and rejects concrete Green sums. Classical diagonal similarity algorithms go back to Engel–Schneider, Saunders–Schneider and others. **Neither the Fourier covariance identity nor the general graph-potential algorithm is claimed novel.**

This checkpoint's *additional* content is:

1. The **two-column Fourier phase pencil** that eliminates every invertible middle diagonal for fixed stages with a single `N x N` determinant, without computing stage inverses.
2. An **exact four-Green witness that is full spark in every square submatrix** yet fails this pencil for **every** middle diagonal.
3. An algebraic lifting argument showing the finite-field witness certifies the existence of a corresponding **complex four-Green full-spark but phase-incompatible network**; and a genericity corollary for its fixed wiring.
4. An explicit delineation of what matroid-intersection rank optimization can and cannot reveal once the middle diagonal is free.

## 2. Fourier normalization and the two-column theorem

Work over a field `K` containing a primitive `N`th root of unity `ω` and of characteristic not dividing `N`. Use the **unnormalized** DFT:

\[
F_{k,j}=\omega^{kj}, \qquad 0\le k,j<N.
\]

Let `S e_j=e_{j+1 (mod N)}` and `Δ=diag(1,ω,...,ω^{N-1})`. Then

\[
F S=\Delta F, \qquad F e_0=\mathbf 1.
\]

For any matrix `M`, these two identities imply `M=F`: repeated multiplication by `S` generates every basis vector from `e_0`.

For **invertible** `A,B in K^{N x N}`, put `b_j=B e_j` and define the **Fourier phase pencil**

\[
K_j(A,B)= A\operatorname{diag}(b_{j+1})-\Delta A\operatorname{diag}(b_j), \quad j\in\mathbb Z_N.
\]

### Theorem 1 — necessary and sufficient fixed-stage gauge compatibility

The following are equivalent:

* (i) There are invertible diagonals `L,D` such that `L A D B=F`.
* (ii) There is a vector `d` with **all `d_i ≠0`** such that `K_j(A,B)d=0` for **all** `j mod N`.
* (iii) There is an invertible diagonal `D=diag(d)` solving `D X=Y D`, where `X=B S B^{-1}` and `Y=A^{-1} Δ A`.

If any holds, compute `c=A D B e_0`. Because `A D B` is invertible, every `c_k` is nonzero, and the exact output normalizer is `L=diag(c_k^{-1})`.

**Proof.** Set `M=A D B`. The `j`th column of `MS-ΔM` equals

\[
A\operatorname{diag}(d)b_{j+1}-\Delta A\operatorname{diag}(d)b_j
=K_j(A,B)d.
\]

Thus (ii) is equivalent to `MS=ΔM`. This forces row `k` of `M` to be `c_k(1,ω^k,...,ω^{k(N-1)})`. Invertibility forces `c_k ≠0`, hence normalizing yields (i). Conversely, (i) gives `MS=ΔM`, since `L` commutes with `Δ`, so (ii) holds. Multiplying `A D B S = Δ A D B` on left/right by `A^{-1},B^{-1}` yields `D(B S B^{-1})=(A^{-1} Δ A)D`, proving (iii). □

### Corollary 1 — one determinant rules out all middle gauges

In particular, if

\[
\boxed{\det K_0(A,B)\neq 0,}
\]

then **no** middle invertible diagonal `D`, and **no** output invertible diagonal `L`, make `LADB` an exact Fourier matrix. This conclusion holds for the **fixed** outer stages `A,B`, not for all choices of their Green-cycle parameters.

This polynomial is the project's **two-column rejection gate**. Unlike a dense Fourier-target difference, it uses only the first two columns of `B`, one matrix `A`, and the known Fourier phase diagonal `Δ`; there is no FFT applied to an input during construction.

`det K_0=0` is **not sufficient**: a kernel vector may contain zeros or fail another `K_j`.

### Corollary 2 — algebraic nongenericity at any certified wiring

When the four Green branch permutations are fixed and the entries are rational functions of branch weights and nonzero endpoint gauges, `det K_0` is rational in those parameters and `ω`. If it is nonzero at **one** admissible parameter specialization, its numerator is a **nonzero polynomial**. Therefore exact Fourier-compatible weights/gauges in that wiring can lie only in a **proper algebraic subset** of the admissible parameter space. For any continuous distribution with a density in the complex parameter coordinates, that success set has measure zero.

This is a theorem of **nongenericity for a specified wiring**, not a universal impossibility theorem: parameter values on the exceptional algebraic locus could still support an FFT.

## 3. The complete graph-potential test (existing Phase 18/19 methodology)

The fixed-stage equation `D X=Y D` is entrywise `d_i X_ij=Y_ij d_j`.

Necessary and sufficient for invertible diagonal `D`:

1. `X_ij=0` exactly when `Y_ij=0`, for every `(i,j)`.
2. For every common nonzero entry, the ratio `Y_ij/X_ij=d_i/d_j` is an integrable multiplicative graph cocycle: its ratios multiply consistently around **every closed walk**, allowing backwards traversal by reciprocal ratios.

A breadth-first spanning forest propagates nonzero vertex potentials; every extra edge verifies a cycle condition. Thus the graph stage takes `O(N+E)` **after the matrices `X,Y` have been obtained**. Dense inversions to obtain them may cost `O(N^3)`. This is a **research/synthesis gate, not an O(N) Fourier executor**. For dense generic matrices `E=Θ(N²)`.

For a complete nonzero pattern, a root vertex determines all `d_i`; one verifies all remaining `N²` equations. Simpler necessary polynomial gates include `X_ii=Y_ii` and the cleared-denominator cocycle identity

\[
Y_{i0}X_{ij}X_{j0}=X_{i0}Y_{ij}Y_{j0}
\]

whenever the participating entries are nonzero.

## 4. Exact exhaustive separation witness at N=8

The supplied primary program constructs four **independently endpoint-permuted and diagonally gauged** cyclic Green inverses over `GF(65537)` and two invertible aggregate sums `A=G_1+G_2`, `B=G_3+G_4`. Each branch uses a genuine Hamiltonian cycle, with nonzero weights and cycle product not equal to 1.

For a saved invertible interface gauge `D`, the candidate `M=A D B` satisfies:

* **All 12,869 nonempty square submatrices of M are nonsingular** (every square minor at `N=8`): actual matrix-level **full spark**, stronger than simply passing all network min-cuts.
* **All 225 Fourier coset rank profiles** indexed by every divisor of `N=8` and every pair of offsets are at least the target Fourier rank.
* An explicit `8×8` two-column pencil `K_0` has

\[
\boxed{\det K_0 \equiv 55687 \pmod{65537}\neq0.}
\]

Therefore **no** invertible middle diagonal can make the *fixed* `A,B` stages satisfy even the first two Fourier column relations. A separate primary implementation also has an `8×8` full-rank covariance coefficient system.

**Independent recheck:** `independent_certificate.py` regenerates the four Green inverses using the **finite geometric-series formula** `G=(I+T+...+T^{N-1})/(1-∏w)` rather than Gaussian inverse construction and calculates `det K_0` using the **Leibniz permutation formula** rather than Gaussian elimination. It returns the identical determinant `55687`.

**Characteristic-zero lift:** The saved weights and endpoint gauges are integer representatives modulo `65537`. The invertibility denominators are nonzero modulo the prime, so the corresponding rational four-Green operators are defined. The primitive 8th root in `GF(65537)` is `4096`, a valid specialization of an abstract cyclotomic root `ζ_8`. If any of the saved minors or `det K_0` were zero over `Q(ζ_8)`, reduction modulo this prime ideal would be zero. Their reductions are not zero. Hence those specific algebraic nonvanishing statements also hold in characteristic zero. In particular, a **complex four-Green network** with these lifted parameters is full spark and has a nonzero two-column phase determinant. Moreover, because every minor is a polynomial in the middle gauge, a Zariski-open dense set of middle gauges remains full spark while **none** can satisfy Fourier covariance for these stages.

The witness does **not** prove four-Green products fail for all parameter choices or all `N`.

## 5. Why optimizing more rank cuts cannot finish Fourier phase correctness

Let `A` be any fixed `m×N` matrix, `B` a fixed `N×r` matrix, and `D=diag(t_1,...,t_N)` with independent indeterminates. For infinite fields,

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}_{K(t)} (A D B)
 =\min_{S\subseteq[N]}\{\operatorname{rank}A[:,S^c]+\operatorname{rank}B[S,:]\}.}
\]

**Proof.** Cauchy–Binet expands each `k×k` minor of `A D B` as a sum indexed by `k`-subsets `I` of `N`, with pairwise distinct squarefree monomials `∏_{i∈I}t_i`. Such a minor is not the zero polynomial iff a common `I` is simultaneously a column-independent subset of `A` and a row-independent subset of `B`. Maximizing `k` is linear matroid intersection. Edmonds' min-max theorem gives exactly the displayed minimum. □

For **any finite family** of row/column rectangles, the set of `D` simultaneously attaining all these generic ranks is nonempty Zariski-open dense, since it is an intersection of finitely many nonempty Zariski-open sets. Over sufficiently large finite fields a random diagonal also succeeds with high probability; the bound depends on the number and degree of the chosen minors.

This is an application of **classical matroid intersection**, not a newly discovered theorem. Its significance here is a rigorous **stop rule**: after all candidate interface rank constraints have been satisfied, further random rank/profile searches cannot establish *Fourier-specific phase compatibility*. The saved full-spark counterexample makes that distinction concrete.

## 6. Additional exact tests and controls

`stress_sizes.py` constructs 40 independently sampled four-Green stage pairs: 10 each at `N=4,8,16,32`. The two-column phase pencil has full rank in all 40. This shows typical failure for these samples; it is **not a probability bound for all random wirings**.

Positive controls include:

* `A=I,B=F` (direct full Fourier factor provided intentionally as a control, **not a fast construction**),
* a hidden arbitrary change of basis,
* a hidden nontrivial middle gauge and nontrivial output diagonal, successfully recovered and normalized.

For 8 chosen row/column rectangles at `N=8`, an exhaustive scan over all `2^8` interface subsets gives the matroid rank bound, equal to the rank of the saved diagonal-twisted product. A separate single-cycle example has a strict rank bottleneck `β=2` (versus a 4×4 block).

## 7. Exact-FFT synthesis target and success criteria

The useful search problem after this result is *not* to pick a random middle diagonal. It is to find **all-length, efficiently describable Green-stage families** `A_N,B_N` such that:

1. `det K_0(A_N,B_N)=0` **as an exact cyclotomic identity** and `ker K_0` includes a full-support vector;
2. the **same** full-support `d` lies in the kernels of every `K_j(A_N,B_N)` — equivalently, the full graph-potential conditions hold;
3. the output normalization `c=A_N diag(d)B_N e_0` is nonzero coordinatewise;
4. cycle permutations, Green coefficients, the gauge `d`, and normalization `c` are all **generated in O(N)** sequential work, including scalar/root preparation;
5. applying the two Green sums, interface gauge and final normalization indeed costs `O(N)` under the stated exact-arithmetic model, with separate stability/bit-cost analysis.

A successful construction meeting all five would give an exact `O(N)` Fourier transform in that model. At present **there is no such construction**.
