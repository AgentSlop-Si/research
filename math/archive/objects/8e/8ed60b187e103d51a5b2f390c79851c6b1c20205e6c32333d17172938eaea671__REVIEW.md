## 1. Growing-radix bank

With M=bm, h=d+cm and zeta=eta^m, q_h=q_d zeta^(-c). A negative-sign c transform gives

    Chat[t,d,ell]=sum_j q_d^(tj) Bhat[j,d,ell+tj mod b].

Splitting t=r+ba, j=s+bv gives tj=rs+b(sa+rv)+b^2av and tj=rs mod b. Therefore the inner length-m geometric transform, its input/output diagonal factors, and the channel-routing index in the report are correct.

The smaller node q_d^(b^2) equals Omega(eta')^(-bd), where Omega=omega^(b^2) has order m^2 and eta'=eta^b has order m. Its exponent relative to Omega is 1-Md, coprime to m, so it indeed has order m^2. As d varies modulo m, exactly m/gcd(b,m) smaller nodes occur.

There are m b^3 inner applications of length m, giving m^2b^3=Nb expanded scalar inputs. Reusing a coefficient table does not identify these distinct input vectors. Shared diagonal tables cost O(N), and streaming keeps O(N) words. The full stated schedule costs O(N[log b+b(1+log m)]). Its comparison follows from the exact nonnegative difference

    log b+b(1+log m)-(1+log M)=(b-1)(1+log m).

This is a count for the specified expanded-transform schedule, not a lower bound on a new collective evaluator of its correlated vectors. The raw row/output Fourier banks also remain charged: inserting V^(-1) followed by V between them cannot improve a circuit that continues to execute both ordinary banks separately.

Independent controls cover 49 complete divisor-radix banks over M=2,3,4,5,6,8,9,10,12,16,20,24. All 10,319 output entries match direct geometric multiplication. The independently counted expanded volume is 67,883 entries. Every smaller-node identity and distinct-node count passes.

## 2. Grid primitive and sparse fraction

The zero-boundary grid denominator U=I-D_a L_t-D_b L_u is unit lower triangular for every edge mask. Its inverse has the exact forward-scan recurrence in the report. There are 2M(M-1) active edges, so given coefficients and routes its complete application is O(N). Supplying a scalar-coefficient formula or a permutation is not free parameter synthesis. Products alone retain the inherited inverse-support bound 3^k; sums require a different analysis.

For invertible component denominators,

    H_i=alpha_i U_i^(-1)+beta_i V_i^(-1)
       =U_i^(-1)W_i V_i^(-1),
    W_i=alpha_i V_i+beta_i U_i.

No commutativity is assumed. In the target F=H_2 C H_1 with invertible monomial C,

    U_2 F V_1=W_2 K^(-1)W_1,
    K=U_1 C^(-1)V_2.

The order is correct: K^(-1)=V_2^(-1) C U_1^(-1). If U_i,V_i have at most three entries in every row/column, K has at most nine and W_i at most six. The block-rank condition follows from the Schur complement at K, which is always invertible. Singular W_i or zero scalar coefficients have not been discarded in this identity. An actual invertible Fourier target would force H_i and W_i invertible. The fraction representation presumes invertible individual routed terms; zero endpoint diagonal entries in a broader singular-term architecture would require another parameterization.

The upper parameter count 16N-8M for four independently routed two-direction grid terms with endpoint diagonals is correct. Its N=16 value 224<256 restricts generic image dimension. It does not decide whether the specific Fourier point lies in the image. The fixed-pencil exclusion below is much narrower than that broad family.

## 3. Common-anchor proof with exact routing

For dyadic M>=2, N=M^2, define G=G(2,3) and J=R G(5,7) R^(-1), with R the log2(N)-bit reversal. Both have row 0 equal to e_0^T and final column equal to e_(N-1). Thus H(alpha,beta)=alpha G+beta J has those anchors multiplied by alpha+beta.

In F=H_2 T H_1 with any invertible middle monomial T, a zero anchor scalar makes the corresponding H singular and is impossible. Otherwise the first row selects exactly one row of H_1. Because the Fourier entry in its final column is nonzero and H_1's final column has support only at its final row, that selected row must be the final row. Fourier's first row then forces it to be a nonzero constant row. This support argument avoids any ambiguity about whether a permutation map is written forward or backward.

The grid Green entry from (t',u') to (t,u) is zero unless both coordinate differences are nonnegative; otherwise it equals

    binomial((t-t')+(u-u'),t-t') a^(t-t') b^(u-u').

This direct path count is independent of the constructor's forward-recursion checker. Bit reversal fixes 0,N-1 and sends

    N-2 -> N/2-1,
    N-1-M -> N-1-M/2.

Writing k=M/2, the last-row entries at columns N-2,N-1-M,N-1 are therefore

    3alpha+5^k beta,  2alpha+7^k beta,  alpha+beta.

Constancy gives the homogeneous coefficient rows (2,5^k-1) and (1,7^k-1). Their determinant 2*7^k-5^k-1 is strictly positive. At N=16 it is 72. Hence only alpha=beta=0 solves the equations, which is not a projective parameter. This covers alpha=0, beta=0, alpha+beta=0, all other complex ratios, and singular right/left sums; no division-based chart omits an admissible case.

## 4. Constant-modulus strengthening

For alpha+beta!=0 put z=beta/(alpha+beta), u=3+(5^k-3)z and v=2+(7^k-2)z. If both selected entries had the modulus of the diagonal, then |u|=|v|=1. But

    (7^k-2)u-(5^k-3)v=3*7^k-2*5^k,

whose positive right side exceeds the triangle upper bound (7^k-2)+(5^k-3) by

    2*7^k-3*5^k+5>0.

At k=1 the gap is 4. At k>=2, 2*7^k>3*5^k. Thus the final row cannot even have constant nonzero modulus. The anchored setting therefore excludes any target with a flat-modulus first row, including Fourier matrices with endpoint permutations and phase-only column masks. Arbitrary nonuniform outer column-amplitude masks do not preserve that hypothesis and are not excluded.

Independent exact integer checks verify all anchors, bit-reversal positions, selected path weights, homogeneous determinants and modulus gaps for M=2,4,8,16,32,64. The proof is characteristic zero; the nonzero integer determinant is not automatically nonzero in every positive characteristic. The constructor's finite-field searches remain finite controls, not the logical basis of this complex exclusion.

## 5. Next boundary

Replacing R by a cyclic shift followed by R moves the routed term's minimal and maximal vertices. The common-anchor proof then loses its premise. This is an actual architectural change, but not a positive Fourier construction. It must still satisfy full Fourier equality, all sparse-fraction/monomial guards, and a charged all-size construction before any speed claim. No broad grid-family infeasibility, practical performance claim or new literature priority is inferred.
