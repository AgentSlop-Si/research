# Cyclotomic Green packets, rational-scan rank, and sparse-inverse separator gates — v19

**Checkpoint:** 10 October 2026  
**Primary target:** exact all-output DFT of an arbitrary fresh input in `o(N log N)` fully charged sequential arithmetic, ideally `O(N)`.  
**Status:** one exact positive Green-bank fusion identity and two new scoped impossibility theorems. **No exact faster FFT is constructed.** The v15 approximate all-length branch and the v18 simultaneous-order theorem remain unchanged.

## 1. Starting point

v18 proved that a prime-order Fourier matrix cannot be a sum of a constant or sublogarithmic number of independently ordered Hamiltonian Green resolvents. An exact representation requires almost `log_2 N` effective orders, and evaluating those orders separately restores `Omega(N log N)` work.

Two escape routes remained:

1. fuse many Green terms into one shared recurrence, hoping that a packet of `b` terms costs only `O(N)`;
2. replace a bank of Green inverses by one sparse **multi-graph denominator** with an `O(N)` direct solve.

v19 attacks both routes.

---

## 2. Positive result: exact cyclotomic packet fusion

Let `P` be any permutation matrix over a field whose characteristic does not divide `b`, and let `xi` be a primitive `b`th root of unity. Put `X=alpha P`. Assume every displayed inverse exists.

### Theorem 2.1 — character packet identity

For `s=0,...,b-1`,

\[
\boxed{
\frac1b\sum_{r=0}^{b-1}\xi^{-rs}(I-\xi^rX)^{-1}
=
X^s(I-X^b)^{-1}.
}
\]

### Proof

For a commuting scalar indeterminate `z`,

\[
(1-\xi^r z)^{-1}=\sum_{m\ge0}\xi^{rm}z^m.
\]

The roots-of-unity filter

\[
\frac1b\sum_r\xi^{r(m-s)}
\]

is one exactly when `m congruent s (mod b)` and zero otherwise. Therefore the left side is

\[
\sum_{q\ge0}z^{s+qb}=z^s(1-z^b)^{-1}.
\]

The identity is rational, hence it remains valid wherever the inverses exist; substitute `z=X`. QED.

### Fully charged application

`P^b` is again a permutation. Applying

\[
X^s(I-X^b)^{-1}
\]

requires:

- one first-order solve on every cycle of `P^b`;
- one permutation by `P^s`;
- one scalar multiplication.

The total is `O(N)` arithmetic and `O(N)` storage, rather than `b` separate `O(N)` Green solves.

This is a real joint evaluator. Exact modular benchmarks in the package show approximately `2.5x` to `8.9x` Python speedups for packet widths `b=4,8,16`; these are prototype timings, not FFT-library comparisons.

### Arbitrary packet weights

Let `c_0,...,c_(b-1)` be arbitrary and define

\[
\widehat c_s=\frac1b\sum_{r=0}^{b-1}c_r\xi^{rs}.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{r=0}^{b-1}c_r(I-\alpha\xi^rP)^{-1}
=
b\sum_{s=0}^{b-1}\widehat c_s(\alpha P)^s
(I-(\alpha P)^b)^{-1}.
}
\]

So the entire packet has one common stride-Green denominator and a numerator polynomial determined by the discrete Fourier transform of its weights.

The fusion is mathematically exact. The question is whether such a short common-order rational scan can equal the Fourier matrix.

## 3. Rational-scan rank gate

Relabel an `N`-cycle as the standard cyclic shift `C e_j=e_(j+1 mod N)`. Let

\[
q(z)=q_0+q_1z+\cdots+q_dz^d,
\qquad q_0q_d\ne0,
\]

and

\[
p(z)=p_0+p_1z+\cdots+p_ez^e.
\]

Assume `q(C)` is invertible and put

\[
T=q(C)^{-1}p(C).
\]

### Lemma 3.1 — separated rectangle rank

Let

\[
m=\left\lfloor\frac{N-e}{2}\right\rfloor,
\]

and suppose `d<m`. Choose

\[
B=\{0,1,\ldots,m-1\},
\qquad
A=\{m+e,m+e+1,\ldots,m+e+m-1\}.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}T[A,B]\le d.}
\]

### Proof

For a fixed `j in B`, the column `y=T e_j` satisfies

\[
q(C)y=p(C)e_j.
\]

The right side is supported only on `j,...,j+e`, hence it vanishes on every row of `A`. In the standard cycle order, on `A` we therefore have the homogeneous recurrence

\[
q_0y_i+q_1y_{i-1}+\cdots+q_dy_{i-d}=0.
\]

Because `q_0 != 0`, the restriction of the column to `A` is determined by its first `d` entries there. All columns of `T[A,B]` lie in the same `d`-dimensional recurrence space. QED.

### Theorem 3.2 — prime Fourier obstruction

Let `N` be prime. Allow arbitrary endpoint row/column permutations and arbitrary nonzero endpoint diagonal scalings. If a matrix monomially equivalent to `F_N` has the form

\[
q(C)^{-1}p(C),
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
d\ge\left\lfloor\frac{N-e}{2}\right\rfloor.
}
\]

### Proof

If `d<m`, Lemma 3.1 gives a rank-at-most-`d` square block of size `m`. Under arbitrary endpoint monomial changes this becomes an arbitrary `m x m` minor of the prime Fourier matrix. Chebotarev's theorem says every such minor is nonzero, hence has rank `m`, contradiction. QED.

### Consequences

1. A one-order recurrence with local numerator degree `e=o(N)` needs state/order `d=Omega(N)`.
2. A cyclotomic packet of width `b`, whose common denominator is `1-(alpha z)^b` and whose numerator degree is at most `b-1`, cannot by itself equal a prime Fourier matrix unless

   \[
   \boxed{b\ge\lceil N/3\rceil.}
   \]

3. The especially cheap character packet from Theorem 2.1 is even less expressive: for prime `N` and `b<N`, `P^b` is again one Hamiltonian cycle, so the packet is only a shifted single Green solve.

Thus the natural roots-of-unity fusion really does save the arithmetic of a same-order Green bank, but it collapses its Fourier expressivity at the same time. A logarithmic-width packet cannot be the missing exact FFT stage.

This theorem concerns one common cycle order. It does **not** rule out a joint evaluator that mixes many genuinely different orders in a non-recurrence architecture.

## 4. Sparse multi-graph inverse: separator rank gate

The second v18 escape route was a sparse matrix containing several permutation graphs, with a direct `O(N)` inverse application.

Let `A` be invertible and partition the coordinates into disjoint sets `U,S,V` such that

\[
A[U,V]=0,
\qquad
A[V,U]=0.
\]

Assume the principal block `A[U,U]` is invertible.

### Lemma 4.1 — inverse communication through a separator

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{rank}A^{-1}[U,V]\le |S|.
}
\]

### Proof

Give the system `Ax=b` a right side supported only on `V`. The `U` block equation is

\[
A[U,U]x_U+A[U,S]x_S=0.
\]

Therefore

\[
x_U=-A[U,U]^{-1}A[U,S]x_S.
\]

The map from `b_V` to `x_U` factors through the `|S|` coordinates of `x_S`, so its rank is at most `|S|`. QED.

Strict row diagonal dominance guarantees every principal block required here is invertible.

### Theorem 4.2 — treewidth gate for a stable sparse Fourier denominator

Let `N` be prime and let `A` be a strictly row diagonally dominant matrix. Let `G(A)` be the undirected sparsity graph joining `i,j` whenever `A_ij` or `A_ji` is nonzero. If `A^{-1}` is monomially equivalent to `F_N`, then

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{tw}(G(A))\ge\left\lceil\frac N4\right\rceil-1.
}
\]

### Proof

A graph of treewidth `w` has a balanced separator `S` of size at most `w+1`, with every component of `G-S` containing at most `N/2` vertices. If `|S|<N/4`, group the components into two sets `U,V` with

\[
|U|,|V|\ge\frac{N-|S|}{3}>|S|.
\]

There are no sparsity edges between `U` and `V`; strict diagonal dominance makes `A[U,U]` invertible. Lemma 4.1 gives

\[
\operatorname{rank}A^{-1}[U,V]\le|S|.
\]

But after the endpoint monomial maps this is a Fourier submatrix with at least `|S|+1` rows and columns. Chebotarev gives full rank, contradiction. Hence `|S|>=N/4`, and `w+1>=N/4`. QED.

### Multi-graph corollary

For

\[
A=I-\sum_{r=1}^{g}\alpha_rP_r,
\qquad
\sum_r|\alpha_r|<1,
\]

`A` is strictly row diagonally dominant. Therefore an exact prime Fourier inverse of this form requires the union graph of the permutation edges to have linear treewidth.

This closes bounded-treewidth, bounded-bandwidth, series-parallel, and similar easy-elimination multi-graph denominators as direct Fourier inverses.

It does **not** close a constant-degree expander denominator with linear treewidth. Such a graph escapes the separator theorem, but no exact `O(N)` solver for the required Fourier-compatible coefficients is supplied here.

## 5. What the two results say together

The most obvious joint Green-bank fusion **does work**:

\[
\text{`b` same-order resolvents}
\longrightarrow
\text{one stride-Green denominator}.
\]

But then:

- if the packet is cheap enough to be one first-order stride solve, it has only one effective Green order;
- if it retains a degree-`d` common-order numerator/denominator, exact Fourier compatibility forces `d=Omega(N)`;
- if we instead put several permutation edges into one sparse denominator and rely on an easy graph decomposition, exact Fourier compatibility forces linear treewidth.

So the surviving exact route is narrower:

> A successful stage must combine several genuinely noncommuting orders, have linear-separator complexity, and nevertheless admit an exact `O(N)` or `O(N log log N)` solve by algebra that is not ordinary sparse elimination or a one-order recurrence.

No such stage is constructed here.

## 6. Reproducibility

The package uses exact arithmetic modulo `998244353` for its executable certificates.

- **12** matrix-level packet cases, covering widths `2,4,8` and arbitrary permutations.
- **48** independent vector-application packet cases through length `509`.
- **48** rational-scan recurrence rectangles; every measured rank was at most the denominator degree.
- **4** exact selected Fourier blocks at length `17`; every block had full rank.
- **4** exact separator matrices; inverse cross-rank equalled or stayed below separator size.
- The selected `7 x 7` length-17 Fourier cross-block had rank `7`, versus separator candidate `3`.
- **8/8** automated test groups pass.
- The complete v18 predecessor is embedded byte-for-byte and its SHA-256 is checked.

Python packet benchmarks compare the literal sum of `b` cycle solves against the fused single solve:

- width `4`: about `2.49x`–`2.72x` faster;
- width `8`: about `4.70x`–`4.88x` faster;
- width `16`: about `7.33x`–`8.87x` faster.

These timings validate the implementation-level sharing, not a Fourier speedup.

## 7. Correctness boundaries

This checkpoint does **not** establish:

- an exact `o(N log N)` FFT;
- a lower bound for unrestricted arithmetic circuits;
- a lower bound for all multi-order joint evaluators;
- a bit-complexity or fixed-precision theorem;
- a practical speed advantage over FFTW, MKL, pocketfft, or cuFFT;
- literature-wide novelty.

The treewidth theorem covers a direct inverse stage with strict diagonal dominance. Products of several stages, indefinite special coefficients, and high-treewidth matrices with special exact solvers remain outside it.

The rational-scan theorem covers one common cyclic order and a local polynomial numerator. A genuinely multiscale or multi-order circuit remains outside it.

---

## 8. Exact next target

The next experiment should no longer group resolvents merely by root-of-unity shifts of the same order. That fusion is now completely understood.

The surviving high-value targets are:

1. a **high-treewidth constant-degree denominator** with an explicit exact linear-time solve not based on separators or hidden Fourier transforms;
2. a structured `Theta(log N)` bank of genuinely different orders whose joint evaluator is not reducible to one rational scan;
3. a product architecture in which global rank is created across stages while the total charged work remains `o(N log N)`.

Any proposed candidate should first be tested against the packet rank gate, the separator gate, the v18 simultaneous-order rectangle, and the older sparse-defect/three-tap compatibility certificates.
