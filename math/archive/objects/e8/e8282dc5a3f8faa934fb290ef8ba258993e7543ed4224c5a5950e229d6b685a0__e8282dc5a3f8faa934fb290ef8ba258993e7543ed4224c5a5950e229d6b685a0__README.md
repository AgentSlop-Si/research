# Exact product reuse with more than one parent

This package extends the earlier A-k correction graph with verified second
differences and polynomial trajectories. It computes complete canonical
integer products. Dense first differences can defeat every sparse one-parent
correction while a zero second difference permits linear-work updates after
two paid seed products. When both operands vary affinely, three paid seed
products suffice. These are applications of standard finite differences;
no mathematical novelty or arbitrary-input multiplication/FFT improvement
is claimed.

`multiparent.py` accepts Q pairs of nonnegative integers, each represented by
n little-endian base-256 digits, including padding. Every requested product
is returned as exactly 2n canonical digits. There is no fixed-prime or root
limit. Seed products use Python's exact integer multiplication explicitly;
each is charged as an opaque multiplication M(8n), together with input packing,
output conversion, and full digit writes. A seed multiplication is never
counted as one word operation. Reuse itself uses digit and carry loops and
does not convert the operands or products into whole-input Python integers.

## Verified secant updates

For a shared B, the implementation constructs the exact residual

\[
R_i=A_i-2A_{i-1}+A_{i-2}.
\]

It tests both the coordinate representation and canonical signed-magnitude
representation and retains the smaller support. Each representation is
computed, including carries or borrows; a small numeric residual is not
assumed from a label or a floating-point test. The product update is

\[
P_i=2P_{i-1}-P_{i-2}+R_iB.
\]

For a residual with s nonzero digits, the correction performs exactly ns
digit products and ns signed accumulator updates. The two-parent combination
and a full carry pass cost O(n). If the verified support exceeds the supplied
`max_patch` limit (default 2), that product is computed by a new paid seed
multiplication. It may still be reused by later updates. All B operands are
checked for equality. This schedule is a fixed ordered DAG, not a claim of
optimal planning over all possible multiple-parent relations.

With k fallback products, the cold cost is

\[
(\min(Q,2)+k)M(8n)+O\!\left(Qn+n\sum_{\text{accepted }i}s_i\right).
\]

This includes reading and validating both operands, constructing both residual
representations, materializing seeds, carries, and all Q complete outputs.
The expression concerns the declared word/RAM model around the seed black
box; it is not a measured Python runtime or a full bit-complexity analysis.

## Both operands may vary

The second mode tests degrees 0 through 3 separately for the two operand
sequences. A candidate degree d is accepted only after every applicable
(d+1)-st finite difference is verified to be zero by exact signed digit
arithmetic. Verification includes carry cancellation, so it does not require
the individual digit coordinates to follow polynomials. Failed candidates
and an unsuccessful discovery pass are charged. Discovery costs O(Qn) for
this fixed degree limit. It checks the supplied order of the batch; finding
an advantageous reordering is outside this algorithm.

If the verified degrees are d_A and d_B, let d=d_A+d_B and h=d+1. The product
sequence obeys

\[
P_i=\sum_{j=1}^{h}(-1)^{j+1}{h\choose j}P_{i-j}.
\]

**Proof.** Vanishing finite differences give Newton interpolating polynomials
over the rationals of the stated degrees that agree with every supplied
operand value. Their product has degree at most d_A+d_B. Its (d+1)-st
difference therefore vanishes, giving the displayed recurrence. This proof
applies to the complete integers, not just their uncarried digit polynomials.
The first min(Q,h) exact products determine the subsequent values.

For A_i=A_0+iD_A and B_i=B_0+iD_B, the product is quadratic and
`Delta^2 P = 2 D_A D_B`. The three-seed recurrence retains this mixed term;
there is no assumption that changing both inputs has only two linear costs.
If one operand is constant and the other affine, h=2.

The verified cold cost is

\[
\min(Q,d_A+d_B+1)M(8n)+O(Qn)
\]

for the fixed supported degrees. Every output is carried and written. If
either operand fails all degree tests, the implementation computes every
remaining product freshly, still paying discovery. This prevents a restricted
trajectory result from being applied to unrelated inputs. No theorem is
claimed for growing degrees with uncharged binomial coefficients.

## A family that separates one-parent and multiple-parent reuse

First take byte vectors `A_i[j]=a_j+i d_j`, with all d_j positive and
`a_j+(Q-1)d_j<256`, and fix any B. Every coordinate first difference is dense.
If also `(Q-1)d_j<256`, the canonical numeric first differences are dense:
there is no interdigit carry. Nevertheless every second difference is zero.
The old one-parent correction kernel needs Theta(n^2) work for each such edge;
the new schedule needs two seed products and O(Qn) subsequent work.

A growing-batch version avoids restricting Q below 256. Let

\[
w=\lceil\log_{256}(Q+1)\rceil,\quad n=kw,\quad
R=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}256^{wj},\quad A_i=(i+1)R.
\]

Each w-byte block contains i+1, and each nonzero difference contains the
same nonzero w-byte integer. Thus **both** coordinate and numeric first
differences have support at least k=n/w. Every direct sparse one-parent
correction costs Omega(n^2/w), while the second difference is exactly zero.
When the seed multiplier is cheaper than these corrections, the original
single-parent model chooses fresh products. For example, with polynomially
growing Q and an O(n polylog n) seed backend, its product cost is Q M(8n),
versus two seeds plus O(Qn) here. Once Q amortizes the two seeds, the batch
approaches its full-output linear-work scale. This is a proved structured
family, not coverage of arbitrary fresh pairs or a lower bound for other
one-parent correction kernels.

## Exact experiments and comparison

The audit passes 254 exact product comparisons, 21 signed carry controls, and
12 invalid-input/cache checks. Sizes include n=1024 bytes, beyond the earlier
fixed-modulus experiment. The table reports seed calls and additional counted
numerical word operations. Input reads, comparisons, copies, conversion bytes,
and output writes are recorded separately in `audit.json` and covered by the
O(Qn) terms above; the numerical column alone is not total work.

| Batch | n bytes | Q | Seed multiplications | Additional numerical word operations |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Fresh baseline, dense affine A and fixed B | 64 | 12 | 12 | 0 |
| Secant schedule, same targets | 64 | 12 | 2 | 10,280 |
| Degree discovery and recurrence, same targets | 64 | 12 | 2 | 13,376 |
| Both operands affine | 64 | 12 | 3 | 16,318 |
| Cubic A and quadratic B | 8 | 12 | 6 | 2,744 |
| Sparse signed second differences | 64 | 12 | 2 | 11,633 |
| Secant schedule, larger operands | 1,024 | 12 | 2 | 163,880 |

The baseline's zero in the last column means its arithmetic is inside the
explicitly paid M(8n) calls. It does not mean multiplication or serialization
is free. No wall-clock advantage is asserted.

A separate comparison uses **identical integer targets** in this package
and `../fft_route_barriers_research/correction_graph.py`: eight A values with
128 repeated hexadecimal digits, paired with the same dense B. Packing two
hex digits into one byte changes only representation. Every one-parent patch
has support 128 even with numeric corrections enabled. The old planner selects
eight fresh products; its arithmetic is 75,064 versus 60,728 for its own
fresh baseline, because unsuccessful discovery adds cost. The new secant
schedule uses two paid builtin seeds plus 6,168 numerical word operations and
the separately recorded linear traffic. These are different seed backends;
their ledgers must not be divided to invent a speed ratio. They establish a
structural reuse separation on the same outputs.

Two deterministic unrelated-input controls select all twelve fresh products,
with extra discovery work. They demonstrate the cost of failing to find the
required structure.

## Cold, warm, storage, and validation scope

`make_seed_cache` explicitly computes and records the cost of a prefix of
canonical seed products. The corresponding warm run makes zero additional
seed multiplications in the verified examples. Adding cache creation restores
the cold number of seed multiplications; validation, copying, and rematerialized
outputs can add repeated work. Warm numbers are never substituted for cold
results. Partial prefix caches are accepted and all missing seeds are paid.

Caches include complete operand keys, dimensions, and canonical product
digits. Key and digit checks prevent accidental stale or malformed reuse.
They are trusted internal products, not cryptographically authenticated proofs
of multiplication: maliciously changed but well-formed product digits would
require independent verification, whose cost is not claimed here.

Signed recurrence coefficients are carried through one extra guard byte. The
final carry and guard byte must both vanish before the 2n digits are accepted.
Accepted secant residuals are applied with their signs; negative intermediate
coefficients are permitted. The verified integer identity ensures the final
value is the desired nonnegative product below 256^(2n).

Storage is O(Qn) because all requested outputs and their reusable product
copies are retained, plus O(n) temporary buffers for these fixed degrees.
There is no unbounded lookup table, free first product, or hidden full FFT.
The implementation's counters are selected logical categories, not an
exhaustive trace of Python allocation, physical memory access, or machine
instructions. Loop/index/copy overhead is bounded by the stated work terms.

The numerical operand widths in these loops are also controlled. Product
recurrence weights have total absolute value at most 127 (order at most 7),
so their redundant digit accumulators and carries fit in a constant number
of bits, independent of n. Secant correction accumulators are bounded by
O((s+1)*256^2), hence have O(log(s+2)) bits; fixed `max_patch` gives a constant
bound. The relation-verification widths are constant as well. Thus outside
the paid seed multiplications, the fixed-degree/fixed-support numerical
arithmetic admits O(Qn) bit work. The separate indexing and interpreter model
still uses word addresses and is not asserted to have this exact bit cost.

Run `python audit.py`. If the earlier correction-graph file is present, the
same-target old-planner comparison is also executed; otherwise the standalone
new algorithm tests still run and the optional comparison is recorded as null.

The remaining research problem is to find similarly cheap verified relations
inside the actual multiplication/FFT computation graph for general inputs,
with relation discovery and all initial evaluations paid. This package gives
an executable multiple-parent family and does not resolve that problem.
