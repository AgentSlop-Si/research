# Monomial Weyl interface classification audit

## Decision

**Accept the necessary normal form, the promised-input linear-work recovery, the actual P-transpose and Q interface orientations, and the support consequence m d_P d_Q >= N.** The result identifies equal-size arbitrary-input Fourier batches inside both exteriors when all four transported Weyl actions are monomial. It does not establish a general FFT lower bound, a fast batch evaluator, or historical novelty.

The important cost qualification is substantive: the two sparse transported actions must be supplied, or their construction must be paid. One genuine nonzero calibration entry is required to recover the intended H rather than a scalar multiple. Finding these actions from an opaque circuit or checking that a claimed circuit realizes them is not solved by the normal-form theorem.

The no-discrete-log recovery is valid. The unique clock cycle whose edge-weight product is one identifies eigenvalue class zero. The shift permutation then labels all remaining classes, so no arbitrary root extraction or logarithm oracle is hidden in the endpoint recovery.

## Precise theorem and field guards

Let K be a field containing a supplied primitive Nth root omega, with characteristic not dividing N. Set

    S e_j=e_(j+1 mod N),  D e_j=omega^j e_j,
    U=H S H^(-1),  V=H D H^(-1),

where H is invertible and U,V are monomial: exactly one nonzero in each row and column. Write column actions U e_i=u_i e_alpha(i), V e_i=v_i e_beta(i). Then for a divisor d of N, with c=N/d,

    H=M_out (I_c tensor F_d^-) M_in,

for invertible monomial endpoints and F_d^-[k,l]=(omega^c)^(-kl). In particular every row and column of H has exactly d nonzeros. The negative sign is an index reversal of positive Fourier, not an uncharged inverse normalization. The result works for composite N; no prime-length full-spark assumption is involved.

The primitive-root requirement ensures distinct D eigenvalues and nonzero Fourier determinants. Over a field of positive characteristic, existence of a primitive Nth root already prevents that characteristic from dividing N; the explicit characteristic guard is useful documentation. No algebraic closure or square-root normalization is needed. The theorem is stated for exact arithmetic, not floating tolerances.

This is a necessary normal form. Arbitrary monomial endpoints around a Fourier batch do not imply that both transported generators are monomial. The constructor explicitly preserves this direction, and our negative control tests it.

## Independent proof review

From DS=omega SD, one has VU=omega UV. Equality of supports forces alpha beta=beta alpha. The pair S,D is irreducible: any common invariant subspace is a span of a subset of D's distinct eigencoordinates, and invariance under S forces either none or all of them. Polynomial projections onto the D eigencoordinates justify this over the stated field.

If the coordinate action generated by alpha,beta had multiple orbits, each orbit span would be a proper common invariant subspace for U,V. Therefore it is transitive. Alpha maps beta cycles to beta cycles and acts transitively on their set. All beta cycles consequently have the same length d, and d divides N.

A weighted beta cycle has characteristic polynomial x^d-kappa, where kappa is its edge-weight product. Since V is similar to D, its spectrum is exactly the N distinct roots omega^j. The spectrum of one cycle must be a coset of the dth roots:

    {omega^(r+c l): 0<=l<d}.

Its product parameter is kappa=omega^(rd). Distinct cycles have disjoint spectra and hence distinct residues r modulo c. There are c cycles, so every residue occurs. Exactly one cycle has kappa=1. This is true for d=1 and c=1 as well.

Order each cycle by beta as v_(r,k), and define h_(r,0)=1 and h_(r,k) as the product of its first k edge weights. Column j of H is the V eigenvector with eigenvalue omega^j. It is supported on cycle r=j mod c, and its recurrence gives

    H[v_(r,k),j]=gamma_j h_(r,k) omega^(-jk).

With j=r+c l this separates into a row factor h_(r,k)omega^(-rk), a Fourier factor (omega^c)^(-kl), and a column factor gamma_j. None vanish. This proves the claimed normal form and exact uniform row/column support.

## Recovery and its true input model

The source's recovery is correct under its promise that the supplied monomial arrays are the two conjugates of the intended H:

1. Traverse beta cycles, accumulate their products and record vertex positions.
2. Find the unique product-one cycle and label it zero.
3. Follow alpha on the cycle set to label the other residues. Because VU=omega UV, alpha increments the eigenvalue exponent, not decrements it.
4. Generate omega's N powers by recurrence and the N weighted-cycle prefixes.
5. Obtain gamma_0=H[v_(0,0),0], a nonzero entry. It can be extracted from one paid H e_0 evaluation.
6. For j=0,...,N-2, let u=alpha^(-1)(v_((j+1) mod c,0)), with u=v_(j mod c,k). Then

       gamma_(j+1)=u_u gamma_j h_(j mod c,k) omega^(-jk).

The recurrence follows from H e_(j+1)=U H e_j. The wrap step is a useful consistency check. The chosen bases within the cycles are arbitrary; the same formula accounts for them.

The traversals, powers, prefixes and coefficient recurrence use O(N) field operations and O(N) index words. Exponent products and reductions modulo N are index work, with their integer bit costs separate. Inverse monomial coefficients can be batch-inverted with linear field work and one inversion. Root generation is not needed beyond the supplied omega; if that root itself must be constructed, its cost is additional. Dense emission of the Nd nonzero entries is not part of the O(N)-sized endpoint plan.

The calibration entry cannot be dropped. H and aH have identical U,V for every nonzero a. Thus sparse actions determine the intended operator only up to a scalar, and exact recovery needs one genuine calibration value. A zero or assumed seed is not sufficient.

Monomial-array checks and algebraic consistency checks are distinct from verifying the source of the arrays. This recovery does not prove that a separately proposed Green circuit realizes H. Extracting actions from dense H can involve quadratic access and much more arithmetic; extracting them from an opaque evaluator or a purported factorization requires a separately charged method. The source correctly does not declare that access free.

Given recovered endpoints, an H evaluator and an evaluator for c arbitrary length-d DFTs simulate each other with O(N) additional monomial work. Endpoint invertibility preserves arbitrary inputs. This is a conditional reduction, not an algorithmic lower bound for those DFTs.

## Actual bank interface orientation

For the positive unnormalized Fourier matrix, F S=D F and F D=S^(-1) F. With F=P B Q, define

    T1=Q S Q^(-1),       T2=Q D Q^(-1),
    A1=P^(-1) D P,      A2=P^(-1) S^(-1) P.

The correct transported equations are B T1=A1 B and B T2=A2 B. Applying the theorem to Q uses U=T1,V=T2 directly.

For P, the correct choice is H=P transpose, U=A2 transpose, V=A1 transpose, since

    A2^T=P^T S (P^T)^(-1),
    A1^T=P^T D (P^T)^(-1).

The inverse shift changes back to S under transpose. No inverse-P or transpose-P evaluator is needed for calibration: H[v,0]=P[0,v] can be read from one paid P e_v application. Transposing the recovered normal form is legitimate because Fourier matrices are symmetric and monomial matrices remain monomial under transpose.

If B has row support at most m, P has row support d_P and Q has row support d_Q, each row of P B Q has support at most m d_P d_Q. Every Fourier row has N nonzeros, giving

    m d_P d_Q >= N.

This argument works more generally for any such sparse B. For the full Fourier bank, every row has exactly m entries. Cancellations can shrink support but cannot invalidate the upper bound.

At N=p squared with m=p prime, the divisors are 1,p,p squared, so at least one exterior has batch size at least p. At dyadic lengths, intermediate divisors can distribute the support budget between the two exteriors; a stronger single-exterior conclusion does not follow. Even the valid support constraint is not a general FFT operation lower bound.

## Exact independent controls

All controls passed:

- 267 normal-form recovery cases at N=1 through 16, and N=18,20,24,27,32,36, across every divisor block size. They include routed/nonzero-reweighted outputs and Clifford-type chirp/Fourier twists that change the resulting batch sizes. The controls use prime fields with the needed roots and include positive characteristic, odd prime squares and composite lengths.
- Each case recovers the full original matrix from the sparse U,V actions and exactly one seed query, checks equal row and column support, and independently checks every column-shift intertwining equation. Replacing the seed by twice its value recovers twice H, confirming the scalar ambiguity rather than hiding it.
- 124 actual P/Q bank interface cases check both correctly oriented transport equations, recovery through P transpose, the forward-P seed access, and m d_P d_Q>=N.
- 30 sharp examples with Q=I attain m d_P=N, so the support-product inequality cannot simply be strengthened to strict inequality.

Negative controls:

1. At N=4 over GF(13), omega=-1 is not primitive. H=I+2S squared is invertible and commutes with both S and D. Both conjugates are monomial, V has one-point cycles, but H has row support two. The primitive/simple-spectrum guard is essential.
2. At N=4, an H with a two-point Fourier block and two scalar blocks makes V monomial with cycle lengths 2,1,1, while U is not monomial. One transported generator alone does not imply equal cycle lengths.
3. At N=3 over GF(13), H=F_3 diag(1,1,2) is monomial-equivalent to F_3 but does not send both Weyl generators to monomials. The unrestricted converse is false.

The checker deliberately creates and multiplies dense reference matrices to generate and compare small cases. Those costs are audit work. Its coefficient-plan loop uses only the supplied arrays and one seed, after which the test separately expands the matrix. Component counters illustrate linear field work; they are not a full bit-operation or Python-runtime ledger.

## Primary literature and provenance

[Prasad and Vemuri, Inductive Algebras for Finite Heisenberg Groups](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0803.2921), Theorem 2.4, classifies maximal normalized abelian algebras via maximal isotropic subgroups. A monomial realization normalizes the diagonal algebra, so this is direct classical context for the classification. Their complex representation-theoretic result is not cited as a ready-made proof of this arbitrary-field recovery-cost statement.

[Appleby and coauthors, The monomial representations of the Clifford group](https://arxiv.org/html/1102.1268), Section 3, equations (25)–(27), explicitly describes square-dimensional monomial Weyl actions and the associated partial Fourier/Zak basis. Their conclusion about the full Clifford group is stronger and has a different scope from asking only that the two Weyl generators become monomial.

These are positive provenance findings, not a claim that a limited search establishes priority for the precise cost lemma. This audit makes no novelty claim.
