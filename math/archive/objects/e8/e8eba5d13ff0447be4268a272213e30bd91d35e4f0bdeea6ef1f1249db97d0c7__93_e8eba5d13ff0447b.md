# Independent review: bidirectional sandwich extension

Review date: 2026-10-09. Reviewer: separate macro-audit agent.

**Conclusion: accepted within the stated scope.** The sandwich identity,
charged gate counts, bounded-component inverse-support theorem, and
permutation-movement inequality are correct. They do not establish an exact
sub-NlogN Fourier algorithm or a general arithmetic lower bound.

## Algebra and cost

Independently expanding LAR gives the three displayed diagonals and no other
nonzero diagonals. The preparation cost is

\[
4(N-1)+3(N-1)+3(N-1)+2(N-1)+3+5(N-2)=17N-19.
\]

The five application portions cost 2(N−1), N, 2(N−1), 2(N−1), and 2(N−1)
gates, respectively, totaling 9N−8. Thus a cold application costs 26N−27
field gates, plus O(N) validation, memory work, and explicit output writes.
These are implementation counts given the generators; discovery of a
Fourier factorization is not included or claimed.

The nonzero p,v preparation contract guards its divisions. The
separate converse statement for arbitrary bidiagonal coefficients is also
correct, including zero coefficients: across a cut, the upper cross block
of R⁻¹ and the upper off-diagonal block of T share the same right boundary
vector after multiplication by the trailing R⁻¹ block. Their sum therefore
has rank at most one. The lower assertion has the analogous shared left
boundary vector. This is a quasiseparable-rank statement, not an automatic
conversion of all such stages to independent literal scalar scan channels.

## Component theorem

The bound relies specifically on contiguous block-diagonal components of T,
with both links across a separating cut zero. Invertibility of A implies
invertibility of T, and hence of each component. A row of RT⁻¹ combines
either one component or two neighboring components. Their union is a
contiguous interval of length at most 2b. Right multiplication by lower
bidiagonal L can extend this interval only one coordinate to the left.
Therefore each inverse row has at most 2b+1 entries.

Multiplying inverse factors multiplies row-support bounds; arbitrary
monomials preserve this property. The Fourier inverse is entrywise nonzero,
so ∏ⱼ(2bⱼ+1)≥N follows. This proof would not justify the same constant for
noncontiguous blocks or wider/general sparse left and right factors.

Focused checks also show that 2b+1 can be attained, for b=1,2,3,4 over F101.
The examples use N=2b+1, component lengths 1,b,b, tridiagonal diagonal 4,
internal off-diagonals 1, and bidiagonal coefficients α=2, β=3. Their inverse
has a row with exactly 2b+1 nonzeros.

## Movement theorem

For a fixed cut, (AB)₁₂=A₁₁B₁₂+A₁₂B₂₂ proves rank subadditivity without
invertibility assumptions on diagonal blocks. For a permutation, the cut
rank equals its one-direction crossing count. Summing those counts gives
half the total absolute displacement.

Over C, a Fourier s×(N−s) submatrix has squared Frobenius norm s(N−s) and
squared operator norm at most N, even after independent endpoint row/column
permutations. Thus its rank is at least s(N−s)/N. Summing over cuts yields
(N²−1)/6 and exactly the stated displacement inequality.

The complex-field scope is essential to this proof. Quadratic total
coordinate displacement remains compatible with O(N) random-access moves;
the README correctly avoids turning this into a RAM time lower bound.

## Focused independent verification

`review_edge_checks.py` imports only the evaluated `prepare` and `Sandwich`
interfaces. Its dense arithmetic, rank/inverse routines, and operation probe
are independently implemented; it does not use the component's Counted,
generator_matrix, dense_parts, cut_ranks, or exact-field helper.

Checks passed:

- 15 generator cases at N=2,3,5, including zero generators, zero diagonals,
  singular stages, and cancellations; 45 fresh-vector applications with
  independently observed preparation/application gate counts;
- all 64 zero/nonzero patterns for the six α,β transitions at N=4,
  verifying both quasiseparable cut-rank bounds;
- four sharpness cases attaining the 2b+1 inverse-support bound;
- 12 identity, reversal, and cyclic-shift permutation cases.

review. Analytic checks above establish the stated proof implications; the
finite checks target the boundary and degenerate cases most likely to expose
