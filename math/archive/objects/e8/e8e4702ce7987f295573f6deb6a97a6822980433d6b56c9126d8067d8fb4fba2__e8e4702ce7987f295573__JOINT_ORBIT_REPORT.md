# Explicit joint universal-orbit recovery and its remaining gate

9 October 2026. Independently derived construction and checks. No uploaded source was executed. This is a useful improvement to the displayed universal-orbit executor, not a sub-N-log-N Fourier algorithm or a novelty claim.

## Result in one paragraph

For the fixed square universal orbit basis, exact recovery of every spectral orbit coefficient from fresh raw data and exact joint evaluation of every output admit O(N log N) arithmetic work and O(N) words. The previous displayed O(N^(3/2)) executor is therefore unnecessary. An explicit inverse of each geometric Vandermonde uses two ordinary convolutions and O(N) shared coefficient preparation, including one global batch inversion. The full recovery/synthesis composition cancels the Vandermonde pair and remains ordinary Cooley–Tukey. Two further exact facts identify the next gate: the inverse kernel obeys a first-order two-variable shear equation but has every admissible leading/shifted Hankel determinant nonzero, and the canonical complex orbit coordinates are exponentially ill-conditioned in sqrt(N). Neither fact excludes a different exact shared circuit.

## 1. Coordinate and field contract

Let N=M^2, M>=2. Let omega have exact order N and eta=omega^M. Set

    x[t+M u] = y_t[u],     0<=t,u<M,
    C_t[h] = sum_u y_t[u] eta^(-h u),
    q_h = omega eta^(-h).

The input-independent orbit basis uses a_j=j and mu_j=omega^j:

    y_t[u] = sum_(j=0)^(M-1) omega^(jt) b_j[u-jt],
    B_j[h] = sum_u b_j[u] eta^(-h u).

Consequently C_t[h]=sum_j B_j[h] q_h^(tj). Write V_h[t,j]=q_h^(tj).

Since gcd(1-Mh,M^2)=1, every q_h has order M^2. In particular q_h^r!=1 for 1<=r<M, so all denominators below are nonzero. M=1 has the direct identity case.

Arithmetic claims apply over exact complex arithmetic with supplied roots, or a field with supplied primitive roots of orders N and a common smooth convolution length L>=3M-2, with L<6M. For example take L a power of two. This extra root is not implied by the existence of omega in an arbitrary finite field. Root/field discovery is excluded from the supplied-root theorem and must be charged separately if required; extension-field arithmetic cannot silently be counted as base-field arithmetic. Characteristic does not divide N or L.

## 2. A two-convolution geometric Vandermonde inverse

Fix q=q_h and abbreviate

    F_0=1,  F_r=product_(k=1)^r (1-q^k),
    T_r=q^(r(r-1)/2).

For given C_0,...,C_(M-1), define

    d_r = sum_(t+s=r) [(-1)^t C_t/F_t] [T_s/F_s],
    a_r = d_r/T_r,
    B_j = (1/F_j) sum_(r=j)^(M-1)
                    a_r F_r (-1)^(r-j) T_(r-j)/F_(r-j).

The first line is an ordinary convolution truncated at M. The last line is another ordinary convolution after reversing the sequence a_r F_r. Thus V_h^-1 C costs two length-O(M) polynomial products plus O(M) scalar work.

### Proof

Interpolate f(z)=sum_j B_j z^j from f(q^t)=C_t in the Newton basis

    P_r(z)=product_(a=0)^(r-1) (z-q^a),
    f(z)=sum_r a_r P_r(z).

The divided-difference denominator at node q^t among q^0,...,q^r is

    product_(a=0,a!=t)^r (q^t-q^a)
      = (-1)^t q^(tr-t(t+1)/2) F_t F_(r-t).

For s=r-t,

    t(t+1)/2-tr = -r(r-1)/2+s(s-1)/2,

which proves the first convolution formula. The finite q-binomial identity gives

    [z^j]P_r(z)
      = (-1)^(r-j) T_(r-j) F_r/(F_j F_(r-j)),

which proves the second convolution formula. All nodes and denominators are distinct/nonzero by the field contract.

## 3. Forward synthesis is one convolution plus a short DFT

The identity

    tj = binom(t+j,2)-binom(t,2)-binom(j,2)

gives

    (V_h B)_t = T_t^(-1) sum_j (B_j/T_j) T_(t+j).

Reverse B_j/T_j, convolve it with T_0,...,T_(2M-2), and extract indices t+M-1. This is one length-O(M) convolution. No half-root is required.

For output k=h+sM,

    X[k] = sum_t C_t[h] omega^(-h t) eta^(-s t)
         = (F_M diag(omega^(-h t)) V_h B[h])_s.

Here F_M is unnormalized with the negative Fourier sign. This also proves the orbit geometric-sum formula jointly, including its apparent z=1 cases, without any Cauchy denominator or division-by-zero branch.

Raw input -> C -> B -> X is therefore exact for every fresh arbitrary x. If physical baselines b_j[u], rather than only the spectral coefficients B_j[h], are explicitly required, add M inverse length-M transforms, still O(N log N), and charge their N scalar outputs.

## 4. Complete charged arithmetic and storage ledger

All length-M Fourier transforms can be evaluated with the same geometric-convolution identity and the supplied smooth L-root. No factorization of M or special-case prime-length FFT is free.

- Root, power, phase, q_h, q_h^r and T_r tables: O(N) multiplications/loads, using consecutive-power recurrences. Root powers are not separately exponentiated at every entry.
- All F_r(q_h): O(N) operations. Batch-invert their MN=N nonzero entries using prefix products, one scalar inversion, and a reverse product pass: O(N) operations and O(N) storage. Inverses of T_r are available from reversed root powers; no scalar inversion bank is needed for them.
- Common length-L FFT twiddles and inverse normalizer: O(M) preparation plus one scalar inversion (or its supplied value). All fixed-kernel FFTs used below are charged, even if an implementation later caches them.
- Fresh raw row transforms: M transforms, O(N log M).
- Explicit spectral orbit-coefficient recovery: 2M convolutions and O(N) diagonal/reversal work, O(N log M).
- Joint orbit forward evaluation: M geometric convolutions, M short DFTs, O(N) twiddles/routing/output writes, O(N log M).
- Optional explicit physical baselines: M inverse DFTs, O(N log M).
- Memory: O(N) field words with channelwise scratch reuse, including root/factorial tables, raw data, explicit coefficients, and explicit outputs.

Thus the direct improved orbit route is O(N log N), with a fixed constant number of length-O(M) convolution banks. In the finite-field word model, the two stated scalar inversions cost O(log p) word operations if Euclidean inversion is used. Arithmetic on log p-bit words and bit complexity remain separate. The check program deliberately uses convenient modular powers and inversions for readability; it verifies the identities and is not the literal optimized preparation schedule in this ledger.

This removes a genuine representation/evaluation overhead. It does not cross the target complexity. Indeed

    F_M diag(omega^(-h t)) V_h V_h^(-1) C[h]
       = F_M diag(omega^(-h t)) C[h].

Cancelling the two unnecessary orbit maps leaves precisely the raw row-transform bank, twiddles, and column-transform bank of Cooley–Tukey.

## 5. Exact two-variable kernel equation and stationary-recurrence gate

Let

    k_s(q)=T_s/F_s,    0<=s<M,
    D_q(z)=sum_(s=0)^(M-1) k_s(q) z^s  mod z^M.

The coefficients satisfy

    (1-q^s) k_s(q)=q^(s-1) k_(s-1)(q),
    D_q(z)=(1+z)D_q(qz) mod z^M.

This is a sparse first-order q-difference equation. It is not a bounded-order constant-coefficient recurrence in s.

More precisely, let ell>=0 and n>=1 with ell+2n-2<M. Set H[i,j]=k_(ell+i+j), x_i=q^(ell+i), 0<=i,j<n. Then

    det H = [product_i k_(ell+i) /
                         product_(a=1)^(n-1)(1-q^(ell+i+a))]
            * q^(sum_(j=0)^(n-1) binom(j,2))
            * product_(i<l)(x_l-x_i).

Every factor is nonzero. To prove the formula, factor k_(ell+i) from row i and q^binom(j,2) from column j; multiply row i by product_(a=1)^(n-1)(1-q^a x_i). The remaining column polynomial is

    p_j(x)=x^j product_(a=j+1)^(n-1)(1-q^a x).

These polynomials have coefficient matrix triangular with diagonal one in 1,x,...,x^(n-1). Their evaluation determinant is the usual Vandermonde.

In particular the leading Hankel rank is ceil(M/2). No scalar homogeneous recurrence of order smaller than ceil(M/2), valid from its usual start through the available coefficient range, can generate this kernel. More generally the nonzero shifted determinants exclude a rational generating function with both numerator and denominator bounded in degree as M grows. This excludes only that bounded stationary-kernel compression. It does not exclude the explicitly nonstationary q-difference equation, a shared two-dimensional circuit, a different representation, or an unrestricted fast Fourier algorithm.

### A precise joint-operator formulation

Let R=K[X]/(X^M-eta). Evaluation at X=q_h identifies R with the M spectral channels. Let S be cyclic shift in the raw u coordinate, with the sign convention chosen so X=omega S has eigenvalues q_h. The kernel relation becomes

    (I-omega^s S^s) k_s = omega^(s-1) S^(s-1) k_(s-1).

For a polynomial in the lag z, define T f(z)=f(Xz), and let M_D multiply by D_X(z) modulo z^M. Then

    M_D T = (I+Z) T M_D,

where Z multiplies by z and truncates at z^M. T is monomial in raw (lag,u) coordinates, Z is a simple one-step shift. This is an explicit, sparse intertwining identity for the actual inverse-convolution kernel, not an evaluator by itself.

## 6. Complex conditioning of the canonical orbit coordinates

For omega=exp(2pi i/M^2), channel h=0 has q=omega. Put

    f(z)=(z-1)^(M-1),
    b_j=(-1)^(M-1-j) binom(M-1,j).

Then ||b||_2^2=binom(2M-2,M-1), while

    (V_0 b)_t=(omega^t-1)^(M-1),
    ||V_0 b||_2 <= sqrt(M) [2pi(M-1)/M^2]^(M-1).

The constant first column gives sigma_max(V_0)>=sqrt(M). Therefore

    kappa_2(V_0) >= sqrt(binom(2M-2,M-1))
                     * [M^2/(2pi(M-1))]^(M-1).

This is exp(Omega(M log M)). It is a basis-conditioning theorem for this canonical choice, not a lower bound for every orbit basis or the Fourier transform. Raw row Fourier transforms are scaled unitary, so this bad channel is not removed by the raw-to-channel conversion. The condition-number lower bound corresponds to at least Omega(M log M) extra bits for worst-case normwise perturbation control of these explicit orbit coefficients. This is not a sufficient precision bound or an algorithm-independent bit lower bound. A rigorous finite-precision algorithm must specify its error measure, scaling, and cancellation analysis.

## 7. Next tractable construction question

The next concrete question is whether the sparse intertwiner

    M_D T = (I+Z) T M_D

admits a bounded-depth product/sum of explicitly applicable sheared cyclic solves in the raw two-dimensional coordinates, with every coefficient generated and every intermediate paid for. A successful design must compose the Newton diagonals and reversal with the actual output map and remove or reduce the raw row-Fourier bank as well. Speeding only one convolution bank leaves Theta(N log M) work elsewhere.

A useful next milestone is an exact circuit for one dyadic block of this raw-coordinate intertwiner, with a recurrence that shrinks the problem and has a demonstrably sub-FFT sum of charged costs. A low displacement rank, the sparse intertwining equation alone, a bounded-rank ansatz contradicted by the Hankel determinant, or an uncharged precomputed dense kernel would not meet that milestone. This is deliberately a specified algebraic construction target, not a claim that a solution exists.

## Evidence

`check_joint_orbit.py` is independently authored, uses only the Python standard library, and never imports or executes an uploaded program. It checks 632 geometric inverses, 56 polynomial products, one batch of 2,784 inverses, and 42 complete fresh-input Fourier transforms over 14 square sizes with M=2,...,32, including non-power-of-two and composite M. All 8,352 explicit output bins agree with direct raw DFTs. Exact identities above supply the all-size proof; finite controls corroborate it.

`explore_kernel_rank.py` is optional exploratory evidence about a different cross-channel kernel matrix. Its rank observations are not used to prove any theorem here. In particular M=4 has rank 3 in the tested reductions; no general full-rank cross-channel claim is made.
