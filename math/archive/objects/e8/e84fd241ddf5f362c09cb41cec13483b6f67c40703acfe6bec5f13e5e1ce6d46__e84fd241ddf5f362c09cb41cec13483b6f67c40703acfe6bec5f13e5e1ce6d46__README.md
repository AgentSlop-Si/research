# Exact sparse decimation of the complete q-kernel

This continuation gives a complete exact recursive evaluator for arbitrary
`p(C)x`, including the full `H_q(C)x`, without calling a Fourier transform.
Its charged online arithmetic is

\[
\Theta\!\left(N^{1+\frac12\log_2 3}\right)
\approx\Theta(N^{1.7925}),
\]

with `O(N log N)` coefficient preparation and `O(N)` storage. It improves the
quadratic sparse-Horner research evaluator asymptotically, but is **slower than
the already available O(N log N) complete-operator comparator**. It does not
improve exact FFT complexity. The useful result is a legal sparse change of
basis, a closed decimated operator family, and an explicit accounting of the
remaining filter and recursion costs. No global novelty is claimed.

## Contract and finite boundary

Let `N=2^L >=2`, and let `q` be a supplied primitive Nth root in an exact field
of characteristic different from 2 and not dividing N. In particular, the
audited prime fields and characteristic-zero cyclotomic fields qualify. Define

\[
f_j=\prod_{a=1}^j(1-q^a),\quad t_j=f_j^{-1},\quad
H_q(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}t_jz^j.
\]

The original operator is the actual finite-boundary operator from the previous
checkpoint:

\[
C=U+W,\qquad U=\operatorname{diag}(q^{-i}),
\]

where `W[i,i+1]=q-q^(-i)` and `W[N-1,0]=-q/N`. The wraparound corner is retained.
Put `Sigma[i,i]=(-1)^i q^(-i)/f_i`. If `J_N` is the upper cyclic shift with a
positive wraparound entry, direct comparison of every edge gives

\[
\Sigma^{-1}C\Sigma=(I+J_N)U.                 \tag{1}
\]

Thus the gauge is two O(N) diagonal passes with O(N) coefficient preparation.
The original Fourier factorization that motivates this C is known; see
Alexey Kuznetsov, *Using q-calculus to study LDL^T factorization of a certain
Vandermonde matrix*, https://arxiv.org/abs/1507.02959 . This checkpoint derives
the following finite identities directly and does not import a faster FFT
algorithm, integer-mult-kappa, or an unrestricted external circuit theorem.

## Sparse grading and the first exact split

In the gauge (1), `J_N^(N/2)` is an involution and anticommutes with C: it
commutes with `I+J_N` and negates U when moving past it. Its two eigenspaces are
therefore the symmetric and antisymmetric pairs of coordinates separated by
N/2. This grading is a monomial action, not a dense transform.

Let `m=N/2`, `D=diag(q^(-i))` for `0<=i<m`, and let `J_+`, `J_-` be the length-m
upper cyclic shifts with wraparound +1 and -1. With

\[
S(s,a)=(s+a,s-a),\qquad
M_\pm=(I+J_\pm)D,
\]

the operator in the paired-coordinate basis is exactly

\[
S^{-1}\bar C S=
\begin{pmatrix}0&M_+\\M_-&0\end{pmatrix}.                 \tag{2}
\]

For N>=4, m is even, so `I+J_-` is invertible. Solving it is a two-pass signed
cyclic first-order recurrence, and D is invertible. Thus M_- and its inverse
both act in O(m). Conjugating (2) by `diag(I,M_-)` gives

\[
\begin{pmatrix}0&D_2\\I&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
D_2=M_+M_-=(I+J_+)(I+qJ_+)\operatorname{diag}(q^{-2i}).    \tag{3}
\]

The last equality uses `D J_-=q J_+ D`, including its wraparound entry.
The implementation uses N=2 as the direct base case.

The first basis change really is linear work. However D_2 is not the previous
degree-one family with merely q replaced by q^2: for N>=8, every row of D_2
has exactly three nonzero entries, whereas `(I+J)U` has two. A coordinate
permutation and diagonal rescaling preserve these row support counts, so such
monomial changes cannot identify the two. This is a restriction on that proposed
closure, not a lower bound against arbitrary basis changes or FFT algorithms.

## The operator family closes, with growing filter degree

The correct closed family at depth d is

\[
r=2^d,\quad n=N/r,\quad
p_r(z)=\prod_{a=0}^{r-1}(1+q^a z),\qquad
D_r=p_r(J_n)\operatorname{diag}(q^{-ri}).                 \tag{4}
\]

Split its coordinates into paired halves of size h=n/2. In (2), replace M_+
and M_- by

\[
M_\pm=p_r(J_\pm)\operatorname{diag}(q^{-ri}).
\]

For n>=4, h is even. Every factor of `p_r(J_-)` is invertible: the denominator
in the cyclic first-order solve for its a-th factor is

\[
1+q^{ah}\ne0,\qquad 0\le a<r,
\]

because q^h has order 2r. Consequently the same normalization is legal. Its
upper-right block is

\[
M_+M_-=p_r(J_+)p_r(q^rJ_+)\operatorname{diag}(q^{-2ri})
=D_{2r}.                                                 \tag{5}
\]

All factors and corners in this identity are finite. Nilpotence is also
retained: multiplying the n conjugated copies of (4) gives

\[
D_r^n=\prod_{a=0}^{N-1}(I+q^aJ_n)=I-J_n^N=0.
\]

The boundary-safe family therefore decimates exactly, but its filter degree
r doubles at each split. The generic even polynomial coefficients likewise
do not become the original half-size q-factorial coefficients. In particular,
for `E(s)=sum_j t_(2j)s^j`,

\[
t_{2j}=\frac1{
\left[\prod_{a=0}^{j-1}(1-q^{2a+1})\right]
\left[\prod_{a=1}^{j}(1-q^{2a})\right]}.
\]

The extra odd-factor product cannot just be removed. The audit checks that
E is not even a scalar variable rescaling of `H_(q^2)` at the tested N>=8.

## Three recursive calls, with all outputs retained

For any polynomial `p(z)=E(z^2)+z O(z^2)`, (3) or (5) implies

\[
p\!\begin{pmatrix}0&D\\I&0\end{pmatrix}
=\begin{pmatrix}E(D)&D O(D)\\O(D)&E(D)\end{pmatrix}.       \tag{6}
\]

For arbitrary input vectors x,y, only three half-size polynomial actions are
needed:

\[
a=E(D)x,\quad b=O(D)y,\quad c=(E+O)(D)(x+y).
\]

The outputs are `(a+D b, c-a-b)`. Equation (6) proves this exactly. The scalar
coefficient addition E+O, vector sums, basis changes, and the D action are all
charged. This is not reuse of an independently given second input or cached
first evaluation. Every fresh input is handled, and all N outputs are written.

Even if every basis change were free and every per-node overhead linear in its
dimension, this generic three-call construction would have an
`N^log_2(3)` recursion, already worse than N log N. Extra structure specific to
H_q would have to reduce those calls or share them by a proved stronger rule;
no such sharing is assumed here.

## Implemented algorithms and complete costs

`inverse_factor` solves `(I+a J_sign)y=x` with two backward scans and the
prepared inverse of `1-sign*(-a)^h`. This counts `2h-1` multiplications and
`2h-2` subtractions for h>=2, plus one negation for a negative wraparound.
Every inverse denominator is an input-independent field constant, but its
construction and inversion are still charged.

At a node of dimension n and filter degree r, applying or inverting the r
factors takes O(rn). When r exceeds the vector dimension, the implementation
instead uses a prepared direct cyclic or negacyclic kernel, taking O(n^2).
No ordinary FFT is called in either path. The coefficient kernel is prepared
by applying the same exact factor scans to a coordinate vector; inverse kernels
use the exact inverse scans. Plans are shared among nodes at the same depth,
while all operations on their distinct input vectors remain charged.

The resulting online work is

\[
\Theta\!\left(
\sum_{d=0}^{L-2}3^d\min\{N,N^2/4^d\}+3^{L-1}
\right)
=\Theta\!\left(N^{1+\frac12\log_2 3}\right).             \tag{7}
\]

The crossover occurs at `d=L/2` up to rounding. Coefficient preparation takes
O(N log N): there are O(log N) depth plans, each costing at most O(N), including
all roots, denominator inversions and dense kernels. Root finding is not hidden
in this count: q is part of the input contract. Each plan has O(n+r) entries,
and the sums of n and r over depths are O(N). Depth-first evaluation uses O(N)
live field elements. Addresses, copies, branches, indices and complete output
writes fit within the stated work and storage. This is exact field arithmetic
with word indices, not a bit-complexity or floating-point stability result.

Representative scalar gate ledgers, counting add/subtract/multiply/negate and
constant inversions as unit field operations:

| N | Preparation | Recursive online | Sparse Horner online |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 64 | 1,455 | 18,577 | 20,224 |
| 128 | 3,160 | 66,033 | 81,408 |
| 256 | 7,079 | 216,025 | 326,656 |
| 512 | 15,160 | 765,057 | 1,308,672 |

These are gate counts, not CPU timings. Beating this quadratic oracle is not
the relevant FFT goal: the existing complete H_q(C) comparator already costs
O(N log N), including coefficient preparation. The recursive evaluator above
loses that asymptotic comparison.

## Reproduce and audit scope

Run `python run_audit.py` from this directory. It uses Python's standard library
and writes `results/decimation_audit.json`. All 13 field/size cases pass:

- F_65537 and F_998244353, N=2,4,8,16,32;
- exact cyclotomic fields Q(zeta_N), N=2,4,8;
- 138 full basis vectors, checking both full H_q(C) and an arbitrary polynomial
  against independent sparse-C Horner, with the original corner intact;
- 224 basis vectors through the normalized splits, checking inverse basis
  maps and the exact off-diagonal block closure;
- signed cyclic solves, monomial anti-commutation, the gauge, recursive call
  counts, dense-band growth and the false simple q-to-q^2 coefficient reduction.

The larger gate ledgers use one dense input each and are not additional
full-basis claims. The algebra above establishes the all-size identities;
finite checks audit the implementation.

`decimation.py` and `run_audit.py` are new. `fixed_kernel.py`, `operators.py`, and
`prior_exact_arithmetic.py` are unchanged copies of the earlier exact research
helpers, included to make this checkpoint standalone.
