# Phase 14: Galois phase coherence for the Green/Haar FFT research

**Date:** 2026-10-09. **Research model:** exact Fourier, powers of two, linear transformations; operations and all phase preparation must be charged for any algorithm claim. **Status:** proved algebraic restrictions, an exact finite-size algebraic construction, and exact reproducible audits. **No new asymptotic FFT bound.**

## Executive results

Let `N=2^m` with `m>=4`; let `K=Q(zeta_8)=Q(i,sqrt(2))`, `E=K(zeta_N)`, `L=N/8`, `omega=zeta_N`, and `F[k,j]=N^(-1/2)*omega^(kj)`. Define `sigma(omega)=omega^9`, fixing `K`, and the row permutation `(P_9 v)[k]=v[9k mod N]`. We have

`[E:K]=L`, `Gal(E/K)=<sigma>` and `sigma(F)=P_9 F`.

**Theorem A (uniform fixed-strength two-stage impossibility):** At any `N>=16`, no exact Fourier intertwiner can be written as `S2 Q P S1`, where `Q` is any invertible complex diagonal, `P` is any permutation, `S1` is projectively over `K`, and `S2` is a fixed-strength Phase 12 Green mixer, Phase 13 Haar–Green mixer, or its inverse, with arbitrary path orders. The proof only needs the last stage's spectral proximity to identity. This upgrades Phase 13's sampled necessary-condition gate to a uniform impossibility theorem for this precise architecture.

**Theorem B (absolute one-mask field threshold):** If `N>=128`, no Fourier factorization `F=S2 Q P S1` exists for *any* invertible `S1,S2` projectively over `K` and *any* invertible complex diagonal `Q`. No unitary, Green, rank, norm, or near-identity restrictions are imposed. At `N=16,32,64`, in contrast, such factorizations *do* exist and can be explicitly synthesized using Galois orbit eigenvectors, although their `K`-valued factors are not proved to have scalable sub-FFT work.

**Theorem C (one-polynomial-mask obstruction):** For all `N>=16`, even if both surrounding matrices are arbitrary invertible matrices projectively over `K`, a single mask `diag(omega^f(j))` with `f` any integer polynomial cannot give the DFT (and neither can a permuted version or the same mask times arbitrary `K`-valued nonzero diagonals). Thus a standard linear ramp or quadratic chirp is *never* a sufficient single bridge in this base-field model.

**Theorem D (multistage Galois phase budget):** Under a common characteristic-zero field automorphism extending `sigma`, for a word of projectively `K`-valued stages, coordinate permutations and diagonal phase gates `Q_ell`, define

`r_sigma(Q)=min_{c!=0} rank(sigma(Q)-c Q)`.

If the word exactly computes `F`, then

`sum_ell r_sigma(Q_ell) >= N - 4 log_2(N) + 4`.

In particular, a collection of high-order phase patches touching fewer than this many total coordinates (up to common scalar changes) is impossible regardless of how many `K`-valued Green/Haar stages surround them. This is a **phase-support** requirement, NOT a transform-time lower bound: a dense phase mask takes only O(N) arithmetic to apply.

## 1. Derivation of the universal Galois invariant

Write `N=2^m`, `L=2^(m-3)`. Since `K` contains all eighth roots and no primitive sixteenth root, the cyclotomic degree formula gives `[Q(zeta_N):Q(zeta_8)]=phi(N)/phi(8)=L`. The `L` vectors `1,omega,...,omega^(L-1)` form a basis for `E/K`; `omega^L=zeta_8`. Multiplication of the Fourier matrix by the Galois automorphism produces

`sigma(F)[k,j]=N^(-1/2)*omega^(9kj)=F[9k,j]=(P_9 F)[k,j]`.

The automorphism `sigma` has order `L` and generates `Gal(E/K)`: `v_2(9^(2^s)-1)=3+s`, so 9 has order `2^(m-3)` modulo `2^m`.

The permutation `P_9` acts on residue indices via multiplication by 9. Exactly eight indices (`j` divisible by `L`) are fixed. For each `t=1..m`, the indices of additive order `2^t` number `2^(t-1)`; their orbit lengths are `1` for `t<=3`, and `2^(t-3)` for `t>=4`. Consequently,

`number of cycles(P_9) = 1+(1+2+4)+4(m-3)=4m-4`.

The odd indices form exactly **four cycles of length `L`**. Every even index has orbit length at most `L/2`. Hence the multiplicity of a primitive `L`-th root among the eigenvalues of `P_9` is **exactly four**.

## 2. Proof of Theorem B and its SHARP small-size converse

`S in PGL_N(K)` means `S=g B` for some `g!=0` and `B in GL_N(K)`. Suppose `F=S2 Q P S1`. Absorb scalar factors and `P` into the adjacent base-field factors. There exist `B1,B2 in GL_N(K)` and diagonal `Q' = B2^(-1) F B1^(-1)` with entries in `E` such that `F=B2 Q' B1`. **Thus arbitrary transcendental phases in an alleged original `Q` cause no issue: the equivalent `Q'` automatically lies in `E`.**

Taking Galois conjugates and multiplying by `F^(-1)` yields

`P_9 = sigma(F) F^(-1) = B2 [sigma(Q') (Q')^(-1)] B2^(-1)`.

Since `B2` and `P_9` have coefficients in `K`, the right side displays a diagonalization of `P_9` over `K`. But `P_9` has a primitive `L`-th root eigenvalue, and `K=Q(zeta_8)` contains this root only if `L<=8`. For `N>=128`, `L>=16`, contradiction.

**Converse for `N=16,32,64`.** Here `L<=8`, so all eigenvalues of `P_9` lie in `K`. On each multiplication-by-9 cycle of length `d|L`, choose the orbitwise DFT eigenvectors `b_e[k_t]=lambda_e^t`, with `lambda_e=zeta_d^e`. These form an invertible `K`-valued eigenbasis `B` with `P_9 B=B Lambda` (`Lambda` diagonal). Pick `r_e` so `lambda_e=zeta_L^(r_e)=omega^(8 r_e)`, and define `Q=diag(omega^(r_e))`. Let `M=B^(-1) F`; then

`sigma(M)=Lambda M`, `sigma(Q)=Lambda Q`.

Thus `A=Q^(-1) M` is fixed under the generator `sigma`, so `A` has all entries in the fixed field `K`. This constructs `F=B Q A`. No symbolic fitting or probabilistic argument is needed.

`field_descent.py` independently materializes this identity for `N=16,32,64` in the true degree-`L` finite-field extension `F_1000000009[X]/(X^L-zeta_8)`. The chosen prime is `9 (mod 512)` and has exactly 8 roots of unity at 2-power order in the base field. Every residual coefficient off the prescribed `X^r` vanishes exactly and every Fourier entry reconstructs. **The construction is not a fast FFT:** `A` is not shown to have an O(N) executor uniformly as N grows. The `N=64` case has a 4096-entry base-field factor.

## 3. Proof of Theorem C: no SINGLE polynomial phase mask

For any alleged `F=B2 Q B1` with `B1,B2 in GL_N(K)`, the Galois argument forces `P_9` to be similar, over `E`, to `R=sigma(Q) Q^(-1)`. If `Q[j,j]=omega^(f(j))` for `f in Z[x]`, then

`R[j,j]=omega^(8 f(j))=zeta_L^(f(j))`.

Since `f(j+L)≡f(j) (mod L)`, every diagonal eigenvalue appears a multiple of **eight** times as `j` ranges `0..N-1=8L`. In contrast, the primitive `L`th root eigenvalue of `P_9` has multiplicity **four**. Multiplication by an overall scalar cannot change the multiset of eigenvalue multiplicities, so similarity is impossible. A permutation of phase coordinates or multiplication of each mask value by an arbitrary nonzero scalar from `K` leaves this Galois ratio eigenvalue multiplicity unchanged. This proves the theorem for *all* integer polynomials, not only chirps.

This does not extend to an arbitrary diagonally specified non-polynomial phase schedule: the exact small-size construction in Section 2 is a counterexample to any such sweeping claim.

## 4. Proof of Theorem A: concentration beats arbitrary phases

Write the final `S2` as a fixed-strength Phase 12 or 13 unitary stage. Its two path Cayleys have adjacency operator 2-norm at most 2 and coupling 1/32. For each path Cayley `C`,

`||C-I||_2 = ||2 i alpha A (I-i alpha A)^(-1)||_2 <= 2 |alpha| ||A||_2 <=1/8`.

The extra fixed-strength unitary `V=(I+i epsilon R)/sqrt(1+epsilon^2)`, with `R` a Hermitian involution and `epsilon=1/512`, satisfies `||V-I||_2 <= epsilon`. Triangle inequalities between unitary products give

`||S2-I||_2 <= 1/4 + 1/512 =129/512`.

The inverse obeys the same bound. Consequently, every diagonal entry satisfies

`|S2[i,i]|^2 >= (1-129/512)^2=(383/512)^2>1/2`.

Let `Z` be the cyclic shift and `D=diag(omega^k)`. A Fourier intertwiner satisfies `A Z = D A`. If `A=S2 Q P S1` then, with `M=S1 Z S1^(-1)` and `B=S2^(-1) D S2`,

`B = Q P M (Q P)^(-1)`.

The diagonal entries of `M` lie in `K` because `S1` is projectively over `K`; monomial similarity only permutes its diagonal, irrespective of the values in `Q`. Hence all diagonal entries of `B` would have to belong to `K`.

But the `(1,1)` diagonal entry of `B` is

`beta = sum_{k=0}^{N-1} omega^k w_k`, with `w_k=(S2^(-1))[1,k]*S2[k,1]=|S2[k,1]|^2 in K`, `w_k>=0`, `sum w_k=1`.

Expand `beta` in the `K`-basis `1,omega,...,omega^(L-1)`. The coefficient of `omega^1` is

`c_1 = sum_{t=0}^7 w_(1+tL) zeta_8^t`.

The first contribution has magnitude `w_1>1/2`. The sum of the magnitudes of all remaining contributions is at most `1-w_1 < 1/2`, so `c_1 != 0`. Therefore `beta` is not in `K`, contradicting the diagonal condition. This proves *all N=2^m>=16*, *all orders*, *all intermediate permutations*, and *every invertible complex diagonal* for the stated last-stage family. Strong-coupling retunings do not meet the norm hypothesis and are not covered by this proof.

`galois_gate.py` contains independent exact finite-field reductions of these `c_1` coefficients using the preserved `S2^(-1) D S2` formula, as a regression check, for sizes 16–128 and three stage types.

## 5. Proof of Theorem D: phase-support budget

Work in a field carrying an automorphism `sigma` that fixes `K` and maps `omega` to `omega^9` (for exact cyclotomic phases take a common Galois extension; the two-stage results above do not require any hypothesis on supplied complex phases). Every projectively `K`-valued stage `S_j` transforms as `sigma(S_j)=c_j S_j`; permutations are fixed. For a diagonal `Q_ell`, select a scalar `b_ell` minimizing `rank(sigma(Q_ell)-b_ell Q_ell)=r_ell`. The product-telescoping identity then gives

`rank(sigma(A)-c A) <= sum_ell r_ell`, where `c` is the product of the selected scalar factors.

For an exact `A=F`, the left side equals

`rank((P_9-c I)F) = rank(P_9-c I) >= N - number_of_cycles(P_9) = N-4m+4`.

On each permutation cycle, `(P-cI)` has nullity at most one, for any nonzero `c`. This proves the bound.

For a root-phase mask `Q[j,j]=omega^(f(j))`, the projective rank is

`r_sigma(Q)=N-max_{b mod L} # {j: f(j)≡b (mod L)}`.

For a linear ramp `f(j)=j`, this is `N-8`; for a standard quadratic chirp the rank may fall below the budget for large N. `galois_gate.py` audits these counts through N=1024. These are necessary constraints, not a new `O(N log N)` lower bound.

## 6. Three-stage independent tests (NOT proof of impossibility)

For a proposed exact `A=S3 Q2 S2 Q1 S1`, one can test both `A Z = D A` and `sigma(A)=P_9 A`, implementing `sigma` on phase schedules by replacing powers `omega^f` by `omega^(9f)` and keeping projectively `K` stage matrices fixed. Each black-box probe costs `O(tN)` if all stages are O(N). Exact matrix ranks of the defects for four prescribed Green/Haar/strong-coupling families and `N=16,32,64` are saved to `three_stage_results.json`. The direct DFT passes both identities; all 12 tested candidates fail. Their failure is *sample-only*, not an all-parameter no-go theorem. A separate sparse-phase control verifies the rank-telescoping budget.

## 7. What is and is not established

**Established in Phase 14:** all-four theorems above for their explicit models and assumptions; a sharp threshold for arbitrary one-mask `K`-sandwiches; explicit Galois-orbit phase extraction at N=16,32,64; exact tests in a degree-preserving extension field; and black-box phase/covariance falsification tools.

**Not established:** an exact scalable FFT factorization using our Green/Haar stages, sub-`N log N` arithmetic, O(N) FFT, bit-complexity improvement, numerical stability of an unknown future composition, or priority over existing Galois/linear-algebra literature. The small Fourier factorizations do not count as any speedup.

**Next best experiment:** Explore *two distinct high-conductor phase masks* separated by a K-stage, guided by the eigenvalue-cycle multiplicities of `P_9`, and derive a **uniform** low-cost executor for the K-valued factor created by Galois descent. If that factor is dense and requires expensive work, no speedup has been achieved. Track exact Weyl covariance, Galois covariance, and all input-independent scalar preparation costs from the outset. Avoid re-running single-chirp optimization or generic matrix-density benchmarks.

## 8. Generalized conductor threshold and an exact sparse-work audit

The field obstruction admits a clean parameterized version that exposes a concrete time/phase tradeoff. Let `B=2^b` with `b>=3`, `N=2^m` with `m>b`, `K_B=Q(zeta_B)`, `L=N/B`, and `a=1+B`. The automorphism `sigma_a(omega)=omega^a` fixes `K_B`, has order `L`, and gives `sigma_a(F)=P_a F`. Its permutation on indices has `B` fixed points, `B/2` cycles of every additional length `2,4,...,L`, and

`c(N,B)=B+(B/2)*log2(N/B)` cycles total.

The odd indices form `B/2` cycles of length `L`, so the primitive `L`th-root eigenvalues have multiplicity exactly `B/2`.

**General single-mask threshold:** An exact factorization `F=S2 Q S1` with arbitrary complex diagonal Q and `S1,S2` projectively defined over `K_B` exists **if and only if** `L<=B`, equivalently `N<=B^2`. Necessity is the failure of the permutation `P_a` to diagonalize over `K_B` when `L>B`; sufficiency uses the same orbitwise character-eigenbasis construction as Section 2. This is a field-factored algebraic statement, not a cost saving. In particular, retaining **fixed conductor B** excludes one-mask factorizations at all sufficiently large lengths.

**Polynomial-phase restriction:** For any single mask `Q[j,j]=omega^(f(j))` with `f in Z[x]`, the eigenvalue multiplicities of `sigma_a(Q)Q^(-1)` are multiples of `B` because `j->f(j) mod L` has period `L` and `N=BL`. The permutation `P_a` has an eigenvalue of multiplicity `B/2`, so no such single mask works, even when `B` itself grows and `N>B`.

**Sparse exact construction when `N<=B^2`:** Since `(1+B)^t ≡ 1+Bt (mod N)`, the orbit starting at `k0` has size `d=L/gcd(L,k0)`. Let the cycle eigenbasis be `B_orbit[k_t,e]=zeta_d^(et)` with character `r_e=eL/d`. For each input index `j`, there is exactly one `e` such that `r_e ≡ k0*j (mod L)`. For this row set

`Q[e,e]=omega^(r_e)`, `A[e,j]=zeta_B^((k0*j-r_e)/L)`, and set the other `d-1` entries of that orbit block's column to zero.

Then the single surviving term at output `k_t` is precisely

`zeta_d^(et) * omega^(r_e) * zeta_B^((k0*j-r_e)/L)
 = omega^(k0*j+B*r_e*t)
 = omega^(k_t*j)`.

Every input column has exactly one nonzero A-entry per Galois cycle; therefore

`nnz(A)=N*c(N,B)=N[B+(B/2)log2(N/B)]`.

This explains the previously observed exact counts: N=16,B=8 gives 192; N=32,B=8 gives 512; N=64,B=8 gives 1,280. The parameterized verifier `conductor_tradeoff.py` checks the Fourier exponent congruences for **every input/output pair** up through N=1024, including N=128,B=16 and N=256,B=16, with no floating-point calculations.

**Crucial accounting:** A direct sparse matrix-vector multiply takes Theta(`N*c(N,B)`) work and its coefficient schedule must be generated. The orbitwise eigenbasis `B_orbit` consists of Fourier transforms of lengths at most `L`, requiring O(`N log L`) conventional FFT work (or O(`N L`) naively). With fixed B, the one-mask algebraic regime `N<=B^2` cannot extend to asymptotically unbounded N. With B≈sqrt(N), the sparse A executor above instead costs Omega(`N sqrt(N)`). Thus merely increasing available roots or extracting a Galois-orbit mask does not yield any improvement on O(N log N); it transfers the work into the K-valued residual. **Any further advance must compress or fuse this residual work across growing numbers of cycles**, and account for the higher-conductor scalar preparation.

## 9. Positive two-mask, three-mixer Fourier control (N=128,256,512)

A negative one-mask theorem is not a claim that three-layer phase synthesis is impossible. Here is a constructive control that exactly passes both Fourier covariance gates using **two** high-order phase masks and only `K=Q(zeta_8)`-defined non-diagonal mixing operators. It is a conventional Cooley–Tukey decomposition composed with our field-descent factorization, and **does not beat O(N log N)**.

For `N=8a` with `a=16,32,64`, index the input by `j=j1+a*j2`, where `0<=j1<a`, `0<=j2<8`, and output by `k=8*k1+k2`, where `0<=k1<a`, `0<=k2<8`. The standard identity is

`F_N=(F_a tensor I_8) T_N (I_a tensor F_8) P_in`,

with `T_N[j1,k2]=omega_N^(j1*k2)`. Substitute our **exact** one-mask descent `F_a=B_a Q_a A_a` from Section 2:

`F_N=(B_a tensor I_8) (Q_a tensor I_8) (A_a tensor I_8) T_N (I_a tensor F_8) P_in`.

There are exactly two phase diagonals outside `K`: `T_N` and `Q_a tensor I_8`. The three remaining non-diagonal operators `(I_a tensor F_8)P_in`, `(A_a tensor I_8)`, and `(B_a tensor I_8)` all have entries in `K` (and are explicitly executable in linear work for these bounded base sizes). This is a sharp control for our narrow **phase-depth**, not a new fast transform. For `N=128,256,512` one mask is mathematically impossible by Theorem B, whereas two masks suffice by this identity.

`two_mask_control.py` independently constructs the specified components and checks **all 128 basis vectors** against the direct exact DFT at `N=128`, plus selected basis vectors and an unrelated dense vector at `N=256,512`, modulo 998244353. The factor count is accurate, but the coefficient construction and direct residual work are fully charged: an asymptotic generalization using the ordinary recursive factorization stays O(N log N) or worse, not o(N log N).

**Implication for the actual Phase 15 search:** A green/Haar construction with two phase gates must replace or fuse the `K`-valued middle residual *and* the orbit-eigenbasis work in a way that genuinely reduces total arithmetic. Moving those costs into a precomputed matrix is invalid. Compare with the explicit two-mask positive control at each finite length before making any lower-bound or speedup claim.
