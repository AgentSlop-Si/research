# Attribution and prior art notes

This project continuation DOES NOT claim to invent geometric-input Fourier sums, sparse-difference Fourier formulas, Hankel rank, Prony's method, binary Hankel-matrix counts, or block diagonal resolvent identities. The phase contributions are the exact restricted-input executors derived from the project's Phase 22 `C Z=R C` representation, their combined adaptive finite-field implementation, the root-edge handling and batch-inversion accounting, and the explicit collision-vs-Hankel and collision-vs-cyclic-variation probability audits.

- Classical rational/geometric-sum Fourier identities and Prony/Hankel factorization are standard algebra.
- For finite-field Hankel nonsingularity, credit Daykin's results and García-Armas, Ghorpade & Ram (2011): https://www.math.iitb.ac.in/~srg/preprints/JCT_OnlinePaper.pdf . For a uniformly random m-by-m Hankel matrix over F_q, nonsingularity probability is 1-1/q; the binary case q=2 is precisely 1/2.
- The classical `F(I-Z)^a=(I-D)^a F` identity underlies sparse cyclic-difference FFTs and singular diagonal Green solves; the project-specific contribution is integration with the real parity residual and exact raw-input detection.
- Linear complexity of binary sequences: Rueppel (1986) and subsequent expositions. Our finite-field count is separate from any claims about shortest recurrence in all boundary cases.
- The real-sinusoidal transform is a classical parity-reduced Fourier transform; Phase22 specifies the ordering and normalization.
- The 2026 exact DFT sub-NlogN result is a separate model-specific result: https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/preprints/An-explicit-power-saving-for-the-exact-discrete-Fourier-transform-September-25-2026/main.pdf . The current phase neither adapts nor supersedes it.

**Mathematical scope:** `O(N)` for recognized rank<=2 exponential inputs, for inputs with at most two nonzero first/second cyclic differences, or `O(rN)` for length-N data given as an r-term exponential sum; standard `O(N log N)` for arbitrary raw input. The rank lower bounds concern this exponential-query algorithm family, NOT arbitrary exact Fourier algorithms.
