# Global quadratic-chirp median Fourier sketch

**Checkpoint:** 9 October 2026  
**Status:** New theorem candidate derived from the earlier exact global-chirp decoder, independently checked by symbolic covariance construction, exhaustive small-state enumeration, numerical recurrence tests, worst-eigenvector simulations, and automated tests.  
**Main result:** For dyadic `N=K M`, `K>=2M`, one randomized global-chirp row has an exact worst-case per-output variance operator norm `2M/N`. When `M=Theta(log N)`, that row is decoded in `O(N)` arithmetic. A constant number of output-domain medians therefore gives an `O(N)` full-output approximate DFT with polynomially shrinking energy-relative error. A growing number of rows gives a stronger `o(N log N)` regime with polylogarithmic error above the natural coefficient-energy scale.  
**Not claimed:** exact arbitrary-input DFT, ordinary floating-point FFT accuracy, all lengths, bit complexity, practical superiority to FFTW/cuFFT, or established novelty.

---

## 1. Why this is the right continuation after v6

The v6 predecode-median construction made robust rows cheap by applying the short Cooley–Tukey decoder only once. Its arbitrary-input proof nevertheless paid an additional factor approximately `sqrt(M)` because the local coordinate errors were bounded separately and then summed through the final decoder.

The exact open target was therefore:

> obtain output-domain robustness without paying `R` ordinary `M`-point decoders.

An earlier branch had already produced a different rank-`K` estimator based on one quadratic chirp projection inside each length-`M` block. That estimator has a first-order recurrence which decodes all `N` outputs using one `K`-point FFT and `O(N)` additional work. The earlier audit rejected it as a universal *single-row* approximation because its total reconstruction variance is `(M-1)||x||_2^2`, and because recursive use compounds error.

The missing fact is finer:

> although the total error energy is `(M-1)||x||_2^2`, every individual Fourier output has worst-case variance at most `2M||x||_2^2/N` when `K>=2M`.

This exact per-output covariance theorem makes output-domain median amplification affordable.

## 2. Randomized block projection

Let

\[
N=KM=2^L,
\]

where `K` and `M` are powers of two. Write the input as contiguous blocks

\[
x_{r,t}=x_{rM+t},
\qquad
0\le r<K,
\quad
0\le t<M.
\]

Let

\[
\zeta=e^{2\pi i/N}.
\]

Choose independently:

- `a`, uniformly among the odd residues modulo `N`;
- `beta`, uniformly in `Z_N`.

Define

\[
c_r=2aMr+\beta \pmod N
\]

and the local chirp

\[
S_{r,t}=\zeta^{a t^2+c_r t}.
\tag{2.1}
\]

The omitted block-only scalar is exactly the scalar appearing when the global chirp

\[
\zeta^{a(rM+t)^2+\beta(rM+t)}
\]

is expanded. It cancels in the block rank-one projection.

For each block compute one summary

\[
d_r=\sum_{u=0}^{M-1}S_{r,u}x_{r,u}.
\tag{2.2}
\]

Reconstruct the randomized rank-one projection

\[
y_{r,t}=\overline{S_{r,t}}d_r.
\tag{2.3}
\]

One row estimates the unitary DFT by

\[
\widehat X=U_Ny.
\tag{2.4}
\]

Materializing `y` and using an ordinary FFT would defeat the purpose. Section 3 gives the exact linear decoder.

## 3. Exact `O(N+K log K)` decoder

Define the chirp polynomial

\[
P_n=\sum_{t=0}^{M-1}\zeta^{-a t^2-n t}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

A one-step index shift gives

\[
\boxed{
P_n
=1+\zeta^{-(a+n)}P_{n+2a}
-\zeta^{-(aM^2+nM)}.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

Indeed,

\[
\zeta^{-(a+n)}P_{n+2a}
=\sum_{s=1}^{M}\zeta^{-a s^2-ns}.
\]

The output can be written

\[
\widehat X_m
=
\frac1{\sqrt N}
\sum_{r=0}^{K-1}
 d_r e^{-2\pi i mr/K}P_{m+c_r}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Let

\[
D_j=\sum_{r=0}^{K-1}d_r e^{-2\pi i jr/K}
\]

be the unnormalized length-`K` DFT of the summaries. Substituting (3.2) into (3.3) gives

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\widehat X_m
={}&\frac{D_{m\bmod K}}{\sqrt N}
+\zeta^{-(a+m+\beta)}\widehat X_{m+2a}\\
&-\zeta^{-(aM^2+mM+\beta M)}
\frac{D_{(m+2aM)\bmod K}}{\sqrt N}.
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

Because `a` is odd and `N` is a power of two,

\[
\gcd(2a,N)=2.
\]

Thus the permutation

\[
m\mapsto m+2a\pmod N
\]

has exactly two cycles: the even and odd indices. Evaluate one seed on each cycle directly using (3.3), then propagate (3.4) through all remaining indices.

### Charged work for one row

- form all `K` summaries: `O(N)`;
- compute one length-`K` FFT: `O(K log K)`;
- evaluate two seeds: `O(N)`;
- execute `N-2` recurrence steps: `O(N)`;
- write `N` outputs: `O(N)`.

Hence

\[
\boxed{
T_{\rm row}(N,M)=O(N+K\log K).
}
\tag{3.5}
\]

When

\[
M=\Omega(\log K),
\]

one row costs `O(N)`.

The prototype checks the recurrence against independently materializing `y` and applying NumPy's reference FFT. Across `N=64,256,1024,4096`, the largest observed discrepancy was approximately `5.47e-13`.

## 4. Unbiasedness

Inside a fixed block,

\[
\overline{S_{r,t}}S_{r,u}
=\zeta^{a(u^2-t^2)+2aMr(u-t)+\beta(u-t)}.
\]

Averaging over uniform `beta` gives

\[
\mathbb E_\beta[\overline{S_{r,t}}S_{r,u}]
=\mathbf 1_{t=u},
\]

because `|u-t|<M<=N`. Therefore

\[
\mathbb E[y]=x
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E[\widehat X_m]=X_m
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

for every input and every output.

## 5. Exact worst-case per-output variance

This section is the new mathematical core.

### 5.1 Error coefficient

Fix an output frequency `m`. Let

\[
n=rM+u.
\]

The coefficient multiplying source value `x_n` in

\[
\widehat X_m-X_m
\]

is

\[
h_n
=\frac{\zeta^{-mn}}{\sqrt N}
\sum_{d\in D_u}
\zeta^{a(2nd-d^2)+(\beta+m)d},
\tag{5.1}
\]

where

\[
D_u=\{u-t:0\le t<M,\ t\ne u\}.
\]

For two source coordinates `n=rM+u` and `n'=r'M+u'`, averaging over `beta` forces the same displacement `d` in both copies. Hence the covariance matrix is

\[
Q^{(m)}_{n,n'}
=\frac{\zeta^{m(n-n')}}N
\sum_{d\in D_u\cap D_{u'}}
\mathbb E_{a\text{ odd}}
\zeta^{2ad(n'-n)}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

The diagonal modulation by `m` only unitarily conjugates the covariance, so its eigenvalues are independent of the chosen output.

### 5.2 Odd-character identity

For `N=2^L`,

\[
\mathbb E_{a\text{ odd}}\zeta^{ac}
=
\begin{cases}
\zeta^c,&N\mid 2c,\\
0,&N\nmid 2c.
\end{cases}
\tag{5.3}
\]

With `c=2d(n'-n)`, a term survives exactly when

\[
N\mid 4d(n'-n).
\tag{5.4}
\]

When it survives, its value is `+1` or `-1`.

### 5.3 Dyadic decoupling when `K>=2M`

Write

\[
d=2^v q,
\qquad q\text{ odd},
\qquad 0\le v<\log_2 M.
\]

Condition (5.4) forces

\[
n'-n\equiv0\pmod{N/2^{v+2}}.
\tag{5.5}
\]

Assume

\[
K\ge2M.
\tag{5.6}
\]

Then `N/2^(v+2)` is a multiple of `M` for every possible `v`. Thus (5.5) implies

\[
u'=u.
\]

The covariance splits into `M` independent blocks indexed by the within-block coordinate `u`.

For fixed `u` and `v`, let

\[
c_{u,v}
=
\#\{d\in D_u:\nu_2(|d|)=v\}.
\tag{5.7}
\]

Let

\[
g_v=2^{v+2}.
\]

On the block index `r in Z_K`, every displacement with valuation `v` produces the same matrix. It is `g_v` times the orthogonal projector onto Fourier modes

\[
\ell\equiv g_v/2\pmod{g_v},
\tag{5.8}
\]

i.e. modes satisfying

\[
\nu_2(\ell)=v+1.
\]

The projectors for distinct `v` are mutually orthogonal.

Therefore the covariance eigenvalues in the `u` block are exactly

\[
\frac{c_{u,v}g_v}{N}.
\tag{5.9}
\]

### 5.4 Counting the displacements

The positive magnitudes in `D_u` are `1,...,u`; the negative magnitudes are `1,...,M-1-u`. For `q=2^(v+1)` and `h=2^v`, the number of integers of valuation `v` in `1,...,L` is

\[
\left\lfloor\frac{L+h}{q}\right\rfloor.
\]

Thus

\[
\begin{aligned}
c_{u,v}
={}&
\left\lfloor\frac{u+2^v}{2^{v+1}}\right\rfloor
+
\left\lfloor\frac{M-1-u+2^v}{2^{v+1}}\right\rfloor\\
\le{}&\frac{M}{2^{v+1}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.10}
\]

Multiplying by `g_v=2^(v+2)` gives

\[
c_{u,v}g_v\le2M.
\tag{5.11}
\]

Equality is attained, for example, at `u=0,v=0`.

### Theorem 5.1 — exact covariance norm

For dyadic `N=KM`, `M>=2`, and `K>=2M`, for every Fourier output `m`,

\[
\boxed{
\left\|Q^{(m)}\right\|_{2\to2}
=\frac{2M}{N}.
}
\tag{5.12}
\]

Consequently, for every input,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E|\widehat X_m-X_m|^2
\le
\frac{2M}{N}\|x\|_2^2.
}
\tag{5.13}
\]

The constant is sharp. A unit vector supported on within-block coordinate `u=0` and block Fourier mode `ell=2 mod 4` attains equality for output `m=0`; modulation gives a corresponding extremizer for every other output.

### Independent verification

The bundle contains three checks:

1. exhaustive enumeration of every odd `a` and every `beta` for a small instance;
2. direct construction and diagonalization of the covariance formula;
3. Monte Carlo simulation on the explicit worst eigenvector.

For all tested theorem-regime pairs, the computed operator norm agreed with `2M/N` to approximately `1e-15` relative precision. With 1,000 randomized rows, the measured worst-eigenvector variance ratios were approximately `0.954` to `0.984` in the saved deterministic run.

## 6. Output-domain median theorem

Take

\[
R=2s-1
\]

independent chirp rows and define the coordinatewise complex median

\[
\widetilde X_m
=
\operatorname{med}_j\Re\widehat X_m^{(j)}
+i\operatorname{med}_j\Im\widehat X_m^{(j)}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

For `2s-1` iid real random variables, the median moment theorem gives

\[
\mathbb E|\operatorname{med}(Y_1,\ldots,Y_{2s-1})-\mathbb EY_1|^{2s}
\le
{2s-1\choose s}
\left(\mathbb E|Y_1-\mathbb EY_1|^2\right)^s.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Apply (6.2) separately to the real and imaginary parts, use (5.13), Markov's inequality, and union over `2N` real coordinates.

### Theorem 6.1 — simultaneous full-output approximation

Let

\[
C_s={2s-1\choose s}.
\]

With probability at least `1-delta`,

\[
\boxed{
\|\widetilde X-U_Nx\|_\infty
\le
2
\left(\frac{2NC_s}{\delta}\right)^{1/(2s)}
\sqrt{\frac{M}{N}}\,\|x\|_2.
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

This is an output-domain bound; no final triangle inequality over `M` local coordinates is used.

## 7. Consequences

Let

\[
L=\log_2N,
\qquad
\ell=\log_2L.
\]

### 7.1 Linear-time full-output approximation

Choose

\[
M=\Theta(L)
\]

as a power of two. Then `K log K=O(N)`, so every row costs `O(N)`.

For any fixed `s>=2` and fixed failure probability `delta`, (6.3) gives

\[
\boxed{
T(N)=O(N),
\qquad
S(N)=O(N),
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\|\widetilde X-U_Nx\|_\infty
=O\!\left(
\sqrt{\log N}\,
N^{-1/2+1/(2s)}
\|x\|_2
\right).
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

Concrete constant-row points:

- `R=3` (`s=2`):

  \[
  O\!\left(\sqrt{\log N}\,N^{-1/4}\|x\|_2\right);
  \]

- `R=5` (`s=3`):

  \[
  O\!\left(\sqrt{\log N}\,N^{-1/3}\|x\|_2\right);
  \]

- `R=9` (`s=5`):

  \[
  O\!\left(\sqrt{\log N}\,N^{-2/5}\|x\|_2\right).
  \]

Since all `N` outputs must be written, `O(N)` is the natural minimum output cost in this explicit-output model.

For polynomial failure `delta=N^{-a}`, any fixed

\[
s>1+a
\]

still yields a polynomially shrinking error while retaining `O(N)` time.

### 7.2 Stronger `o(N log N)` coefficient-scale regime

Let `f(N)` satisfy

\[
1\le f(N)=o(\log\log N)
\]

and choose

\[
M=\Theta\!\left(\frac{L}{f(N)}\right).
\tag{7.3}
\]

Then one row costs

\[
O(Nf(N)).
\]

Fix constants `a>=0`, `epsilon>0`, set

\[
\delta=N^{-a},
\]

and choose

\[
s=\Theta\!\left(
\frac{(1+a)L}{\varepsilon\ell}
\right).
\tag{7.4}
\]

Since `C_s<=2^(2s-1)`, the amplification factor in (6.3) is

\[
O(L^\varepsilon).
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
T(N)
=O\!\left(
\frac{1+a}{\varepsilon}
\frac{f(N)}{\log\log N}
N\log N
\right)
=o(N\log N),
}
\tag{7.5}
\]

and, with probability at least `1-N^(-a)`,

\[
\boxed{
\|\widetilde X-U_Nx\|_\infty
=O\!\left(
\frac{(\log N)^{1/2+\varepsilon}}{\sqrt{f(N)}}
\frac{\|x\|_2}{\sqrt N}
\right).
}
\tag{7.6}
\]

For `f=1`, the time is

\[
O\!\left(\frac{N\log N}{\log\log N}\right)
\]

and the error is only a `(log N)^(1/2+epsilon)` factor above the natural coefficient-energy scale `||x||_2/sqrt(N)`.

A slowly growing `f=o(log log N)` improves the error by `sqrt(f)` while preserving strict little-`o(N log N)` time.

### Memory qualification

- Constant `R`: storing all rows and taking medians costs `O(N)` memory.
- Growing `R=Theta(log N/log log N)`: the straightforward exact coordinatewise median uses `O(RN)` storage.

The time theorem remains valid, but an `O(N)`-memory implementation of the growing-row regime is **not** claimed. Exact medians of many arbitrary streaming values have a genuine storage/recomputation issue. This is now an explicit open engineering/theory obligation rather than a hidden omission.

## 9. What changed relative to the earlier global-chirp audit

The earlier negative result was correct but too coarse for this use:

\[
\mathbb E\|y-x\|_2^2=(M-1)\|x\|_2^2.
\]

That identity says one row is not a uniformly accurate reconstruction in total `l2` norm. It does **not** imply that a single Fourier output has variance of that same scale.

The dyadic covariance decomposition proves the sharper statement

\[
\sup_{\|x\|_2=1}
\mathbb E|\widehat X_m-X_m|^2
=\frac{2M}{N}.
\]

The `1/N` factor is what allows robust output medians to give a useful full-output theorem.

## 10. Prior-art boundary

The proof uses two known ingredients:

1. quadratic chirps and chirp recurrences;
2. the general moment inequality for medians of iid estimators.

The median theorem used here appears in:

- K. G. Larsen, R. Pagh, J. Tětek, *CountSketches, Feature Hashing and the Median of Three*, ICML 2021, Theorem 5.

Quadratic chirps, chirp sensing matrices, and chirp-based sparse recovery are also established areas. A limited search did not identify this exact combination:

- contiguous rank-one chirp block projection;
- exact two-cycle full-output decoder;
- exact dyadic covariance norm `2M/N`;
- output-domain median theorem for all `N` coefficients.

That search is not a novelty proof. The result should be treated as a project-originated theorem candidate until independently reviewed against the broader randomized linear algebra, chirp sensing, fast transform, and approximate matrix-vector multiplication literature.

## 11. Honest boundary and next target

This checkpoint establishes, subject to independent proof review:

- fresh arbitrary dense input;
- all `N` output coefficients explicitly written;
- `O(N)` time for fixed-row polynomially shrinking energy-relative error;
- `o(N log N)` time for a stronger polylogarithmic coefficient-scale guarantee;
- an exact sharp one-row covariance theorem in the stated dyadic regime.

It does not establish:

- exact arbitrary-input DFT;
- coefficientwise relative error;
- machine-precision error;
- numerical stability of an `N`-step recurrence at huge `N`;
- finite-precision or bit complexity;
- all non-dyadic lengths;
- linear memory for the growing-row exact-median regime;
- practical runtime wins.

The immediate next target is no longer vague:

> stabilize and finite-precision-audit the recurrence, then remove the `O(RN)` median storage in the growing-row regime without restoring `N log N` work.

A second mathematical target is to extend the covariance decomposition beyond `K>=2M` and beyond powers of two.
