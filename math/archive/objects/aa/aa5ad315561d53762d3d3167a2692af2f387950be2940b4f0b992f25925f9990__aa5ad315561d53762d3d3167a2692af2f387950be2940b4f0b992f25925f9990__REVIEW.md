# Independent review: the four-point calibration and quadrant obstruction

9 October 2026 UTC. No supplied program was run or imported. The independent checker reconstructs the operators from sparse denominators and checks the claims exactly.

## Verdict

- The supplied full four-point Fourier matrix is exactly a sum of three independently routed 2-by-2 grid Green operators with nonzero endpoint diagonals. Three is the minimum number of additive terms in that explicitly defined class.
- A sum of the four native quadrant-oriented grid Green operators, even with arbitrary edge masks and arbitrary endpoint diagonals, cannot equal the full Fourier matrix of size b^2 for any integer b>=4.
- These are scoped structural results. They do not exclude products of sums, arbitrary unrelated routes, or general exact Fourier algorithms, and they do not establish a speedup.

## 1. Exact three-term F4 calibration

For a 2-by-2 grid define

    U = I - b E10 - a E20 - c E31 - d E32,
    G(b,a,c,d)=U^(-1).

Its four rows are

    [1,0,0,0], [b,1,0,0], [a,0,1,0], [cb+da,c,d,1].

The independent test reconstructs each G by inverting U rather than using this displayed formula. Set

    A = diag(1/2,-i,1,-i/2) G(i,1,-2,-2i).

Start the second term with

    B0 = diag(-i/2,-2,-2,1/2) G(1/2,-i/2,2,2),

and place its (r,c) entry at row (3,2,1,0)[r] and column (3,1,2,0)[c] to obtain B. Set

    C = diag((1+i)/2,1,1,3) P_reverse.

C is a routed identity Green operator with all edge weights zero. Its endpoint diagonal is nonzero. All three components are invertible, with determinants -1/4, i, and 3/2+3i/2. Direct exact matrix arithmetic gives

    A+B+C = F4,   (F4)[r,c]=(-i)^(r c).

Thus this is a genuine full-input finite-size positive calibration of the primitive family. It is not an all-size construction or an asymptotic claim.

## 2. Why two additive terms cannot suffice

Each 2-by-2 grid Green operator, with any masks and endpoint diagonals, has support contained in the nine-position grid-comparability pattern

    S={(0,0),(1,0),(1,1),(2,0),(2,2),(3,0),(3,1),(3,2),(3,3)}.

Normalize the first term's independent row/column permutations away. The Fourier target is then a row/column permutation of F4. Of all 4! times 4!=576 relative row/column routes for the other term, exactly two have S union routed(S) equal to the full 4-by-4 support. In both cases the overlap is exactly {(0,0),(3,3)}. This remains a necessary support condition when masks or endpoint diagonals vanish; smaller actual support cannot create another cover.

For any endpoint-scaled Green term X=D G E, the exact path invariant is

    X30 = X31 X10 / X11 + X32 X20 / X22.

The entries used in this invariant are all outside the overlap. Therefore they must equal their corresponding Fourier entries individually. In particular X11 and X22 are nonzero, justifying the divisions even if endpoint diagonals were initially allowed to vanish. Dividing by the nonzero X30 gives

    1 = u+v,

where u and v are fourth roots of unity. No two fourth roots have sum 1. Hence no two-term decomposition exists.

The invariant is unchanged by nonzero target row/column scalings, since the diagonal factors cancel from both cross ratios. In particular normalization of the Fourier matrix does not affect the argument. Independent controls verified all 576 row/column-permuted Fourier targets and all 16 pairs of fourth roots. The symbolic identity was also checked with arbitrary nonzero endpoint symbols. Together with the three-term construction, this establishes the exact additive minimum of three for the specified four-point class.

A separate two-stage/two-monomial-terms-per-stage identity reconstructs the ordinary radix-two F4 circuit exactly. Zero-edge Green operators contain monomial terms, so this is expected positive calibration. Its full-size extension has logarithmically many stages and retains ordinary FFT work.

## 3. All-size four-quadrant exclusion

Fix any integer b>=4 and N=b^2. On a b-by-b grid, let the four terms have the four orientations (++),(+-),(-+),(--). An orientation is obtained by conjugating the standard zero-boundary directed-grid Green solve by coordinate flips t->b-1-t and/or u->b-1-u. Every term may have independent, arbitrary complex edge masks and arbitrary left and right diagonal factors. Scalars can be absorbed into these diagonals.

The theorem concerns the sum of these four terms in native row-major grid order. It does not allow arbitrary additional row/column routes or products between the terms.

Choose the three target positions

    i=(3,3),   i_t=(2,3),   i_u=(3,2),

and source positions

    j0=(0,0),   j1=(0,1),   jb=(1,0).

Each source is strictly below-left of each target. Therefore only the (++) term can contribute to any of these nine entries. The other three orientations have structural zero entries there, independently of their masks and diagonals.

Write the (++) term as X=D G E. If the four-term sum equals F_N, then on the nine selected positions X equals F_N and is nonzero. Thus the three relevant row-diagonal values D_i,D_(i_t),D_(i_u) and selected column-diagonal values are forced nonzero. This explicitly covers branches with zero endpoint entries elsewhere.

The Green recurrence at row i, where none of the sources equals i, now gives constants A,B independent of j such that

    F[i,j] = A F[i_t,j] + B F[i_u,j]

for j=0,1,b in row-major indexing. Specifically A and B absorb the two edge weights and the appropriate row-diagonal ratios. For the negative-sign Fourier convention F[r,j]=omega^(-r j), division by its nonzero entries yields

    1 = A omega^(b j) + B omega^j,   j=0,1,b.

Since omega^(b^2)=1, the coefficient matrix for these three equations is, up to column signs,

    [1,1,1], [1,omega^b,omega], [1,1,omega^b].

Its determinant is (omega^b-1)^2. It is nonzero: omega is primitive of order b^2, so omega^b has order b>1. The three equations are inconsistent. Equivalently j=0 and j=b force B=0 and A=1, then j=1 would force omega^b=1.

This proves the exclusion for every integer b>=4 and both Fourier signs. It does not depend on a finite search, rationality of masks, generic invertibility of stage sums, or nonzero edge weights. The component Green denominators remain unit triangular for all masks.
