# Opposite-sign cycle pairs: exact all-pass escape and a scoped logarithmic gate

9 October 2026. Independently derived and verified. No Fourier speedup or literature-priority claim.

Addendum: `COMMUTING_PHASE_GATE.md` extends the logarithmic factor-count gate to a larger, precisely specified family of phase-rotated commuting cycles. It proves complete pairing for scalar-unitary words shorter than N/2 and handles longer words by their factor count. The literal fixed-operator result in Section 8 remains stronger in its narrower family: impossibility of flatness at any depth.

## Outcome

Allowing inverse cycle stages really does escape the forward-only singular-value obstruction: a forward/inverse pair can produce a nontrivial dense unitary in linear scalar arithmetic. The pair is not necessarily a disguised identity. However, every opposite-sign two-stage scalar-unitary product with unitary monomial separators has one of exactly two forms when N >= 3: monomial cancellation, or a geometrically decaying/rising all-pass unitary followed by a monomial. Neither form is Fourier. Products assembled from these individually unitary pair blocks need Omega(log N) nontrivial pairs to produce Fourier. A commuting word using literally one fixed weighted operator M and its adjoint M* cannot produce a flat unitary at any depth.

This main proof does not classify longer mixed words whose individual adjacent pairs are not unitary; the phase-rotated commuting subfamily is addressed separately in `COMMUTING_PHASE_GATE.md`. Global cancellation across three or more stages remains outside this result. In particular, it is not a general arithmetic lower bound and does not resolve an exact arbitrary-input, all-output o(N log N) Fourier algorithm.

## 1. Model and notation

Let M be a unit-modulus weighted full N-cycle with total cycle weight -1. Thus M is unitary, M^N=-I, and all N distinct roots of z^N+1 are its eigenvalues. Define

    K(M) = I+(I-M)^-1 = (2I-M)(I-M)^-1.

A unitary monomial Q is a permutation times a unit-modulus diagonal. No arbitrary dense unitaries, unequal-magnitude diagonal gains, or uncharged roots are supplied for a construction. Both K and K^-1 are permitted. We treat N >= 3 unless explicitly stated otherwise.

Write a star for conjugate transpose. Since M is normal, all displayed rational functions of M and M* commute with one another. The inverses exist: 1 and 2 are not eigenvalues of M.

## 2. A Gram identity that recovers the undirected cycle

Let L(M)=2I-M-M*=(I-M)*(I-M), which is positive definite. Direct multiplication gives

    K(M)* K(M) = 2I + L(M)^-1.                         (1)

For example, at an eigenvalue exp(i theta), the squared singular value is 2+1/(2-2 cos theta). In addition,

    det K(M) = (2^N+1)/2.                              (2)

The determinant is independent of the cycle order and weights, not just independent in magnitude.

## 3. Exact classification of two opposite-sign stages

Theorem. Let Q be a unitary monomial and epsilon be +1 or -1. If

    A = K(M1)^epsilon Q K(M2)^(-epsilon)

is scalar-unitary, then its scalar has magnitude 1, so A itself is unitary. Moreover, with C=Q M2 Q*, exactly one of the following occurs:

    C=M1:       A=Q;
    C=M1*:      A=V(M1)^epsilon Q,

where

    V(M) = K(M) K(M*)^-1 = (M-2I)(2M-I)^-1.           (3)

Conversely, each of these two conditions makes A unitary.

Proof. Equation (2) and |det Q|=1 imply |det A|=1. If A=beta W for a unitary W, then |beta|^N=1, hence |beta|=1. For epsilon=+1, the equality A*A=I is equivalent to

    Q* [K(M1)*K(M1)] Q = K(M2)*K(M2).

For epsilon=-1, the same conclusion follows by taking inverses of the corresponding Gram equality, using the normality of K(M2). Applying (1), subtracting 2I, and inverting gives

    M1+M1* = Q(M2+M2*)Q* = C+C*.                      (4)

For N >= 3, each nonzero undirected edge of a full directed N-cycle contributes exactly one directed entry from M and its conjugate transpose. No edge overlaps its reverse within M. Thus the support of M+M* is exactly the underlying undirected N-cycle, with nonzero edge weights. Equation (4) forces C and M1 to have the same underlying undirected cycle.

A directed full N-cycle on a fixed undirected N-cycle has only the two global orientations. One cannot reverse some proper subset of edges and remain a directed full cycle. Equality of the individual edge entries in (4) then fixes all weights. Hence C=M1 or C=M1*. Functional calculus gives Q K(M2) Q*=K(C), so

    A=K(M1)^epsilon K(C)^(-epsilon)Q.

This is precisely the stated result. The converse follows from normality: K(M*)=K(M)*, so the ratio K(M)K(M)*^-1 is unitary. QED.

Unitary monomial factors before or after the displayed pair do not affect either its scalar-unitary property or the classification up to such endpoints. Separate nonzero global scalars may be removed first. If the resulting pair is scalar-unitary, the normalized pair has the classification above.

Boundary warning. At N=2, M*=-M and M+M*=0 for every allowed cycle. The Gram matrix is (5/2)I and does not recover the weighted cycle. The classification proof therefore does not apply at N=2. No limiting or asymptotic claim here needs that case.

## 4. The nontrivial all-pass unitary is explicit

It is convenient to describe V and its inverse U:

    U(M)=V(M)^-1=K(M*) K(M)^-1
        =(I-2M)(2I-M)^-1=2I-3(2I-M)^-1.              (5)

This is unitary because it is the ratio of a normal matrix and its adjoint. Equivalently, numerator and denominator in the first rational expression in (5) have equal squared modulus on the unit circle.

Using M^N=-I,

    (2I-M)^-1 = [sum(j=0,...,N-1) 2^(N-1-j) M^j]
                 /(2^N+1).

Consequently

    U(M) = [(2^(N-1)+2)I
            - sum(j=1,...,N-1) 3*2^(N-1-j) M^j]
           /(2^N+1),                                 (6)

and, by taking adjoints and reducing powers using M^N=-I,

    V(M) = [(2^(N-1)+2)I
            + sum(j=1,...,N-1) 3*2^(j-1) M^j]
           /(2^N+1).                                 (7)

In any column, the N powers I,M,...,M^(N-1) have their unit-magnitude nonzero entries in N different positions. Thus U and V are fully dense, and every row/column has the coefficient magnitudes displayed in (6)/(7).

This also proves that one opposite-sign pair, even with monomial endpoints, cannot be a scalar Fourier matrix for N >= 3. Cancellation gives a monomial, which is not dense. In the nontrivial case, the coefficients of M and M^2 have magnitudes differing by a factor of 2. Fourier has constant entry magnitude, and monomial endpoints do not change entry magnitudes except by reordering them. No finite-size fit is used in this argument.

## 5. An exact mixing-norm budget

Summing the coefficient magnitudes in (6) or (7) gives

    ||U||_1 = ||U^-1||_1 = ||V||_1 = ||V^-1||_1
            = c_N = (2^(N+1)-1)/(2^N+1)
            = 2 - 3/(2^N+1) < 2.                     (8)

Therefore kappa_1(U)=kappa_1(V)=c_N^2<4. These identities hold for every allowed cycle order and unit weights, because the powers have disjoint support within each column. The same norm bounds hold after unitary monomial dressing.

Let an exact product of r nontrivial, individually unitary opposite-sign pair blocks, any number of monomials, and global scalars equal Fourier up to a nonzero scalar. Remove global scalars. The remaining product is unitary, so the normalized target is a phase times normalized Fourier F, with ||F||_1=sqrt(N). Submultiplicativity then forces

    c_N^r >= sqrt(N),
    r >= log(N)/(2 log(c_N)) > log(N)/log(4).          (9)

Trivial cancelling pair blocks have norm 1 and do not help the budget. Equation (9) counts nontrivial unitary blocks, each represented by two K-type stages but also independently executable as one O(N) all-pass pass.

## 6. Combining these pairs with the earlier Green/rank-one family

Suppose a factorization contains:

- s invertible bare Green stages or their inverses with kappa_1 <= 7;
- r nontrivial all-pass pair blocks covered above, with kappa_1=c_N^2;
- B rank-one perturbations of identity, including Householder reflections;
- arbitrary unitary monomials and nonzero global scalars.

The order of all these blocks may be arbitrary. Delete the rank-one perturbations. A telescoping expansion gives an invertible remaining product G whose difference from the target has rank at most B. The bundled sparse-kernel/condition lemma in LOWRANK_LEMMA.md says that an invertible matrix within rank B of normalized Fourier must have condition number at least N/(B+1)^2. Its conclusion is also valid for B >= N by the trivial condition-number lower bound 1. Global scaling does not affect condition number. Hence

    7^s c_N^(2r) (B+1)^2 >= N.                        (10)

Since c_N^2<4<=7 and (B+1)^2<=4^B<=7^B,

    s+r+B >= ceil(log_7 N).                            (11)

This is a new extension only to *locally scalar-unitary opposite-sign pair blocks*. It does not replace a globally cancelling mixed K/K^-1 word by such blocks without proving that decomposition.

As with the previous gate, an Omega(N log N) work consequence requires an execution model charging Omega(N) for each separately applied full-array block. Factor-count bounds alone do not exclude algebraic fusion or a different evaluation method, and a rank-one factor with sparse support is not automatically a full-array pass.

## 7. Fully charged linear-arithmetic application

The primitive itself is constructive, not an abstract arbitrary unitary. Consider the canonical signed cycle S defined by

    (Sx)_0=-x_(N-1),  (Sx)_j=x_(j-1) for 1<=j<N.

To apply U(S), first solve (2I-S)z=x, then set y=2x-3z. Prepare

    a=2^(-(N-1)), h=1/(2+a).

For a fresh input, compute b=0, then b=(x_j+b)/2 for j=1,...,N-1. Set z_0=(x_0-b)h and then z_j=(x_j+z_(j-1))/2 for j=1,...,N-1. These satisfy both the non-wrap equations and 2z_0+z_(N-1)=x_0. Return y_j=2x_j-3z_j.

The same recurrence with indices reversed solves (2I-S*)z=x and applies V(S)=U(S*). No dense inverse, Fourier transform, or input-dependent preprocessing is used.

Counting a field multiplication, addition/subtraction, or inversion as one operation, repeated halving prepares a and h in N+1 operations. Each fresh application of U or V uses 7N-2 operations with this deliberately simple implementation. It has O(N) memory and O(N) indexing/data movement; all N input and output coordinates are touched. Computing 1/2 costs one more operation if it is not a supplied field constant. One can instead prepare powers by repeated squaring, but that is unnecessary for the linear bound.

Every weighted full cycle is a unitary monomial conjugate of S. Its cycle order and diagonal gauge are found with O(N) index and scalar operations from the supplied cycle weights. Applying the gauge and its inverse costs O(N). Thus arbitrary allowed U(M) and V(M) also have fully charged O(N) scalar-arithmetic preparation and fresh-input application, assuming the N supplied weights and cycle order themselves have a charged O(N) representation. This is not permission to treat arbitrary phase synthesis as free.

This is not an O(N) bit-complexity result. The displayed rational denominator 2^N+1 has Theta(N) bits, and exact intermediate numbers can grow accordingly. Input/output bit costs and numerical stability remain separate. The result gives a valid exact scalar-arithmetic primitive and then proves that composing these primitives as explicit passes cannot itself beat the logarithmic-stage barrier.

## 8. A stronger fixed-operator-and-adjoint obstruction

Consider any word such that, after its monomial separators are pushed to one end by conjugating subsequent stages, every remaining cycle is one fixed M or its reverse M*. This includes arbitrary words in K(M), K(M*) and their inverses, and separators that normalize this unordered pair. It does not include all commuting cycles, phase-rotated cycles zeta M, or arbitrary cycles sharing an eigenbasis.

All remaining factors commute, so the word has the form

    K(M)^p K(M*)^q Q,                                  (12)

for integers p,q and a unitary monomial Q. If (12) is scalar-unitary and N >= 3, then p+q=0. Indeed, its singular values are the singular values of K(M) raised to the integer power p+q. At N >= 3, the eigenangles pi/N and 3pi/N give different squared singular values in (1), so only exponent zero can make those magnitudes constant. Thus every such scalar-unitary word is exactly

    V(M)^p Q.                                          (13)

Here is a stronger obstruction than the logarithmic norm bound: no integer power V(M)^p is flat for any N >= 3.

To prove it, conjugate M by a unitary monomial to the real signed cycle S. This changes no entry magnitudes. Let D=2^N+1, which is odd. Equations (6) and (7) show that V(S) and V(S)^-1 are real rational matrices whose entries have denominators dividing D. Therefore, for every integer p, V(S)^p is a real orthogonal matrix, and every entry has an odd reduced denominator.

- If N is even, flatness would require an entry x with x^2=1/N. The square of a rational number with odd reduced denominator again has odd reduced denominator; 1/N has even reduced denominator. This is impossible.
- If N is odd and greater than one, a flat real orthogonal matrix would have entries +/-1/sqrt(N). The inner product of any two distinct rows would be 1/N times a sum of N signs. An odd number of signs cannot sum to zero. This too is impossible.

Monomial endpoints and global scalars do not evade this: after unitary normalization, Fourier flatness is still precisely magnitude 1/sqrt(N), and monomial endpoints only permute those magnitudes. The proof covers p=0 as well, or that case may simply be recognized as a monomial.

Consequently the entire fixed-operator-and-adjoint family fails to realize Fourier at any number of stages. To pursue this route constructively, changing the cycle beyond this single forward/reverse pair is essential. This is a precise restriction, not a theorem about all longer mixed words.

## 9. Verification and remaining gap

The independently authored check_pairs.py imports no uploaded executable source. The reusable apply_allpass.py implements the weighted-cycle primitive and its inverse with linear-size arrays and exact-capable scalar operations. It verifies the exact algebra, Gram identities, polynomial expansions, coefficient norms, unitary products and linear solver on canonical and monomial-dressed cycles at finitely many lengths. It also exhausts all directed cycle orientations on each fixed undirected N-cycle for N=3,...,7 and checks that only the two global orientations survive. Separate instrumented scalar-operation tests verify the implementation counts. Integer all-pass powers are checked for realness, orthogonality, odd-denominator entries, and nonflatness at selected lengths. The proofs above, not the finite checks, establish the all-N claims.

Remaining gap: an arbitrary longer word of K and K^-1 with monomial separators might become unitary through cancellation that spans multiple factors and cannot be cut into unitary pairs. None of the norm estimates above bounds such a word by c_N per opposite-sign pair without an additional theorem. A useful next finite, explicitly bounded target would be a classification or obstruction for the shortest unclassified globally cancelling mixed words with genuinely changing cycles, rather than claiming that inverse stages are now ruled out. No constructive uniform Fourier family was found in this bounded pass.
