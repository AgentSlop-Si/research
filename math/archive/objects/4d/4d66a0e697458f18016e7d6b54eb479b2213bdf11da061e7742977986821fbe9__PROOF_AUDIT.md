10 October 2026 UTC. Verdict: **ACCEPT the all-height theorem and the stated structural non-containment.**

The audit independently checked the complete candidate proof, the actual inherited separation-rank definition, the accepted separator theorem's admissibility hypotheses, the primary graph theorem, and fresh exact controls. ## 1. Accepted statement and the precise consequence

Let T_h be the perfect rooted binary tree with h edges on each root-to-leaf path and N=2^(h+1)-1 vertices. Put P[u,v]=1 when u is v's parent, and Q=I-P. Over every field Q is invertible, has at most two nonzeros per column and three per row, and

    floor((h+1)/4) <= sr(Q^(-1)) = sr(Q) <= h.

Here sr is the minimum over *independent* row and column orders of the largest rank of a strictly separated rectangle in *either* orientation. It is exactly the parameter in the inherited theorem, not an alternative one-sided or simultaneous-permutation parameter.

Thus sr(Q_h^(-1)) grows as Theta(log N_h), while the bipartite support lift of Q_h is a tree of maximum degree three. The accepted separator framework therefore applies with its constant q=2 throughout this family. For each fixed integer s>=0, choose h=4s+3. Then sr(Q_h^(-1))>=s+1. This proves that the family of inverses of invertible column-two matrices is not contained in any fixed-s ordered-separation class.

That is the precise structural strictness gate posed in the publication assessment. It does not assert the reverse containment, so it should not be restated as a literal proper-superset relationship between the two matrix classes. The accepted wording, coverage beyond every fixed ordered-separation budget, is appropriate. Nor does the result say the inherited theorem ceases to apply with a growing rank budget.

    A[R_k,C_k^c],  A[R_k^c,C_k]

## 3. Inversion, including the required swap of orders

For invertible A and arbitrary sets I,J,

    rank A^(-1)[I,J] = rank A[J^c,I^c] + |I|+|J|-N.

This is the classical nullity identity. The candidate's proof is valid over any field. A vector y supported on J lies in the kernel of the restricted inverse map precisely when z=A^(-1)y is supported on I^c and Az vanishes on J^c. The map y -> z bijects the two kernels. Comparing domain dimensions gives the formula with the displayed sign and swapped complements.

For A ordered by (r,c), A^(-1) must be ordered by (c,r). Taking (I,J)=(C_k,R_k^c) and then (C_k^c,R_k) makes the cardinality correction zero and yields the two displayed equalities of cut ranks. The swap is a bijection of all independent order pairs, so minimization gives sr(A^(-1))=sr(A). This is an exact equality, not merely a one-direction inequality or an assertion confined to common permutations. No principal block is assumed invertible.

The primary source, Vandebril–Van Barel, Corollary 1 (PDF page 7), has the same complementary-rank formula. The fresh field-general proof also handles the empty-block endpoints not covered by the source's nonempty-set statement. [Primary manuscript](https://www.cs.kuleuven.be/publicaties/rapporten/tw/TW397.pdf).

## 4. Family construction and admissibility

Parent-before-child ordering makes P strictly upper triangular, and P^(h+1)=0. Hence Q^(-1)=I+P+...+P^h. Unique ancestral paths imply that the inverse entry is 1 exactly at ancestor-descendant pairs, including equality. These formulas also hold in characteristic two: -1 remains nonzero and no multiple-path cancellation occurs.

Each nonroot column of Q consists of its diagonal and its parent entry; the root column is a singleton. Each row has its diagonal and at most two child entries. Thus the column-two and row-three assertions hold without genericity or nonzero-weight qualifications.

For the bipartite lift, use distinct row vertices rho_v and column vertices gamma_v. For nonroot v, gamma_v lies between rho_parent(v) and rho_v. Gamma_root is a pendant vertex at rho_root. The lift is consequently a subdivision of T_h with one added pendant vertex. It is a simple tree on 2N vertices with 2N-1 edges and maximum degree at most three. Contracting each diagonal edge rho_v--gamma_v gives T_h directly; equivalently delete gamma_root and contract the other subdivision edges.

Its treewidth is one, so the accepted bag-centroid separator theorem supplies q=2. The row-support multigraph's root loop becomes a pendant edge in the bipartite lift, not a cycle requiring a larger budget. Identity endpoint factors are permitted by the accepted inverse-sum theorem, so no endpoint or routing hypothesis excludes the witness.

## 5. Forest support and field independence

For any rectangular matrix with nonzero entries on exactly a bipartite forest's edges, rank equals maximum matching size. A nonzero t-minor supplies a matching of size t, proving the upper bound. For the reverse inequality, select the endpoints of a maximum matching. The induced forest on these endpoints has that matching as a perfect matching. Two distinct perfect matchings would have an alternating cycle, which a forest cannot contain. The determinant therefore has one nonzero term, a product of nonzero entries up to sign. It cannot vanish in any field.

This proves equality with the unweighted support matrix's rank over GF(2), without transferring nonvanishing from one characteristic to another by an invalid genericity argument. Every submatrix of Q_h has forest support, as do all crossing subgraphs of its lift. The lemma is applicable at every order pair and every cut, not just a convenient layout.

## 6. The independent-order to graph-layout reduction

Fix any order pair of Q_h of width s. Interleave row and column vertices:

    rho_r1, gamma_c1, rho_r2, gamma_c2, ... .

At an even cut after 2k vertices, the graph cut-adjacency matrix is, after grouping its two bipartite vertex types, a block matrix with two diagonal blocks Q[R_k,C_k^c] and the transpose of Q[R_k^c,C_k], with weights replaced by support indicators. The forest lemma shows its GF(2) rank is the sum of the two original ranks, at most 2s.

Moving one vertex across a cut removes one column and adds one row. Column deletion cannot increase rank; adding one row increases it by at most one. Thus every intervening odd cut has rank at most 2s+1. This gives

    lrw(G_h) <= 2s+1,
    lrw(G_h) <= 2 sr(Q_h)+1.

Only this direction is used. The argument never assumes an optimal graph layout alternates row and column vertices, and never identifies independent-order sr with graph linear rank-width.

The primary Adler–Kanté Theorem 2 states that a forest's linear rank-width equals its pathwidth. The lift is a forest, so applying it is legitimate. Minor monotonicity is then applied to **pathwidth**, not to linear rank-width (which is not generally minor-monotone). Therefore

    2 sr(Q_h)+1 >= pw(G_h) >= pw(T_h) = ceil(h/2).

Rounding the resulting half-integer lower bound gives exactly floor((h+1)/4), for all four residue classes of h modulo four. At h=0 both claimed bounds on sr are zero. [Adler–Kanté author manuscript](https://perso.isima.fr/~makante/articles/kanteA13.pdf), Theorem 2 on PDF page 2 and definitions on page 4.

## 7. Independent review of the binary-tree pathwidth proof

The candidate supplies its own proof, so the numerical pathwidth formula is not dependent solely on an external citation.

For the forward path-removal lemma, choose x in the first nonempty bag of an optimal tree path decomposition and y in the last. Each vertex has an interval of bag indices. Consecutive vertices of the unique x-y tree path have intersecting intervals because their edge is covered. The union of intervals along this path is consequently an interval meeting the first and last bags, hence every bag. Removing that path decreases every bag's cardinality by at least one. Restricting the resulting bags to any remaining component gives width at most k-1. Empty bags can be retained or removed. The width-zero singleton case has no remaining components.

Conversely, each component off a tree path attaches to a unique path vertex. Concatenate its decomposition with that vertex added to every bag, process attachments in path order, and connect successive path vertices by two-vertex bags. For k>=1, this gives maximum bag size k+1 and preserves every interval condition. Multiple attachments at the same path vertex cause no break.

For h>=2 every simple path leaves a full copy of T_(h-2) untouched in a component: a path missing the root lies in one root-child subtree; a path containing the root can continue down at most one branch at either root child. The forward lemma gives the lower recurrence. A leaf-to-leaf path through the root, using opposite root-child subtrees, leaves only complete subtrees of heights at most h-2, giving the upper recurrence. Together with pw(T_0)=0 and pw(T_1)=1, this proves pw(T_h)=ceil(h/2).

The candidate's proof of pathwidth minor monotonicity is also sound: contracting an edge replaces two intersecting vertex intervals by their union and never enlarges a bag.

## 8. Upper bound and the fully elementary fallback

In common depth-first preorder, all lower Q cut blocks vanish. At any upper cut, each processed row with an unprocessed child belongs to the active ancestor stack before the next vertex is visited. There are at most h such rows. The diagonal cannot cross a cut using the same order on both sides. Hence sr(Q_h)<=h; inversion preserves the result. This supplies a logarithmic upper bound alongside the lower bound, rather than just unboundedness.

The elementary fallback also passes audit. A maximum-degree-three forest admits a proper three-edge-coloring by rooting its components and assigning child-edge colors different from the parent-edge color. Its edges therefore split into three matchings. For a cut subgraph, the edge count is at most three times its matching number, hence at most three times its cut rank by the forest lemma. The interleaved layout has at most 6s+3 crossing edges at every cut.

Each processed vertex with an unprocessed neighbor accounts for a distinct crossing edge when one edge is selected per processed endpoint. Its frontier size is therefore at most 6s+3. The bags consisting of the previous frontier plus the next vertex form a path decomposition: every edge is covered upon reaching its later endpoint, and each vertex persists exactly until its last later neighbor. Thus pw(G_h)<=6s+3. Combining with the preceding elementary pathwidth proof yields

    sr(Q_h) >= max(0, ceil((ceil(h/2)-3)/6)).

This weaker bound already diverges. The structural non-containment therefore survives independently of invoking the external forest rank-width theorem. The stronger stated constant remains correctly attributed to that theorem.

## 10. One wording correction and remaining boundaries

The draft initially said the example does not rely on ill-conditioned coefficient choices. Bounded entries do not imply good conditioning: the root row of B_h consists of N ones, so over R or C, ||B_h||_2>=sqrt(N), while ||Q_h||_2>=1. Thus its spectral condition number is at least sqrt(N). The correct observation is that no large, fine-tuned, or generic coefficients are required; all entries are 0 or +/-1. This is a wording issue, not a defect in the exact-rank theorem, which has no conditioning hypothesis. The author corrected it, and the final candidate wording was verified before hashing.

No novelty certificate follows. Nullity, inverse quasiseparable ranks, forest matching ranks, graph width comparisons, and binary-tree pathwidth are established mathematics. The audit certifies this witness and its relevance to the exact inherited gate; it does not exclude prior appearance of the witness or the separator-sum application. It also provides no unrestricted Fourier arithmetic lower bound, no conclusion about fused evaluation or products, and no impossibility of rewriting B_h as a sum of other structured terms.
