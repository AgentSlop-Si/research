# Independent audit: complete adjacent-anchor boundary and diagonal-orbit closure

## Verdict

**PASS at the stated scope.** All five boundary certificates are valid. Together they exclude every legal point of

`u0 B1 B5 B6 = 0`

for the stipulated adjacent-pair anchor and stipulated second-factor support cycles, over every characteristic-zero field containing a primitive eighth root of unity. Arbitrary zero cycle-edge coefficients and zero pivots are allowed. The anchor ratios remain nonzero, as required by the nonsingular diagonal-orbit grammar.

Combining this result with the **accepted nonboundary theorem** in `adjacent_obstruction_independent_audit_0323/AUDIT_VERDICT.md` excludes the **whole nonsingular diagonal orbit of the adjacent anchor for this fixed support pair**. This combination is justified: the two theorems use the same matrices, support orientation, variables, and boundary product, and their domains are complementary.

No fatal algebraic error, missing boundary branch, unguarded division, support-orientation error, or zero-pivot necessity gap was found. This verdict makes no claim about other anchors, a free first layer, other support pairs, arbitrary endpoint permutations, a general Fourier factorization obstruction, FFT complexity, or novelty.

## 1. Field, support, and full-equation necessity

The coefficient field is `K=Q[z]/(z^4+1)`. The translated polynomial is

`(z+1)^4+1 = z^4+4z^3+6z^2+4z+2`,

which is Eisenstein at 2. Thus the quotient is a field. The checker verifies `z^4=-1`, `z^8=1`, and primitivity, and compares eight fresh field products/cubes with independent formal-polynomial remainders modulo `z^4+1`. All certificates are identities in this abstract field, so the conclusions transport under every embedding into a characteristic-zero extension field.

The independently reconstructed maps are

`pi=(1,2,3,4,5,6,7,0)`,

`sigma=(2,7,5,0,6,1,3,4)`.

Both are Hamiltonian cycles, with disjoint directed edges. With `P e_j=e_map(j)`, the support of column j on the right-hand side of

`U M V = U D_y + D_x V`

is exactly `{j,pi(j),sigma(j)}`. The checker verifies all 64 entries of this right-hand side with generic coefficients. It separately multiplies `F T K_adj` and matches all 64 entries to the stated Fourier formulas.

Legal inverse branches with nonzero diagonal coefficients normalize as `Q0=D_rho U`, `Q1=V D_tau`, where `U=I+D_u P_pi`, `V=I+P_sigma D_v`. Only the diagonal coefficients are divided by in this normalization. Zero edge coefficients are harmless. Multiplying a legal inverse sum by U and V gives the full equation above.

Put `A=UM` and `B_j=A[0,j]`. For `J={1,2,4,5,6}`, row zero is off support. At any eligible off-support row r, the identity

`B_sigma(j) (A[r,j]+v_j A[r,sigma(j)])`
`  - A[r,sigma(j)] (B_j+v_j B_sigma(j))`
`= b[r,j]+u_r c[r,j]`

proves the necessary minor equation without dividing by either pivot. The checker verifies this identity universally, including the cases where one or both pivots vanish.

It verifies all 20 eligible raw resultants using

`R[r;j,k] = c[r,k] h[r,j] - c[r,j] h[r,k]`.

For a zero edge `v_l=0`, the full off-support equation is `M[r,l]+u_r M[r-1,l]=0`, and

`E^l[r,j] = M[r-1,l] h[r,j] - c[r,j](M[r,l]+u_r M[r-1,l])`.

This identity also uses no division. Every row and column used by the certificates is checked to be off support. The chain-quotient identities used in the proof are checked as polynomial identities; necessity after canceling their B factors is handled by the guards below.

These are **necessary**, not sufficient, equations. No reconstruction from a degenerate minor system is asserted.

## 2. Complete ordered boundary partition

The five row-zero equations are

`B1+v1 B7=0`, `B2+v2 B5=0`, `B4+v4 B6=0`,

`B5+v5 B1=0`, `B6+v6 B3=0`.

They imply:

- `B1=0` forces `B5=B2=0`.
- `B5=0` forces `B2=0`; with `B1!=0`, it also forces `v5=0`.
- `B6=0` forces `B4=0`; with `B3!=0`, it also forces `v6=0`.

Therefore the disjoint ordered cases are:

1. `w=u0=0`.
2. `w!=0`, `B1=0`.
3. `w B1!=0`, `B5=0`.
4. `w B1 B5!=0`, `B6=0`, `B3=0`.
5. `w B1 B5!=0`, `B6=0`, `B3!=0`, hence `v6=0`.

All intersections enter the first applicable case. The checker additionally verifies the partition on all 30 relevant zero/nonzero patterns of these five quantities. The logical implications forcing `v5=0` and `v6=0` come from the full row-zero equations, not from a vanishing minor.

For each pair, `N_a=1+w z^(-2a)` and `D_a=1+w z^(-(2a+1))`, with

`B_(2a)=N_a+t_a D_a`, `B_(2a+1)=N_a-t_a D_a`.

If either B entry is zero, D cannot be zero: substituting `D_a=0` gives `N_a=1-z!=0`. Because the legal ratio `t_a` is nonzero, N cannot be zero either. Solving the zero B entry as `t_a=±N_a/D_a` therefore loses no legal point, and its paired entry is exactly `2N_a!=0`.

## 3. Five verified eliminations and all division guards

### Case 1: w=0

The coefficients `c[1,2],c[1,4],c[1,6]` vanish identically, so the actual necessary h equations force their b coefficients to vanish. The checker verifies exact symbolic affineness of `b[1,6]` in t1 and `b[1,4]` in t2. The stored numerator Bézout identities have right side 1, so the respective solved coefficients cannot vanish at a solution.

After substitution, `b[1,2]=0` gives the stated monic quadratic Q. The affine coefficient of t0 in `R[2;4,5]` is protected by the verified identity `S Q+T num(A0)=1`. Substituting the resulting t0 in `R[3;1,4]` gives the stated monic quartic H. The final exact identity is `S_final Q+T_final H=1`.

This case requires no nonzero t ratio in the elimination itself.

### Case 2: B1=0 after w=0 has been excluded

The substitutions for t0, t1 and t2 follow from `B1=B2=B5=0`. Their N and D factors are legal nonzero guards. Since `B4=2N2!=0`, the row-zero equation forces `B6!=0`, justifying the chain quotient L46.

The independently regenerated `L46[1]/w` is exactly affine in t3. A numerator Bézout identity with right side 1 protects its solved coefficient. The remaining `L46[2]` and **full** `R[3;1,4]` give polynomials of degrees 5 and 8 with a final identity equal to 1. No B7 factor is canceled; the B7-zero subcase remains included.

### Case 3: B5=0 with w and B1 nonzero

Here `B2=0`, `v5=0`, and again `B4=2N2!=0` forces `B6!=0`. The same affine L46 equation solves t3. The final equations are `L46[2]` and `E^5[2,4]`, of degrees 5 and 4, with a final identity equal to 1.

The independent checker retains t0 as a genuinely free polynomial indeterminate. It verifies that all expressions used in this case are independent of it. Thus the supplied implementation's zero placeholder is harmless; no legal nonzero t0 was excluded.

### Case 4: B6=B3=0

The row-zero equation forces `B4=0`, giving the stated t1,t2,t3 substitutions. Their N and D factors are nonzero. The paired entry `B5=2N2!=0` and `B5+v5 B1=0` force B1 nonzero as well. This justifies both the B1 and B5 chain cancellations without adding a hypothesis.

The exact affine equation `L15[3]=0` solves t0 with a constant-one numerator Bézout guard. `L25[4]` and `L25[7]` give the stated two quintics and a final identity equal to 1. This certificate actually needs neither the preceding w-nonzero assumption nor independent B1/B5 assumptions.

### Case 5: B6=0, B3!=0, hence v6=0

Here B4 is zero, giving the t2,t3 substitutions. The numerator Bézout identity for the affine coefficient pair in `E^6[1,2]` has right side **w**, exactly as reported. It excludes simultaneous coefficient vanishing because w is already nonzero. It does not prove the corresponding statement at w=0, and no such use is made.

After solving t1, `E^6[2,5]` solves t0 using a separate constant-one numerator Bézout identity. The final equations `E^6[4,1]` and `E^6[4,2]` have degrees 5 and 4 and a final identity equal to 1. B3 is never divided by; its nonvanishing is used only to infer `v6=0`.

### Exhaustiveness of denominator checks

Every stored fraction is compared with its independently reconstructed rational function. Both denominators are then stripped by repeated exact polynomial gcd/division against only the guards justified at that stage. A nonconstant remainder would fail the audit. Solved coefficient numerators are added to the permitted guard set only after the relevant Bézout identity is verified.

This checks the original stored denominators, not merely a conveniently canceled presentation. It allows only nonzero field scalars, legally justified N/D factors, w where already excluded from zero, and the protected solved coefficients. The B6, B1 and B5 cancellations are separate polynomial identities with the logical nonvanishing arguments above. There is no endpoint-scale division or determinant guard hidden in the elimination.

Affineness is checked by complete polynomial support in the free ratio variables, **not** by agreement at a finite sample of values.

## 4. Fresh legal boundary controls and negative tests

Seven new rational controls are manufactured as

`M=U^(-1) D_x + D_y V^(-1)`.

All endpoint coordinates are nonzero and both U and V are exactly invertible. Each control verifies the dense inverse-sum identity, the full matrix equation, all 40 full off-support equations, all relevant row-zero/minor equations, all 20 raw resultants, all applicable zero-edge equations and eliminants, and the actual-second-factor scaling identity using `2Q0,2Q1`.

The controls are:

- u0 zero, other edges nonzero: case 1.
- v1 zero: case 2, with `B1=B2=B5=0`.
- v5 and v6 zero: case 3, with `B2=B4=B5=B6=0`.
- v3 zero: case 4, with `B3=B4=B6=0`.
- v6 zero: case 5, with `B4=B6=0` and B3 nonzero.
- all edges zero: simultaneous zero pivots.
- mixed zero edges: further simultaneous zero pivots.

These are explicitly **non-Fourier controls**, not Fourier witnesses.

For each control, four entries of M are separately perturbed by `1/149`, away from rows 0 and 7. All B pivots, the ordered boundary case, the nonzero endpoints and both determinant guards are preserved. All 28 perturbed candidates fail the full necessary off-support equations and the full matrix equation.

Some degenerate-pivot perturbations still satisfy all the reduced minors/resultants. The checker records this rather than misclassifying those reduced equations as sufficient. In the all-zero-edge control, for example, the pivots make the reduced minors vacuous, while the direct zero-edge equations reject the perturbations. This is consistent with the proof, which uses the full equations whenever it derives a zero-edge constraint.

## 5. Combination with the accepted nonboundary theorem

The accepted nonboundary audit identifies its guards as precisely `u0,B1,B5,B6` for the same M, U, pi and sigma. It proves impossibility when their product is nonzero. The five newly audited cases prove impossibility when their product is zero, under the legal nonzero anchor ratios. These alternatives exhaust every field-valued legal candidate.

The accepted diagonal-orbit reduction gives, for arbitrary nonsingular diagonal endpoints of the adjacent anchor, an equivalent prescribed inverse-sum problem `M=F T K_adj`, with four nonzero ratios. Since `K_adj^2=2I`, a putative solution reconstructs as

`F=(M/2)(K_adj T^(-1))`.

The fresh checker also verifies `M K_adj=2 F T`, retaining the factor of 2. Nonsingular left/right diagonal scaling preserves the specified inverse support and nonzero diagonal coefficients. Thus the whole claimed diagonal orbit, and only that orbit for these fixed second-factor supports, is excluded. Endpoint rescaling cannot evade a contradiction already obtained from necessary off-support equations.
