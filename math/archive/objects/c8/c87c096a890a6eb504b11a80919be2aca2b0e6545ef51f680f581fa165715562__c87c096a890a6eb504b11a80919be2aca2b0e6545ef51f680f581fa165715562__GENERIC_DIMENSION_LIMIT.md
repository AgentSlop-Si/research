# Exact generic dimension of the N16 four-QS1 R-G-R image

Date: 2026-10-09 UTC. This result concerns the dimension of a factorization image. It does not decide whether the particular Fourier matrix belongs to that image.

## 1. The full QS1 stage variety has dimension 5N-6

Assume `N>=2` and work in characteristic zero. Every upper QS1 part has a scalar-transition representation with `N-1` left generators, `N-1` right generators, and `N-2` transitions. This representation covers zero transitions and all degenerate cuts. The analogous statement holds independently below the diagonal.

The parameter map is polynomial, so the image of the nonempty open subset where all transitions are nonzero is dense in the closure of the full image. On this subset, rescaling the scalar states absorbs all transitions, giving the separated form `u_i v_j` for `i<j`. Its dimension is exactly `2N-3`: there are `2N-2` generator parameters and one common scaling freedom. Explicitly, on the open subset where the first-row generators and last-column generator are nonzero, fix `u_0=1`, read all `v_j` from the first row, and read the remaining `u_i` from the last column. No other generic freedom remains.

The lower part has the same dimension and the diagonal contributes `N`. Therefore the full QS1 variety is irreducible with dimension `N+2(2N-3)=5N-6`. Its invertible part is nonempty and open, with the same dimension. No extra high-dimensional zero-transition component exists, since the complete polynomial transition representation covers every QS1 matrix and its source affine space is irreducible.

## 2. The usual internal diagonal gauges

Let

\[
\Phi(S_1,S_2,S_3,S_4)=S_4P_3S_3P_2S_2P_1S_1.
\]

Diagonal row or column scaling preserves all cut ranks. For three invertible diagonal matrices `D1,D2,D3`, replace

\[
S'_1=D_1S_1,
\]
\[
S'_2=D_2S_2P_1D_1^{-1}P_1^{-1},
\]
\[
S'_3=D_3S_3P_2D_2^{-1}P_2^{-1},
\]
\[
S'_4=S_4P_3D_3^{-1}P_3^{-1}.
\]

Their product is unchanged by exact cancellation. On invertible factor tuples this action is free: `S1'=S1` forces `D1=I`, then `S2'=S2` forces `D2=I`, and finally `S3'=S3` forces `D3=I`. Thus every such fiber has dimension at least `3N`, yielding the general bound `4(5N-6)-3N=17N-24`.

## 3. Gray permits two more independent gauge dimensions

Let `H` be the group of block-diagonal matrices `diag(B,d2,...,d_(N-1))`, where `B` is any invertible 2-by-2 matrix. Its dimension is `4+(N-2)=N+2`.

Both left and right multiplication by an element of `H` preserve full QS1. Across a natural cut `k>=2`, the first two coordinates lie on the same side, so the operation either leaves the off-diagonal block unchanged or applies an invertible row/column operation within it. Across `k=1`, the upper block has one row and the lower block one column, hence their rank is automatically at most one. This proves preservation of both off-diagonal directions on both multiplication sides, without any nonzero-generator assumption.

The Gray permutation fixes coordinates zero and one individually. Consequently `G H G^{-1}=H`: its action permutes only the remaining one-dimensional diagonal blocks. In the gauge formulas above, with `P2=G`, allow `D2` to be any element of `H`, keeping `D1,D3` diagonal. All transformed stages remain QS1.

This is a direct product gauge group of dimension `N+(N+2)+N=3N+2`. The same sequential argument proves freeness. This count already includes the `N` diagonal dimensions inside `H`; they are not added again. Equivalently, the extra two dimensions are precisely the two off-diagonal coordinates of the 2-by-2 block, modulo the diagonal subgroup.

Therefore the image dimension for `R-G-R` is at most

\[
4(5N-6)-(3N+2)=17N-26.
\]

At `N=16` this is 246, so the image has codimension at least ten in the 256-dimensional matrix space.

## 4. Exact matching lower bound and characteristic-zero lift

The newly authored `check_generic_jacobian.py` uses the dense separated QS1 chart, retaining its harmless internal generator scaling redundancies. There are `5N-4` raw parameters per stage, hence 304 columns in the N16 Jacobian. Every product entry is a polynomial with integer coefficients in these parameters, because the fixed permutation matrices have entries zero or one.

At two independently generated, reproducible integer parameter tuples, the exact Jacobian modulo 1009 has rank 246. `JACOBIAN_RESULTS.json` includes row and column indices and nonzero determinants for explicit 246-by-246 minors. Each stage is also checked invertible modulo 1009.

Lift the parameter integers unchanged into characteristic zero and evaluate the original polynomial map without modular reduction. The same minor is an integer whose reduction is the recorded nonzero residue, so it is nonzero in characteristic zero. Likewise every lifted stage determinant is nonzero. Thus the lifted points lie in the invertible domain used by the upper-bound proof, and their characteristic-zero Jacobian rank is at least 246. A polynomial image has dimension at least the rank of its differential at any point; combined with the upper bound this proves that the N16 R-G-R image closure has dimension exactly 246.

Only the lower bound uses modular computation. The exact upper bound is the geometric gauge proof, not an inference from two sampled ranks. The verifier separately checks all 48 ordinary diagonal-gauge tangent cancellations at each N16 sample; this is a control on multiplication order, not the proof of the extra GL2 gauge.

## 5. Consequence for certification strategy

A polynomial parameterization of this full four-stage image cannot have a 256-row full-rank output Jacobian at N16. Thus the straightforward square inverse-Jacobian Banach certificate used for the positive F8 three-stage result cannot simply be enlarged to this N16 target. This obstruction applies even if redundant full scalar-transition parameters are supplied: extra parameters do not enlarge the image variety.

The special Fourier target might still lie in the codimension-ten image. Certifying that would require exact compatibility relations, an appropriate reduced system with proven dependent equations, an algebraic identity, or another valid existence certificate. Generic dominance, finite network-capacity survival and small residuals do not establish it.
