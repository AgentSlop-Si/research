# What deficient local gates leave: one exact three-point construction and one five-point rejection

## Results

1. The odd-chirp forced B has an explicit sum-of-two-Green realization at N=3, both over F241 with entirely supplied coefficients and over C with one explicitly declared algebraic root choice. Every entry and feedback guard is verified. This is a finite base case, not a growing-size FFT.
2. A noncommuting N=5 order pair passes every half-cut budget and every individual deficient local gate, but fails global consistency of the row directions. A two-by-two return-map commutator gives an exact exclusion, including zero-edge and singular-endpoint branches. No floating fitting or permutation-space search is used.

These results refine the four-by-four coefficient gate. Passing its rank test is not enough to solve the remaining nonlinear compatibility equations.

## 1. Meaning of rank deficiency

For a fixed pair of denominator rows i,k, the previous report builds a matrix R_ik with four columns, whose nullspace must contain a nonzero tensor

    z=(a_i a_k,b_i a_k,a_i b_k,b_i b_k),
    z0 z3=z1 z2.

The exact local alternatives are:

- Rank four: reject.
- Rank three: one projective null direction remains; it must satisfy the displayed quadratic tensor identity.
- Rank two: the kernel is a two-dimensional vector space. Its projective line meets the tensor quadric according to an explicit homogeneous quadratic; over the working field it may have no admissible point, one, two, or the whole line if that quadratic vanishes identically.
- Rank one or zero: more local freedom remains, not a factorization certificate.

After choosing local directions, the same row direction must be used consistently in every row-pair equation. For each column, all zero-entry equations must admit the same column direction. The nonzero supported entries of Q0 B Q1=Q0+Q1 determine further scale constraints. Finally the original Green denominators, endpoint representation, and full target identity must hold. Singular endpoint branches are checked through the original forward Green terms or valid polynomial continuation, not by assuming their inverses exist.

## 2. Exact N=3 target over the complex numbers

Let w be either primitive complex cube root, w^2+w+1=0. Use the unnormalized F3 and positive chirp

    A=diag(1,w^2,w^2),
    B=(F3^(-1)+A)^(-1).

Direct exact algebra gives

    B=[[a,b,b],[b,d,e],[b,e,d]],
    a=(w+10)/14,
    b=(1-2w)/14,
    d=(9w-1)/14,
    e=-(w+3)/14.

These agree with the equivalent expressions d=(-17-16w)/(4+26w) and e=(1-4w)/(4+26w). The key identities are

    b=w e,   a=w^2 d,
    a/d=(b/e)^2.

All displayed denominators, d, and e are nonzero over C. Let

    D=diag(w,1,1),
    r=2e/d=(6+8w)/13,
    t^2+t=r,
    kappa=d(1-t^3)/2.

An explicit choice is

    t=(-1+sqrt((37+32w)/13))/2,

with either square-root sign allowed. This is an actual additional algebraic root choice, not a field-arithmetic operation silently supplied by w. For this fixed three-point base case it can be declared as a fixed coefficient; no uniform root-generation claim for growing lengths is made.

Both roots satisfy t^3!=1. Indeed, if t is a cube root of unity, then t^2+t is either 2 or -1. Neither equals r for primitive w. Also r!=0, so t is nonzero, and d!=0 implies kappa!=0.

Let P be the row-action natural three-cycle, (Px)[j]=x[j+1 mod3]. Then

    B=kappa D(I-tP)^(-1)D
       +kappa D(I-tP^(-1))^(-1)D.                    (P3)

Proof. Since P^3=I,

    (I-tP)^(-1)+(I-tP^(-1))^(-1)
      =[2I+(t+t^2)(P+P^(-1))]/(1-t^3).

Multiply by kappa D on the left and D on the right. The diagonal becomes d D^2 and every off-diagonal position becomes e D_i D_j, exactly the displayed B. Both Green inverses and endpoints are valid by the proved guards.

A itself is a permitted paired-Green map using two zero-edge identity plans with left endpoints A/2. Thus this supplies all four plans at N=3. The opposite cycle permutations commute; no noncommuting positive family is claimed.

The special identities b=w e and a=w^2 d are exact three-point relations. In the corresponding phase gauge, their simplification uses 3ij=0 modulo3. They cannot simply be copied to general odd N.

The complex feedback loop also has an exact quadratic closure. With M=F3 A and K=BA,

    M^2-(2+w)M+(3+3w)I=0,
    mu(K)=0,
    mu(z)=(6+4w)z^2-(8+7w)z+(3+3w),
    mu(1)=1,
    F3=(-2-3w)B+(6+4w)BAB.

The coefficient 6+4w and an off-diagonal entry of K are nonzero, so the minimal polynomial really has degree two. The complex construction therefore has the same four-Green-scan arbitrary-input evaluator as the finite-field specialization below, once its fixed coefficients and closure inverse are supplied.

## 3. Fully supplied finite-field instance and runtime

Over F241 choose w=15, so w^2=225 and w has exact order3. Take

    A=diag(1,225,225),
    D=diag(15,1,1),
    t=207,
    kappa=2.

Then t^2+t=158=2e/d, 1-t^3=22, and

    B=[[19,84,84],[84,44,102],[84,102,44]]

is exactly the sum in P3. The positive chirp Gauss scalar is G=210, c=3G=148, and 1+c=149!=0. All target and Green inverses exist. These modular constants were obtained from the analytic quadratic equation and are independently verified; characteristic-zero existence is proved separately in Section2, not inferred from this modular example.

Here the loop K=BA has exact quadratic minimal polynomial proportional to

    mu(z)=66z^2+128z+48,
    mu(1)=1.

Therefore

    (I-K)^(-1)=194I+66K,
    F3=194B+66BAB.

The online algorithm uses B twice, diagonal A once, and the final vector combination. Each B is two three-coordinate Green scans, so this is four scan calls with explicitly supplied coefficients. Setup includes the two cyclic inverse plans and their nonzero closure inversions; they share the denominator22. No Fourier action or dense inversion is used online. The A/2 identity plans satisfy the formal four-plan architecture, though direct diagonal application is cheaper here.

This is constant-size work. A conventional three-point Fourier base case is already constant-size, so this construction establishes finite admissible existence and tests the feedback synthesis equations; it establishes neither a practical speedup nor an asymptotic improvement. Dense Fourier matrices appear only in the verifier to check every output entry.

## 4. A noncommuting N=5 local survivor

For N=5 choose

    pi=[2,3,4,0,1],
    tau=[3,2,4,1,0],

so pi(i)=i+2 and tau=(0 1)pi(0 1). Both are Hamiltonian and do not commute. The first cycle crosses every cyclic two-coordinate interval twice, so the pair passes every half-cut budget.

Use the odd-chirp target over F241 with primitive fifth root w=91, G=103 and c=33; 1+c=34 is nonzero. For diagonal-ended branches let u_i=(a_i,b_i)^T denote row i's denominator coefficients. Every row-pair local gate has at most one equation. Seven pairs give a nonzero rank-one row; three give no equation. Every individual equation admits a nonzero projective tensor, so none of these local gates alone rejects the pair.

The seven coefficient rows, ordered (a_i a_k,b_i a_k,a_i b_k,b_i b_k), are:

    pair 01, target column4: [ 50,144, 20, 74]
    pair 02, target column1: [ 20,  0, 50, 63]
    pair 03, target column4: [ 20, 74,  0,101]
    pair 12, target column0: [ 53,184,113,213]
    pair 13, target column4: [178,  0,191,221]
    pair 24, target column3: [ 20,  0, 77,238]
    pair 34, target column2: [213,188,110,213].

Pairs04,14,23 have no eligible column. This is a concrete necessary-condition survivor, until the shared row directions are reconciled.

## 5. Global row-direction obstruction

For one local relation with coefficients (c0,c1,c2,c3), define

    H_ik=[[c2,c3],[-c0,-c1]].

If H_ik is invertible, the local equation is equivalent to

    u_k proportional to H_ik u_i.

This is projective and includes infinite ratios or a zero component: no division by a_i or b_i is used. For the seven rows above, the determinants of H_ik in the listed order are

    [97,55,92,137,55,181,107],

all nonzero modulo241.

Propagate from row0 along a spanning tree. The two routes to row2 and the two routes to row4 force u0 to be an eigenvector of both return maps

    C1=H02^(-1) H12 H01,
    C3=(H24 H02)^(-1) H34 H03.

Exactly,

    C1=[[75,4],[33,140]],
    C3=[[106,125],[20,52]].

A common eigenvector of two matrices is in the kernel of their commutator. But

    C1 C3-C3 C1=[[52,94],[190,189]],
    det(C1 C3-C3 C1)=162 != 0 mod241.

There is no nonzero shared row direction. Thus the pairwise local survivors cannot be made globally compatible; there is no need to fit weights or solve the remaining supported entries. This rules out every invertible diagonal-ended two-Green representation at these orders.

## 6. Boundary branches and characteristic-zero lift of the N=5 exclusion

The target-entry homography determinants are explicitly nonzero. If a putative representation had singular endpoint masks, replace its zero endpoint scales by a formal epsilon while leaving every valid Green denominator unchanged. The perturbed branches are invertible over E(epsilon), and their sum tends to the target B. Each target-entry homography determinant has a nonzero constant term, so all the homographies remain invertible over E(epsilon). The common-eigenline argument forces the rational commutator determinant to vanish identically, and it can be specialized at epsilon=0 because all its displayed denominators remain nonzero. The nonzero target determinant gives a contradiction. Thus the N=5 exclusion includes singular endpoint masks and zero edge weights as well.

For complex primitive fifth roots, all target entries and homographies belong to Q(zeta5). Reduction zeta5 ->91 modulo241 is valid because91 has exact order5. The feedback denominator and every homography denominator used above reduce to nonzero elements. The nonzero commutator determinant162 therefore proves nonzero characteristic-zero determinant. Its Galois conjugates cover every primitive complex fifth root. This is an exact lift, not an inference from fitted modular parameters.

The conclusion is restricted to the displayed cycle orders with diagonal endpoints. Arbitrary endpoint permutations or other cycles need their own compatibility proof. The common-eigenline obstruction is necessary; its absence would not by itself prove a factorization.

## 7. Verification, comparison, and remaining construction target

The newly authored verifier passes the symbolic N=3 target and construction identities (nine entries each), the exact complex quadratic closure, all nine finite-field B entries, all nine scan-evaluated B entries and all three Fourier basis outputs. It checks every displayed inverse guard and the exact loop degree. At N=5 it independently reconstructs all25 target entries, all five half-cut budgets and cut ranks, all ten local pair ranks, seven individual projective witnesses, seven homography guards, both return maps and the commutator determinant. Four genuine two-Green controls include zero edges and singular endpoint masks; their homography guard failures are retained rather than divided away. The two invertible-endpoint controls also check Q0 B Q1=Q0+Q1 and all seven coefficient equations directly. All are exact arithmetic checks.

The N=3 example is stronger than a dense-Fourier oracle control in one specific respect: it supplies explicit admissible Green plans whose online evaluator contains no Fourier call. It remains only a finite base case. The N=5 example shows why rank-deficient local gates cannot be reported as constructive successes.

A growing family must still provide order/weight/endpoint formulas satisfying globally compatible row and column equations, every supported-entry identity and all Green guards, with setup and application inside the claimed budget. Nothing in these small controls supplies that size-lifting mechanism.
