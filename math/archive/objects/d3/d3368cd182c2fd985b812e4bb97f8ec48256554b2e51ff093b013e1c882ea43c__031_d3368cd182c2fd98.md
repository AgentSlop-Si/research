# Exact sparse Fourier expansion: two precise obstructions

## Result

I do not have a constructive worst-case o(N log k) algorithm. There are, however, two clean and complementary obstructions to the proposed routes:

1. At prime lengths, root-of-unity divisibility supplies neither a coefficient-sparse minimal recurrence nor a coefficient-sparse complementary quotient: both are completely dense, for every proper nonempty support.
2. At power-of-two lengths, the usual adaptive residue-splitting FFT, including compression of unary paths, costs Theta(N log k) on typical k-element supports. This is a lower bound for that explicit computation scheme, not for all support-adaptive algorithms.

Neither obstruction excludes a new batched transform or another algebraic identity. In particular, dense coefficients do not imply high arithmetic circuit complexity.

## Model and notation

Let z_s = zeta_N^{j_s} be distinct and f_m = sum_{s=1}^k a_s z_s^m, for 0 <= m < N. Arithmetic counts are exact scalar field operations; the usual FFT arithmetic model permits the required fixed roots of unity. Bit complexity and numerical stability are separate questions. Let M(d) denote the cost of multiplying degree-O(d) polynomials.

When k = Theta(log N), O(k^2 + k log N) preprocessing is negligible compared with N. It must still be included, rather than treating the recurrence or its quotient as free.

## 1. Prime-length density lemma

**Lemma.** Let p be prime, zeta a primitive pth root, and T any subset of Z/pZ of cardinality d < p. Every coefficient of

    R_T(t) = product_{j in T} (1 - zeta^j t)

is nonzero.

**Proof.** For 0 <= r <= d, apart from a sign its coefficient is

    E_r = sum_{U subset T, |U|=r} zeta^{sum_{j in U} j}.

Group the summands by their exponent modulo p. Write E_r = C(zeta), where C(x) has degree at most p-1, nonnegative integer coefficients, and C(1) = binomial(d,r).

If E_r were zero, the minimal polynomial 1+x+...+x^{p-1} of zeta over Q would divide C. Thus C would be an integer constant multiple of that polynomial, and p would divide C(1). But p does not divide binomial(d,r): all three factorials in d!/(r!(d-r)!) use integers strictly below p. Contradiction. This includes the constant and leading coefficients.

**Application.** For any nonempty proper support S,

    Q(t) = product_{j in S}(1-zeta^j t),
    H(t) = (1-t^p)/Q(t) = product_{j not in S}(1-zeta^j t).

Q has exactly k+1 nonzero coefficients and H has exactly p-k+1 nonzero coefficients. This holds for every support, without randomness assumptions.

Consequently, the exact identity Q divides 1-t^p does not create a sparse coefficient representation of either polynomial. The monic degree-k characteristic polynomial of the k distinct exponential modes is also dense (it is the reversed Q up to scaling). This specifically rules out savings obtained merely by dropping zero coefficients from the minimal-order recurrence or its complementary quotient.

The elementary proof alone does not rule out higher-degree sparse annihilators. A standard stronger theorem does: for a nonzero polynomial A of degree less than p, its coefficient support and the support of (A(zeta^j))_{j=0}^{p-1} have sizes summing to at least p+1. If A vanishes on k specified roots, it therefore has at least k+1 monomials. This is a direct application of the prime cyclic uncertainty principle in [Terence Tao, An uncertainty principle for cyclic groups of prime order](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0308286). Thus increasing the recurrence order while keeping it below p cannot lower its number of nonzero taps below k+1. The order-p polynomial 1-t^p is the boundary exception.

These statements do not rule out compact product representations, fast dense multiplication, or low-cost circuits producing dense vectors. Computing the quotient's coefficients can conceivably be linear-time for some supports; coefficient density alone says nothing to the contrary. No novelty claim is made for these algebraic facts or their corollaries.

## 2. Radix-2 occupancy cost

Take N = 2^L. At recursion level ell, split the frequency support by j modulo 2^ell. Let r_ell be the number of occupied residue classes, and let b_ell be the number of those classes whose two children at level ell+1 are both occupied.

Each occupied node represents a transform of length n_ell = N/2^ell. At a binary branch the usual merge produces n_ell output scalars from its two child transforms, with Theta(n_ell) arithmetic work. Unoccupied nodes are omitted. Unary paths can be compressed, so a robust lower bound on this particular scheme counts only genuine binary branches:

    W_branch = Theta(sum_{ell=0}^{L-1} b_ell N/2^ell).

The standard uncompressed upper bound is

    W <= O(sum_{ell=0}^{L-1} r_ell N/2^ell)
      <= O(N log k + N),

since r_ell <= min(2^ell,k). Modulation constants can be generated by geometric progressions at the same order of work; partitioning support lists adds at most O(k log N) elementary operations.

**Random-support saturation.** Choose a uniform k-element subset of Z/NZ, with k growing and k much smaller than N. For an integer h dividing N, any fixed class modulo h is empty with probability at most

    (1 - 1/h)^k <= exp(-k/h).

The union bound makes the probability that some class is empty at most h exp(-k/h). Let h be the largest power of two at most k/(3 ln k), so for sufficiently large k,

    k/(6 ln k) < h <= k/(3 ln k).

Then all h classes are occupied with probability at least 1-O(1/(k^2 ln k)). On this event all shallower classes are occupied as well. For every ell = 0,...,log2(h)-1, every node has two occupied children, giving b_ell = 2^ell. Each such level costs Theta(N), so

    W_branch >= Omega(N log h)
             = Omega(N (log k - log log k))
             = Omega(N log k).

Together with the upper bound, this is Theta(N log k) for typical supports. Unary-path compression and skipping empty branches cannot remove the leading term. Since k = Theta(log N), the saturated shallow depth is Theta(log log N).

This argument counts an explicit radix-2 factorization. It is not a general DFT lower bound, not a rank argument against support adaptation, and not an obstruction to fusing many levels through a genuinely different algorithm.

## 3. Block recurrences: where the cost remains

The generating series is P(t)/Q(t), where deg P < k. Forming the z_s by repeated squaring costs O(k log N); forming Q and the first k values by straightforward arithmetic costs O(k^2). A product tree can improve construction of Q but is not necessary for k = Theta(log N).

Write Q(t)=1+q_1 t+...+q_k t^k. Once the first k values are available,

    f_m = -sum_{i=1}^k q_i f_{m-i},  m >= k.

An exact length-k block can be generated with two polynomial products of length O(k):

- Given the preceding k values and a new block starting at n, form the known-history forcing terms

      g_r = -sum_{i=r+1}^k q_i f_{n+r-i},  0 <= r < k.

  This is a triangular Toeplitz map, computable by one convolution and extraction.
- If G(t)=sum g_r t^r, the new block polynomial is G(t) Q(t)^(-1) modulo t^k. Precompute Q^(-1) modulo t^k once, then use one truncated product per block.

Thus the full cost, including preprocessing, is

    O(k log N + k^2 + M(k) + ceil(N/k) M(k)).

With FFT polynomial multiplication this gives O(N log k + polylog N), for arbitrary N and support. It explains the existing bound without relying on a factorization of N.

Simply choosing larger blocks does not establish an improvement. Segmenting a long product by a degree-k polynomial gives O(B log k) work for B new coefficients using the usual convolution method. Repeating over N/B blocks still gives O(N log k).

### Modal state advancement is already cheap

For a block length B, define v_{b,s}=a_s z_s^{bB}. Then

    v_{b+1,s}=z_s^B v_{b,s},
    f_{bB+r}=sum_s z_s^r v_{b,s},  0 <= r < B.

All block-state advancements cost O(kN/B), after O(k log B) preprocessing of z_s^B. For B >= k this is O(N). Therefore fast advancement of the modal state alone is not the missing improvement. The unaccounted step is applying the fixed B-by-k geometric evaluation matrix to every block state. An algorithm that genuinely batches these applications more cheaply would be substantive progress; merely calling the matrix fixed or precomputed does not pay for its applications.

If B divides N, state eigenvalues alias precisely when j_s are congruent modulo N/B. This can reduce the state dimension on structured supports. Arbitrary supports need not have those collisions. In particular, for prime N and 1 <= B < N, powering is injective on the Nth roots, so the state still has k distinct modes.

## 4. A constructive structured class: few cosets

Suppose d divides N and the support lies in c cosets of the order-d subgroup of the Nth roots. Write the frequencies in coset l as

    j = r_l + (N/d)t,  0 <= t < d,

and set absent amplitudes to zero. Compute c length-d DFTs

    h_l[u] = sum_{t=0}^{d-1} a_{l,t} zeta_d^{tu}.

Then all requested outputs follow exactly from

    f_m = sum_{l=1}^c zeta_N^{r_l m} h_l[m mod d].

Generate each modulation by one geometric progression. Given this decomposition, the full cost is

    O(k log N + k log k + cd log d + Nc),

including root setup, support grouping, the small DFTs, and every output. When the support is the union of the c complete cosets, k=cd; for k=Theta(log N), any c=o(log k) yields o(N log k). A fixed number of such cosets gives O(N+polylog N).

This is a genuinely exact fast class, but does not cover arbitrary supports. At prime N the only subgroup orders are 1 and N, so the useful intermediate-size case is absent. The unknown step is extending the savings to arbitrary supports without assuming a decomposition with small c and small cd.

## 5. What a successful general candidate must supply

To obtain o(N log k), a proposal must give a new cost bound for at least one of the following, with preprocessing included:

- Simultaneously applying the block evaluation matrices to their special orbit of modal states.
- Generating a long dense rational series faster than the standard repeated length-k convolutions, using the root-of-unity restriction.
- Fusing the Theta(log k) fully occupied shallow FFT levels for arbitrary supports.

At prime lengths the dense denominator/complement lemma rules out a coefficient-zero shortcut. At dyadic lengths the occupancy calculation rules out a pruning-only shortcut. Neither is a proof that the desired algorithm cannot exist.
