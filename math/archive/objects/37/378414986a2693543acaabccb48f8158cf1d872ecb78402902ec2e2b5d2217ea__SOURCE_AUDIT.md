## Established mathematical ingredients / independent public leads

- Fourier transform on finite abelian groups; subgroup annihilators and orthogonality. The spectral calculation in `THEOREMS.md` is elementary harmonic analysis and *should not be marketed as an entirely new harmonic-analysis identity*. General reference: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourier_transform_on_finite_groups
- Terry Tao, a 2020 MathOverflow discussion of partial Fourier matrices with subgroup-selected rows and explicit singular values: https://mathoverflow.net/questions/351118/large-fourier-submatrices-with-small-operator-norm . This supports prior art for related subgroup spectral calculations.
- P. Boito and Y. Eidelman, *Computation of quasiseparable representations of Green matrices*, Linear Algebra and its Applications, 2025, https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0024379524001976 . The Green/quasiseparable representation and linear-work scans are preexisting mathematics.
- C. Pernet and A. Storjohann, *Time and space efficient generators for quasiseparable matrices*, Journal of Symbolic Computation, 2018, https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0747717117300767 .
- Berlekamp–Massey/Prony, transposed Vandermonde interpolation and amortization are classical. The user's original `fast_vandermonde.py` supplied the O(r²) amplitude approach.
- The OpenAI Math family #130 uniform exact complex-arithmetic result: https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/lean/docs/130.md . It reports an all-length `O(N(log N)^(1-10^-13))` bound in a permissive exact-complex model. Our project does not beat it for arbitrary fresh inputs.

## Novelty assessment

- **Mathematical correctness:** the local subgroup spectrum, the cross-cut Green rank proof, and the trie accumulation have self-contained proofs, and the positive aggregate-mode algorithm has a fully charged recurrence/recognition argument. Both are independently checked at sample sizes.
- **New within compared project artifacts:** the complete all-divisor spectral cut formula as a *Green-network lower-bound test*; the O(N+E) simultaneous dyadic-coset certificate; and replacing Hero #17's per-fiber cap by a total-mode cap with a heavy-fiber fallback complexity proof.
- **Publication originality:** UNKNOWN. Each component draws on classical techniques. The searches above did not establish a precedence-free claim. Expert peer review and detailed literature tracing are required before a publication novelty statement.
- **Limitations:** no universal exact O(N) FFT and no new unconditional lower bound. Neither successful finite tests nor structural input families imply one.
