# Research report — Fourier spectral determinant / adjacent-tail gate

**October 9, 2026 — exact algebraic analysis, no improved FFT complexity.**

## Research objective

We continue the project's own line of research: fresh-input, exact full discrete Fourier transforms implemented with genuinely smaller **total** scalar arithmetic than ordinary `N log N` butterflies, rather than hiding costs inside preparation, threads, data-dependent caching, nontrivial constants, or renamed stages. The narrower mathematical question in this checkpoint is whether the Fourier matrix at `N=8` factors through **two global, overlapping, nonunitary rank-one separated-generator stages** with the **fixed internal permutation** `[7,0,6,3,4,1,2,5]`.

This is a *candidate architecture study*. A negative answer for it would not settle the general FFT complexity problem. A positive answer at N=8 would not prove asymptotic improvement.

## A. Lower 4x4 spectral-minor obstruction

A Fourier column indexed by `P[3]=3` has, in rows `i=4..7`, the form

`eta3*T_head[i] + z^(3i) + V3*T_tail[i]`.

For rank-one lower B, this must be proportional to `T_last[i]`. Therefore the four length-4 vectors `T_head,T_tail,z^(3i),T_last` must be linearly dependent. Their 4x4 determinant vanishes. Clearing the lower-generator denominator `t(z-1)` produces:

`Q(t,h0,h1,h2,h6,beta)=t*A(h)+beta*B(h)+C(h)`.

Exactly 45 terms, degree 4, with `A,B,C` containing 12/21/12 terms. Coefficients live in the exact cyclotomic ring `Z[z]/(z^4+1)`. On `B(h)!=0` this necessary equation uniquely determines `beta` as a rational function of five remaining variables. Tested in four finite-field embeddings, with an independent direct numeric determinant implementation.

## B. Three upper-stage polynomial resultants

Eliminating row-generator ratios gives `R1,R2,R5` of degree 7 with respective term counts 192, 300, 330. Exact coefficient dictionaries are in `exact_upper_resultants.json`. The four-polynomial Jacobian `(Q,R1,R2,R5)` has a 4x4 minor congruent to 10570 modulo 12289 at the recorded parameter point. Consequently the four polynomials are algebraically independent in characteristic zero; this **does not say** whether they have a common zero.

Each `Rk` is affine in `gamma1,gamma2` (no squares/products), so their joint necessary condition is the vanishing of a 3x3 gamma-coefficient determinant. The determinant has degree at most 18 in six variables and nonzero witness 4050 modulo 12289.

Combining that gamma determinant with the lower quartic yields a **five-variable arithmetic circuit H**, represented *without polynomial expansion*:

`m=-(t*A+C); Ehat_ij = B^2 E0_ij + B*m E1_ij + m^2 E2_ij; H=det(Ehat)`.

Then `H=B^6*G(t,h,m/B)` where B≠0. Its evaluated witness is H=4730 modulo 12289. The zero-B branch is explicitly not discarded.

## C. Adjacent-column tail lemma and the new cubic W

For the chosen shuffle, B upper columns 5 and 6 originate from C columns 1 and 2. Their Fourier transforms restricted to five top rows must be proportional. Their C-column tails are multiples of the same lower-generator tail `L3..L7`. Because C is invertible, the shared tail cannot disappear from both columns. Subtracting a nonzero head-only linear combination and exploiting the full rank of the 5x3 Fourier-Vandermonde head block shows the tail belongs to the Fourier subspace of characters `z^k,z^(2k),z^(3k)`.

It follows that the tail must satisfy two exact recurrences E3=E4=0. After using the known formulas for the lower-generator entries, E3,E4 and Q are **simultaneously affine in t,beta**. Their 3x3 coefficient determinant W depends only on `(h0,h1,h2,h6)`:

`W(h)=det( [coeff_t(E3),coeff_beta(E3),constant(E3)]; [coeff_t(E4),coeff_beta(E4),constant(E4)]; [coeff_t(Q),coeff_beta(Q),constant(Q)] )`.

The symbolic result is a degree-3, 31-term polynomial, not the zero polynomial (evaluation 9966 mod 12289 at h=(2,3,5,7)). The complete 31 terms are in `adjacent_tail_elimination.json`; a reproduction script derives them using *independent* Fourier algebra and an exact cyclotomic determinant.

A solution of all original matrix equations must satisfy W=0 on the relevant regular chart, **but W=0 alone need not imply a valid solution**. Singular 2x2 tail pivots must be analyzed independently. This avoids a common false argument arising from dividing through the very coefficient determinant that vanishes for special Fourier-compatible parameters.

## E. All-length defect cancellation

For n=8,16,32,64, in two exact prime fields, a candidate upper-generator sequence prescribed by three low modes has a simple boundary-defect Fourier transform in output column 0. For frequencies i≥3, only defects at n-2 and n-1 remain:

`B[i,0]=(h_(n-2)-h*_(n-2))*z^(-2i) + (1-h*_(n-1))*z^(-i)`.

The corresponding verifier checked more than 2000 exact rows. This is a useful representation theorem within the chart; it is not a new Fourier-transform algorithm.

## Current frontier and recommendation

A future proof needs to combine the **full 12 residual equations** with the cubic W (or the exact five-variable circuit H), then analyze each exceptional denominator locus systematically. Promising computational algebra approaches include:

1. Project by E3/E4 and Q, keeping separate nonzero and zero tail pivots; reduce coefficient field to `Q[z]/(z^4+1)`.
2. Attempt regular-chain / triangular decomposition or saturation of the ideal by all required nonzero determinants, returning an exact rational Nullstellensatz certificate if empty.
3. Cross-check any certificate against known exact Cooley–Tukey controls, remembering that classical block-diagonal stages may be outside this *globally separated* subclass.
4. If a complex solution exists, independently multiply its matrices to recover the whole Fourier matrix and **fully count** the stage-generator arithmetic, setup, and memory accesses before claiming speed.
5. For long-term asymptotics, move beyond fixed N=8 or two globally separated rank-one stages, and retain arbitrary endpoint and internal permutations as separate problems.

**Conclusion:** New exact **low-degree necessary polynomial invariants** plus an exhaustive finite-field branch rejection. No proven impossibility over the complex numbers, no exact sub-`N log N` construction, and no claim that classical FFT's asymptotic exponent is universally optimal.
