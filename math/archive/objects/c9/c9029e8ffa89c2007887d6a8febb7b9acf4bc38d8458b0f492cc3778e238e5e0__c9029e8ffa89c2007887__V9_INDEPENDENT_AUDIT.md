# Independent audit of the unit-step binomial-chirp v9

9 October 2026. Uploaded source, scripts, executable, logs and JSON were read as data only. None of the uploaded programs was imported, executed, compiled, or linked. The attached standard-library checker is independently authored. This report audits the approximate-transform mechanism; it does not establish an exact arbitrary-input sub-N-log-N DFT or a general Fourier lower bound.

## Verdict and real delta from the master

The main v9 algebra is correct for `N=p^L=KM`, `M>=2`, `K>=M`, with K and M powers of the same prime. The binomial phase and parity twist yield one output cycle and recover the seed from half the scan total. The exact one-output covariance is `(M/N)` times an orthogonal projector of rank `N-K`. Relative to v8, the dyadic covariance norm is halved, the number of cycles drops from two to one, direct seeds disappear, and `K>=2M` relaxes to `K>=M`. Odd prime powers are genuinely added.

There is an especially simple closed form not stated in the source. Set `K=N/M`, let `D_m=diag(exp(2 pi i m n/N))`, and define the orthogonal projection

    (P_K x)_n = (1/M) sum_{j congruent to n mod K} x_j.

Then

    Q^(m) = (M/N) D_m (I-P_K) D_m*,
    Q^(0)[n,n'] = [M 1(n=n') - 1(n congruent to n' mod K)]/N.

Thus, for each fixed input,

    E |Xhat_m-X_m|^2 = (M/N) ||(I-P_K) D_m* x||_2^2.

The nullspace consists of inputs `x_n=exp(2 pi i m n/N) f(n mod K)`. Equivalently, their Fourier support lies in the residue class `j congruent to m mod M`. This is an exact structural description, not just an eigenvalue computation.

The master already contained an independent full v8 numerical theorem at `additions/1520/v8_audit/V8_PRECISION_AUDIT.md`. Consequently the v9 assertion that v8 had only a conditional prefix bound is stale relative to that checkpoint. The new scan permits a simpler transfer of that theorem; it is not the first full precision bridge in the audited research chain. The companion `V9_PRECISION_EXTENSION.md` supplies the explicit transfer.

## Necessary corrections and boundaries

1. **M=1.** The matrix/projector identity remains true with the zero projector, but its norm is zero, not `M/N=1/N`. Require `M>=2` for the positive norm and extremizer claims. The uploaded dimension validator accepts M=1, so its norm headline needs this amendment.
2. **Prime lengths.** For `N=p` the only admissible M is 1. The row then computes the exact transform by a length-N FFT and has no sub-N-log-N gain. A nontrivial row needs exponent L at least 2.
3. **Growing primes.** A nearby p-power need not be `Theta(log N)` uniformly over all prime powers. The advertised fixed-row and growing-row error rates apply to fixed/bounded p, or to families admitting the stated divisor M. For `N=p^2` and growing p, the choices are only M=1 or M=p; neither is uniformly comparable to log N. The algebraic theorem is uniform in p; these asymptotic substitutions are not.
4. **Fresh arbitrary inputs.** The probability statements hold for each fixed input, with independent row randomness. They are not a single-random-draw operator guarantee simultaneously for every input, nor an adaptive-input guarantee after the row randomness is revealed.
5. **Arithmetic model.** Exact real-to-grid floor/quantization is an additional primitive, not an ordinary field operation. A finite-word implementation must certify classification, a norm envelope, range, integer widths, roots and output rounding. O(N) space means numerical words, with counters wide enough for R, not bits.
6. **Numerical status.** The source proves an O-constant conditional row-error statement, not the default constant 64 in its helper function, not the supplied NumPy/C++ code, and not fixed-binary64 stability for arbitrary sizes. See the separate numerical report.
7. **Composite lengths.** The recurrence and its antiperiodic closure work for any N and unit a. The scaled-projector covariance does not. The direct composite shortcut is conclusively false; the source's cofactor route retains the charged exact cofactor transform.
8. **Evidence.** Saved tests and timing reports remain source claims. Our exact checks independently corroborate the mathematical identities. They do not rerun or certify the source programs or benchmark machine.

## 1. Reconstruction of the one-cycle decoder

Write `zeta=exp(2 pi i/N)`, `eta=exp(pi i/N)`, `h=N mod 2`, and `B(t)=t(t-1)/2`. Define

    S[r,t] = zeta^(a B(t)+(aMr+beta)t) eta^(ht),
    d_r = sum_t S[r,t] x[rM+t],
    y[rM+t] = conjugate(S[r,t]) d_r.

Uniform beta kills every off-diagonal term because the within-block indices differ by less than N. Hence `E y=x` and the unitary DFT estimate is unbiased.

For `P_n=sum_{0<=t<M} zeta^(-aB(t)-nt) eta^(-ht)`, the identity `B(t+1)=B(t)+t` gives

    P_n = 1 + zeta^(-n) eta^(-h) P_(n+a)
              - zeta^(-nM-aB(M)) eta^(-hM).

Inserting it into the block representation gives exactly

    Y_m = D_(m mod K)/sqrt(N) + zeta^(-(m+beta)) eta^(-h) Y_(m+a)
          - zeta^(-M(m+beta)-aB(M)) eta^(-hM)
            D_((m+aM) mod K)/sqrt(N),

where D is the unnormalized K-DFT of d. The block-dependent phase in the middle term changes the Fourier index from m to m+a; the boundary term changes it to m+aM. No missing cross-block phase remains.

Since a is a unit, `m_j=aj mod N` visits each index once. Let

    A_j = zeta^(a B(j)+beta j) eta^(hj),
    F_m = D_(m mod K)/sqrt(N)
          - zeta^(-M(m+beta)-aB(M)) eta^(-hM)
            D_((m+aM) mod K)/sqrt(N),
    q_j = F_(m_j)/A_j.

Then `Y_(m_(j+1))/A_(j+1)=Y_(m_j)/A_j-q_j`. If N is even, every unit a is odd and `zeta^(aB(N))=-1`. If N is odd, B(N) is a multiple of N and `eta^N=-1`. Thus `A_N=-1` in both cases, including general composite N. Closing the cycle yields

    Y_0 = (1/2) sum_j q_j,
    Y_(m_j) = A_j [(1/2) sum_q q_q - sum_(q<j) q_q].

A padded Blelloch scan has O(N) work and O(log N) depth; its total is available before resetting the root for the down-sweep. The row uses O(N+K log K) arithmetic/word work, provided the specified K-FFT and phase preparation are charged in that model. The source C++ does share this total. The Python prototype separately evaluates a pairwise total and a prefix scan; that is still O(N), but does not literally share the same up-sweep traffic.

## 2. Reconstruction of the covariance and explicit projection

For source coordinate `n=rM+u`, its error coefficient has displacements `D_u={u-t:0<=t<M,t!=u}`. Averaging products over beta forces equal displacements. This uses `|d-d'|<=2M-2<N`, ensured by `N>=M^2` for M>=2. The deterministic eta factors then cancel. With the convention `E|sum_n h_n x_n|^2=x*Qx`,

    Q^(m)[n,n'] = zeta^(m(n-n'))/N
                 sum_(d in D_u intersect D_u') E_a zeta^(a d(n'-n)).

For `N=p^L`, the normalized unit character is 1 when N divides its argument, `-1/(p-1)` when N/p divides it but N does not, and zero otherwise. If `v=nu_p(d)<mu=log_p M`, nonzero covariance requires `N/p^(v+1)` to divide `n'-n`. This modulus is divisible by M because `L>=2mu`, so necessarily u'=u.

On the remaining K-cycle of block indices, a displacement of valuation v has kernel

    [p 1_(H_v) - 1_(H_(v+1))]/(p-1),

where H_v is the subgroup of size p^v. Its Fourier eigenvalue is `p^(v+1)/(p-1)` on exactly the modes of p-valuation v, and zero elsewhere.

For fixed u, the values `u-t mod M` run through all residues modulo M. Their nonzero residues of valuation v number exactly `(p-1)M/p^(v+1)`. This proves the shell count without a case distinction for negative displacements or local endpoints. Multiplying the count, shell eigenvalue and 1/N gives M/N. The active K-Fourier modes are exactly those not divisible by M; there are `K-K/M` per local coordinate, hence rank N-K overall.

The projector onto the complementary modes `ell divisible by M` has matrix entry `1/M` when `r-r'` is divisible by `K/M`, and zero otherwise. Since K is divisible by M, this is exactly the projection P_K displayed above. In particular each residue class modulo K is a group of M coordinates, and Q0 is a direct sum, after permutation, of blocks

    (1/N) (M I_M - J_M).

This directly proves positivity, idempotence after scaling, eigenvalue, rank, trace M-1, and the nullspace. The vector on local coordinate u=0 with block Fourier mode ell=1 is an extremizer for M>=2. Multiplication by D_m supplies the other outputs' extremizers. Summing Q^(m) over all m yields `(M-1)I`, giving the total expected squared error `(M-1)||x||_2^2`; this does not turn the approximation into exact recovery.

## 3. Robust aggregation and its precise asymptotics

For each real or imaginary coordinate, complex variance bounds the scalar second moment by `(M/N)||x||_2^2`. With `tau=g sqrt(M/N)||x||_2`, Chebyshev gives scalar bad-row probability at most g^-2. The three-grid v8 argument transfers unchanged: width 7 tau, offsets 0, 7 tau/3, 14 tau/3, and one Boyer–Moore state per grid. The union of boundaries has spacing 7 tau/3 greater than 2 tau. At most one grid cuts the entire good interval; in each other grid good values form a strict-majority cell whose center lies within 5 tau/2 of the target. Median of the three centers yields complex error at most `(5/sqrt(2)) tau`.

For independent rows, a union bound over the 2N scalar coordinates gives

    failure <= 2N exp[-R D(1/2 || g^-2)].

For odd `R=2s-1`, the stronger fixed-row tail bound useful here is

    failure <= 2N binom(2s-1,s) g^(-2s).

Choose g proportional to N^(1/(2s)) at fixed target failure. If `M=Theta(log N)` is an admissible divisor, this gives exactly the source's fixed-row error exponent and O(N) row work. For s=1 that error radius does not decay; useful vanishing relative-to-energy rates need s>1.

For `g=(log N)^epsilon`, `D(1/2||g^-2)~epsilon log log N`. Taking R large enough as an explicit multiple of `log(2N/delta)/D`, and `M=Theta(log N/f)` with `1<=f=o(log log N)`, gives the claimed o(N log N) work and polylogarithmic energy-relative radius. Constants must be chosen to enforce the probability bound; an unspecified Theta with an arbitrarily small hidden multiplier does not by itself enforce confidence. At a=0 in the shorthand delta=N^-a, use a nonvacuous constant confidence such as 1/2. All choices of M remain subject to the divisor/growing-prime qualification above.

The source's Good–Thomas cofactor extension is valid in exact arithmetic: an exact B-stage preserves total norm, then B independent A-column approximations can use the global norm envelope and a union bound over all N outputs. Its work remains `O(N log B + R[N+(N/M)log(A/M)])`, including routing and explicit output cost. A small cofactor does not remove the separate requirement that the A-dimension admit a suitable M. Finite precision also needs the cofactor-stage error charged; Section 8 of the source is not itself a certified finite-precision mixed-length theorem.

## 4. Composite counterexamples are exact, despite floating source code

The source composite script implements the rational Ramanujan formula using Python `/`, so its stored covariance entries are floats and its eigenvalues are numerical. Calling that program an exact rational covariance computation would be inaccurate. Its mathematical formula is correct. Our independently authored checker uses Fraction throughout and proves that `Q^2-(M/N)Q` is nonzero for each of the source's twelve parameter pairs, recording a rational matrix-entry witness.

A particularly small exact witness is N=12, M=2. On even coordinates 0,2,4,6,8,10 place the vector `(2,1,-1,-2,-1,1)` and put zero on odd coordinates. Direct rational matrix multiplication gives `Qv=v/4`. Since M/N=1/6, its norm is at least 3/2 times the proposed projector norm. This alone decisively rejects the unrestricted direct extension without relying on eigenvalue tolerances. The other source numerical ratios are plausible source evidence, not necessary to this exact rejection.

The source integer/rational projector certificate correctly checks 141 parameter triples. It checks shell counts and assigned rank/eigenvalue formulas, not the full covariance derivation, decoder, root identities, positivity, or all infinitely many parameters. It is useful finite evidence, not a proof assistant or an independent mathematical proof of the whole theorem. Our report gives the symbolic proof and the independent script checks those missing connections on exact small instances.

## 5. Source implementation and benchmark observations

- The NumPy complex64 experiment has summaries computed in complex128, an FFT computed in complex128 before casting, and roots generated with float64 transcendental calls. It is mixed precision, and its reference reuses numerical summary formation. It is not a full end-to-end single-precision guarantee.
- `compress_blocks` uses NumPy sum rather than the specified explicit pairwise summary reduction. NumPy's implementation choices do not supply the theorem's stated contract by themselves.
- The C++ implementation is dyadic-only. It has the desired one scan, zero direct seeds, O(N) storage, modular products in unsigned 128-bit intermediates, and no R-by-N estimate bank. Its block summaries are sequential accumulations, so it is not the pairwise-summary implementation analyzed by the general precision theorem.
- Both source implementations use approximate grid arithmetic followed by floor, uncertified norm computation and fixed-width counters/cell IDs. Their grids are not the exact represented-grid implementation in the prior audit.
- Neither the source constant 64 nor its sufficient-mantissa helper is an implementation-specific certified bound. The helper also does not separately enforce the small `u log N` and range assumptions.
- The saved native timing sweep excludes Engine construction, root-table construction and FFT planning from timed rows. Both FFT baseline and sketch are measured in a prepared setting; those figures are not fully charged fresh-instance setup timings.
- The source records row/FFTW ratios approximately 3.19 to 11.54 and five-row streaming ratios 25.98 to 71.91. These are an openly negative practical result. They have not been rerun here.
- The native examples use g=4 and R=5. Their generic all-output failure bound is vacuous at the saved sizes: `2N exp(-5 D(1/2||1/16))` exceeds one already at N=4096. The reported errors do not validate a nontrivial simultaneous confidence guarantee for those benchmark parameters. Grid-center quantization is intentionally coarse; error about half the conservative radius is not FFT-like accuracy.

## 6. Additional restricted exact-input corollary

This is a new derivation prompted during the independent review, not a claim found in the v9 upload. Let an input have at most h nonzero Fourier coefficients and retain the same prime-power row. In Fourier coordinates let H denote the row map. Summation over the K block indices shows that

    H[m,j]-1(m=j) can be nonzero only if
    m-j congruent to a d mod K for some 0<|d|<M.

In particular H[m,m]=1. If the p-valuation of m-j is at least mu=log_p M, there is no such edge. Otherwise its valuation v matches that of d. There are `2(p-1)M/p^(v+1)` signed candidate displacements, each occurring with probability `1/phi(K/p^v)` under a uniform unit a; the union bound is at most 2M/K. Thus a given output equals the true output exactly with probability at least `1-2hM/K`.

Run exact-value Boyer–Moore separately on each complex output over R=2s-1 independent rows. If a strict majority of rows are exact, it returns exactly the correct complex value. Consequently

    Pr[any output wrong] <= N binom(2s-1,s) (2hM/K)^s.

The bound can be capped at one. This is a structured-input exact arithmetic and exact-complex-equality theorem, not an arbitrary-input result or a numerically robust equality algorithm. An output may be correct even when an edge occurs, so the union estimate is conservative. No support-recovery oracle is required, but exact equality itself is a stated primitive. This does not certify the source's shifted-grid estimator as exact.

## Bottom line

v9 provides a genuine stronger approximate row and a clean exact covariance theorem. The exact all-input sub-N-log-N goal is still unresolved. The next practical numerical step is a specifically certified implementation; the next mathematical length step needs more than the false direct-projector extension. The structured sparse-Fourier corollary must remain labeled as a restricted exact-arithmetic result.
