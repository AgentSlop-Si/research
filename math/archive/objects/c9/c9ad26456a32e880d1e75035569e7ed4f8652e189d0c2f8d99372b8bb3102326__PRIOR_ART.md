# Prior art and source boundaries

The Phase20 contribution is **not** the original discovery of sparse rational function interpolation, Prony's method, Hankel rank, resultant elimination or sparse polynomial reconstruction.

Relevant historical sources include:

- Michael Ben-Or and Prasoon Tiwari, *A deterministic algorithm for sparse multivariate polynomial interpolation*, STOC 1988: https://doi.org/10.1145/62212.62241
- Annie Cuyt and Wen-shin Lee, *Sparse interpolation of multivariate rational functions*, Theoretical Computer Science 412 (2011), 1445-1456: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tcs.2010.11.050
- Mark Giesbrecht, George Labahn and Wen-shin Lee, *Symbolic–numeric sparse interpolation of multivariate polynomials*, Journal of Symbolic Computation 44 (2009): https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jsc.2008.11.003
- Joris van der Hoeven, *On sparse interpolation of rational functions and gcds*, 2020: https://www.texmacs.org/joris/sparserat/sparserat.html
- Standard Sylvester resultant and Prony/Hankel rank identities.

Our specific internal work: use a 3x3 Hankel rank condition to eliminate an *unknown* graph-binomial multiplier, bound the resultant's shift-root degree by 45, search it using a paid sparse root-group evaluator, certify every candidate in the time domain, repair single resonances, and preserve the Phase19 Green/sparse-inverse fallback.

No global mathematical novelty or external peer validation is asserted. The theorem and code can be submitted for independent review.
