## 2. Phase21: correct exact representations with a stronger architectural obstruction

The Weyl-shift chirp polynomial identity is exact at every even length under the source's conventions. It expresses the classical quadratic-phase Fourier structure; applying the polynomial directly costs quadratic work and does not itself improve the FFT. One valid weighted cyclic Green inverse can be flat and dense in linear arithmetic work, but the displayed example is ill-conditioned as N grows and is not Fourier up to outside monomial gauges. The full-rank commutator example is also correct: a cheap map can have maximal commutator rank.

The common-Weyl resolvent sum requires at least N/2 poles even with arbitrary invertible outer diagonal scalings. Cross-ratio curvature forces the coefficient sequence to be a scaled chirp, and an N/2-square Hankel Vandermonde matrix has full rank. This bounds representation size, not the work of every possible shared evaluator.

The source's degree-N/2 Prony polynomial has been independently reconstructed by a different q-binomial recurrence. Nonzero modular resultants with its derivative and with z^N+1 certify distinct valid complex poles at all supplied dyadic lengths 4 through 8192. The audit additionally certifies 18 specified non-dyadic lengths. Each modular reduction is a homomorphism on cyclotomic integer coefficients, so these nonzero resultants are characteristic-zero existence certificates at the checked lengths. The finite list is not a proof for all even lengths. Roots, residues, conditioning and evaluation work are not free consequences of existence.

### Uniform one-extra-pole construction

A separate independent proof closes the representation existence question to within one pole at every even length: writing N=2h, the minimum admissible common-Weyl pole count is h or h+1. All initial chirp Hankel determinants are nonzero. The associated consecutive monic moment polynomials P_h and P_(h−1) are therefore coprime, and P_(h−1)(0) is nonzero. The polynomial pencil

`B_t(z)=zP_h(z)+tP_(h−1)(z)`

obeys every recurrence needed for the N chirp moments. At most2h−1 complex t values create a repeated root and at most2h further values create a forbidden root alpha^N=−1. Thus some t in {1,…,4h} gives h+1 distinct nonzero valid poles. Vandermonde matching and the recurrence reconstruct all N moments, hence the Fourier resolvent representation.

This is a universal algebraic proof, separately corroborated by symbolic identities through degree8 and67finite-size checks. It does not prove the original h-pole polynomial uniformly nondegenerate, nor make root/residue preparation or evaluation cheap. Full proof and checks: `prony_extension/PROOF_OR_OBSTRUCTION.md`.

### Cut-rank accounting extends beyond a single order

For a weighted cyclic shift S and an arbitrary coordinate cut R,C with C the complement of R, the inverse-nullity identity gives

`rank((I-S)^(-1)[R,C]) = rank((I-S)[R,C])`.

The right side counts the nonzero directed cycle edges crossing from C into R, because the block is a partial weighted permutation. Thus the same-order rank-one result extends to independently ordered cycles by charging each term its actual crossing count. Products add upper-right block ranks, sums add them, and intermediate diagonal masks cost zero in this budget, including singular masks. For the contiguous Fourier half cut, exactness requires a total crossing budget of at least N/2.

A reversed coordinate cycle still has a rank-one half cut. Merely reversing the order does not evade the obstruction. Arbitrary independently ordered cycles can have a large crossing budget, so the theorem does not exclude them in general. Many same-order terms with an efficient shared evaluator also remain open: a bound on term count is not an unrestricted scalar-operation lower bound.

### A stronger spectral floor tends to one

Let C be the normalized Fourier half block, A=CC*, and v_N=tr(A-A²). The audit obtains an exact finite trigonometric sum for v_N and an O(log N) harmonic bound. A candidate half block of rank at most q<N/4 has spectral approximation error at least

`sqrt(max(0, 1-v_N/(N/4-q)))`.

This tends to one for q=o(N), and also for q≤(1/4-epsilon)N with fixed epsilon>0. It strengthens the source's valid floor tending to 1/sqrt(2). The bound applies through the crossing budget above and remains independent of conditioning. It is a restriction on those candidate blocks, not a lower bound for unrestricted Fourier computation.

## 3. v11: useful approximate fusion, with a genuine numerical-contract gap

Clenshaw's recurrence removes the previous rank-times-length Chebyshev table. Neumann's Bessel addition identity correctly separates each weighted input into local-offset and block-location factors. For fixed local halfwidth H, the streamed architecture uses O(N) scalar storage, and its repeated-state covariance argument has no extra multiplier R. There are still R short FFT/decoder/output passes, so the stated shrinking-error work remains O(RN), with R of order log N/log log N.

The saved implementation relies on special-function and root/FFT primitives. Its black-box numerical bound for a full row on a freshly supplied weighted input does not, by itself, certify a different algorithm that computes rounded channels and synthesized summaries first. That algorithm needs an explicit summary-decoder error contract, including cancellation and propagation through the short transform and scan. The constant-weight Bessel sum is useful, but does not erase this logical obligation. Source floating tests and formal bit-budget plots do not close it.

Two independent refinements make the mathematical approximate result sharper:

- For H=0, the additional kernel bias is at most Delta=pi(M-1)/Q, rather than the looser O(R Delta) bound. Together with expansion truncation the bias is at most tau_R+Delta, using the Bessel integral and |J0|≤1. The H=0 variance envelope remains (M/N)(1+tau_R)² times the squared input norm; Delta need only enter deterministic bias.
- Negative local channels add no independent information: G_(-h)=(-1)^h G_h. At most H+1 channels per block are needed. A fixed L-row decoder using only these fused summaries has rank at most L(H+1)K on the original input, regardless of expansion rank R. Uniform exact recovery from those summaries requires L(H+1)K≥N, and the sharper blockwise condition L(H+1)≥M whenever a complete input block is present. Increasing R alone cannot repair lost information.

The audit also specifies a simpler frozen-block rational-Taylor variant: use the ordinary unweighted chirp summary once, approximate the block-level residual factor with streamed powers, and charge rational pi preparation. Its deterministic bias is bounded by Delta plus the rational-phase and Taylor-tail errors. Input-side work is O(N+RK), total decoder work remains O(RN), and each row still carries only K complex summaries. Its exact-arithmetic statement is separate from any finite-precision certification of a fused implementation.

## 4. Implications for the exact target

The new common-order bounds explain why dense, flat, noncommuting or complex-phase Green examples need not have Fourier coherence. The new fused approximation explains why sharing an input pass can reduce repeated work without increasing the information retained. These are complementary checks, not evidence of an exact speedup.

The preceding exact completion of the full chirp bank still reduces to Cooley–Tukey under the present evaluator. The exact near-grid residual kernel still has rank N/gcd(N,Q), including full rank at odd N with dyadic Q. None of v11's approximate fusion changes either fact.

A surviving exact proposal must supply arbitrary-input/all-output identities, a scalable family, all exceptional branches, and a genuinely charged shared evaluator including coefficient generation, setup, outputs and its chosen precision/bit model. A large necessary representation does not prove every evaluator slow; an approximate low-rank compression does not prove exact recovery.
