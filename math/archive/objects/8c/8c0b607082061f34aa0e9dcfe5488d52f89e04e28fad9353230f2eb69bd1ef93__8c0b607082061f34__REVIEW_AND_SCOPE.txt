Review and scope
9 October 2026

Independent skeptical mathematical review checked the transparent replay
identity, cancellation-safe height propagation, primitive scalar-instruction
expansion, serial full-state conditioning reset, constant-bound chirp estimate,
dirty-scratch all-length lift, root-only normalization, and asymptotic selector.
No gap was found under the stated uniform exact-linear primitive and effective
preparation hypotheses.

Corrections incorporated during review:
- Local condition alone does not control identity padding; use determinant-one
  anchoring at shear boundaries and strong norm/inverse bounds at scalar steps.
- Only one macro fiber is active at a time. Asynchronous blocks require a
  different bound.
- Every original shear expands into six invertible scalar register updates,
  including zero coefficients, without hidden multiplication scratch.
- Full-state conditioning is measured after input loading. Initial zero output
  banks are not reset destructively during the algorithm.
- A budget must tend to infinity, not merely be unbounded on a subsequence.
- Local heights use the local coefficient field, not the larger global root field.
- Arbitrary lengths use additive diagonal application. No convolution diagonal
  entry is inverted; zero entries are harmless.
- The normalized chirp multiplier is uniformly bounded by 32, proved by
  elementary phase-increment telescoping.
- The all-length budget is selected from the requested N, even though its
  dyadic working length M is larger.
- Output normalization, scratch, companion banks, coefficient preparation and
  one-root preparation are accounted for explicitly.

This review does not independently prove the source #130 theorem or establish
finite-precision stability, bit-operation complexity, practical speedup,
Ailon-model equivalence, or novelty. The bundle does not implement the #130
primitive. The source theorem is an explicit conditional input to this work.

The tests distinguish exact algebraic identities from floating-point sanity
checks. Passing small tests is not the proof of the asymptotic theorem.
