# Independent review: routed Green products and the growing-radix ledger

9 October 2026 UTC. This is a new review package; all inherited sealed files were preserved and their seven recorded hashes verified. No producer or uploaded program was executed or imported. The three test programs here were independently authored for this review.

## Verdict

1. The inherited growing-radix algebra and charged ledger are correct for the specified evaluation schedule. They do not give a faster exact full Fourier transform.
2. The shared-anchor obstruction for the unshifted fixed rational pencil is correct, including its characteristic-zero all-coupling argument and constant-modulus strengthening.
3. Every stored N=16 shifted-pencil certificate is independently valid, with every projective branch covered.
4. A simpler derived rational-span lemma strengthens the shifted-pencil exclusion to **every dyadic square N >= 16**, for every complex pair of stage couplings and every invertible middle monomial. Its novelty has not been assessed. It is not an unrestricted Fourier lower bound or an exclusion of arbitrary Green masks and endpoint diagonals.

## 1. A rational-span obstruction

Let K be a subfield of C. Let B_1,...,B_q be fixed N-by-N matrices over K and let

    B = sum_(l=1)^q theta_l B_l,

where the theta_l are arbitrary complex numbers. Let A be any complex matrix and T any monomial matrix. Suppose row i of A has at most s nonzero entries. Then the coordinates of row i of A T B span a K-vector space of dimension at most s q.

Proof: row i of A T is supported on a set S of at most s indices. Its output row is

    sum_(j in S) sum_(l=1)^q c_j theta_l (B_l)[j,:].

Every coordinate therefore belongs to the K-span of the at most s q complex numbers c_j theta_l. This proof does not require the theta_l, c_j or A to lie in K, nor any stage to be invertible, nor any division by a coupling.

For a Fourier row indexed by i, let n=N/gcd(i,N), with n=1 at i=0. Its coordinates span K(zeta_n) over K. A nonzero scalar multiplying the whole row does not change this dimension. Hence the necessary condition is

    [K(zeta_n):K] <= s q.

In particular, for a primitive-frequency row and K=Q,

    phi(N) <= s q.

If K is a number field of degree d over Q, a useful weaker form is phi(N) <= d s q. Thus bounded field degree and bounded row support cannot represent primitive Fourier rows at unbounded cyclotomic degree. If K already contains the Fourier roots, this particular argument can become vacuous.

Scope matters: arbitrary nonuniform complex outer column scalings can destroy the coordinate-span bound. They are not permitted for the fixed pencil below. The lemma allows nonzero output-row scalings. It also applies to fixed right coefficient matrices over a specified number field, with that field charged in the bound. It does not assume an arbitrary parametric Green family has bounded field degree.

## 2. Exact all-size application to the shifted fixed pencil

Let M=2^h, h>=1, N=M^2. In row-major grid order define

    G = (I - 2 L_t - 3 L_u)^(-1),
    J_0 = (I - 5 L_t - 7 L_u)^(-1),
    pi(j) = (bitreverse_(2h)(j) + 1) mod N,
    J = P_pi J_0 P_pi^(-1),
    H(alpha,beta) = alpha G + beta J.

Both G and J are integer matrices: their Green entries are finite weighted path counts. Consider the exact target

    F_N = H(alpha_2,beta_2) T H(alpha_1,beta_1)

with arbitrary complex couplings and arbitrary invertible complex monomial T.

The crucial support statement holds for every size, not merely tested sizes. Grid index 1 has coordinates (0,1), so

    G[1,:] = 3 e_0^T + e_1^T.

Since pi(0)=1 for every bit length, the minimum vertex of the routed grid is index 1, and

    J[1,:] = e_1^T.

Consequently row 1 of the left factor is exactly

    3 alpha_2 e_0^T + (alpha_2+beta_2) e_1^T,

which has support at most two. The right factor is a complex linear combination of two fixed rational matrices. The lemma gives an output-coordinate Q-span dimension at most 4. Fourier row 1 has coordinate span Q(zeta_N), with

    phi(N)=N/2,

because N is a power of two and Phi_N(z)=z^(N/2)+1. For every dyadic square N>=16, N/2>4. This is a contradiction.

This exclusion covers alpha_i=0, beta_i=0, alpha_i+beta_i=0, every other complex ratio, singular stage sums, and arbitrary middle permutations and nonzero complex diagonal entries. Zero or singular factors cannot produce the invertible Fourier matrix; the lemma itself does not need to discard them. Both Fourier signs and nonzero global normalization are covered. No parameter-chart division or characteristic-p specialization is involved.

N=4 is not excluded by this degree argument. No claim is made here about that small size. The theorem uses the fixed rational-weight pencil; arbitrary edge weights, independent endpoint column masks, additional sum terms, further stages, and other routes have not been excluded.

A cheap necessary gate for the next rational two-term route is that every primitive-frequency output row must have support at least ceil(phi(N)/2). This is necessary only. Passing it is not evidence of Fourier equality or an efficient parameter synthesis algorithm.

## 3. Independent audit of the stored N=16 certificates

The supplied JSON identifies every unordered pair of rows of the right pencil. The claim is that Fourier row 1 lies in no two-row span of H(alpha,beta). This is sufficient because the left row has support at most two after the middle monomial. A one-row span is included in a two-row span by adjoining any distinct row.

I reconstructed G and J directly from the closed path formula

    G(a,b)[(t,u),(t',u')] = binomial(dt+du,dt) a^dt b^du

when dt=t-t' and du=u-u' are nonnegative, and zero otherwise. The bit permutation was reconstructed bitwise, independently of the producer's string reversal. The certificate arithmetic was reconstructed with symbolic integer polynomials modulo z^8+1; irreducibility was separately verified.

All 120 row pairs pass:

- 78 missing-coordinate certificates, valid for every projective parameter.
- 24 nonzero constant 3-by-3 minors for the finite chart.
- 18 pairs of minors with nonzero resultants for the finite chart.
- 42 nonzero pure-J minors at projective infinity, one for every pair requiring algebraic certification.

Each stored polynomial and resultant matches exact reconstruction. Resultants were computed by a complete Sylvester determinant, not the producer's specialized quadratic formula. The independent test includes a shared nonrational-root zero-resultant control and nonzero-resultant controls. A nonzero residue of degree below eight cannot vanish at a primitive sixteenth root because z^8+1 is irreducible; therefore these are characteristic-zero certificates, not merely modular evidence.

The certificate is valid but stronger algebra is not required for the exclusion: the dimension lemma already forbids the Fourier row from the span of any four rational vectors. This also makes clear why finite search is not the logical basis of the all-size conclusion.

Structural support is not enough. Independently, H=G+J has positive entries at column 0 in every row, and row 0 of J is positive in every column. Thus H^2 is entrywise positive and its support is fully dense. This is consistent with, and does not weaken, the exact Fourier exclusion.

## 4. Shared-anchor result retained

For the unshifted route R=bit reversal, both constant Green matrices have row 0=e_0^T and final column=e_(N-1). The pencil anchors are scaled by alpha+beta. If either relevant anchor scalar vanishes, the factor is singular. Otherwise Fourier's first row and the final-column support force the selected right-factor row to be the last row and to be constant.

At k=M/2, the three selected last-row entries are

    alpha+beta,
    3 alpha + 5^k beta,
    2 alpha + 7^k beta.

The two constancy equations have determinant 2*7^k-5^k-1>0. Thus no nonzero projective coupling works at any dyadic square M>=2. The stronger modulus argument uses gap 2*7^k-3*5^k+5>0; it correctly excludes constant nonzero modulus. Arbitrary nonuniform column amplitudes remain outside that argument.

The bit-reversal positions, integer determinant sign, and modulus-gap sign were independently checked for M=2,...,4096 in powers of two. These finite tests corroborate the symbolic proof; they are not asserted to replace it.

## 5. Growing-radix ledger retained

For M=bm and h=d+cm, the channel transform identity and t=r+ba, j=s+bv split follow by exponent expansion:

    t j = r s + b(s a+r v) + b^2 a v,
    t j mod b = r s mod b.

The smaller node is q_d^(b^2)=Omega*(eta')^(-b d), where Omega=omega^(b^2) has order m^2 and eta'=eta^b has order m. Its exponent 1-Md is coprime to m, and exactly m/gcd(b,m) distinct smaller nodes occur.

There are m*b^3 distinct inner vector applications of length m, so their expanded input volume is m^2*b^3=N*b. Reusing equal kernel tables does not identify the distinct input vectors. The shared coefficient tables have O(N) entries, and streaming can keep O(N) working storage. The complete stated schedule costs

    O(N [log b + b(1+log m)]).

With log M=log b+log m, the exact difference from 1+log M is

    (b-1)(1+log m) >= 0.

For the ordinary full two-dimensional FFT compilation, varying radices have sum of log radices equal to log M; the charged small-transform work remains Theta(N log M). The inserted orbit basis cancels exactly between its inverse and forward maps. If the raw and output short-Fourier banks continue to be executed separately by ordinary FFTs, their existing Fourier-scale work remains charged.

This is a ledger for the specified circuit, not a lower bound on all collective evaluators or all Fourier algorithms. Roots, convolution plans, coefficient synthesis, permutations, and gathers must be supplied and charged. Unit-cost arithmetic accounting does not establish bit complexity or numerical stability.

Independent finite-field controls cover 35 divisor-radix bank applications over M=2,3,4,5,6,8,9,10,12,16, with 3,311 exactly checked output entries and 16,523 expanded inputs. They compare independently evaluated routed sums with direct geometric multiplication on deterministic arbitrary field inputs. The proof by exponent expansion supplies the all-input identity; the finite tests alone would not.

## 6. Grid primitive and sparse fraction ledger

For arbitrary edge masks, U=I-D_a L_t-D_b L_u is unit lower triangular. Its inverse applies by a forward grid scan with exactly 2M(M-1) active edges. Given masks and routes, this is O(N) arithmetic and O(N) storage. This does not synthesize arbitrary masks for free.

For invertible component denominators,

    H_i=U_i^(-1) W_i V_i^(-1),
    W_i=alpha_i V_i+beta_i U_i,
    K=U_1 T^(-1) V_2,
    U_2 F V_1 = W_2 K^(-1) W_1.

The multiplication order is correct without commutativity. W_i can be singular and no inverse of W_i is used. K is always invertible. The corresponding block matrix has rank N exactly when its Schur complement vanishes. With three-sparse component denominators, W_i has at most six and K at most nine entries per row and per column.

Independent exact rational controls cover noncommuting routes, nonunit endpoint diagonals, pure-term branches, and a zero-sum singular branch at N=4 and N=9. All eight identities and block-rank tests pass. Five additional exact finite-field controls verify variable-mask forward applications. The broad four-term, two-stage parameter upper count 16N-8M is valid as an upper count; its N=16 value 224<256 is not an exclusion of the special Fourier point.
