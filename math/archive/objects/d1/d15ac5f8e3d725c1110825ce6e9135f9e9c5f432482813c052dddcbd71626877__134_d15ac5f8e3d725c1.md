## Primary literature

1. Matteo Frigo, *A Fast Fourier Transform Compiler* (PLDI 1999).
   <https://fftw.org/pldi99.pdf>.
   The compiler constructs a DFT DAG and applies algebraic rewriting and
   common-subexpression elimination, including transformations that expose
   additional common subexpressions. Graph rewriting for Fourier computation
   is established prior art; this experiment does not claim to invent it.

2. Max Willsey et al., *egg: Fast and Extensible Equality Saturation* (2021).
   <https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.03082>.
   A relevant framework for exploring equivalent expressions. The bounded
   direct matcher implemented here is not an e-graph or an equality-saturation
   engine and makes no globally optimal extraction claim.

3. Swapnil Jain, *integer-mult-kappa*, current repository README.
   <https://github.com/Swapnil-jain/integer-mult-kappa>.
   The retrieved README labels its result conditional and distinguishes its
   asymptotic witnesses from practical runtime comparisons. This package does
   not audit or change that certificate stack. The user has chosen to develop
   the graph-reuse route independently before considering that integration.

All performance ratios in this package come from the supplied reproducible
experiment. The counting bound and local identity proof in `RESEARCH.md` are
derived there; they are not presented as newly discovered general lower bounds.
