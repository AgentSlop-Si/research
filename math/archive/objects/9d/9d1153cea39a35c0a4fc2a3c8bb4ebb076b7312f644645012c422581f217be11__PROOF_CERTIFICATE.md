# Proof certificate: exact algebraic FFT reformulations

**Claims proven here:** corrected regular-patch equivalence; degree 4 for 30 equations in 26 coordinates; analytical elimination of five Fourier-specific constraints to obtain 25 degree-6-or-less equations in 21 variables; exception-complete 176-quadratic/184-variable system; two exact degenerate counterexamples. **Neither existence nor nonexistence of an `F8` factorization has been established.**

## 1. Inverse preserves quasiseparable crossing ranks

For an invertible `n×n` matrix `S` and complementary index sets `I=[k,n)` and `J=[0,k)`, the classical nullity theorem states

`rank(S^-1[I,J]) = rank(S[J^c,I^c]) + |I| + |J| - n`.

Because `|I|+|J|=n`, and `J^c=I`, `I^c=J`, the crossing ranks agree. Repeat with the upper crossing block. Thus if `S` has QS order at most one, then `S^-1` does too. When both inverse corners `(n-1,0)` and `(0,n-1)` are nonzero, every required strict-lower (respectively upper) entry can be reconstructed from those corner pivots and the rank-one cross-cut identities, giving a global separated-generator representation. The exceptional 3×3 example in `exceptional_charts.py` proves that the nonzero-corner condition cannot simply be dropped.

## 2. Explicit inverse-chart Fourier polynomial map

Take `z^4=-1`, `P=[7,0,6,3,4,1,2,5]`, `F_ij=z^(ij)` with exponents reduced modulo eight. Represent `C=A^-1` on the chart `diag(C)=1`, `L7=1`, `U0=1`, with 26 unknowns, then `B=F C P^-1`. Every `B_ij` is degree≤2; every rank-one membership equation is a difference of two products `B_{ij}B_{anchor}`, hence degree≤4. There are exactly 15 lower and 15 upper equations. Sufficiency follows by reconstructing all lower entries from the 2×2 minors using `B70≠0`, and all upper entries using `B07≠0`.

**Necessity/sufficiency scope:** `det(C)≠0`, `C` must come from the normalized separated chart, inverse `C^-1` must lie in that chart (guaranteed by nonzero inverse corners), and the `B` corner anchors must be nonzero. Forward normalization also needs nonzero diagonal of `C`. The system's roots away from this open locus must not be used as evidence of factorization.

The file `quartic_cyclotomic_equations.json` is a complete exact coefficient export: each monomial is a list of variable indices; each coefficient is a 4-vector representing `a0 + a1 z + a2 z² + a3 z³`. The checker evaluates every polynomial in two prime fields and in two root embeddings per field against an independently constructed matrix product.

## 3. Exact Fourier-support elimination

Write `beta=B71/B70` when `B70≠0`. The five `lower_i_1` equations, `i=2..6`, imply `B_i1-beta B_i0=0` for all `i=2..7` (the `i=7` case is the definition of beta). Because `F8` is invertible, `C[:,0]-beta*C[:,7]` is in the two-dimensional inverse Fourier subspace spanned by `(1)_k` and `(z^-k)_k`. Therefore

`C[k,0]-beta*C[k,7]=a+b*z^-k`.

At `k=0` and `k=7`, `C[0,0]=1`, `C[0,7]=s`, `C[7,0]=t`, `C[7,7]=1`, so

`a+b=1-beta*s`, `a+b*z=t-beta`.

As `z!=1`, `b=(t-1+beta*(s-1))/(z-1)` and `a=1-beta*s-b`. For `1<=k<=6`, `C[k,0]=t*L[k]` and `C[k,7]=s*U[k]`, hence

`L[k]=(a+b*z^-k+beta*s*U[k])/t` (requires `t!=0`).

Conversely these explicit formulas force all six frequency vanishings, hence all five original lower constraints. Thus six original L variables are removed and one beta introduced: net five variables and five equations eliminated.

The file `reduced_sparse_sextic.py` replaces `s*U[i]` by `h[i]` and `W[j]/s` by `eta[j]` (requires `s!=0`), and multiplies the entire stage by `D=t*(z-1)`. It builds **formal polynomial** entries `Ctilde`, then `Btilde=F Ctilde P^-1`. The FIVE eliminated rank-one identities are identically ZERO polynomials in the quotient ring, as programmatically asserted before any modular substitution; the remaining 25 are exported exactly. There are 21 coordinates and maximum total degree exactly six. For `D!=0`, this system and the original 30-equation system are equivalent on the Fourier-support patch. Further determinant/corner saturation is still mandatory to infer an actual FFT factorization.

## 4. Complete quadratic fallback

Introduce separate entry variables `a_ij,b_ij` (128 total) and generator coordinates `L[1..7],V[0..6],U[0..6],W[1..7]` for *each* of `A,B` (56 total). The 112 off-diagonal stage equations use degree 2. The 64 equations `(B P A)_ij-z^(ij)=0` also use degree 2. There are exactly 176 quadratics in 184 variables. The polynomial system is **equivalent to** `F8=BPA` with two global separated-generator rank-one stages even at zero anchors. Invertibility is forced: `det(F8) != 0` over characteristic zero, so `det(A),det(B) != 0`. This auxiliary lift is not necessarily more efficient computationally.

## 5. Degeneracy certificates

- `C=[[2,1,1],[1,1,1],[1,1,3]]` is invertible and globally separated rank-one, but its inverse has lower positions `(1,0)=-1,(2,1)=-1/2,(2,0)=0`, impossible for one global outer product. This proves the difference between the dense separated chart and general QS order-one boundary.
- `C=J8` has all normalized stage coordinates equal to one and rank one. `F8*J8` has only the first row nonzero; consequently the 30 quartic identities vanish, but the determinant and the lower output anchor vanish. This proves the raw ideal is not enough: it contains nongenuine solutions.
