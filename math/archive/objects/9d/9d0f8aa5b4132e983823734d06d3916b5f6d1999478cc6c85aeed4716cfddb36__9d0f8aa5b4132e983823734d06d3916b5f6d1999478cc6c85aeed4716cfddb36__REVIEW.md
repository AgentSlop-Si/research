# Independent appendix: a monomial Weyl-pair support gate

9 October 2026 UTC. This is a scoped representation-theoretic obstruction. Novelty has not been assessed. It does not exclude general sparse mixed-wire transports or the full routed product family.

## Lemma and proof

Let q be primitive of order N over C, W=diag(q^-j), and Z e_j=e_(j+1). Suppose H is invertible and

    H Z=X H,   H W=Y H,

where X,Y are invertible monomial matrices. Then every row of H has exactly d nonzeros for one divisor d of N.

First, X Y=q Y X, so the permutation parts of X and Y commute. Their joint coordinate action is transitive: otherwise an orbit's coordinate span would be a proper common invariant subspace. The pair is similar to Z,W, which is irreducible because W has distinct diagonal eigenvalues and Z cycles all its coordinate eigenspaces.

The X permutation maps each Y-permutation cycle to another cycle of the same length. Transitivity of the joint action therefore forces all Y cycles to have one common length d, necessarily dividing N.

The simple-spectrum condition is essential. Y is similar to W, so its entire spectrum is simple. Consequently spectra from different Y cycles are disjoint. An eigenvector of a weighted monomial cycle has a nonzero entry at every coordinate of that cycle; each cycle supplies exactly d eigenvalues. Columns of H are the eigenvectors of Y with eigenvalues q^-j. Every row therefore belongs to one cycle and is nonzero in precisely its d corresponding eigenvector columns. This proves uniform row support d. There is no assumption that the weights or H are unitary.

## Application to the balanced phase-grid pencil

Let N=M^2, M>=2. Let sigma reverse the stable predecessor-area order, rho(j)=N-1-j, and

    H=alpha G_q+beta P_sigma G_(q^-1) Q_rho.

The phase Green entries are nonzero at every comparable grid pair because their Gaussian-binomial numerator/denominator factors have exponents smaller than N. This is the specified phase-mask family, not an arbitrary-mask assertion.

Stable area ordering gives sigma(0)=N-1 and sigma(1)=N-2 for every M>=2. Thus, whenever beta!=0, row 0 consists of a fully nonzero routed last row plus one native entry. At most that one entry can cancel, so row 0 has at least N-1 nonzeros.

Row 1 is structurally zero at column M: its native row cannot reach grid source (1,0), and its routed row N-2 cannot reach the reversed source N-1-M, whose horizontal coordinate is larger. In fact its union support has size N-M+1<N. Uniform support d dividing N and d>=N-1 would force d=N, a contradiction.

This includes alpha=0 whenever beta!=0. If beta=0 and alpha!=0, the pure Green has a one-entry first row and an N-entry last row, again contradicting uniform support. If both coefficients vanish, H is not invertible. Any other singular sum is outside the conjugacy premise and cannot serve as an invertible factor in a full square Fourier product. Nonzero endpoint row/column scalings preserve this support contradiction; zero endpoint entries make the factor singular.

Therefore no invertible balanced phase-grid pencil in this class can carry both standard Fourier Weyl generators to monomial images. This is not an exclusion of products of such sums with more general state actions. A successful construction can evade the premise through nonmonomial images or mixed-wire transport.

## Independent controls

A standalone checker constructs 12 exact finite-field partial-Fourier intertwiners at N=4,8,16. Their two conjugated generators are monomial, have the correct XY=qYX sign, and attain every divisor-sized row support in these cases. It also independently generates balanced routes by counting sort and verifies the explicit first-two-row geometry for every integer M=2,...,128.

The all-size conclusion follows from the written support and cycle proof, not from these finite controls. The checker has no external file dependency and executes no uploaded source.
