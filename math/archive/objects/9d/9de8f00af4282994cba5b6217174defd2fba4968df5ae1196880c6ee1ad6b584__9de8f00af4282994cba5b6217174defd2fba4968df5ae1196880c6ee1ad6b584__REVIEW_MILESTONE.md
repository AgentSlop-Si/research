## 2. Correcting ranking entry 3: the cyclic additive separator theorem

The cited theoretical note defines

F = L(A⁻¹+B⁻¹)R, A=I−D_a P_a, B=I−D_b P_b,

with L,R invertible diagonal and P_a,P_b Hamiltonian permutations. The sparse-sandwich identity A L⁻¹ F R⁻¹ B=A+B is correct whenever A,B are invertible. Escaping the note's constant-diagonal obstruction does not escape the stronger additive cross-cut obstruction already in the master.

### 2.1 Why cycles have the required cut ranks

List vertices in the order of one directed cycle. Across any proper prefix/suffix principal partition, a weighted-cycle denominator I−DP has at most one nonzero crossing entry in either orientation: one is the advancing boundary edge and the other is the wrap edge. Each crossing block therefore has rank at most one. Zero edge weights only reduce this rank.

For an arbitrary permutation, concatenate its disjoint cycle orders. A prefix cut lies within at most one component cycle, so the same rank bound holds. This extension does not require a single Hamiltonian cycle.

The inverse nullity identity says rank((A⁻¹)[I,J])=rank(A[I,J]) at a complementary principal cut I|J. It needs only invertibility of the complete A, not of either principal diagonal block. Thus every such inverse has crossing rank at most one in its own order. Arbitrary left/right diagonal gauges, even independent gauges on the individual summands, cannot increase submatrix rank.

### 2.2 The common separator

In the first order take the first and last floor(N/2) labels. Sort these two groups together in the second order and split them into equal halves. One opposite-color orientation retains at least ceil(floor(N/2)/2) labels in each group. The retained row and column groups are strictly separated in both orders, though their orientations may differ.

For groups of size m=ceil(floor(N/2)/2), each summand's selected block has rank≤1; the sum has rank≤2. A normalized complex Fourier m×m submatrix H has ||H||_F²=m²/N and ||H||₂≤1, hence rank(H)≥m²/N. Exact equality implies m²≤2N.

Therefore N=30,31 and every N≥34 are excluded by this elementary complex theorem. N=32,33 are not excluded by this particular inequality. These are sufficient exclusions, not a complete feasible-size classification. For dyadic N the necessary bound is N≤32. The same reasoning gives q individually ordered weighted-permutation inverses the condition ceil(floor(N/2)/2^(q−1))²≤qN. Bounded q cannot work at unbounded N.

The corresponding normalized operator-norm gap, when m>2 and m²>2N, is at least sqrt((m²/N−2)/(m−2)); it tends to 1/2 at growing N. No condition-number bound or bounded-coefficient assumption is used.

The proof is over C. Its norm step is not transferred to arbitrary finite fields. Fifty-five independent modular examples check the algebraic inverse/cross-cut/separator steps, including zero weights, multiple cycles, and independent nonzero term gauges; they corroborate the proof, not replace it. See `SEPARATOR_RESULTS.json` and `separator_checks.py`.

Products of Green sums, non-diagonal outer mixers, and other global operators remain outside this additive argument. Term-count restrictions are not automatically scalar-operation lower bounds for every shared-state implementation.

## 4. Necessary invariants versus sufficient certificates; zero and singular branches

For nowhere-zero M, R(M)_ij=Σ_k M_ik/M_jk transforms by diagonal/permutation similarity under two-sided monomial equivalence. Its traces, characteristic polynomial, and the row/column pair-moment multisets are necessary invariants. Q_s means the trace-square invariant of the entrywise s-th power; it is not generally tr(R(M)^(2s)).

These invariants do not suffice. The unnormalized complex F4 and real Sylvester H4 both have R=4I and Q1=64, yet cannot be two-sided monomial equivalent: all cross-ratios of H4 lie in {±1}, whereas F4 has one equal to i. Even the full characteristic polynomial of R therefore fails as a complete classifier.

For prescribed row/column permutations and nowhere-zero entries, agreement of all anchored cross-ratios is necessary and sufficient: reconstruct one row and one column of diagonal scales, then every remaining entry is forced. Unique vertex signatures only select a candidate matching; final full-entry reconstruction remains necessary. Signature collisions are inconclusive unless the remaining permutations are searched or rejected by another rigorous gate.

When T or B has zeros, the ratio invariant is undefined. Two-sided invertible scaling preserves the entire bipartite support graph up to row/column relabeling; equality of zero counts is only a weak necessary test. For a fixed support matching, nonzero edge ratios admit row and column factors exactly when the alternating ratio product is one around every bipartite cycle. Propagating values along a spanning forest and testing the remaining edges is a sufficient support-aware test, with one free scale per connected component. It handles zero entries without division by zero.

In the specified Phase28/29 families T is nowhere zero, so any B with a zero entry is impossible. Retuning outer parameters can create zeros in T, and such branches must not be globally discarded. An algebraic solver on the regular chart must saturate by every required denominator, det(A), det(C), and the chosen nonzero entries, then cover other admissible support charts separately if its intended scope includes them.

Finite square factors A,B,C that are singular cannot multiply to invertible F. Infinite-parameter limits with pole cancellations are not additional points of the original finite-coefficient factorization; they require a separately defined reparameterized architecture. Zero term weights and zero cycle edge weights, however, can be fully legitimate and are retained in the appropriate domains.

## 6. v16: accepted theorems and scope repairs

For unweighted P and D with distinct Nth-root diagonal, decompose P into cycles. Each block of D−P has nullity one exactly when its index sum is zero modulo N, and otherwise has nullity zero. Thus rank(D−P)=N−z(P). Fourier shift covariance gives the same rank for ZF−FP.

Only {0} is a zero-sum singleton; other zero-sum cycles use at least two labels. Hence z(P)≤ceil(N/2), with equality for negation. The minimum rank is floor(N/2).

The source's one-line uniqueness conclusion skips a small even-N case. At odd N equality forces {0} plus all opposite pairs immediately. At even N=2m, if {0} were not a zero-sum singleton, m zero-sum cycles would each have size≥2 and exhaust all labels, which is impossible because the total label sum is m modulo 2m. If {0} is present, a size-three zero-sum cycle plus m−2 pairs would likewise exhaust all labels and contradict that total sum. Therefore equality has {0}, m−1 opposite pairs, and one unused singleton; that singleton must be m. This establishes unique negation rigorously. The theorem itself is correct.

Independent exhaustive enumeration covers every permutation N=2,…,9, with one minimizer each. Forty-two independent direct checks compare the cycle formula, rank(D−P), and rank(ZF−FP) at larger prime/composite lengths. This theorem concerns unweighted permutations: arbitrary monomial weights can trivialize it, for example choosing the monomial D itself gives zero displacement. It is not a gate against every weighted/noncommuting Green architecture.

The minimal-state statement is correct for a scalar homogeneous constant-coefficient recurrence valid for every input, or an autonomous linear time-invariant state realization: basis inputs supply N distinct exponential roots, forcing order≥N. It does not cover input-dependent coefficients, raw-input forcing throughout the scan, arbitrary time-varying states, or nonlinear acquisition.

A fixed linear transcript with fewer than N scalar measurements has a nonzero kernel, so no decoder, even nonlinear, can recover every input's invertible Fourier image. Following an adaptive deterministic algorithm's all-zero branch proves the analogous adaptive statement: a common-kernel vector reproduces the identical branch. Continued access to unmeasured raw input is outside this compressed-transcript model. We independently built a 28×32 overlapping measurement matrix, nonzero common-kernel input, and nonzero Fourier image; Hankel rank and polynomial-filter identities were also checked independently at six lengths.

The separate-row work theorem is valid under its expressly imposed schedule: one charged N-scan per row and an assumed K_r log2 K_r transform cost. Convexity and ΣK_r≥N imply RN+Nlog2(N/R)≥Nlog2N+N for integer 1≤R≤N, since R−1≥log2R. It is not a proof that every length-K transform necessarily costs KlogK, nor a restriction on all joint batching.

F p(Z)=p(D)F is the standard exact filtering identity. It gives no new evaluator by itself. The prior v15 approximate all-length branch is unchanged, and its stronger precision/implementation caveats remain inherited.

## 7. Coefficient preparation and current frontier

Every bounded-term Green mixer applies in O(N) exact arithmetic once all cycle orders, edge weights, gauges, and coefficients are available. This is a candidate-matrix application guarantee. It does not synthesize the correct Fourier coefficients.

A fully charged construction must include at least: producing/validating the permutations, cycle decomposition, recurrence/root tables, diagonal data, scalar inversions and exceptional cases, all vector passes, addressing and N outputs. A bitmap permutation check and root-power recurrence can be O(N) in a stated word/exact-field model; sorting validation and independently exponentiating every root can cost O(NlogN). Field construction, roots, algebraic constants, bit lengths, and numerical conditioning require their own stated contract.

Dense outer inversion and projective tests in these audits are offline computations: generic inversion is O(N³), pair moments are O(N³), and polynomial/Macaulay synthesis is not shown to be linear-time uniform coefficient preparation. A compact certificate or a finite exceptional locus does not change that cost.

The remaining precise constructive target is a product of genuinely noncommuting global sums or other dense-inverse O(N)-applicable stages outside these exclusions, with a symbolic Fourier identity and uniformly charged coefficient generation. Phase29 closes additional fixed seeded pencils; it does not close every exceptional family. The paired finite F4 controls make good regression tests but do not supply the needed unbounded family.
