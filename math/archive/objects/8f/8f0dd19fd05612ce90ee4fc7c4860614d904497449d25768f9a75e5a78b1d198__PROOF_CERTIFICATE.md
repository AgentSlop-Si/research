# Phase 12 — Proof certificate: low-rank Green-Union versus Fourier uncertainty

**Date:** 2026-10-10. **Status:** Exact deductions in the stated arithmetic/circuit model. The central uncertainty theorems are classical; this application to Green-with-low-rank-Union circuits is a research checkpoint, **not** a claim of publication priority or a universal FFT lower bound.

## Definitions

Let `F_N=(omega^(ij))_(i,j=0)^(N-1)` with `omega=exp(2 pi i/N)`. The Fourier transform and its inverse have the same nonzero support profiles, up to scaling and conjugation. For any matrix `B`, let `colsp(B)` be the maximum number of nonzero entries in a column. All ranks in this proof are over `C`.

A **Green-plus-Union network** is a product `H_L ... H_1`, where every `H_i` is invertible and `H_i=G_i+R_i` with `G_i` invertible, `rank(R_i)<=r_i`, and `colsp(G_i^{-1})<=s_i`. Here a `G_i` can be a cyclic Green solve conjugated by an arbitrary invertible monomial matrix (permutations and nonzero diagonal scalings), or other allowed invertible Green operations with the advertised sparse inverse. There is **no** assumption that different `G_i` commute, are unitary or have aligned cyclic shifts.

For one-pole shifted cyclic Green stages, `G_i=(I-alpha_i P_i S^{a_i} P_i^{-1})^{-1}` up to monomial gauges, `s_i<=2`. Long-range shifts `a_i`, nontrivial permutations `P_i`, and arbitrary scalar `alpha_i` preserving invertibility are permitted.

## Lemma 1 — Sparse plus low-rank Fourier inverse cannot be arbitrarily thin

Let `F_N^{-1}=B+R` with `colsp(B)<=s` and `rank(R)<=r`, where `s>=1` and `0<=r<N`. Then

` s (r+1)^2 >= N. `

For prime `N=p`, the stronger inequality holds:

` (s+1)(r+1) >= p+1. `

**Proof.** Choose any `r+1` column indices `J`. The corresponding `N x (r+1)` restriction of `R` has rank at most `r`, so there exists a nonzero `x` supported in `J` with `R x=0`. Let `k=|supp x|<=r+1`. Then `F_N^{-1}x=B x` is supported on at most `s k` positions, because the support of `Bx` lies in the union of at most `k` columns of `B`. The classical Donoho–Stark uncertainty inequality for the cyclic Fourier transform gives `|supp x| |supp F_N^{-1} x|>=N`, so `s k^2>=N` and hence `s(r+1)^2>=N`.

For prime `p`, Chebotarev full nonvanishing of the complex `p x p` Fourier matrix minors yields the Tao additive uncertainty inequality `|supp x|+|supp F_p^{-1}x|>=p+1`. Consequently `(s+1)k>=p+1`, establishing the claim. QED.

**Proof of the product uncertainty inequality (self-contained).** Normalize `U=F_N/sqrt(N)`, which is unitary. If `a=|supp x|`, Cauchy–Schwarz gives `|(Ux)_j|<=sqrt(a/N)||x||_2`. If `Ux` has `b` nonzero entries, Parseval gives `||x||_2^2=||Ux||_2^2<=ab/N ||x||_2^2`. Thus `ab>=N` for every nonzero `x`.

**Prime additive scope.** The `p+1` theorem is invoked over `C` (or another field whose `p`-DFT has all minors nonzero). This conclusion is *not* automatically valid in every finite field with a primitive pth root. The code exhaustively certifies the full-spark condition separately for `F_7/F_11/F_29` at transform lengths 3/5/7, respectively.

## Lemma 2 — Closure of sparse-inverse networks under low-rank stage perturbations

Let `H_i=G_i+R_i`, both `H_i,G_i` invertible and `rank(R_i)<=r_i`. Then

`rank(H_i^{-1}-G_i^{-1}) <= r_i`

because `H_i^{-1}-G_i^{-1}=-H_i^{-1} R_i G_i^{-1}`.

Set `H=H_L ... H_1`, and `G=G_L ... G_1`. Telescoping the inverse products

`H^{-1}=H_1^{-1} ... H_L^{-1}` and `G^{-1}=G_1^{-1} ... G_L^{-1}`

shows `rank(H^{-1}-G^{-1})<=sum_i r_i`. Specifically, replace one inverse factor at a time; each replacement is a product of fixed matrices around a rank-`<=r_i` difference. Since `colsp(AB)<=colsp(A) colsp(B)` (by taking unions of column supports),

`colsp(G^{-1}) <= product_i s_i`.

Both facts remain valid with arbitrary orderings, monomial coordinate gauges and noncommuting stages. QED.

## Theorem 3 — Green–Union rigidity gate

If `H_L ... H_1=F_N`, then, with `r=sum_i r_i` and `s=product_i s_i`,

` s(r+1)^2 >= N. `

If `N=p` is prime (over complex scalars),

` (s+1)(r+1) >= p+1. `

In particular, for inverse column sparsity <=2 per stage,

` 2^L(r+1)^2>=N ` (all lengths),

` (2^L+1)(r+1)>=N+1 ` (prime lengths).

**Proof.** From Lemma 2, `F_N^{-1}=G^{-1}+R` with `colsp(G^{-1})<=s` and `rank(R)<=r`. Apply Lemma 1. QED.

**Corollary (bounded rank per stage).** If each layer adds at most a fixed `r_0` to the low-rank budget, then `r<=r_0 L`, hence `2^L(r_0 L+1)^2>=N`. Therefore `L>=log_2 N-O(log log N)`. In a *materialized sequential layer executor* charging `Theta(N)` work per full-vector Green layer, this requires `Omega(N log N)` operations. This last statement is **not** an unrestricted circuit lower bound; a genuinely joint evaluator that does not materialize each layer lies outside that execution contract.

## Lemma 4 — Maximum Weyl noncommutativity is insufficient

Let `S e_j=e_(j+1)` and `T e_j=omega^j e_j`. Then `T S=omega S T`. Let `A=I-alpha S`, `B=I-beta T` with `alpha,beta!=0` and both invertible. Put `G_S=A^{-1}`, `G_T=B^{-1}`. Then

`[G_S,G_T]=G_S G_T [A,B] G_T G_S`,

and `[A,B]=alpha beta (S T-T S)=alpha beta (1-omega) S T` is invertible. Consequently

`rank([G_S,G_T])=N`.

Nevertheless `G_S` is applicable in O(N) field operations by a cyclic first-order recurrence, and `G_T` by O(N) pointwise field operations and O(N) charged inverse preparation. The inverse of `G_S G_T` is `(I-beta T)(I-alpha S)`, at most **2-sparse per column**. By Theorem 3 a rank-r addition to this full-rank-noncommuting pair cannot equal Fourier unless

`3(r+1)>=p+1` (prime lengths), or `2(r+1)^2>=N` (all lengths).

Thus commutator rank is not itself evidence of Fourier-equivalent computational capacity. QED.

## Lemma 5 — Chirp circulant's inverse is dense (odd lengths)

For odd N, put `h=2^{-1} mod N`, `C_(i,j)=omega^{-h(i-j)^2}` and `D_jj=omega^{h j^2}`. The identity `F_N=D C D` follows by completing the square in `ij=h(i^2+j^2-(i-j)^2)` modulo N.

The `N` Fourier eigenvalues of `C` are a nonzero Gauss sum times a quadratic phase. Nonvanishing follows by completing the square and using orthogonality (the squared Gauss-sum magnitude is N over complex scalars). Their reciprocals also form a quadratic phase, so the inverse Fourier transform gives a **nonzero quadratic chirp in every coordinate**. Hence every entry of `C^{-1}` is nonzero.

If `C` were a product of L invertible one-pole cyclic Green factors `(I-alpha_i S^{a_i})^{-1}`, then its inverse would be a product of L binomials, having at most `2^L` support positions per column. Therefore **L>=ceil(log_2 N)**. This special case is weaker than Theorem 3's full-network treatment but highlights why the known Bluestein chirp identity alone does not deliver O(N). QED.

## Theorem 6 — Exact Weyl-orbit Green fusion (a positive but scoped result)

Suppose `K|N`, `zeta=omega^(N/K)` and `U e_j=zeta^j e_j`, so `U S U^-1=zeta S`. If `alpha^N!=1`, then every factor below is invertible and

` (1/K) sum_(j=0)^(K-1) U^j (I-alpha S)^-1 U^(-j) = (I-alpha^K S^K)^-1. `

This is an identity of **full N x N operators**, hence applies to every raw input, not only structured signals. **Proof:** Expand each inverse by the finite cyclic geometric identity

`(I-beta S)^-1 = (1-beta^N)^-1 sum_(d=0)^(N-1) beta^d S^d`.

Because `zeta` is primitive of order K, averaging its powers over `j` annihilates all terms whose exponent `d` is not divisible by K. The remaining sum is exactly the geometric expansion of `(I-alpha^K S^K)^-1` on K disjoint cyclic lanes of length `N/K`. QED.

**Cost:** K independent one-pole Green passes take `Theta(KN)` work in the literal evaluator. The right-hand side requires one `O(N)` stride-K cyclic solve with `O(N)` storage and charged scalar preparation. This is a genuine joint savings for **the averaged Green operator**. It is also a symmetry projection onto a higher-stride *Green* operator, not a new full DFT algorithm. The resulting operator remains inside the sparse-inverse Green model and is subject to Theorem 3 when proposed as a Fourier factor.

## What these results DO NOT prove

- No lower bound on unrestricted arithmetic circuits, on sums of multiple Green-network branches, or on arbitrary noninvertible overlapping stages.
- No lower bound on all complex DFT algorithms operating in the exact-register model, and certainly no refutation of the published uniform sub-N-log-N construction.
- No implication that a dense/high-rank matrix is expensive; a full-rank correction can itself have a linear-time executor.
- No claim of numerical stability or bit-complexity optimality from exact finite-field tests.
