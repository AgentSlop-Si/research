# Phase 29: Exact two-coupling Green synthesis and Macaulay obstructions

**Date:** 10 October 2026.  **Scope:** Exact arithmetic, a specifically seeded family of independently ordered cyclic Green sums, and two arbitrary internal monomial masks. **This is not a new sub-`N log N` Fourier algorithm, nor a uniform no-go theorem for every Green circuit.**

## 0. Source and definitions

We continue from Phase 28, particularly `F=A D_1 P_1 B P_2 D_2 C` and the type-II/projective row-pair moment invariants. `F_N=[zeta_N^(jk)]` denotes the **unnormalized** DFT; a global `sqrt(N)` normalization can be included in a diagonal mask. `A,C` are fixed invertible Green sums. Set `T=A^-1 F_N C^-1`, which is everywhere nonzero in our seeded algebraic examples. The *new* middle network is

```
B(lambda,mu) = G0 + lambda G1 + mu G2,
G_t = L_t (I-alpha_t W_t)^(-1) R_t,
alpha=(1/4,1/5,1/7).
```

Each `W_t` is a Hamiltonian successor permutation generated from the prescribed seed; each diagonal gauge uses powers of the same primitive `N`th root of unity. The three cycles may be independent and non-affine. Applying `B` requires **three O(N) cyclic Green solves**; finding Fourier-equivalent masks and permutations is an entirely separate, offline algebraic problem.

A complete factorization would require

```
T = D_1 P_1 B(lambda,mu) P_2 D_2,
```

with arbitrary nonzero diagonal entries and arbitrary independent row/column permutations. We allow **all complex values** of lambda and mu, including near-singular and nonunit-modulus choices; the Green denominators themselves remain fixed and invertible.

The arithmetic certificates are first produced over `Fp`, `p=998244353`, using primitive root 3; at N=4 we also independently use `p=2013265921`, primitive root 31. These are reductions of the *same* characteristic-zero construction over `K=Q(zeta_N)`, with exact rational denominators invertible modulo the chosen primes.

## 1. A one-slice theorem for two tunable couplings

For nowhere-zero `M`, let

```
h_s(i,j;M) = (sum_k (M_ik/M_jk)^s) (sum_k (M_jk/M_ik)^s),
Q_s(M)=N^3 + 2 sum_{i<j} h_s(i,j;M),     s=1,2.
```

Each `Q_s` is invariant under two-sided diagonal scalings and independent row/column permutations. Consequently Fourier equivalence requires `Q_s(B(lambda,mu)) = Q_s(T)` for s=1,2.

Let `D(lambda,mu)=prod_ij B(lambda,mu)[i,j]`. Clearing denominators gives

```
P_s(lambda,mu) = D(lambda,mu)^s * (Q_s(B(lambda,mu))-Q_s(T)).
```

Each `P_s` is a bivariate polynomial of total degree at most `s N^2`. As `mu` tends formally to infinity, `B(lambda,mu)=mu G2+O(1)`, hence

```
[mu^(s N^2)] P_s(lambda,mu)
 = (prod_ij G2_ij)^s (Q_s(G2)-Q_s(T)),
```

**independent of lambda**. In our verified examples this coefficient is nonzero in the original characteristic-zero field, certified by its nonzero modular reduction. Thus the degree in mu of each `P_s` is **exactly** `s N^2`, and its leading coefficient in `K[lambda]` is a unit.

**One-slice lemma.** Let P,Q in K[lambda,mu] each have nonzero constant leading coefficient as polynomials in mu. If there exists a scalar lambda0 such that `gcd(P(lambda0,mu), Q(lambda0,mu))=1`, then P and Q share no nonconstant polynomial factor in K[lambda,mu].

**Proof.** Any common irreducible factor H of positive mu-degree must have mu-leading coefficient a unit: it divides the constant unit mu-leading coefficients of P,Q in K[lambda]. Thus H(lambda0,mu) retains positive degree and divides both specializations, contradiction. A common factor depending only on lambda likewise divides a nonzero constant mu-leading coefficient, impossible. QED.

**Modular-to-characteristic-zero certificate.** For the exact cyclotomic-rational coefficients, preservation of the two highest mu-degrees and a modular Bézout identity `U*P_1(lambda0,mu)+V*P_2(lambda0,mu)=1 mod p` imply the specialized characteristic-zero resultant is nonzero. (An exact zero resultant would reduce to zero after localizing the verified nonzero denominators.) Then the one-slice lemma applies over K.

By Bézout's theorem, two plane algebraic curves with no common component have at most `(N^2)(2N^2)=2N^4` joint affine complex points, counted with multiplicities. Exclude any point for which `D(lambda,mu)=0` before interpreting it as an eligible Green phase factorization. **This is a finite-locus theorem, not an empty-locus theorem.**

Full modular polynomial coefficients and explicit Bézout multipliers are in `certificates/bezout_two_param_N*.json`. A **deliberate bad slice** at N=4, lambda=0, produces a shared linear factor solely because three matrix entries vanish at the candidate mu. The corresponding Green matrix is inadmissible as a two-sided monomial equivalent of the nowhere-zero Fourier target. This is recorded as a guard against falsely treating denominator roots as Fourier solutions.

## 2. Complete exclusion at N=4 using homogeneous quadrics

The finite-locus theorem led to a stronger result, including both arbitrary internal permutations. Fix a row mapping `r` and column mapping `c`. For every target row i>0 and column j>0, the cross-ratio equality is necessary:

```
T_ij*T_00*B_{r_i,c_0}(lambda,mu)*B_{r_0,c_j}(lambda,mu)
 - T_i0*T_0j*B_{r_i,c_j}(lambda,mu)*B_{r_0,c_0}(lambda,mu) = 0.
```

Homogenize `B` as `B_h(lambda,mu,nu)=nu G0+lambda G1+mu G2`. The resulting equations are homogeneous **quadrics in three variables**. If a complex coupling pair exists, `[lambda:mu:1]` is a common nonzero projective zero.

For N=4, take all i,j in {1,2,3}, giving nine quadrics f_ij. Multiply each by the six degree-two monomials and express all products in the 15-dimensional basis of ternary forms of degree four. This gives a 54-by-15 Macaulay coefficient matrix `M_4(r,c)`.

**Certificate theorem.** If `rank(M_4(r,c))=15`, the nine f_ij have no common nonzero projective zero, hence the proposed Fourier factorization is impossible for the fixed r,c and for **every** complex lambda,mu. Indeed the degree-four multiples span all ternary degree-four forms, which would have to vanish at a common zero. In particular `lambda^4=mu^4=nu^4=0`, forcing the forbidden zero vector.

The script exhaustively enumerates **4!^2=576** row/column permutation pairs. Every Macaulay matrix has rank exactly 15 mod `998244353`, and again mod `2013265921`. For the first prime the certificate stores 576 separate choices of 15 independent rows and their nonzero 15x15 determinant. Each nonzero modular minor is a nonzero algebraic number over K, proving the characteristic-zero rank statement. This is a **complete fixed-seed, all-couplings/all-masks/all-permutations exclusion** at N=4, not a generic statement.

The deliberately planted positive control `T=B_h(2,3,1)` fails the full-rank condition for the correct mapping (rank 14), as it must.

## 3. Complete exclusion at N=8 using only three row/column images

An exhaustive search over all `(8!)^2` full permutations is unnecessary. Every complete row/column permutation induces two **ordered, distinct triples** consisting of the images of target rows 0,1,2 and target columns 0,1,2. There are `8*7*6=336` triples per side, or only **112,896** possible paired images.

For each triple pair, restrict to the 3-by-3 anchored target minor. The four cross-ratio equations i,j in {1,2} produce homogeneous ternary quadrics. Their degree-four multiples form a 24-by-15 Macaulay matrix.

The exact script checked **all 112,896 paired triples**. Every matrix has rank **15** modulo `998244353`. Thus no full row/column permutation, no nonzero internal diagonal masks, and **no complex choice of lambda,mu** can make the precisely seeded N=8 Green triple Fourier-correct between the fixed outer mixers. This conclusion follows from a nonzero characteristic-zero minor for each triple pair; it is not a probabilistic test. A sample of explicit determinant witnesses, the complete rank histogram, the iteration digest, and the deterministic generator are in `certificates/anchored_exhaustive_N8_p998244353.json`.

At N=16, only the finite exceptional-locus theorem is proved. Two thousand sampled triple pairs gave full ranks, but they **do not** exclude every mapping; we do not elevate this finite sample to a theorem.

## 4. Verification and cost

- Eleven new regression tests passed. The tests independently compare cleared polynomials against direct moment evaluations, verify N=4/8/16 Bézout identities, check saved N=4 determinants, re-enumerate N=8 triples, test genuine positive controls, and compare the three-term `O(N)` Green executor against dense matrices through N=128.
- The **Green executor** is a real `O(N)` per-input algorithm for its *candidate* matrix, not an FFT. Matrix inversions and symbolic tests used in the feasibility audit are expensive offline computations; no claim is made that they form an efficient Fourier construction.
- The N=4 and N=8 no-coupling results concern exactly the **seeded 3-term affine pencil** and fixed A,C. Other independently permuted Green terms, more free parameters, and deeper circuits remain unexcluded.
- The two exact finite fields audit different algebraic reductions. Neither numerical fitting nor a floating-point near-zero threshold appears in the certificates.
