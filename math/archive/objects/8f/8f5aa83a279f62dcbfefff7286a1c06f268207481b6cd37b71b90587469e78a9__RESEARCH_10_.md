# FFT research continuation — algebraic elimination and exact local compatibility

**Date:** 2026-10-09  
**Status:** New exact 30-polynomial Fourier-feasibility reduction (on a specified regular chart), exact second-order obstruction with a 61×61 modular determinant certificate, and an independently verified boundary between general quasiseparable stages and the narrower separated-generator chart. **No proof that `F_8` violates the polynomial system, no `O(N)` FFT, and no new Fourier asymptotic bound.**

## Research objective and provenance

Our independent research path asks whether a fixed, input-independent network of globally overlapping, possibly nonunitary quasiseparable stages with arbitrary permutations can compute all DFT outputs on fresh arbitrary inputs in `o(N log N)` charged arithmetic, ideally `O(N)`. We do not count caching, many queries sharing factors, threads, numerical near-matches, or a dressed ordinary FFT as a win. All previous code and its 29 tests are preserved recursively in `prior_checkpoint/`.

This work is independent of the recently circulated exact-complex Fourier power-saving claim in the OpenAI Mathematics collection, which assumes unrestricted exact coefficients. Our result here is **only** about a particular algebraic architecture and finite exact certificates, not practical speed or a universal lower bound.

## A. Stage model and original min-cut-blind permutation

Fix `n=8` and

```
p = [7, 0, 6, 3, 4, 1, 2, 5].
```

A separated-generator rank-one stage has

\[
S_{ij}=\begin{cases}
\ell_i v_j,&i>j,\\
d_i,&i=j,\\
u_i w_j,&i<j.
\end{cases}
\]

Writing `P` for the row-permutation matrix with `(P A)[i,:]=A[p[i],:]`, the product is `M=B P A`. The earlier checkpoint exhaustively certified that the *signal network* has maximum min-cut rank for **all** source/output subset pairs, even though the complex algebraic image of this generator map has dimension exactly 60 inside 64 dimensions. The previous certificate is preserved; `prior_checkpoint/superconcentrator_gate.py` regenerates it.

The rank-one chart is narrower than the complete set of quasiseparable-order-one matrices (see Section D). All claims below are scoped accordingly.

## B. New theorem: eliminate the second stage for F8

Let `F=F_8` with entries `F[i,j]=ζ_8^(ij)` and suppose

\[
F=BPA.
\]

Because `F` is invertible, both `A` and `B` must be invertible. On the dense regular chart with every diagonal entry of `A` nonzero, use the **intermediate diagonal gauge** to make `diag(A)=1`. On the additional open patch with `ell_A[7] != 0` and `u_A[0] != 0`, use the two generator rescaling gauges to normalize `ell_A[7]=1` and `u_A[0]=1`. This leaves exactly:

- `6+7=13` lower coordinates, and
- `6+7=13` upper coordinates,

for **26 independent first-stage parameters**.

Now set

\[
U=PA,\qquad G=F\operatorname{adj}(U).
\]

For any solution `det(U) != 0`, so `B=G/det(U)`. On the further open patch `B[7,0] != 0` and `B[0,7] != 0`, `B` belongs to the separated-generator rank-one chart **if and only if** the following **30 polynomial equations** hold:

**15 lower equations**

\[
G_{ij}G_{7,0}-G_{i,0}G_{7,j}=0,
\quad 2\le i\le6,\ 1\le j<i.
\]

**15 upper equations**

\[
G_{ij}G_{0,7}-G_{i,7}G_{0,j}=0,
\quad 1\le i\le5,\ i<j\le6.
\]

**Proof (sufficiency as well as necessity):** If `B` is rank-one strictly lower, `B[i,j]=ell_i v_j`, giving the first identities. Conversely, with `B[7,0] != 0`, set `ell_7=1`, `ell_i=B[i,0]/B[7,0]` for `1<=i<=6`, `v_j=B[7,j]` for `0<=j<=6`; the 15 identities reconstruct every strictly lower entry. The upper part is analogous: set `u_0=1`, `u_i=B[i,7]/B[0,7]`, `w_j=B[0,j]`. The diagonal is unconstrained. Replacing `B` by `G/det(U)` multiplies every quadratic identity by the same nonzero denominator squared, and thus preserves zero loci. **QED on the open patch.**

Every `G[i,j]` is a polynomial in the 26 first-stage variables over the cyclotomic field `Q(ζ_8)`:

\[
G_{ij}=\sum_{k=0}^{7}\zeta_8^{ik}(-1)^{j+k}
\det U_{\widehat{j},\widehat{k}}.
\]

Since each `A[i,j]` has degree at most two in the normalized coordinates, each 7×7 determinant has degree at most 14, and the displayed 30 equations have total degree at most 28. **These are equations in the unknown first-stage coefficients**, not already an implicit polynomial in the target matrix alone. The existence of a solution for `F8` remains open in this research pass. Do not infer nonexistence from overdeterminacy or from finite-field random failures.

`fft_elimination_gate.py` regenerates the identities and checks their necessity and sufficiency numerically over exact prime fields. Twenty independently generated *actual* two-stage products passed all 30 equations and were reconstructed exactly. Twenty generic first-stage samples failed the equations for `F8`; this only checks sensitivity, not unsatisfiability. All positive checks were repeated both modulo **12,289** and **65,537**, each supporting a primitive eighth root.

**Next decisive research gate:** use exact algebraic elimination, Gröbner/regular-chain methods with chart saturation, or certified homotopy plus symbolic reconstruction on these 30 equations. The zero-diagonal and zero-anchor exceptional patches must be covered separately to make any negative result universal for this stage class. A numerical optimizer failure and mere absence of modular solutions are not characteristic-zero exclusion proofs.

## C. New theorem: a nontrivial exact second-order compatibility obstruction

Let `Φ(A,B)=BPA`. At the recorded integer generator point (seed `1011`) the Jacobian `J` has complex rank exactly 60. All 20 kernel dimensions are accounted for by explicit, linearly independent generator gauges: six per stage (unused boundary factors and two off-diagonal rescalings) plus eight intermediate diagonal gauge directions.

Choose the generator tangent which increases only `d_A[7]` and `d_B[0]`, each by `t`. Since `p[0]=7`, its exact polynomial output is

\[
\Phi(t)=M_0+t\Delta+t^2 E_{0,7},
\]

where `Δ` is a legitimate generator tangent and `E_(0,7)` has only one nonzero entry equal to one.

The exact modular rank computations show

\[
\operatorname{rank}_{12289}(J)=60,\qquad
\operatorname{rank}_{12289}[J\mid\operatorname{vec}(E_{0,7})]=61.
\]

A stored **61×61 integer-polynomial minor** of the augmented matrix has determinant **6,913 modulo 12,289** (`curvature_minor_certificate.json`). Since a nonzero modular integer determinant is nonzero over the rational and complex numbers, `E_(0,7)` does not lie in the complex Jacobian image at this explicitly given parameter point.

The 20 explicit gauge vectors are independent modulo 12,289 and span the entire Jacobian kernel. Differentiating the parameter symmetries shows that the conormal pairing of the Hessian with any gauge tangent is zero. This was independently checked for all 20 generators, plus combinations, in `gauge_curvature.py`.

**Consequent local restriction:** The first-order output displacement `Δ` is realizable. But no formal parameter curve **through this given generator point** can realize the **straight-line target** `M0+tΔ+0·t²` to second order. The missing quadratic term has a nonzero conormal component. This statement is independent of which first-order parameter lift is chosen inside that point's 20-dimensional gauge kernel.

**Important limitation:** This is a local compatibility restriction for the recorded realization point. It is not a global implicit `P(M)=0`, it does not show that the straight-line target has no other representation elsewhere, and it does not exclude the Fourier matrix.

### Exact local second-order membership algorithm

`jet_compatibility.py` implements the full necessary-and-sufficient second-order **formal lifting test through the fixed parameter point** over `GF(12289)`:

For target jet `M(t)=M0+t Δ1+t² Δ2`, first find `v` satisfying `Jv=Δ1`; then test whether

\[
\Delta_2-\tfrac12 H(v,v)\in\operatorname{im}J.
\]

A second generator coefficient `w` exists exactly when the latter holds. Because all kernel directions are gauges, the result is independent of choice of `v`. The code accepted 20 random **genuine** output 2-jets, passed 20 gauge invariance tests, and independently validated the Hessian against the first three coefficients obtained from exact degree-four polynomial interpolation. It rejected the straight-line jet and accepted the same first-order direction with its correct quadratic correction.

For `n=16,r=2`, a separate exact audit found Jacobian ranks **232** for a random shuffle and **230** for the 2-lane block lift of our 8-point shuffle. All 16 tested diagonal quadratic directions were transverse in both configurations. These are measured tangent ranks and do not establish global image dimensions in the special lifted case.

## D. Important positive control: complete quasiseparable vs separated chart

A standard two-factor Cooley–Tukey decomposition can be arranged with **two quasiseparable-order-one block stages** for `n=8` (`2×4`) and `n=16` (`4×4`), using suitable input/output orderings. Ordering the four-point Fourier matrix's rows and columns as `[0,2,1,3]` makes its upper-right and lower-left `2×2` corner blocks rank one. Hence a block-diagonal union of such F4 blocks has quasiseparable order one. `ct_positive_control.py` verifies every entry over `GF(12289)` for `n=8,16,32`; the first two have stage orders `(1,1)`.

**But those block-diagonal stages do not, in general, fit one global rank-one separated-generator chart:** if two different diagonal blocks have nonzero lower entries `S[i,j]` and `S[x,y]`, then a global representation `S[k,l]=L[k]V[l]` would force a nonzero cross-block entry `S[x,j]`, contradicting block diagonality. The saved code provides explicit nonzero/zero coordinates.

This distinction is essential. A chart-specific algebraic obstruction **must not** be claimed as a lower bound for all quasiseparable FFT factorizations. The two-factor construction is classical Cooley–Tukey, is not asymptotically novel, and does not reduce total all-length arithmetic below `N log N`.

## E. Numerical feasibility probe (not a proof)

`complex_solver.py` uses exactly 60 complex gauge-fixed parameters for two full separated-generator stages. Its analytic Jacobian agrees with finite differences (max error approximately `2×10^-9` for the specified step), and it recovers a synthetic target to normalized residual `2.4×10^-16`. Five fresh starts against the **fixed naturally ordered F8** and shuffle did not achieve a near-zero residual, with normalized residuals approximately `0.0825–0.1151` in those trials. These are **local optimizer outcomes** and cannot establish unsatisfiability. No claim of Fourier exclusion is made on this basis.
