# Next research gate — permutations AND mixing, without hiding the costs

**Current scoped theorem:** A product of any number of full-array one-state weighted forward/backward scans and diagonals, with arbitrary endpoint row/column permutations but **no permutations between scan passes**, requires >=N/2 total one-way scan channels to realize F_N when N is divisible by 4. In the natural order it needs >=N. The theorem is mathematically exact over C and is independently checked at small lengths over finite fields. It does not rule out FFTs with repeated intermediate shuffles.

## The unresolved, falsifiable target

Design an **input-independent wide-stage operator family** in which each stage has O(N) *elementary* operations, including permutations, scalar preparation and memory traffic, and all N exact outputs are produced. Give an executable and independently checked construction for arbitrarily large N requiring **o(log N)** stages (or lower aggregate fully charged arithmetic than the existing best exact general DFT in the *same model*).

Before any large implementation:
1. Prove the matrix identity for all N, not just one root/modulus or optimized finite case.
2. State which stage can mix distant positions and how it does so in O(N) charged arithmetic without a dense hidden macro.
3. Explain why its gain does not contradict bounded-coefficient or stable 2-coordinate lower bounds; if using large coefficients, charge generation and conditioning.
4. Attempt a *multilayout* rank or information-flow certificate tracking several cuts **through the intermediate shuffles**. The one-fixed-cut quasiseparable bound is provably insufficient when a shuffle crosses N/2 positions.
5. Compare against Cooley–Tukey, split-radix, known butterfly factorizations and OpenAI #130; do **not** jump to CrocSwap's rank-certificate constant race or revisit our collision-lookup projects.
6. Reject an approach that only reduces the number of named macros, produces an implicit spectrum, uses a few output bins, requires known input structure, or lets a compressed giant integer act as a unit-cost scalar.

**Research status:** No such new stage family found yet. The rank/cut theorem provides a concrete rejection test for candidate architectures before any benchmark-driven optimism.
