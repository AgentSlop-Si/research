# Phase 17 — Sparse-Graph Fourier Feedback and Magnetic Green Covariance

**Hero #17 research checkpoint · 10 October 2026**  
**Overall status:** Two proof-level obstruction mechanisms for well-defined circuit grammars, one new executable linear-work **non-DFT** operator family, independent exact modular audits. **No arbitrary-input universal `O(N)` FFT. No asserted historical priority.**

## Executive summary

Previous Phase 16 excluded representing the Fourier matrix as `D_L P Q^-1 D_R` when both `P` and `Q` have too-narrow consecutive cyclic supports. The surviving proposed route was **branching, noncommuting Green feedback** with long-distance sparse shifts and quadratic endpoint gauges.

Phase 17 addresses exactly two ways to escape that earlier restriction:

1. Make the sparse supports **arbitrary directed graphs**, not intervals, and add **global low-rank feedback simultaneously in numerator and denominator**. A graph counting/Vandermonde argument gives the necessary total feedback rank

   \[
   \boxed{r_P+r_Q\ge\lfloor N/(1+st)\rfloor},
   \]

   with no restrictions on edge distances or on input/output permutations/scalings. Here `s,t` are maximum column sparsity degrees of the two base maps.

2. Abandon graph sparsity as the invariant and check the complete **Fourier covariance defect**. For the mixed noncommuting Green baseline `Q^-1=(I-αS-βT)^-1`, we prove for `α !=0`

   \[
   \boxed{\operatorname{rank}(Q^{-1}S-T^{-1}Q^{-1})\ge N-2}.
   \]

   Consequently, turning this Green operator into the DFT with a rank-`r` additive correction (including a rank-`r` Woodbury feedback modification of the denominator) requires `r>=ceil((N-2)/2)`. In even dimensions the generic required rank rises to `N/2`.

Both theorems apply to **all inputs and all appropriate lengths** within their specified operator models, but neither is an unrestricted arithmetic circuit lower bound.

## Exact graph/Hall–Vandermonde certificate

For a physical output-row set `I` corresponding to consecutive Fourier frequencies, and a physical input-column set `B`:

- the base numerator has no edges from `B` to `I`: `P[I,B]=0`;
- the base denominator's neighbor union `J=Γ_Q(B)` has cardinality `|J|<=|I|`.

Then the total low-rank correction dimension must be at least `|B|`. A hypothetical smaller correction would have a nonzero common kernel direction that travels only through `J`, while its Fourier transform is required to vanish on the consecutive-frequency rows `I`. The restricted Vandermonde map is injective, a contradiction.

A double-counting argument generates such a certificate *for every sparse support graph* with `|B|=floor(N/(1+st))`. This works over finite fields with a primitive `N`-th root, over complex numbers, and for **arbitrary independent endpoint permutations** because consecutive-in-frequency rows can be considered in the permuted physical row order. See `PROOF_CERTIFICATE.md` for every step.

The bound improves in an aligned local-band setting:

\[
\boxed{r_P+r_Q\ge\max(0,\lfloor N/2\rfloor-a-b)},
\]

where the base numerator and denominator have cyclic column-band radii `a` and `b`. This extends the prior no-feedback Phase 16 rigidity theorem, while respecting its coordinate-order assumptions.

**Example: dense nonlocal output interaction.** `Q=I-αS^{N/2}` has only two nonzeros per column but a long-range edge crossing a balanced input cut. `P=I` has one. A quotient with low-rank corrections must still have `r_P+r_Q>=floor(N/3)` by the graph theorem, even though the old narrow-band argument cannot be applied. This is a *rank* obstruction, not a proof that such a high-rank correction costs `Ω(N²)` arithmetic.

## A distinct, basis-independent covariance test

A true negative-exponent Fourier transform satisfies `F S=T^-1 F`, where `S` cyclically shifts, and `T` modulates by powers of the root. For any operator `A`, its intertwining defect is `Δ(A)=AS-T^-1A`. Rank-`r` additive corrections can change the defect by at most rank `2r`.

For `Q=I-αS-βT`, the rank of `Δ(Q^-1)` equals that of `S Q-QT^-1` by invertible multiplication. The latter is a second-order cyclic difference operator with a nonzero `-α y_(i-2)` coefficient. A kernel vector is completely determined by two terminal coordinates, hence its nullity is at most two. This proves `rank Δ(Q^-1)>=N-2` **uniformly**.

For even `N`, `Δ(I)=S-T^-1` is invertible; determinant-polynomial continuity implies the mixed Green defect has **full rank for generic parameters** over complex numbers. The independent finite-field audit also finds only rank `N` or occasionally `N-1` in its tested nonsingular parameter grid; this observation is not asserted as a universal corank-one theorem.

## Positive control: dense global feedback in linear work

The mixed magnetic Green equation

\[
(1-\beta\omega^j)y_j-\alpha y_{j-1}=x_j
\]

is solvable by an exact, charged cyclic recurrence after `O(N)` preparation (with stated nonzero-pivot assumptions). We further insert an **all-to-all rank-one feedback** `uv^T` into the denominator and evaluate the inverse using one precomputed Green solve and a Sherman–Morrison scalar correction.

The code calculates every entry for any fresh raw input `x`, not merely a compressed input family. All `N` row residual equations are verified independently, and small cases are compared with dense Gaussian elimination.

For a supplied primitive root and a fixed prime-field model, the implementation's counted scalar arithmetic is:

| Transform length parameter `N` | Green plan prep | Complete rank-one feedback prep | Feedback online | Prep + one online |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 256 | 2,050 | 3,845 | 2,306 | 6,151 |
| 1,024 | 8,194 | 15,365 | 9,218 | 24,583 |
| 4,096 | 32,770 | 61,445 | 36,866 | 98,311 |
| 65,536 | 524,290 | 983,045 | 589,826 | 1,572,871 |

These are **algebraic operation counts, not elapsed-time benchmarks**. The given root and field are considered part of the transform specification; their discovery/bit complexity is not included. Scalar batch inversion, generation of all `N` powers for the Green coefficients, feedback preparation and all online vector writes *are* included. As expected, both preparation and execution scale linearly with `N`.

Crucially, this operator is **NOT** the Fourier matrix. The covariance theorem explains why a single global feedback loop cannot fix it at growing `N`, even though the feedback makes the coefficient matrix dense.

## Why this matters for the FFT goal

Graph + Union can make *many* noncommuting Green branches jointly cheap, as the earlier Phase 12–15 examples showed. But merely adding `O(1)` global feedback dimensions to a bounded-degree sparse quotient now has a quantitative obstruction. The more general covariance-rank approach also tests low-rank corrections to a particular fast Green baseline **without needing sparsity information**.

The viable next architecture must involve genuinely shared **multibranch nonseparable execution** that cannot be reduced to a sparse quotient plus low-rank update or a single Green inverse with small additive correction. It must pass `M S=T^-1M` and `M e0=ones`, then demonstrate fewer charged operations for the **entire** DFT on every input.

The current exact-complex-arithmetic benchmark to beat is the formalized OpenAI Math family #130 uniform all-length result `O(N (log N)^(1-10^-13))` in its stated unrestricted-coefficient arithmetic model, not the classical `N log N` baseline alone: https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/lean/docs/130.md .

## Historical novelty / publication audit

The ingredient Vandermonde lemma is classical; Fourier uncertainty is classical (Tao 2005 and earlier work); sparse/low-rank decompositions and matrix-rigidity arguments have substantial prior art; Sherman–Morrison is classical; covariance as a linear map is standard. The **combined feedback-cut proposition is a new proof in this research package**, but an independent literature search is required before asserting it has not appeared elsewhere. No historical-first discovery or journal-ready novelty is claimed.
