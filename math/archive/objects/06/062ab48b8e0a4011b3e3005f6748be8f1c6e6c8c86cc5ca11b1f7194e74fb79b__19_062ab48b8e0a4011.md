# Phase 12 — Dense-safe algebraic reductions and the remaining transform barrier

**Date:** 2026-10-09  
**Scope:** fresh arbitrary input polynomials over a finite prime field, plus a separate exact signed-digit integer experiment. All runnable methods are sequential, self-contained, and do not rely on cached values or related previous inputs. **No new universal sub-`N log N` DFT or integer-multiplication theorem is claimed.**

## Executive conclusion

The user's `AB=(A−k)B+kB` idea motivated a search for algebraic decompositions that save work even when every short annihilator leaves inputs dense. This produced a clear **positive** and a clear **barrier**:

- **Positive, guaranteed all-input reduction:** Karatsuba converts four smaller polynomial products into three; evaluation/interpolation reduces the count of nonscalar, input-dependent products to **`2n−1`** for two length-`n` inputs over a sufficiently large field. We implemented and exactly verified both approaches.
- **Barrier:** `2n−1` is already the optimal **bilinear multiplicative rank** for ordinary full polynomial multiplication. Even that optimal algorithm requires `Theta(n^2)` surrounding field operations in our straightforward implementation, making it slower than an ordinary `Theta(n log n)` NTT at larger lengths. The genuine target is the cost of linear transforms, scalar preparation, and movement—not further reducing the count of pointwise input-dependent products.
- **Second barrier:** A small family of affine low-rank templates plus sparse residuals cannot represent every input vector over `F2`. A simple cardinality argument generalizes Phase 11's short-filter obstruction far beyond recurrence filters.

All the mathematics in the first two bullets builds on known Karatsuba/Toom/interpolation and standard tensor-rank or counting facts. We do not claim novelty of the identities; the project contribution is a falsifiable, exact, measured comparison and a sharper research target.

## 1. What does `A−k` actually buy?

For integers `A,B,k`, the identity `AB=(A-k)B+kB` is tautologically correct. If `k=2^t`, then `kB` is a shift. It helps dramatically when `A−2^t` is a tiny number, but to work for *all* `A` we can recursively write `A=Σ_j d_j 2^j` with signed digits `d_j ∈{-1,0,1}`. This is classical **non-adjacent form (NAF)**. It guarantees no adjacent nonzero digits and reduces shift-add counts compared with plain binary recoding.

For random 4,096-bit `A` in our reproducible sample, standard binary used 2,069 nonzero digits versus 1,344 signed digits. But each shifted addition touches potentially Θ(n) bits; the general method still takes O(n²) bit work. The `shift_multiply` prototype was hundreds of times slower than Python's optimized built-in multiplication for that random input. This disproves the naive hope that a constant-factor reduction in the number of summands changes worst-case asymptotic complexity.

A fixed `k` does not reduce the intrinsic bilinear rank: it only changes the affine representation of A and adds a linear `kB` correction. Choosing a data-dependent `k` needs its selection and the cost of `(A-k)B` charged too. Choosing `k=A` makes the residual zero but leaves the original `AB` in the correction.

## 2. A uniform identity that always removes a recursive multiplication

Write `A=A0+z^h A1` and `B=B0+z^h B1`. Define

```
U = A0*B0
V = A1*B1
W = (A0+A1)*(B0+B1) - U - V
A*B = U + z^h*W + z^(2h)*V.
```

This is Karatsuba and holds for **all** coefficient arrays, independent of their values. For n=2^L, recursively to scalar products, this uses `3^L=n^(log2 3)` input-dependent scalar multiplications, compared with `4^L=n²` for schoolbook. Additional additions are charged; the overall field-arithmetic bound is `O(n^(log2 3))`, still asymptotically worse than FFT/NTT's `O(n log n)`.

## 3. The optimal bilinear multiplication bound

Consider all coefficients of `C(z)=A(z)B(z)` with `deg A,deg B<n`. There are `m=2n−1` coefficients `c_0,...,c_(m-1)`, and they are linearly independent bilinear forms in the `a_i,b_j`: each has a monomial `a_i*b_j` with unique degree i+j. A bilinear algorithm that uses `r` nonscalar products of linear forms can only span a space of output bilinear forms of dimension at most `r`. Hence **`r ≥ 2n−1`**.

Over any field containing at least `2n−1` distinct points, evaluation/interpolation attains the bound:

1. Select `m=2n−1` distinct field points `x_0,...,x_(m−1)`.
2. Evaluate both polynomials at those points using linear operations.
3. Compute `m` products `A(x_j)*B(x_j)`.
4. Interpolate the unique degree-`m−1` polynomial C using linear operations.

Thus the minimal number of input-dependent bilinear products is exactly `2n−1` in that model. The method is classical (Toom/Winograd). **This is not a lower bound on total arithmetic operations, bit operations, or nonlinear algorithms using other operations.** In particular, every scalar multiplier by an input-independent constant is charged in OpenAI #130's arithmetic model, so this multiplicative-rank result does not beat #130.

Our executable implementation evaluates at `x_j=j` over `F_998244353`; converts values to Newton divided differences, then converts Newton form to coefficients. It uses only `2n−1` input-dependent products but `O(n²)` additions and scalar multiplications for evaluation and interpolation. A standard NTT likewise uses `O(n)` input-dependent pointwise products, but runs the surrounding transforms in `O(n log n)` field operations.

## 4. Why arbitrary low-rank baselines cannot always make inputs sparse

Let `A` range over all `2^n` binary vectors. Suppose an optimizer offers `M` affine subspaces `S_1,...,S_M` of `F2^n`, each of dimension at most `r`; the optimizer may select the best subspace for the current A. It also permits an edit vector e of Hamming weight at most k:

`A = s+e`, for some `s∈S_t` and `||e||_0 <= k`.

Each affine subspace contains at most `2^r` elements, and there are `V(n,k)=Σ_(j=0)^k binom(n,j)` possible edit vectors. So all permitted representations together cover at most

`M * 2^r * V(n,k)` inputs.

**Necessary condition to handle every input:**

`M * 2^r * V(n,k) >= 2^n.`

In particular, if `log2 M=o(n)`, `r=o(n)`, and `k=o(n)`, then `log2 V(n,k)=o(n)`, so the number of covered inputs is `2^{o(n)}`, vastly smaller than `2^n`. Some inputs necessarily remain far from every available low-dimensional baseline. This applies to arbitrary affine subspaces in this limited family, not only constant or recurrence templates.

For `n=12`, `r=2`, `k=2`, `M=4`, the union-bound ceiling is `4*4*(1+12+66)=1,264` of 4,096 total binary inputs, leaving at least 2,832 inputs uncovered **regardless of how the four affine subspaces are chosen**. Our concrete four-template audit covered only 881, leaving 3,215 uncovered. These are exact finite counts.

This is **not** a theorem that all nonlinear or large-rank representations fail, nor that general Fourier transforms require `Omega(N log N)` in unrestricted models. It precisely rejects the narrow idea that selecting among a modest family of simple `k`-like baselines can guarantee a sparse correction for all random dense inputs.

### Stronger obstruction directly over the complex numbers

There is an even stronger algebraic version for the exact-complex model, which needs **no probability or finite-field counting**. Fix a dimension n and any **finite** list of affine subspaces of `C^n`, each of dimension at most r. For each chosen baseline, allow an arbitrary residual supported in at most k coordinates. For each support `J`, the union `S + span{e_j : j in J}` has dimension at most `r+k`. If `r+k<n`, this is a **proper affine subspace** of `C^n`. There are only finitely many baselines and finitely many supports, so their union is a finite union of proper affine subspaces and therefore cannot cover `C^n`.

A constructive witness argument uses the moment curve `v(t)=(t,t²,...,t^n)`. Each excluded proper affine subspace is contained in a nontrivial hyperplane `α·x=β`. Substituting `v(t)` gives the nonzero polynomial `Σ_(i=1)^n α_i t^i−β`; it has only finitely many roots. Choose t outside the finite union of all forbidden root sets. Then `v(t)` is **not representable** using any of the allowed baselines and k-sparse corrections.

This rules out *guaranteed* low-rank-plus-sparse decompositions from a **finite input-independent template family** when `r+k<n`, no matter how many finite templates are offered. It **does not** rule out arbitrary input-dependent subspaces (such as choosing span{x} itself), infinitely parameterized template families, dense representations that are still cheap to evaluate, or unrestricted FFT methods unrelated to sparse residuals. `complex_dimension_audit.py` provides a small exact-rational illustrative witness for three rank-2 affine templates plus one-coordinate edits in dimension 6.

## 5. What changes about the research target?

A core lesson: **the multiplication-count optimization has essentially reached its natural limit.** We should search for ways to reduce the linear algebra needed to evaluate/interpolate, not shave more variable products from an already optimal bilinear multiplication algorithm.

OpenAI #130 (its separate 2026 exact DFT theorem) uses a specialized unrestricted exact complex arithmetic model and claims `O(n(log n)^(1−10^(−13)))` for arbitrary fresh DFTs, with further distinctions between theorem and formalization. That result is about the *linear transforms themselves*. Our field-limited Python NTT and our bilinear rank experiments do not improve its exponent or transfer to its model automatically.

Three falsifiable follow-ups:

1. **Charged linear-preconditioner search.** Fix N=8,16,32 and a class of invertible, input-independent matrix factorizations. Verify equality to the exact DFT matrix and count *all* scalar multiplications, additions, index changes and saved intermediate values. Compare against split-radix and the local compiler of #130, not schoolbook convolution alone.
2. **Compact evaluation/interpolation schedules.** For a fixed small N, seek an exact factorization of the Vandermonde evaluation and interpolation maps that preserves their `2N−1` bilinear products and strictly reduces *linear* operations. An improved finite circuit would be a candidate, not a universal theorem.
3. **Model-aware uniformity.** Any proposed recursive improvement must be specified for a family of all N, with time to prepare scalars, construct schedules, and move data included. A finite N operation-count win or a model-dependent word-RAM lookup speedup is not a new all-length bound.
