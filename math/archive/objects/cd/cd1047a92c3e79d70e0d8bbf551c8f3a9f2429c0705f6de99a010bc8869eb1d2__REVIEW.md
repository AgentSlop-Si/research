## Bessel-cycle mathematics

The order recurrence used in Theorem1 is classical: [NIST DLMF10.6.1](https://dlmf.nist.gov/10.6.E1). Multiplying that scalar recurrence by each input coefficient and conjugating by the Fourier matrix proves the asserted operator identity for nonzero sample indices. At index0, the reciprocal multiplier is zero; only the r=1 step subtracts the J0(0)=1 channel, requiring the stated rank-one constant-vector correction. The correction's sign and unitary normalization are correct.

The inverse identity H+P0=(A+P0)^−1 follows immediately in their common Fourier eigenbasis: the eigenvalues are1 at index0 and respectively1/b and b elsewhere. Every disjoint equally sized off-diagonal minor is a nonzero Cauchy determinant.

This excludes the specified sparse-plus-low-rank inverse model. It proves no general arithmetic lower bound.
