# HERO #17 — Phase 15: Multiscale Weyl support, chirp rigidity, and exact Green fusion

**October 10, 2026 · Exact symbolic finite-field research · Target: arbitrary fresh full DFT in O(N).**  
**Status: Target still UNSOLVED.** This checkpoint contains two independently verified restricted lower-bound families, a high-rank/linear-generation counterexample, and a real linear-time multiscale fusion for a different operator. It does **not** give a new arbitrary-input Fourier speedup or a new integer-multiplication upper bound.

## Why this phase differs from Phase 14

Phase 14 showed that a long stride `S^(N/2)` transports maximum cut rank in `O(N)` work, invalidating a nearest-neighbor cut argument. Phase 15 therefore abandons fixed coordinate-cut rank. We instead count **exact Weyl monomials in the inverse of the complete operator**, allowing every stage to choose different long-range shift/modulation directions. This prevents a rotated Weyl frame from disguising a low support count.

A second branch examines the cyclic quadratic-chirp convolution inside the exact Bluestein factorization. Its inverse has full shift support and full binary tensor rank; neither observation alone proves high arithmetic cost. An independent, classical dyadic fusion construction confirms that operation sharing can genuinely collapse logarithmically many stages to linear work.

## Result A — a mixed-orientation, all-stride inverse support theorem

For any odd `N`, the inverse Fourier operator has exactly `N²` nonzero coefficients in the standard Weyl basis `S^a T^b`; for even `N`, exactly `N²/2`. This follows from quadratic Gauss sums and holds over suitable exact prime fields too.

A product of `L` Green stages whose **inverse** is a linear combination of at most three Weyl monomials has an inverse with at most `3^L` Weyl terms. Arbitrary long shifts, orientation changes and Weyl monomial gauges do not change this counting argument. Thus any *exact product representation* of the full Fourier matrix obeys

\[
\boxed{L\ge\lceil\log_3(N^2/2)\rceil \quad (N\text{ even}).}
\]

For dyadic lengths:

| `N` | Radix-2 levels | Required three-atom inverse stages |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 16 | 4 | 5 |
| 256 | 8 | 10 |
| 4,096 | 12 | 15 |
| 65,536 | 16 | 20 |
| 1,048,576 | 20 | 25 |

Theorem A covers **products**, not sums of branches or unrestricted shared graphs. Its minimum number of named stages says nothing by itself about whether a joint evaluator can avoid per-stage `N`-length passes.

## Result B — Bluestein's convolution cannot be one perfect dyadic Green sweep

For `N=2^m>=4`, choose a primitive `2N`th root `zeta`. The cyclic chirp matrix

\[
C_{ij}=\zeta^{(i-j)^2}
\]

satisfies `C=D F_- D` with `D[j,j]=zeta^(j²)` and has inverse convolution kernel `q_j=zeta^(-j²)/N`, **nonzero at every coordinate**.

Any product of `L` ordinary cyclic-shift Green resolvents has an inverse with at most `2^L` shift monomials, so `L>=m`. The equality case `L=m` would require every subset of shift strides to produce a distinct residue. Character orthogonality then forces distinct `2`-adic valuations of all shifts, but the actual quadratic chirp violates the necessary multiplicative parallelogram identity:

\[
\frac{q_0q_{s+t}}{q_sq_t}=\zeta^{-2st}\ne1
\]

for the uniquely required odd stride `s` and valuation-one stride `t`. Therefore **`L>=m+1`**, even if the shifts were chosen arbitrarily. Weyl monomial gauges cannot rescue the pure-shift chain. Arbitrary diagonal gauges and branching sums remain unrestricted by this theorem. Proof details are in `PROOF_CERTIFICATE.md`.

This strengthens the simpler *support-only* log-stage certificate for the specific chirp-convolution grammar; it does not say `m+1` stages suffice or imply a new lower bound for convolution in general.

## Result C — exact high tensor rank, but cheap kernel generation

Reshape the inverse chirp impulse response by splitting the binary digits `j=u+2^a v`. Its matrix rank is exactly

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}=2^{\min(a,m-a)}.}
\]

That is maximal for the rectangular dimensions and reaches `sqrt(N)` across a balanced split. However, the entire vector is generated in O(N) work via `q[j+1]/q[j]=zeta^(-(2j+1))`, itself updated by multiplying by the fixed `zeta^-2` each time.

The independently verified `N=65,536` generator used `131,072` charged prime-field operations and output every kernel entry. **This is not an O(N) convolution of the chirp with arbitrary input.** Full tensor rank of a stored kernel does not block cheap generation or automatically force convolution cost.

## Result D — genuine O(N) joint evaluation of many dyadic stages

The classical factorization

\[
\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}(I+\alpha^{2^j}S^{2^j})
=(1-\alpha^N)(I-\alpha S)^{-1}
\]

shows that `m=log2(N)` dyadic shift stages collapse exactly to **one cyclic recurrence**. Unlike a stage-count-only argument, the executor is implemented and every scalar field operation is charged.

At `N=65,536`, on a new unrelated random vector:

| Same Green-derived operator | Literal 16 stages | Fused one-recurrence executor |
|---|---:|---:|
| Total charged field operations | 2,097,167 | 393,214 |
| Array writes | 1,048,576 | 131,072 |
| Complete outputs compared | 65,536 | 65,536 |

The exact operations ratio is **5.33×**. Every output agreed, all 65,536 cyclic recurrence equations held, and 12 outputs were checked by independent direct convolution. Eight separately computed DFT coefficients differed, confirming that the operator is not a Fourier transform.

This identity is classical and is a **positive control** for our larger goal: finding truly shared multiscale execution *can* eliminate the apparent `N log N` cost of a literal stage network, but it has not done so for `F_N`.

## Novelty and general-model caution

These are exact mathematical statements for specified operator families. Gauss sums, Bluestein factorization, Weyl operators and binary geometric factorization are all existing mathematics. We have not established whether the *combinations of restrictions* are new in the literature. Published all-length exact DFT work already gives `O(N (log N)^(1-10^-13))` in an exact-complex-arithmetic model with charged scalar preparation (OpenAI Math 2026, result 130); none of this Phase 15 work improves that unrestricted bound. Likewise none implies practical FFT wall-clock speedup or fixed-precision stability.

## Phase 16 — the constructive surviving problem

A true step toward universal `O(N)` must **escape the simple product grammar** and survive complete end-to-end accounting. The most precise targets now are branching sums of noncommuting long-stride Green operators, or interleaved non-Weyl diagonal chirp gauges, with their *joint* evaluator and coefficient construction fully charged.

Use exact certificates, not just random tests. For an explicit proposed operator `M`, under our negative-exponent convention the conditions

\[
\boxed{M S=T^{-1}M,\qquad M e_0=\mathbf1}
\]

are necessary and sufficient for `M=F_-`. Search for a genuinely shared circuit satisfying both, prove its cost, check every basis vector at small `N`, and compare arbitrary fresh-input full-spectrum work to both radix-2 and the genuine exact-arithmetic research frontier. Reject cheap kernel generation, internal low-rank factorization, or an O(N) Green operator **unless its output is the full DFT**.
