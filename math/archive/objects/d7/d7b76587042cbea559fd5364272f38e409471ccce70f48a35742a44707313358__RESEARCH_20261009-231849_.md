# Research report: a square-root depth obstruction for repeatedly bit-reversed global scans

**Date:** October 10, 2026  
**Result:** Exact all-length characteristic-zero theorem plus four finite computational cut certificates.  
**Goal:** Determine whether overlapping nonunitary global scan operators can compute every Fourier output with asymptotically less arithmetic than \(N\log N\).

## What changed

The previous checkpoint proved that three quasiseparable order-one stages separated by repeated bit reversal cannot factor \(F_N\) for powers of two \(N\ge64\). Its specially crafted 32-position network cut failed to rule out four stages. Randomized four-stage mincut queries also failed to find a contradiction.

The successful new idea was **not** a larger or better random cut. It was a global binary partition whose membership is invariant under reversal of bit order. Low-order quasiseparable stages can transfer only a small amount of independent information between the two groups. The Fourier transform, in contrast, maps either group into either output group with **full rank**.

For \(N=128\), this immediately forces rank \(64\) through a channel budget of only \(60\) for four stages. The same proof generalizes to *all* power-of-two lengths with a square-root lower bound on total channel order.

## The architecture and exact result

We consider

\[
F_N=S_t P_{t-1}S_{t-1}\cdots P_1S_1,
\]

where each \(S_i\) has upper and lower off-diagonal cut ranks at most \(r_i\). Its coefficients are completely arbitrary, including zero values; stages need not be invertible. In the strongest form, each intermediate permutation can reorder arbitrarily *inside* the central-bit groups and can also exchange those two groups. Repeated bit reversal is a strict special case.

Define \(d_N\) as the number of transitions of the central-bit-group membership along naturally ordered indices:

\[
d_N=\begin{cases}
2^{(k+1)/2}-1,&N=2^k,\ k\text{ odd},\\
2^{k/2},&N=2^k,\ k\text{ even}.
\end{cases}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{\sum_i r_i\ge\left\lceil\frac{N}{2d_N}\right\rceil=\Omega(\sqrt N).}
\]

This is a **stage-width/depth** bound for the stated architecture, not an FFT arithmetic lower bound. The complete proof, including exact Fourier block factorization in the odd- and even-exponent cases, is in `PROOF_CERTIFICATE.md`.

## A useful boundary at \(N=64\)

At \(N=64\), the balanced central-bit partition has \(d_N=8\), so the four-stage budget is \(4d_N=32=N/2\). The theorem does **not** exclude four-stage factorizations at that length.

An auxiliary integer-optimization search over every reversal-orbit-invariant two-coloring, with the rank bound \(\min(|A|,|B|)\), finds no positive excess \(\min(|A|,|B|)-4d\) at \(N=64\). This is computational exploration of a *particular proof method*, not evidence that an exact factorization exists.

## Consequences for the original speed target

The central-partition result says that increasing the number of repeated-bit-reversal global scan stages by a fixed constant will **never** become a scalable exact FFT construction. Even allowing nonuniform channel ranks requires total order \(\Omega(\sqrt N)\). Under the usual stage-by-stage \(O(Nr_i)\) scan implementation, that channel budget is far more expensive than the classical \(O(N\log N)\) FFT in the asymptotic regime.

This does not constrain completely different global stages or shuffles that mix the partition. It also does not imply an arithmetic-circuit lower bound for arbitrary Fourier algorithms; one must not infer that from stage-width accounting.

## Next research gates

1. **Escape the invariant partition intelligently.** Design a family of interstage permutations that moves the middle bits into different positions at multiple stages. The single-rotation and single-XOR examples at \(N=256\) are still obstructed.
2. **Find exact nonlinear compatibility tests** for promising mixing networks that are rank-feasible. A full-rank cut is necessary, not sufficient, for exact Fourier factorization.
3. **Audit the arithmetic globally.** A candidate must compute fresh arbitrary inputs, include all generators, twiddle preparation, index work and arithmetic, and outperform a properly optimized FFT. Parallel threads, caching prior inputs, or arithmetic rebasing of operands do not satisfy the original worst-case goal.
4. **Avoid repeating the homogeneous/two-stage/three-stage Green cases:** they are closed within their stated assumptions and archived as controls.

**Scientific conclusion:** A novel-in-this-project, checkable \(\Omega(\sqrt N)\) **depth/channel-order obstruction** rules out the formerly surviving four-stage repeated-bit-reversal family for every power-of-two \(N\ge128\). It does not establish a new faster FFT or an unrestricted lower bound.
