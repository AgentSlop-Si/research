# Continuing the exact four-stage Fourier compatibility research

**Date:** October 10, 2026  
**Scope:** Fixed 256-point order-one quasiseparable scan network, exact arithmetic, arbitrary fresh inputs.  
**Status:** New characteristic-zero rank-rigidity inequalities; **no new Fourier factorization or sub-N-log-N FFT**.

## Starting point

The previous checkpoint `fft_exact_expander_coefficient_gate_2026-10-10.zip` proved that a specific four-stage affine mixer has graph spectral gap >2.02. Its moving-partition rank tests cannot rule out coefficient feasibility. It also introduced an inverse-outer-stage gate requiring a 128×128 selected Fourier block to lose rank from 128 to at most 16 after unknown inverse quasiseparable transformations.

This pass asks how much **ordinary matrix rank** the genuine non-diagonal parts of the four stages must contribute to make such a Fourier factorization possible. It is a quantitative way to rule out rank-one gauge changes, cheap low-global-rank corrections, or diagonal-plus-small-Woodbury variants as explanations of the apparently well-mixing graph.

## Findings

For any diagonal baselines `Dj` (even singular), define `hj = rank(Sj-Dj)`. A complete 15-subset audit gives the necessary inequalities in `PROOF_CERTIFICATE.md`, including:

\[
h_1\ge38,\quad h_2\ge44,\quad h_3\ge69,\quad h_4\ge46,
\]
\[
h_1+h_2\ge119,\ h_1+h_3\ge118,\ h_1+h_4\ge112,
\]
\[
h_2+h_3\ge109,\ h_2+h_4\ge95,\ h_3+h_4\ge105.
\]

Every triple of replaced stages requires total correction rank ≥127. Replacing all four requires rank sum ≥128. Combining the strongest disjoint constraints yields

\[
\boxed{h_1+h_2+h_3+h_4\ge234.}
\]

This is a theorem in complex arithmetic for the saved permutations and the stated scan class. The proof is coefficient-independent: its only inputs are the quasiseparable rank-one crossing property, rank subadditivity under factor replacement, and 15 certified nonzero Fourier determinants.

## Independent audits

- Base project: `fourier_compatibility_gate.py` passed in both fields; identity inverse control rank128 and actual two-stage middle control rank16.
- New standalone certificate: 15 rank inequalities and **30 nonzero 128×128 determinants** over `GF(12289)` and `GF(65537)`.
- Cut correctness: 225 random-stage small-model modular rank tests for arbitrary zero-generator branches and arbitrary diagonal stage replacement patterns.
- Exact full scan network: 15 selected max-flows match all 15 boundary budgets, including cases with completely diagonal replacement stages.
- Exhaustive parity family: 15×255=3,825 min-cut computations, and all 255 selected Fourier parity-block ranks in both fields (510 exact modular rank checks). No stronger full-network bound was found inside this specified parity-mask family.
- Heuristic exploration: 25 additional balanced KL seeds per replacement pattern did not improve the bounds. **This is not an exhaustive non-parity search.**

## Why the arithmetic goal remains open

The lower bounds are in *ordinary matrix rank* relative to a diagonal matrix, not in the quasiseparable order controlling scan execution cost. A single order-one separated-generator stage can have full or nearly full ordinary rank relative to its diagonal while requiring linear work. Thus the 234 bound does not imply a superlinear arithmetic cost and does not prove the four-stage Fourier factorization impossible.

The next mathematically relevant gate is **nonlinear Fourier coefficient compatibility with fully independent generator families**. In particular, combine the earlier middle-rank-16 gate with *multiple* selected row/column sets and eliminate the unknown outer inverse matrices without dividing by generator entries. Any exact impossibility certificate must cover degenerate generator patches; any claimed construction must be tested on all basis inputs and fresh random inputs, then have every arithmetic operation counted.

## Position relative to existing algorithms

Classical Cooley–Tukey and butterfly matrix factorizations already provide O(N log N) transforms. See Dao et al., *Learning Fast Algorithms for Linear Transforms Using Butterfly Factorizations*, PMLR 2019 (https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/dao19a.html), and Zheng–Riccietti–Gribonval, *Efficient Identification of Butterfly Sparse Matrix Factorizations* (https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.01230). This checkpoint is a restriction on one attempted scan architecture, **not a new FFT algorithm**.
