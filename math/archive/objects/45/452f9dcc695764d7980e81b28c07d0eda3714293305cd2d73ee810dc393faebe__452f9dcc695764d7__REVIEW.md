# Review of the constructive conditioning archive

**Conclusion:** the new archive substantially closes the earlier mathematical
gaps. Under its explicitly stated uniform input-linear primitive, verified
coefficient-DAG, polynomial preparation, and efficient-budget hypotheses, the
composition argument is coherent. I found no substantive error in the reviewed
conditional derivation. This is stronger than the preceding proposal, but it
does not implement its assumed fast primitive or establish a new independent
FFT algorithm.

## Scalar certificate audit

Let G=2^A bound every original shear coefficient and choose C=2G. For
beta in {C, alpha-C}, C/2<=|beta|<=3C/2. Thus both new divisions are safe.
The six instructions restore the source and add alpha times it to the target.
Their nontrivial local prefix matrices and inverses have norms at most 4C;
each completed original shear has both norms at most 1+G.

Before the Lth original shear, at most L-1 completed factors contribute. Hence

    (1+G)^(L-1) 4C <= 2^[L(A+1)+2].

Squaring the strong envelope gives the stated condition certificate exponent
2L(A+1)+4. The exponent remains valid with identity padding because it bounds
the norm and inverse separately. The code computes this formula correctly.

The height pass is a **conditional envelope extractor**. A nonempty string
referencing a nonzero proof is not a verified nonzero proof. Nor does the code
verify the declared number field, a Fourier identity, or a #130 gate list. Its
documentation makes these limitations explicit. The sample DAG is illustrative.

## All-length wrapper audit

The chirp estimate's counting and telescoping steps are consistent. For N>=16,
delta=1/sqrt(N), the monotone increments meet at most four excluded integer
neighborhoods. There are at most 8sqrt(N)+4 bad terms and five good runs. On a
good run, the two endpoint terms total at most 1/(2delta); the cotangent
variation is at most 1/(pi delta). Their sum is less than 1/delta. Therefore
the argument gives 13sqrt(N)+4<=14sqrt(N), safely below the chosen 32sqrt(N).
The smaller sizes satisfy the trivial estimate. This is a mathematical review,
not an inference from sampled Gauss sums.

For M>=2N-1, the chirp support has distinct residues. With normalized Fourier
matrices the correct middle diagonal is F_M b/sqrt(N); no sqrt(M) is missing.
Thus U_N=P U_M* D U_M E is correctly normalized. The compute/uncompute order
restores both dirty scratch banks. Separate companion banks permit inverse
dyadic lifts to undo their own unitary companion actions exactly.

The norm of D is bounded above, with no lower bound required. Its additions
use C=64, including when a D entry is zero. Eight additive passes per ADD and
three ADDs give a constant wrapper multiplier; the source's deliberately large
Cwrap=(4*256^8)^2 is sufficient. The transform used to prepare D is charged
but excluded from the input-linear defining-matrix metric, as explicitly stated.
That exclusion is why the result is not a finite-precision coefficient theorem.

The root-only normalization uses the canonical positive root. Murty and Pathak,
Theorem 1.1, gives the needed sum at modulus 4N as 2(1+i)sqrt(N):
https://www.cmi.ac.in/~siddhi/Quadratic_Gauss_Sum.pdf . I checked that statement
in the primary source. The phase-increment proof, not this Gauss identity,
supplies the uniform upper bound on D.

## What remains conditional or unimplemented

1. The supplied archive does not print or execute a local #130 Fourier DAG.
   The local arithmetic bound, polynomial printer/preparation bounds, field
   bound, and nonzero proofs are hypotheses or imported source statements.
2. The explicit selection formula requires effectively known constants C,a and
   exceptional-size handling. The quantitative formula is valid under those
   assumptions; no concrete #130 threshold table is shipped.
3. The stated metric is the square full-state condition after input loading,
   with two data banks and reused scratch. It excludes prepared constants and
   their nonlinear preparation. Ailon-model equivalence is not proved or claimed.
4. The low-condition output is the normalized transform. Final scaling of just
   its bank by sqrt(N) need not preserve that bound.
5. Finite-precision error, coefficient rounding, range safety and bit work remain
   separate obligations. Unitary endpoints do not erase earlier roundoff.

The pinned #130 `local.tex` and `all-lengths.tex` URLs were unavailable to this
review's web fetch. I have therefore not re-verified the numerical root-order
constant or the complete underlying local program. This does not invalidate
the abstract conditional theorem, whose hypotheses specify what is needed.

## Consequences for the independent orbit route

This archive supplies a usable way to certify a future fast dyadic primitive
and extend it to other lengths. It neither makes diag(d)R an accepted orbit
path nor reduces the arithmetic needed by our current primitive. The term
A(B)/(B log B) must still tend to zero; a wrapper around a Theta(B log B)
local evaluator does not make it do so. Using #130 as that primitive remains
the separate route the user chose not to substitute for independent research.

## Constructive coefficient-envelope theorem

Let B>=2 be a power of two, q any complex primitive Bth root, and

    f_j = product_(r=1..j) (1-q^r),
    t_j = 1/f_j,
    d_j = (-1)^j q^[j(j-1)/2] t_j,           0<=j<B.

Consider an explicit shear word with L shears whose coefficients belong to
the following verified alphabet: roots of unity; f_j, t_j, d_j and their
negatives or complex conjugates; length-2B Fourier samples of the zero-padded
t or d kernel; and scalars of modulus at most 2B used for normalization.
Then every coefficient has modulus at most B^B. The six-instruction expansion
therefore has the **integer, explicitly printable certificate**

    A = B log2(B),
    E = 2L(A+1)+4,
    max_prefix kappa_2 <= 2^E.

Both a prefix and its inverse have norm at most 2^(E/2), so this also controls
identity padding. The statement concerns complex arithmetic. Finite-field
exactness tests do not certify its analytic norms.

**Proof.** Since powers of a primitive root permute the nontrivial Bth roots,
the smallest nonzero |1-q^r| is 2 sin(pi/B). Concavity of sin on [0,pi/2]
gives sin(pi/B)>=2/B. Hence

    4/B <= |1-q^r| <= 2,
    |f_j| <= 2^j,
    |t_j| = |d_j| <= max(1,(B/4)^(B-1)).

For a Fourier sample, the triangle inequality gives at most B times the last
bound. For B=2 this is 2<=B^B; for B>=4 it is at most B^B as well. The other
listed coefficients obey the same bound. Substitution in the already-proved
scalar certificate gives E. Since B is dyadic, log2(B) and A are integers;
only the exponent need be printed. This proof uses no floating approximations,
minimal-polynomial construction, or numerical condition-number estimate.

If L<=C_L B^s for a known constant s, then E is polynomial in B. In particular,
our existing local FFT/convolution evaluator and its constant-overhead replay
use L=Theta(B log B); this gives E=O(B^2 (log B)^2). Its coefficient preparation
is already charged. The exact arithmetic cost of that evaluator remains
Theta(B log B), so this theorem supplies **no arithmetic speedup by itself**.

The alphabet requirement is substantive. Reciprocals of arbitrary kernel
spectrum entries, uncontrolled linear combinations, or coefficients from a
different compiler are not covered. A bounded number of products or sums can
be added by proving a new polynomial exponent. Nothing here transfers the
bound to an unimplemented #130 coefficient DAG.

## Conditional improvement of the selector

Suppose a future verified local word has all of the following properties:

- arithmetic A_local(B)=o(B log B);
- polynomially many shears with the preceding coefficient envelope (or another
  proved envelope giving K(B)<=2^[C B^a]);
- effectively known constants and a polynomial-cost printer, preparation and
  certificate, under the same exact-arithmetic/root model as the archive.

The source's serial reset and wrapper can then use

    B <= [(log2 R(N))/C]^(1/a)

with dyadic rounding and its stated preparation caps, instead of a constraint
involving log2 log2 R(N). Write L_j(N) for the j-fold base-2 iterated logarithm.
For the two explicit budget families, the local radix exponent b=log2(B)
changes as follows:

| Full-state condition budget R(N) | Generic double-exponential certificate | Polynomial exponent certificate |
|---|---|---|
| (log N)^c, c>0 fixed | b=Theta(L_4(N)) | b=Theta(L_3(N)) |
| N^c, c>0 fixed | b=Theta(L_3(N)) | b=Theta(L_2(N)) |

For arbitrary lengths, replacing R by R/Cwrap changes only constants for
these budgets. The global arithmetic ratio is still bounded by

    O(A_local(B)/(B b) + 1/b + b/log N + Cprep(B)/(N log N)).

All terms tend to zero only when the assumed local arithmetic improvement is
actually supplied. For example, if independently proved in this same class,
A_local(B)=O(B (log B)^(1-delta) (log log B)^p), delta>0, gives the term
b^(-delta)(log b)^p; the table improves which iterated logarithm enters it.
This is a constructive conditional improvement in conditioning selection,
not an assertion that such a new local fast word has been built.
