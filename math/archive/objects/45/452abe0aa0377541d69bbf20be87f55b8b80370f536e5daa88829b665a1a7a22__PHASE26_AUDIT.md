# Independent audit: Hero1 Phase26 graph tolls and field circuits

## Verdict

Accept the exact split-radix/radix-2 scalar-count equality, the specified lifted-butterfly penalty over the two stated large prime fields, and the restricted complex entropy/determinant inequalities. These are classical circuit identities and model-specific bounds, with useful paid comparisons. They do not give a new general FFT improvement or unrestricted lower bound.

One helper claim needs correction: its clamped value `max(1, raw_bound)` is a necessary lower bound on a user-declared entry cap C constrained to be at least one, not on the circuit's actual largest entry. The latter can be below one. Three identical gates `2^(−1/3) F₂` multiply exactly to F₂, and every entry has modulus below one. The main theorem only draws actual-height consequences when the raw bound exceeds one and remains valid.

The arbitrary-odd-characteristic lift wording also needs its identity-coefficient qualification: in characteristic 3, −2=1 and the extra multiplication can disappear. The reported large-prime benchmark counts are unaffected. The previously found characteristic-7 GT3 identity-coefficient exception persists in the carried code.

## 2. Exact field circuit and count proof

For N=2^t and a primitive Nth root ω over an odd-characteristic field, the positive-exponent radix-2 DIF node sends `(u,v)` to `(u+v,ω^j(u−v))`. Skipping the identity twiddles gives

`A₂(N)=Nt`, `M₂(N)=Nt/2−N+1`.

For split radix, pair opposite inputs to form s and d. With q=N/4 and i=ω^q, the odd-frequency subinputs are

`c_j=ω^j(d_j+i d_(j+q))`,
`e_j=ω^(3j)(d_j−i d_(j+q))`.

The even frequencies come from F_(N/2)(s), and the two odd classes from F_(N/4)(c), F_(N/4)(e). Expanding the original Fourier sum proves each output class and its positive-exponent sign. The top node uses 3N/2 additions and 3N/4−2 multiplications, including all q multiplications by i. No j>0 twiddle in either branch equals one: j<N/4 and 3j<N. The recursive counts therefore satisfy the displayed one-half/two-quarter recurrences. Substituting the formulas above, with bases N=1,2, proves equality separately for additions and multiplications at every dyadic N.

This is the specified ordinary split-radix implementation in a field model where multiplying by i is paid. It is not an exclusion of every split-radix variant or of savings in a real-floating-operation model. The actual code returns canonical Fourier order; our complete-basis controls check that order directly.

For N=3G, G dyadic, the CRT input map is `j=e_G j_G+e_3 j_3`, with `e_G=3·3^−1 mod G`, `e_3=G·G^−1 mod3`; the output map is `k=3k_G+Gk_3`. Then `ω^(jk)=(ω³)^(j_Gk_G)(ω^G)^(j_3k_3)`. Replacing the three length-G row transforms by split radix changes neither values nor these field counts. With a literal 6-add/2-multiply triple codelet, the full count remains `3C₂(G)+8G`, where `C₂=A₂+M₂`.

## 3. Lifted butterfly and characteristic exceptions

The exact identity

`[[1,1],[1,−1]] = [[1,0],[1,1]] diag(1,−2) [[1,1],[0,1]]`

holds in every odd characteristic. Its literal application uses two additions and a multiplication by −2. In the stated fields 2013265921 and 754974721, −2 is nonidentity, so every butterfly incurs one additional field multiplication: `Nt/2` extra operations in a dyadic circuit, or three times the row penalty in GT3. This is a result about this particular lift, not all nonunitary factorizations.

If the phrase is extended to all odd-characteristic fields with identity multiplications free, the penalty is not universal. In F3 at N=2, −2=1; the optimized lift costs the same two additions as the direct butterfly. The preserved code still literally executes and increments a multiplication, but it cannot be described as an unavoidable nontrivial multiplication under the stated identity-skipping model.

Likewise the carried GT3 code always charges two triple multiplications. At N=6 over F7, ω=5 gives `(η−η²)/2=1`, so skipping this identity costs 20 total field operations instead of the literal 22. This is the exact Phase25 exception, independently reproduced here. Neither exception changes the two large-prime tables, but both must accompany broader all-field cost wording.

## 4. Complex entropy and determinant bounds

These sections concern complex matrices, with the stated normalization, in-place gates on N registers and no extra workspace. They do not transfer to finite-field circuits.

For a normalized unitary row circuit, define `Φ(A)=−Σ|A_ij|² log₂|A_ij|²`. A b-row unitary gate preserves each affected column's total mass s_j. Factoring that mass from its within-block distribution makes its entropy change at most `s_j log₂b`. Since the affected unitary rows have total squared mass b, `|ΔΦ|≤b log₂b`. Starting at I and ending at normalized Fourier gives the exact necessary budget

`Σ_g b_g log₂b_g ≥ N log₂N`.

For unnormalized Fourier, `|det F|=N^(N/2)`. A local invertible b×b gate whose entries have modulus at most C has determinant modulus at most `(C√b)^b` by Hadamard. Hence

`(N/2) ln N ≤ Σ_g b_g(ln C + (1/2)ln b_g)`.

With C≥1, at most G gates and each arity at most B, monotonicity gives

`C ≥ B^(−1/2) N^[N/(2GB)]`.

For fixed B,C the necessary gate count is Ω(N log N) in this restricted model. There is no claim about arbitrary workspace, unbounded coefficients, nonlinear operations, data-dependent circuits or finite fields. High-arity gates must still be implemented and paid when the goal is scalar operations.

### Actual coefficient-height helper correction

`required_max_entry_for_gate_budget` returns `max(1, raw)` and calls this the necessary actual largest coefficient. The clamp is not justified for that interpretation. Let H=F₂, c>0 satisfy c³=1/2. Since H²=2I,

`(cH)³=2c³H=H`.

Every entry of each of the three gates has modulus c=2^(−1/3)<1. At N=2,G=3,B=2, the raw formula is exactly c and is saturated, whereas the helper returns one. Our checker verifies the identity exactly in Q[c]/(2c³−1), not merely by floating point.

Two valid repairs are available: return the raw bound for actual coefficient magnitude, or rename/reword the clamped quantity as a lower bound on a chosen cap parameter restricted by C≥1. The raw actual-height bound can also be justified without assuming the actual maximum M≥1: if M√B≤1 every gate determinant is at most one, impossible for N>1; otherwise `(M√b)^b≤(M√B)^B`, yielding the same raw formula. The main source theorem's consequences where raw>1 are unchanged.

### Gate-block saturation and routing

For GT3, the counted unnormalized Fourier blocks comprise `3Gt/2` two-input butterflies and G three-input codelets. Their exact determinant growth is `3Gt ln2/2 + 3G ln3/2 = N lnN/2`. Normalized counterparts have total entropy capacity `3Gt+3G log₂3=N log₂N`.

This is saturation by these transform blocks with their wiring/routing accounted separately. Input/output permutations preserve both potentials, but if a strict fixed-register model charges swaps as additional two-register gates, their Hadamard/entropy *upper capacities* introduce slack. Thus the count is not a scalar-operation optimum or a proof that the canonical transform has exactly the stated total fixed-register gate count when routing gates are also counted. This preserves the output-order qualification from the Phase25 audit.

## 5. Cold setup, memory and implementation limitations

`root_table(N)` really uses N−1 multiplications for consecutive powers once a primitive root is known. The root-generation exponent, factor checks and generator premise are outside that returned number. For mixed N=3G, the new prototype builds N roots rather than the predecessor's G row roots, adding exactly 2G table multiplications before other constant/setup terms. Triple constants, CRT integer inverses/index arithmetic and root verification still need separate charges. The mixed benchmark discards the returned root-table count; its reported transform tally is steady-state arithmetic, not a complete cold-call total.

The split-radix routine is depth-first recursive with O(N) peak live mathematical vector storage, but O(N log N) total data movement/allocation work. Radix-2 also copies its input and explicitly constructs reordered output. Its `int(binary_string_reversal)` output index computation performs Θ(N log N) elementary bit/string work in that literal Python implementation; a different bit-reversal generator would need to be specified for a tighter address ledger. Counters are selected scalar accesses, not complete platform-independent byte traffic. The source largely states these limitations correctly.

Phase26 supplies a full transform, not the predecessor's deferred preview/reuse protocol. The two carried Phase25/24 implementation files retain their previously documented limitations; no fix should be inferred from packaging them here. Exact modular identities give no floating-point conditioning/precision guarantee. The finite numerical entropy checks are diagnostic controls accompanying the analytic proof, not exact proofs of all complex inputs.

## 7. Primary literature and novelty boundary

[Ailon's primary paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1305.4745) explicitly restricts its entropy argument to unitary 2×2 gates and distinguishes the normalized target from determinant-based bounds for unnormalized Fourier. The arity-dependent mass argument above is a direct extension under the same unitary/no-extra-workspace premise. [Johnson–Frigo's primary abstract](https://www.fftw.org/~athena/abstracts/abstract5.html) describes reduced real addition/multiplication counts for a modified split-radix algorithm; it does not contradict equality for this particular paid prime-field recursion. Morgenstern's cited DOI was not accessible in this pass; the displayed determinant/Hadamard argument was independently verified directly, and its historical attribution is consistent with Ailon's discussion. No claim of priority or novel unrestricted lower bound is accepted.
