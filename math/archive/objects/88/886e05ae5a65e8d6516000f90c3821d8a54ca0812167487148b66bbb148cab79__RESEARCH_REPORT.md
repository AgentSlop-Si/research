# Intrinsic overlap at −1: ramified squarefreeness for five more composite families

## Result and boundary

This report resolves the intrinsic simple-overlap case left open by the accepted separated-cluster theorem. It proves a paired-residue sufficient criterion at complementary orders `d≡3 (mod4)`, then verifies that criterion at five fixed bases.

> For every prime `p≡3 (mod4)` and every `d∈{3,7,11,15,19}`, the polynomial `H_((dp−1)/2)(z;ζ_dp)` is squarefree in characteristic zero, for every primitive `ζ_dp`.

The uniform ramified argument requires `p∤d`. Only the five exceptions `N=9,49,121,45,361` are supplied by existing accepted finite certificates, rechecked here as inert data with fresh arithmetic. These exceptions are not applications of the uniform argument. There is no new target-order census.

In the already accepted common-cycle scalar-resolvent model with one free diagonal, all these orders are `1 mod4`, so the accepted resonance and nonzero-node guards give minimum `(N−1)/2`. This is theoretical classification progress. It provides no paid algorithm or fastFFT performance improvement.

The local overlap polynomial is a two-step Hermite combination, not an ordinary Hermite polynomial:

`J_(A+1)(y;τ)=y He_A(y)−Aτ He_(A−1)(y)=He_(A+1)(y)+A(1−τ)He_(A−1)(y)`.

The central additional fact is that, at `A=(p−1)/2` with `p≡3 mod4`, two parameters satisfying `1/τ+1/τ′=1` cannot both give a repeated root. The lower Rogers–Szegő data at `ω` and `ω⁻¹` supply exactly these paired parameters.

No all-composite theorem, new prime-power theorem, uniform assertion about all lower contents, or publication-priority claim is made. Literature priority remains unresolved.

## 1. Definitions

Let

`H_n(z;q)=Σ_(k=0)^n [n choose k]_q z^k`,

`D_n(q)=Disc_z H_n(z;q)∈Z[q]`.

All discriminants below are ordinary derivative discriminants, with the convention `Disc 1=Disc(linear)=1`. The monic probabilists' Hermite polynomials are denoted `He_a`, so

`He_0=1`, `He_1=y`, `He_a=y He_(a−1)−(a−1)He_(a−2)`,

`He_a′=a He_(a−1)`, `Disc He_a=P_a=∏_(j=1)^a j^j`.

The last formula follows directly from differentiation and the recurrence by the ordinary resultant identity. In particular the adjacent Hermite polynomials are coprime in characteristic zero, and also in characteristic `p` when all relevant positive indices are less than `p`.

Fix odd `d≥3`, odd `b` with `1≤b<d`, `A≥1`, and a primitive `d`th root `ω`. Set

`n=Ad+b`, `F=z^d+1`, `G=H_b(z;ω)=(z+1)U`,

`g=U(−1)=G′(−1)`, `Δ_U=Disc U`, `R_U=Res_z(F,U)`,

`W=(ω;ω)_b/(ω²;ω²)_((b+d)/2)`, `τ=dW/g`.

The symbol `τ` is used only when `g≠0`. Every factor in the denominator defining `W` is nonzero because `(b+d)/2<d` and `d` is odd. At primes not dividing `d` these factors are units as well.

The q-Lucas identity is known:

`H_(Ad+b)(z;ω)=(z^d+1)^A H_b(z;ω)`.

It is an immediate sum of the q-Lucas congruence in Bruce Sagan, *Congruence properties of q-analogs*, Advances in Mathematics 95 (1992), Theorem 2.2, pp.131–132: https://users.math.msu.edu/users/bsagan/Papers/Old/cpq-pub.pdf . No novelty is claimed for this degeneration.

## 2. Shared-cluster leading polynomial

Put

`q(ε)=ω(1+ε²)^(1/d)`, so `q(ε)^d−1=ε²` exactly.

### Local overlap lemma

If `g≠0`, then

`H_n(−1+εx;q(ε))`

`=ε^(A+1)(−1)^(A+1)d^((A−1)/2)g J_(A+1)(−√d x;τ)+O(ε^(A+2)).`  (2.1)

The square roots cancel coefficientwise. An equivalent formula containing only rational powers of `d` is obtained by putting

`T_0=1`, `T_1=−x`, `T_a=−xT_(a−1)−(a−1)T_(a−2)/d`:

`[ε^(A+1)]H_n(−1+εx;q(ε))=(−1)^A d^A[gxT_A(x)+A W T_(A−1)(x)].`  (2.2)

### Proof

Use the formal generating series

`Σ_(n≥0) H_n(z;q)t^n/(q;q)_n = exp(Σ_(k≥1)(1+z^k)t^k/[k(1−q^k)]).`  (2.3)

This follows by multiplying the two q-exponential series; its logarithm follows from `E(qt)=(1−t)E(t)`. The argument is formal, with no analytic convergence assumption.

Separate the exponent into terms with `d|k` and the rest, and let `B(t;z,q)` be the exponential of the regular part. Write `u=t^d`. At `z=−1+εx`, the coefficient of `u^a` in the singular exponential has leading term

`ε^(−a) d^(−a/2) He_a(−√d x)/a!`.

Indeed its first two logarithmic terms are `−xu/ε−u²/(2dε²)`; all terms of u-degree at least three have pole order smaller than their degree. This is the same elementary singular-series calculation as in the accepted separated-cluster lemma.

Here the regular coefficient of degree `b` vanishes at `ε=0` and has first term

`[t^b]B(t;−1+εx,q(ε))=εxg/(ω;ω)_b+O(ε²)`.

This uses `b<d` and `H_b(−1;q)=0` identically for odd `b`. The next possible regular coefficient is of degree `b+d`. At `z=−1`, the full logarithm contains only even t-powers. There is no even positive multiple of `d` below `2d`, while `b+d<2d`. Hence

`[t^(b+d)]B(t;−1,ω)=1/(ω²;ω²)_((b+d)/2)`.

This last equality also follows directly from `E(t)E(−t)=1/(t²;q²)_∞` as a formal series. The regular coefficients of degrees `b+jd`, `j≥2`, contribute only at order `ε^(A+2)` or higher after multiplication by the factorial factor. Thus precisely two contributions survive at order `ε^(A+1)`.

Finally

`(q(ε);q(ε))_n=(−1)^A A! d^A(ω;ω)_b ε^(2A)(1+O(ε²))`.

Multiplication proves (2.2) and hence (2.1).

### Base d=3

For `b=1`, `U=g=1`, `W=1/(1−ω²)`, and

`τ=3/(1−ω²)=1−ω`.

The first overlap models, with `q` representing `ω`, are

- `A=1`: `3x²+q−1`;
- `A=2`: `9x³+(6q−9)x`;
- `A=3`: `27x⁴+(27q−54)x²+9−9q`.

They already show why replacing the shared cluster by an unmodified `He_(A+1)` is wrong.

## 3. Exact ordinary-discriminant order and coefficient

Suppose

`Δ_U R_U Disc J_(A+1)(·;τ) ≠ 0`.

The first two factors mean that `U` is squarefree and disjoint from `F`. In particular `g≠0`, so exactly one simple residual root overlaps with the cluster at `−1`. Define

`L=dA(A−1)/2+A`, `S_A(τ)=Disc_y J_(A+1)(y;τ)`.

Then

`D_n(q(ε))=ε^(2L)[C(ω)+O(ε²)]`,  (3.1)

where

`C(ω)=(−1)^(A(d−1)/2) d^[dA(A+1)/2+A] P_A^(d−1) S_A(τ) Δ_U R_U^(2A) g².`  (3.2)

Consequently the exact order of `D_n` at `q=ω` is `L`.

### Proof

At each root `α≠−1` of `F`, there are `A` branches

`z=α+ε(α/√d)h_j+O(ε²)`,

where `h_j` are the roots of `He_A`. At `−1`, (2.1) gives `A+1` branches with first displacements `−j_k/√d`, where `j_k` are the roots of `J_(A+1)`. The remaining `b−1` branches lift the distinct roots of `U`. The local hypotheses and formal implicit lifting account for all `Ad+b` branches.

The within-cluster squared differences contribute

`ε^(2L) d^(−L) P_A^(d−1) S_A(τ)`.

There is no residual product of roots `α`: the product of the `d−1` roots other than `−1` is one. Put `F_0=F/(z+1)`. Since `F_0(−1)=d`,

`Disc F_0=(−1)^((d−1)/2)d^(d−2)`.

The cross-differences between the nonshared clusters contribute `(Disc F_0)^(A²)`. Those between the shared cluster and the other clusters contribute `d^(2A(A+1))`. Their combined contribution is

`(−1)^(A(d−1)/2)d^(dA²+2A)`.

The residual roots contribute `Δ_U`; their cross-differences with the clusters contribute `R_U^(2A)g²`, because the shared cluster has one extra branch. Multiplying gives (3.2). All factors are nonzero, so the order is exact. The remainder in (3.1) is even because `q(ε)` is even.

This gives `ord_(Φ_3)D_7=5` and `ord_(Φ_3)D_10=12`, agreeing with the accepted controls and explaining their excess over the separated-cluster order.

## 4. Reciprocal identity for the two overlap parameters

From now on let `d=4r+3`, `b=2r+1=(d−1)/2`, `Q=ω²`, and `v=dW`. At a primitive root,

`v=(Q;Q)_r(ω;Q)_(2r+1)`.  (4.1)

To see this, complete the denominator `(ω²;ω²)_(3r+2)` to the full product of the `d−1` nontrivial powers of `ω²`, whose product is `d`, and cancel against `(ω;ω)_(2r+1)`.

Differentiate the Rogers–Szegő recurrence at `z=−1`. For odd degrees it gives

`g_(2r+1)=(ω;ω²)_r+(1−ω^(2r))g_(2r−1)`.

Starting with `g_1=1`, this yields

`g=(Q;Q)_r Σ_(j=0)^r (ω;Q)_j/(Q;Q)_j`.  (4.2)

Set `m=2r+1`, `a_j=(ω;Q)_j/(Q;Q)_j`, `M=(ω;Q)_m`. Since `ω=Q^(−m)`, the finite q-binomial theorem gives

`Σ_(j=0)^m a_j=M`.  (4.3)

More explicitly, `a_j=(−1)^j Q^(−mj+j(j−1)/2)[m choose j]_Q`, so the sum is the finite product `(Q^(−m);Q)_m`.

Write a superscript `ι` for replacing `ω` by `ω⁻¹`. Direct cancellation gives

`a_j^ι=ω^j a_j`,

`a_(m−j)=(M/M^ι)a_j^ι`.  (4.4)

For the second identity use Gaussian symmetry and `2m+1=d`: explicitly, `a_(m−j)/a_j=−Q^(−m(m+1)/2)ω^j`, while `−Q^(−m(m+1)/2)=M/M^ι`; combine this with `a_j^ι=ω^j a_j`. The constant-factor equality holds because its exponent difference is a multiple of `d`.

Split (4.3) at `r` and pair its upper half by (4.4). Equations (4.1)–(4.2) imply the denominator-free identity

`g v^ι + g^ι v = v v^ι`.  (4.5)

Whenever `g,g^ι` are nonzero this is

`1/τ(ω)+1/τ(ω⁻¹)=1`.  (4.6)

Every factor of `v` in (4.1) is of the form `1−ω^k`, `0<k<d`. Thus `v` and `v^ι` remain nonzero at every primitive dth root in characteristic `p∤d`. No extra bad primes arise from the denominator in (4.6).

## 5. A characteristic-p Hermite pairing lemma

### Lemma

Let `p≡3 (mod4)` be prime and `A=(p−1)/2`. In any field of characteristic `p`, let `τ,τ′` be nonzero with

`1/τ+1/τ′=1`.

Then at least one of `J_(A+1)(y;τ)` and `J_(A+1)(y;τ′)` is squarefree over the algebraic closure.

### Proof

All relevant Hermite indices are less than `p`. Their adjacent resultant is therefore nonzero. Suppose `J(y;τ)=0`. Then `He_A(y)≠0`, because otherwise the definition of `J` would force `He_(A−1)(y)=0` as well.

Using `He_A′=A He_(A−1)` and the Hermite recurrence (or direct calculation when `A=1`), at any root of `J` we have

`J′(y;τ)=[1+Aτ+(1−τ)y²/τ]He_A(y)`.

Since `τ,τ′` are nonzero and reciprocals sum to one, `τ≠1`, `τ′≠1`, and `τ′=τ/(τ−1)`. Thus every multiple root at parameter `τ` satisfies

`y²=t(τ)=−τ(1+Aτ)/(1−τ)`.

In characteristic `p`, `A=−1/2`, giving

`t(τ)=τ(τ−2)/[2(1−τ)]`, `t(τ′)=−t(τ)`.

Because `A` is odd, `He_(A−1)(0)` is a product of nonzero odd integers of magnitude below `p`. Thus

`J(0;τ)=−Aτ He_(A−1)(0)≠0`.

Every multiple root is nonzero. If both polynomials had multiple roots, choose one `y` for the first. Over the algebraic closure choose `i²=−1`. The second has a multiple root at `iy` or `−iy`; its even parity means we can choose `iy`.

Let `U_0(y)=He_A(y)` and `V_0(y)=i^(−A)He_A(iy)`. Both are monic of degree `A`. Their differential equations give

`[U_0′V_0−U_0V_0′−yU_0V_0]′=−(2A+1)U_0V_0=0`.

The expression has degree `2A+1=p`, leading coefficient `−1`, and zero constant term. A polynomial of degree at most `p` with derivative zero has only constant and pth-power terms. Hence

`U_0′V_0−U_0V_0′−yU_0V_0=−y^p`.  (5.1)

At the proposed two multiple roots, `U_0,V_0` are nonzero and

`U_0′/U_0=y/τ`, `V_0′/V_0=−y/τ′`.

The left side of (5.1) becomes

`y U_0 V_0(1/τ+1/τ′−1)=0`,

contradicting `y≠0`. This proves the lemma.

The proof is uniform in `p`; finite experiments are not its basis. The congruence `p≡3 mod4` is used to exclude the zero-root obstruction. When `p≡1 mod4`, the shared cluster degree is odd, and both parameters `τ=τ′=2` can have a multiple root at zero. That case is not silently covered here.

## 6. Exact cyclotomic division and paired lower-order criterion

For `d≡3 mod4` define the polynomial with integer coefficients

`U_d(z;q)=H_((d−1)/2)(z;q)/(z+1)`.

Let

`Γ_d(q)=Disc_z U_d(z;q) · Res_z(z^d+1,U_d(z;q))`,

`B_d^pair(q)=rem_(Φ_d(q))[Γ_d(q)Γ_d(q^(d−1))]`.

If this remainder is nonzero, let `c_d^pair` be its positive integer coefficient content; if it is zero, the criterion is inapplicable.

### Paired ramified criterion

Let `p≡3 mod4` be prime, `p∤d`, and suppose `c_d^pair≠0` and `p∤c_d^pair`. Then

`D_((dp−1)/2)(ζ_dp)≠0`.

### Proof

Because `p∤d`, the polynomial `Φ_d` is squarefree modulo `p`. Its quotient algebra is a product of fields. The nonzero reduction of the canonical remainder `B_d^pair` therefore has at least one nonzero component, yielding a primitive `d`th root `ω̄` in a finite extension of `F_p` for which

`Γ_d(ω̄)Γ_d(ω̄⁻¹)≠0`.

Thus the residual discriminant and resultant guards are nonzero at both inverse roots. In particular `g,g^ι` are units, since the resultant with `z^d+1` contains the evaluations at `−1`. Equation (4.1) makes `v,v^ι` units too. Equation (4.6) applies with nonzero finite parameters `τ,τ′`. The lemma in Section 5 gives one of the two roots, call it `ω̄_*`, where `S_A(τ)` is also nonzero.

Lift `ω̄_*` to a primitive complex dth root and the corresponding prime of `Z[ω]` above `p`. All factors in (3.2) are then p-units: this includes `d`, `P_A` since `A<p`, both residual guards, `g`, and `S_A`. In particular they are nonzero in characteristic zero. The local expansion has exact order `L`. Galois conjugacy gives this same exact order at every primitive dth root. Therefore monic exact division over the integers gives

`D_n(q)=Φ_d(q)^L E_n,d(q)`, `E_n,d∈Z[q]`.  (6.1)

Writing `Q_d=(q^d−1)/Φ_d`, the exact coefficient identity is

`E_n,d(ω)=Q_d(ω)^L C(ω)`.  (6.2)

Here `Q_d(ω)` is a p-unit because `q^d−1` is squarefree modulo `p`; thus the right side is nonzero at the chosen residue factor.

If `D_n(ζ_dp)=0`, irreducibility and Gauss's lemma give `Φ_dp|D_n` in `Z[q]`. Because `Φ_dp` and `Φ_d` are distinct irreducibles, (6.1) implies `Φ_dp|E_n,d`. The cyclotomic identity

`Φ_dp(q)=Φ_d(q^p)/Φ_d(q)`

reduces to

`Φ_dp(q)≡Φ_d(q)^(p−1) (mod p)`.

It would force `E_n,d(ω)` to vanish at every residue factor above `p`, contradicting (6.2).

All of `Φ_d^L` was divided out before reduction. There is no requirement `L<p−1`, no neglected p-divisible lower term, and no assertion that an arbitrary p-adic leading term controls a specialization by itself.

## 10. Prior mathematics and scope of the addition

The q-Lucas specialization is established prior mathematics, as cited in Section 1. Root-of-unity orthogonal-polynomial theory also predates this work. Spiridonov and Zhedanov, *Zeros and orthogonality of the Askey–Wilson polynomials for q a root of unity*, Duke Mathematical Journal 89 (1997), study explicit root-of-unity polynomials and finite orthogonality; their Section 2 uses `q=exp(ε+2πiM/N)` and takes `ε→0`. See https://arxiv.org/abs/q-alg/9605034 and the primary manuscript https://arxiv.org/pdf/q-alg/9605034 . This is directly relevant prior context and precludes portraying root-of-unity limits themselves as new.

Generalized Hermite/Dunkl systems and Uglov's root-of-unity Macdonald degeneration are further nearby bodies of work: Rosenblum, https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9307224 ; Rösler, https://arxiv.org/abs/q-alg/9703006 ; Uglov, https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/9702020 . The present `J` is explicitly a linear combination of two ordinary Hermite polynomials two degrees apart. It is not identified here with a fixed-parameter Dunkl-Hermite family or a Uglov polynomial. No inference of novelty follows from not finding that identification in a targeted search.

Quasi-orthogonal combinations and their discriminants are also well studied. Sawa and Uchida, *Discriminants of classical quasi-orthogonal polynomials, with combinatorial and number-theoretic applications*, https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.00605 , give classical background and related resultant/discriminant methods. Their Section 2 discusses quasi-orthogonal combinations of more than two degrees; Section 4 treats compact discriminant formulas. This report proves the specific coefficient and finite-characteristic pairing needed here directly instead of claiming a general new Hermite-discriminant theory.

The project-level addition consists of the explicit simple-overlap model, its ordinary-discriminant coefficient, the reciprocal parameter identity, the characteristic-p pairing lemma, and their use with exact cyclotomic division to establish the five fixed-base families. Establishing whether those exact statements or a broader theorem already occur in the literature needs a separate priority review. Global novelty is unresolved.

## 11. Remaining boundary

The paired lower-content criterion is conditional for arbitrary `d≡3 mod4`. Five contents do not prove that all nonzero paired contents have prime factors only dividing `d`, nor that the residual polynomial is always squarefree and disjoint after deflation. Higher ramification `N=p^a d`, `a≥2`, still has `A=(p^a−1)/2≥p`, so the factor `P_A^(d−1)` is divisible by `p`; the present p-unit bridge fails. The five finite exceptions do not repair that failure uniformly.

Thus the intrinsically overlapping complementary orders now have a working uniform mechanism and five proved families. The arbitrary-composite classification and any computational speedup remain separate unfinished questions.
