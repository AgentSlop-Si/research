# A central-partition depth barrier for bit-reversed quasiseparable Fourier circuits

**Date:** 2026-10-10  
**Status:** characteristic-zero theorem with elementary proof; exact independent finite-field and graph-cut certificates.  
**Scope:** stage-factorization architecture; *not* an unrestricted lower bound on FFT arithmetic circuits.

## Definitions and theorem

Let \(N=2^k\), \(k\ge2\), and let \(F_N=(\omega^{ij})_{0\le i,j<N}\) be the unnormalized complex Fourier matrix, where \(\omega\) is a primitive \(N\)-th root of unity.

A square matrix \(S\) has **two-sided quasiseparable order at most \(r\)** if, at every cut \(0<c<N\),

\[
\operatorname{rank} S[c:N,0:c]\le r,\qquad
\operatorname{rank} S[0:c,c:N]\le r.
\]

Let \(A\sqcup B=\{0,\ldots,N-1\}\) be the following central-bit partition:

- **Odd bit count:** if \(k=2m+1\), put \(L=2^m\), \(N=2L^2\), and let \(A\) be the indices whose bit at position \(m\) (least significant bit is bit 0) is 0; \(B\) has that bit 1.
- **Even bit count:** if \(k=2m\), put \(q=2^{m-1}\), \(N=4q^2\), and let \(A\) be indices whose two central bits (positions \(m-1,m\)) agree; \(B\) has those bits unequal.

Both sets have \(N/2\) indices. Define

\[
d_N=\begin{cases}2L-1,&N=2L^2,\\2q,&N=4q^2.\end{cases}
\]

A permutation is **block-monomial for \(A,B\)** when it either preserves \(A,B\) individually or swaps them as whole sets. Its order *inside* either set can be completely arbitrary. Bit reversal is block-monomial (in fact preserving) for both central-bit partitions.

**THEOREM.** Suppose

\[
F_N=S_tP_{t-1}S_{t-1}\cdots P_1S_1,
\]

where the \(S_i\) have two-sided quasiseparable orders \(r_i\) and every intermediate permutation \(P_i\) is block-monomial for the central-bit partition. Then

\[
\boxed{\quad\frac N2\le d_N\sum_{i=1}^t r_i.\quad}
\]

In particular, when \(r_i=1\) for every stage, the depth must satisfy

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
N=2L^2:&\quad t\ge \left\lceil\frac{L^2}{2L-1}\right\rceil,\\
N=4q^2:&\quad t\ge q.
\end{aligned}}
\]

Thus **\(t=\Omega(\sqrt N)\)**. Four such order-one stages are impossible for every power-of-two \(N\ge128\), with repeated bit reversal included. The conclusion allows arbitrary complex coefficients, zero generators, and singular stages.

### Lemma 1: the rank cost of crossing a binary partition

Let \(X\sqcup Y\) partition the coordinates and let \(d\) be the number of adjacent changes of membership in the natural index order:

\[
d=\#\{0\le j<N-1:\mathbf1_X(j)\ne\mathbf1_X(j+1)\}.
\]

If \(S\) has two-sided QS order \(r\), then

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}S[X,Y]\le dr,\qquad
\operatorname{rank}S[Y,X]\le dr.}
\]

**Proof:** divide the rows of \(X\) into maximal consecutive runs \(I\). Because the columns of \(Y\) are in the complement, each lies entirely to the left or right of \(I\). The left-hand submatrix is a submatrix of a lower off-diagonal cut block and therefore has rank at most \(r\); the right-hand submatrix is a submatrix of an upper off-diagonal cut block and also has rank at most \(r\). If one side is absent its contribution is zero. Summing rank bounds across all row runs counts every boundary between \(X\) and \(Y\) exactly once, giving \(dr\). Exchange \(X\) and \(Y\) for the reverse block. No generator chart or invertibility assumption is needed. **QED.**

For the central-bit partition, the membership sequence consists of:

- Odd \(k\): \(L\) repetitions of a zero run of length \(L\) followed by a one run of length \(L\): \(d_N=2L-1\).
- Even \(k\): \(q\) repetitions of the four two-bit values \(00,01,10,11\), each for \(q\) coordinates; membership is `A B B A`, with the adjacent `A` runs between periods merged: \(d_N=2q\).

### Lemma 2: rank budget across all stages, including swapping shuffles

Fix one of the two input sets \(X=A\) or \(X=B\). Track the group in which its signals are *supposed* to lie if every stage \(S_i\) is replaced by its block-diagonal part. Each block-monomial permutation simply permutes or swaps that group.

After the first stage, the unexpected-group output block has rank at most \(d_Nr_1\), by Lemma 1. At a later stage \(S_j\), the unexpected-group block is the sum of (i) a contribution from the previous unexpected group, with rank at most the previous bound, and (ii) a contribution crossing from the expected group, with rank at most \(d_Nr_j\). Therefore its rank grows by at most \(d_Nr_j\). Intermediate block-monomial permutations only relabel the two groups and cannot increase these ranks.

Induction gives: for the *opposite* final group relative to the net permutation parity, the output-input block rank is at most

\[
\boxed{d_N\sum_{j=1}^t r_j.}
\]

The corresponding block of \(F_N\) is invertible, by Lemma 3 below, and hence has rank \(N/2\). This proves the main theorem. **QED.**

### Lemma 3: every Fourier block \(F_N[X,Y]\) is invertible

**Odd exponent, \(N=2L^2\):** take arbitrary \(X,Y\in\{A,B\}\). For their respective fixed middle-bit values \(u,v\in\{0,1\}\), write

\[
i=2Lz+uL+a,\quad j=2Lw+vL+b,
\quad 0\le z,w,a,b<L.
\]

Set \(\xi=\omega^{2L}\), a primitive \(L\)-th root of unity. Then

\[
\omega^{ij}=\xi^{zb+aw}\,\omega^{(uL+a)(vL+b)}.
\]

An invertible \(L\)-point Fourier transform on the \(z\) row coordinate isolates \(b\); an invertible transform on the \(w\) column coordinate isolates \(a\). The resulting matrix is diagonal, indexed by \((a,b)\), with nonzero diagonal entries proportional to \(\omega^{(uL+a)(vL+b)}\). Therefore **all four choices of \(X,Y\)** give invertible \(L^2\times L^2\) blocks.

**Even exponent, \(N=4q^2\):** the two central bits represent a value \(u\in\{0,1,2,3\}\). The allowed row values are \(U_A=\{0,3\}\) or \(U_B=\{1,2\}\); the column values are likewise \(U_A\) or \(U_B\). Write

\[
i=4qz+uq+a,\quad j=4qw+vq+b,\quad 0\le z,w,a,b<q.
\]

Set \(\xi=\omega^{4q}\), a primitive \(q\)-th root. Again,

\[
\omega^{ij}=\xi^{zb+aw}\,\omega^{(uq+a)(vq+b)}.
\]

Fourier transforms on the \(z\) and \(w\) coordinates reduce every chosen \(2q^2\times 2q^2\) block to \(q^2\) independent \(2\times2\) blocks

\[
T_{a,b}(u,v)=\omega^{(uq+a)(vq+b)},\quad u\in U_X,\ v\in U_Y.
\]

Let \(\Delta u\in\{1,3\}\) and \(\Delta v\in\{1,3\}\) be the positive differences of the two allowed values. The determinant of each block is a nonzero monomial times

\[
1-\omega^{q^2\Delta u\Delta v}=1-i^{\Delta u\Delta v}\ne0,
\]

because both differences are odd. Therefore every \(F_N[X,Y]\), \(X,Y\in\{A,B\}\), is invertible. **QED.**

## Exact consequences

| \(N\) | \(d_N\) | Fourier block rank \(N/2\) | Minimal \(\sum r_i\) | Rank-one depth 4 |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 32 | 7 | 16 | 3 | not excluded |
| 64 | 8 | 32 | 4 | not excluded |
| 128 | 15 | 64 | 5 | **excluded** |
| 256 | 16 | 128 | 8 | **excluded** |
| 512 | 31 | 256 | 9 | **excluded** |
| 1,024 | 32 | 512 | 16 | **excluded** |
| 2,048 | 63 | 1,024 | 17 | **excluded** |

For rank-two stages, the sum-of-orders bound excludes four stages at \(N=512\) and all larger powers of two. More generally, any fixed channel order \(r\) requires \(t=\Omega(\sqrt N/r)\) in this permutation class.

## Independent finite computational certificates

The elementary all-length proof does **not** rely on modular lifting or numeric graph searches. Nevertheless:

- `test_gate.py` checks **all four** blocks over two exact prime fields \(\mathbb F_{12289}\) and \(\mathbb F_{65537}\), at \(N=4,8,16,32,64,128,256\), plus the Fourier index decompositions through \(N=1024\).
- The full dependency-graph certificates `EXACT_NETWORK_CERTIFICATE_N128_all_bitrev.json` and `EXACT_NETWORK_CERTIFICATE_N256_all_bitrev.json` contain respectively **60 and 64 explicit scalar wires**. Removing these wires disconnects the selected \(N/2\) input and output coordinates; `verify_cut_certificates.py` independently constructs the graph and verifies this by reachability.
- The same verifier checks exact modular Fourier determinants (nonzero in both fields), including \(\det F_{128}[A,B]\equiv3504\pmod{12289}\) and \(\equiv4096\pmod{65537}\), and \(\det F_{256}[A,B]\equiv696\pmod{12289}\), \(\equiv4096\pmod{65537}\).
- At \(N=256\), the alternative four-stage shuffles `P, rotl(P), P` and `P, xor-middle(P), P` have independent exact network cut certificates of **112 and 124 scalar wires** against Fourier rank **128**. These are **finite-size** obstructions; unlike the main theorem, they are not all-length results.
- A separate optional integer-programming search finds no central-partition transition-rank contradiction for four stages at \(N=64\). This is **not** an existence proof for a four-stage factorization at that size.

## Important scientific limitations

1. The theorem is about quasiseparable-stage factorizations whose shuffles preserve or swap one specially chosen coarse partition. **Permutations mixing the two sets internally are not covered.**
2. The lower bound is on the **sum of quasiseparable channel orders**, not on arbitrary arithmetic circuits. Standard \(O(Nr)\) scan evaluators for this architecture do not become an FFT speedup, but no universal \(\Omega(N\log N)\) result is claimed.
3. Exact finite-field rank certificates verify arithmetic identities. The all-length, complex-number theorem rests on the explicit Fourier block factorization and rank propagation proof, not on an inference that a finite field has no solution.
4. This proof may overlap pre-existing matrix-network/communication-rank results; originality beyond this project has **not** been independently established.
