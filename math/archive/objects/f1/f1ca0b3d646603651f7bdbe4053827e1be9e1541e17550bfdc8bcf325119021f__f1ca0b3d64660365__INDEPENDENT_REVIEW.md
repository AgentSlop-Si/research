# Independent mathematical and cost review

Reviewed 9 October 2026: `README.md`, `scan_fft.py`, `run_audit.py`, and the
saved `results.json`. No source files were changed and no earlier experiment
suite was rerun. The results below follow from inspecting the exact stage
definition, algebra, loops, and recorded checks.

**Finding: the theorem, construction, and stated cost are correct within the
declared square-product scan model.** They do not establish a general FFT
arithmetic lower bound or a faster FFT.

## Inverse-support theorem

For the source archive's scan, the output vector is the accumulated state:
`y_i = a_i y_(i-1) + b_i x_i`, with the boundary equation lacking a previous
state. An invertible product of square factors forces each factor to be
invertible. The scan is triangular with determinant the product of its
`b_i`, so its inverse is bidiagonal and has maximum row support at most two.
It also has a boundary row of support exactly one. Backward scans satisfy
the same assertions with the opposite boundary.

For arbitrary matrices, the support of a row of `AB` lies in the union of
the supports of the rows of `B` indexed by nonzero entries of that row of
`A`. Thus maximum row support is submultiplicative. Inverse permutations
and invertible diagonal matrices are monomial and do not increase the
support bound. Consequently a word with `t` scans has inverse maximum row
support at most `2^t`, independently of any internal permutations.

The sharpened bound is valid as well. In the inverse word, take the
leftmost scan inverse. Any monomial factors before it only scale and
permute rows, so a singleton row survives. On multiplying the remaining
factors, this row gains at most a factor of two in support for each of the
remaining `t-1` scans. Therefore at least one final inverse row has support
at most `2^(t-1)`. Every row of `F_N^(-1)` has support exactly `N`, giving
`t >= 1 + ceil(log2 N)` for ordinary noncyclic scans and `N >= 2`. With no
scans the whole word is monomial and cannot equal the dense Fourier matrix.

The weaker `product d_i >= N` statement for inverse row degrees `d_i` is
also correct. For cyclic scans with cyclic-bidiagonal inverses it gives
the logarithmic bound, but the singleton-row sharpening does not follow.

## Constructive realization and exact field count

For each routed pair `(u,v)` with twiddle `w`, the backward scan gives
`(u+w*v, -2*w*v)`. The following forward scan gives
`(u+w*v, u-w*v)`. The zero recurrence coefficients at pair boundaries
prevent leakage between pairs. The supplied primitive dyadic root is in a
field where two is invertible, so the `-1/2` coefficient is valid.

The bit reversal, layer pair routing, twiddle exponent `j*N/length`, and
scatter implement the ordinary positive-sign radix-two DFT. Both scan
functions really traverse all `N` coordinates; there is no hidden dense
operator or call to another Fourier transform.

Each literal scan performs `2N-1` multiplications and `N-1` additions,
including zero and identity coefficients. A layer therefore costs `6N-4`
scan operations plus `N/2` twiddle-coefficient multiplications. Root
enumeration/order validation costs `N` multiplications; forming `-2` and
`-1/2` costs one addition, one negation, and one inversion. The total is
exactly

    N + 3 + log2(N) * (13N/2 - 4).

The stated routing and coefficient arrays cost linear additional word work
per layer. Only a constant number of length-`N` arrays coexist, and retained
layer records are constant-sized, so live implementation storage is
`O(N)` field slots plus `O(N)` index words. The audit's separately formed
matrices are verification machinery, not algorithm workspace. Field-slot
accounting is not a bound on coefficient bit lengths or exact-complex bit
complexity.

## Evidence and limits

The saved audit reports PASS for 136 Fourier basis inputs, 36 mixed full
outputs, 206 scan/inverse matrix checks, and 72 internally shuffled products.
Its field-count assertion agrees with the independently derived formula.
The tests cover two prime fields and small exact cyclotomic fields; the
all-size statement rests on the support proof, not finite experiments.

The lower bound concerns numbers of scan factors. An `Omega(N log N)` work
consequence requires executing each factor as a full-array `Theta(N)`
pass, as the implementation does. It is not a lower bound on optimized
arithmetic that fuses stages or skips degenerate operations. The README
correctly excludes additive branches, independently chosen feedthrough
outputs, wider state or auxiliary workspace, and general linear-time
operators whose inverses may be dense. Adaptive restricted-input
recurrence evaluators are also outside this matrix-factorization theorem.

There is no unresolved mathematical or cost issue within these assumptions.
