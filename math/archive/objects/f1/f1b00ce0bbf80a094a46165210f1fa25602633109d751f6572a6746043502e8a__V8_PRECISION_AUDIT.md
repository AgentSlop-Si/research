# Independent v8 audit and a charged finite-precision extension

9 October 2026. Uploaded Python was read as text, never imported or executed. This report certifies mathematics and a specified implementation variant. It does not certify the supplied NumPy program, fixed IEEE precision at arbitrary N, novelty, or exact DFT recovery.

## 1. Verdict and comparison

The gauge identity, balanced-scan work/depth statement, deterministic three-grid lemma, and probability argument are correct. For each cycle m_j=s+2aj modulo N, A_j=zeta^(a j^2+(beta+s)j) satisfies A_(j+1)=zeta^(a+beta+m_j) A_j; consequently Y_(m_j)=A_j[Y_s-sum_(h<j) F_(m_h)/A_h]. A Blelloch scan has O(N) work and O(log N) depth.

The grid proof correctly uses the *whole* interval [mu-tau,mu+tau], not just its sampled points. The union of width-7tau boundaries with offsets 0,7tau/3,14tau/3 has spacing 7tau/3>2tau. At most one grid has any boundary in that closed interval. In each of the other two grids all good values share a strict-majority cell; its center lies within 5tau/2 of mu. Boyer–Moore returns the cell without a verification pass, conditional on the independently established majority event. Taking three centers' median proves the claim, including endpoint cases. The KL bound and the fixed-row combinatorial bound follow.

Relative to our earlier five-counter selector, v8 needs three candidate cells rather than five representatives, tolerates any bad fraction below one half rather than below one quarter, and improves the scalar deterministic constant from 3 to 2.5 at a common radius. It is a different estimator from both exact median and the five-counter scheme.

There is a genuine computational-model qualification: calculating floor((z-offset)/W) on an arbitrary exact real is not a unit-cost field operation or comparison. An O(log N)-bit *answer* does not prove O(1)-work conversion. Thus v8's claimed scalar work is conditional on an explicit quantization/word primitive. Our five-counter arithmetic-and-comparison proof avoids this extra primitive and can use the new gauge scan unchanged. Section 5 below gives an explicit finite-word grid implementation instead.

Minor inherited qualifications: require M>=2 for the exact covariance-norm headline; M=1 is the zero-variance exact limit. For a=0 in delta=N^(-a), the confidence statement is vacuous; use delta=min(1/2,N^(-a)). The O(N) space claim means numerical words/registers plus counters of O(log R) bits, not O(N) bits. For arbitrary enormous R the counter width must be charged.

## 2. A complete deterministic row-error theorem

### Implementation and arithmetic contract

N=KM is dyadic, M>=2, K>=2M, L=log2 N. The fixed input x is represented exactly by the supplied input numbers; input-conversion error is considered separately below. All integer exponents and indices are computed exactly modulo N with sufficient integer width. No signed-integer overflow is permitted.

Use the following implementation of the *same exact row*:

- form summaries with pairwise reductions;
- use a normalized radix-2 length-K FFT, then scale by 1/sqrt(M), obtaining H=D/sqrt(N);
- evaluate each seed by a flattened pairwise reduction of N terms d_r zeta^(-a t^2-(2aMr+beta+s)t-sMr), followed by scaling by 1/sqrt(N); this only combines the prototype's two exact phase factors into one root lookup;
- form F_m=H_(m mod K)-b_m H_((m+2aM) mod K);
- form q_j=F_(m_j) conjugate(A_j), using the exact identity 1/A_j=conjugate(A_j), rather than numerically dividing by an approximate unit root;
- apply a balanced exclusive scan; multiply A_j by the rounded seed-minus-prefix.

Assume a common number t>0 bounds all the following:

1. Complex addition/subtraction: the rounding residual has magnitude at most t(|v|+|w|).
2. Complex multiplication: the rounding residual has magnitude at most t|v||w|.
3. Every stored root approximation differs in complex magnitude from its intended exact Nth root by at most t. Conjugation itself is exact.
4. Each stored positive normalization factor 1/sqrt(2), 1/sqrt(M), 1/sqrt(N) has relative error at most t; multiplication then satisfies item 2.
5. No overflow or underflow invalidates these bounds. Each prefix output has an addition-expression tree of depth at most 2L, with each input term occurring at most once.

These assumptions are concrete local numerical contracts, not an assumption that summaries, forcing terms, or FFT outputs are already accurate. The analysis below bounds all those stages.

### Explicit error ledger

Let X=||x||_2 and define

    c = (1+t)^2-1
    e = c+(1+c)[c+t(2+c)]
    a = (1+c)(1+t)^(log2 M)-1
    b = (1+e)^(log2 K)-1
    h = (1+a)(1+b)(1+c)-1
    s = (1+a)(1+c)(1+t)^L(1+c)-1
    f = 2h+c(1+h)+t(2+c)(1+h)
    q = (1+c)f+2c
    gamma = (1+t)^(2L)-1
    P = q+gamma(2+q).

Then every output of every seed choice obeys

    |computed Y_m - exact Y_m|
      <= { (1+c)(1+t)[M s+sqrt(NM) P]
           +[3t(1+c)+c]M } X.                         (A)

In particular, when t L <= 1/100,

    max_m |computed Y_m - exact Y_m|
      <= 100 sqrt(NM) L t ||x||_2.                   (B)

This is deterministic, simultaneous in all outputs, and uniform over the row's odd a and beta. It therefore composes with the independent-row probability proof without assigning probability to rounding errors or assuming those errors are unbiased.

### Proof of the ledger

Summary formation has phase-plus-product relative coefficient error at most c. Pairwise depth log2 M adds a factor (1+t)^(log2 M). Cauchy–Schwarz within each block gives

    ||computed d-d||_2 <= a sqrt(M) X,
    ||d||_2 <= sqrt(M) X.

For one normalized FFT butterfly (v +/- w z)/sqrt(2), phase-plus-product error contributes at most c times the pair's Euclidean norm after ideal normalization. The two addition residuals contribute at most t sqrt(1+(1+c)^2) times that norm, which is at most t(2+c) times it. Approximate normalization contributes c and amplifies previous errors by at most 1+c. Thus a whole FFT layer's pointwise normwise error is at most e times its input norm. The exact layer is unitary. Composing log2 K layers yields the relative normwise bound b, by induction on layers, without needing any linearity of rounding. Scaling by 1/sqrt(M) gives

    ||computed H-H||_2 <= h X,
    ||H||_2 <= X.

For each seed, the N-term absolute coefficient sum before normalization is M||d||_1 <= M sqrt(N) X. Summary perturbation, one phase/product factor, pairwise depth L, and normalization give seed error at most M s X. Both seeds are included; their errors need not be independent.

Every H index occurs M times over all N forcing positions, and the shifted index map is a permutation. Hence

    sum_m |F_m| <= 2M ||H||_1 <= 2 sqrt(NM) X.

Apply the same repetition bound to the H error. The first two terms of f bound propagated H error and boundary phase/product error; its last term bounds subtraction residuals. Thus

    sum_m |computed F_m-F_m| <= sqrt(NM) f X.

Multiplication by the conjugate gauge gives the q ledger:

    sum_m |computed q_m-q_m| <= sqrt(NM) q X,
    sum_m |computed q_m| <= sqrt(NM)(2+q) X.

Each cycle is a subset of these positions. Each prefix is a sum of a subset, so the balanced-tree perturbation bound gamma times the absolute term sum gives prefix error at most sqrt(NM) P X. This is an absolute bound and remains valid under catastrophic relative cancellation.

Exact row magnitude is at most M X because the block map has operator norm M and the DFT is unitary. Since A has unit magnitude, exact seed magnitude is at most M X, and an exact prefix, being seed minus Y/A, has magnitude at most 2M X. The rounded seed-minus-prefix subtraction therefore incurs at most t[3M X+M s X+sqrt(NM)P X]. The final gauge multiplication/phase perturbation gives (A).

For (B), L>=3 and tL<=0.01 imply c<=3t, e<=10t, a<=3Lt, b<=11Lt, h<=20Lt, s<=7Lt, f<=43Lt, q<=46Lt, gamma<=3Lt, and P<=54Lt. Also M<=sqrt(NM). Substitution into (A) gives less than 65 sqrt(NM)Lt X; 100 is a convenient conservative constant.

## 3. Root preparation, charged precision, and input model

For usual correctly rounded real floating operations, complex addition/subtraction satisfies item 1 with t>=u. Straightforward complex multiplication can conservatively be bounded by 8u|v||w| for sufficiently small unit roundoff u. With certified roots and normalizers accurate enough, take t=8u. This yields

    eta_row = 800 sqrt(NM) log2(N) u ||x||_2,
    8u log2(N) <= 0.01.                              (C)

A root table generated by *working-precision* repeated multiplication must not silently be assigned O(u) error: its error can be O(Nu). Two valid options are:

- certify a uniform root-error rho and replace t by max(8u,rho,normalizer relative errors), paying the resulting bound; or
- generate the table at guard precision and round it into working precision.

For the second option, let guard unit roundoff v<=u/(64N), start from a certified primitive root with complex error <=v, and use complex products with residual <=8v times operand magnitudes. Induction gives each table error less than 10Nv for Nv sufficiently small. Thus after rounding the bounded root components into working precision the uniform error is comfortably below 8u. This needs p+ceil(log2 N)+6 guard mantissa bits when u=2^(-p), O(N) table products, and O(N) stored working-precision roots. Guard registers can be reused; the whole table need not remain at guard precision. The radix-2 FFT uses compatible roots by modular lookup.

The initial root and normalization constants are not free. They may be supplied with certificates, in which case preparation cost must be charged to their supplier, or constructed. A simple self-contained construction is certified rational interval evaluation: Machin's identity pi=16 arctan(1/5)-4 arctan(1/239), alternating-series tail bounds, then sine/cosine Taylor intervals at 2pi/N; bisection with rational square comparisons supplies reciprocal square roots. O(P+log N) terms/iterations at P+O(log(P+N)) internal bits suffice, where P is the required guard accuracy. This gives a conservative polynomial(P+log N) bit setup bound, separate from row evaluation. No O(1) transcendental/root oracle is being claimed. Faster standard constant construction is unnecessary for the stated logarithmic-precision regimes.

If the desired deterministic numerical allowance is theta times the stochastic scalar radius tau=g sqrt(2M/N)||x||_2, it is sufficient to require

    u <= min{1/[800 log2 N], theta g/[800 N log2 N]}.

The omitted sqrt(2) only makes this choice more conservative. Thus

    p = O(log N + log(1/theta) + max(0,log(1/g)))

mantissa bits suffice in the fixed- and growing-row regimes; for g>=sqrt(2), this is O(log N+log(1/theta)). Guard precision is also O(log N+log(1/theta)). This does not say that a fixed p such as float64 works asymptotically.

The theorem assumes exact represented inputs and no invalid range events. For genuinely real or higher-precision inputs first rounded to x', apply the theorem and stochastic statement to x', then add ||x-x'||_2 to the final Fourier error, since each row of the unitary DFT has norm one. Use a certified norm upper bound for x' in the tolerance. Reading arbitrarily many input bits is not free.

One usable bounded-input word corollary assumes a common dyadic input lattice: all real/imaginary components have the form 2^e z_j with signed p0=O(log N)-bit integers z_j, with the common exponent e and its storage charged. It also assumes a certified dyadic norm envelope B satisfying ||x||_2<=B<=C||x||_2 for fixed C, requested theta bounded below by an inverse polynomial, polynomially bounded g and R, and sufficient exponent range for the local contracts. Inputs on different lattices require their alignment width/range to be charged; they are not silently included in this bounded-word assumption. The norm envelope can be constructed within the same budget, rather than assumed free. Compute the exact nonnegative integer E=sum_j[(Re z_j)^2+(Im z_j)^2], whose width is at most 2p0+ceil(log2(2N))+O(1) bits. This uses O(N) fixed-number-word products/additions. If E=0 return zero. Otherwise let q=floor(log2 E) and B=2^(e+ceil((q+1)/2)); then ||x||_2<=B<=2||x||_2. Its bit length is obtained by a specified bit-length primitive or O(log N) comparisons/doublings on this O(log N)-bit integer. Rescaling input by this power of two is exact and brings its norm into (1/2,1], which also avoids an absolute-error loss for tiny original inputs. The zero input is handled separately. Mantissas, guard mantissas, indices, cell IDs and counters then require O(log N) bits each (plus the specified exponent representation). Precision/range must be selected rather than inferred from NumPy types. With a word-RAM explicitly supporting the needed multiplication, shifts and division at this width, the same scalar/word work order is preserved. In a bit model, write w for the full numerical/integer word width and C_arith(w) for a valid cost of its operations; charge

    O(R [N+K log K] C_arith(w) + N C_arith(w_guard)
      + polynomial(w_guard+log N) + input/output bit costs),

and O(Nw) bits of storage (plus input storage if wider). Do not turn this into a bit-linear or arbitrary-input-precision claim. The conservative setup construction is polylogarithmic for w=O(log N), so does not change the scalar asymptotic regimes.

## 4. Finite-precision robust theorem

Let B>=||x||_2 be a certified envelope and set tau0=g sqrt(2M/N) B with g>sqrt(2). Suppose all computed row errors are bounded by eta B, as in (A) or (B). Choose a common certified positive dyadic radius r satisfying

    r >= tau0 + eta B.

The exact ideal row is good when its scalar error is <=tau0; Chebyshev bounds its bad probability by p=1/g^2. Every good ideal row produces a computed scalar within r of its target. Independent ideal row seeds therefore give

    Pr[final complex error > (5 sqrt(2)/2) r + eta_out]
       <= 2N exp[-R D(1/2 || 1/g^2)],                 (D)

provided classification and candidate-center ordering are exact for the represented data, and eta_out bounds final complex output rounding. There is no need for rounded rows to remain unbiased.

For odd R=2s-1 the alternative bound N 2^R g^(-2s) remains valid. A certified r within a factor 1+nu of tau0+eta B changes only this explicit factor. Power-of-two upward rounding gives the simpler factor at most two. If eta B<=theta tau0 and B<=C||x||_2, the finite implementation retains both v8 error exponents, with the displayed constants multiplied by C(1+theta)(1+nu), plus eta_out.

The source's final suggestion to inflate tau by row error is mathematically right, but a complete finite implementation must also handle grid calculation and output rounding, and must use the *inflated* common width and offsets. Approximate division followed by floor, with no boundary certificate, is not covered by an ideal-floor proof.

## 5. An exact represented-grid implementation

For represented dyadic z and dyadic r>0, W=7r and grid k in {0,1,2}, calculate

    cell_k(z) = floor((3z-7kr)/(21r)).

This is exact signed integer division after scaling represented dyadics to a common lattice; floor for negative numerators must be used, not truncation toward zero. Candidate-center numerators are proportional to

    42 cell_k + 14k + 21,

with common factor r/6. Compare these integer numerators to take the median, then round only the chosen final center. This realizes the exact three-grid lemma including values exactly on boundaries. Counters and IDs are exact integers.

When the working data and r already use a common O(log N)-bit fixed-point lattice, the quotient and cell update take O(1) specified word operations, with O(log N) bits of candidate state under the source's magnitude bound. If the row calculation uses floating-point, a final certified rounding to a common fixed-point lattice is a permitted conversion: add its worst-case scalar error to r first. Choose the lattice relative to B, e.g. mesh at most a constant fraction of the numerical allowance, to keep both its fractional precision and range O(log N) in the bounded-input regime. Conversion work and the chosen range are charged. This supplies the missing word-model implementation rather than silently inserting a floor oracle.

The five-counter alternative needs neither real-to-cell floor nor rational grid centers. It can compare represented dyadic distances to twice an inflated dyadic radius exactly, and gives scalar error 3r whenever fewer than a quarter of ideal rows are bad. The v8 balanced decoder and the earlier five-counter selector therefore give a clean arithmetic/comparison-only exact-arithmetic theorem and a correspondingly explicit finite-word variant, at worse constants/row count.

## 6. Evidence and limits

`independent_v8_checks.py` and `independent_v8_results.json` contain independently authored checks. No uploaded module is executed. Checks supplement, not replace, the proofs above. They check exact modular gauge decoding, exact integer/rational grid classification including endpoints, and the explicit ledger's numerical constant over its parameter range. Completed checks: 18 exact modular row/seed/gauge comparisons through N=256; 9 exact scans; 360,297 integer-grid words, including 307,755 strict-majority cases and 261,198 majority cases containing interval endpoints; 3,000 longer adversarial randomized streams; and 6,176 evaluations of the explicit error ledger. All pass. The largest tested ledger ratio to sqrt(NM)Lt is 21.524, below the proved conservative constant 100. See the results file for parameters and counts.

The supplied complex64 comparisons are mixed-precision experiments: the prototype forms summaries in complex128, computes its FFT in complex128 before casting, and obtains phases through float64 exponential calls. They are not end-to-end single-precision FFT error tests. The prototype also uses fixed int64 modular products and int32 counters. Nothing in (A)–(D) certifies those unchecked platform operations at arbitrary size.

The meaningful new conclusion is narrower and constructive: v8's structural scan can support a complete bounded-input finite-precision approximation theorem at logarithmically growing precision, while retaining its scalar-work and linear-register bounds. It remains an approximate, dyadic, energy-relative algorithm, with honest numerical, integer and bit costs.
