# Any fixed finite bank of binary-affine cycle orders has a common low-crossing parity cut

## Scope

This preserves and generalizes the parity-cut route behind `three_stage/recursive_bank_constraints/BIT_AFFINE_BANK_EXTENSION.md`. It is an asymptotic exclusion for a specified representation, not an unrestricted FFT lower bound. It applies over characteristic zero to Fourier or any flat unitary target, with diagonal or cut-preserving endpoint factors. The specialized natural-plus-one-affine result in the earlier note is sharper for that pair and is not replaced here.

Let N=2^k, r=floor(k/2), m=2^r. Let Z be the ordinary cyclic binary counter. There are d>=1 distinct allowed orders

    P_j = sigma_j Z sigma_j^(-1),
    sigma_j(x)=A_j x+b_j over GF(2)^k,

where each A_j is invertible. The maps may depend on N. All banks are block diagonal on the same contiguous m-blocks. Endpoint monomials preserve each such block. Suppose a product-of-sums expression contains M Green terms in total, all with these orders; arbitrary additional same-block banks may be inserted. All Green inverses must exist; weights may vanish. Reusing an order does not increase d, but every occurrence is charged in M.

Put

    u = floor((k-r-1)/d).

For k>=1 this is nonnegative. Then every exact realization of a characteristic-zero flat-unitary target requires

    M >= 2^(u-1).

If each of L layers contains at most d terms, the convenient consequence is

    Ld >= 2^(u-1).

For fixed L and d, u tends to infinity as k grows. Therefore no fixed-depth family formed from any fixed finite number of binary-affine conjugates can realize the Fourier transform in this scope, even if all the affine maps change with N.

## 1. Dimension budget

Choose a nonzero linear form f on GF(2)^k subject to

* f has zero coefficients on the first r low input bits;
* each pullback A_j^T f has zero coefficients on its first u low bits.

There are at most r+du<=k-1 homogeneous linear constraints on k unknowns, so a nonzero solution exists. No independence of these constraints is assumed. The translation b_j only changes the constant part of f composed with sigma_j.

Let S={x:f(x)=0}. It has N/2 vertices and is a union of whole contiguous m-blocks. Thus every permitted bank and endpoint monomial preserves S. Under sigma_j, its pullback is a balanced affine parity set with its first u coefficients zero.

## 2. Crossing count and rank

If a parity mask g has its first u coefficients zero, its parity can change under x -> x+1 only when the counter carries past bit u-1. There are N/2^u such counter transitions, including wrap. Every cyclic binary word has equally many transitions in either direction. Therefore its incoming count is at most N/2^(u+1). This includes affine parity constants, which merely swap the two sides.

Equivalently the exact incoming count for nonzero g is

    e(g)=1/2 [sum_(h=1)^k 2^(k-h) parity(g & (2^h-1))
              + parity(g & (2^k-1))].

The complementary inverse-submatrix rank identity bounds each weighted Green term's S-to-S-complement cross rank by that cycle's incoming count. Sum/product rank subadditivity therefore bounds the whole expression's cross rank by M N/2^(u+1). Inserted banks add zero.

For any flat unitary target normalized to have entries of magnitude one, its balanced cross block has squared Frobenius norm N^2/4 and operator norm at most sqrt(N). Hence its rank is at least N/4. Comparing ranks and cancelling N proves M>=2^(u-1).

For Fourier matrices the characteristic-zero statement follows over the cyclotomic field and its characteristic-zero extensions. This argument does not establish the corresponding statement in arbitrary positive characteristic.

## 3. A sharper allocation when the natural order is present

The common-cut argument permits different low-bit budgets u_j as long as

    r + sum_j u_j <= k-1.

An explicitly included natural order already has r low zeros from block compatibility and costs no extra pullback constraints. If there are d-1 other orders, one may set u=floor((k-r-1)/(d-1)) for them. A layer containing one term on each order then has cross-rank budget at most

    N/2^(r+1) + (d-1)N/2^(u+1).

For d=2 this recovers q/2+m with q=N/m, and the previously established condition L(q/2+m)>=N/4. More general multiplicities can be charged order by order. The argument may be loose, but a fixed finite bank still has a common asymptotically sparse cut.

## 4. Relation to the MGG geometry

The Hamiltonian construction in `EXPANDING_HAMILTONIAN_CYCLE_BANK.md` guarantees a positive constant times N incoming-count sum across every balanced cut, once N is large. Therefore its fixed eight orders cannot all be binary-affine conjugates of the counter at arbitrarily large N. This is an asymptotic implication; no claim is made that every individual order is non-affine at every small size.

The negative affine result and the positive MGG edge-count result leave the central Fourier coefficient problem open. Passing the edge-count budget is strictly weaker than realizing a Fourier matrix.

## 5. Controls and provenance

The dimension argument and crossing formula above are proved directly. `check_affine_bank.py` freshly constructs random invertible binary-affine maps, solves the simultaneous homogeneous constraints, verifies that the resulting parity cut is balanced and bank-compatible, and checks the actual counter crossings and the stated bounds. It executes no uploaded source. The fixed finite bank statement is derived here; publication priority is not claimed.
