# Phase 24 — falsifiable research targets

1. **Generic-input sharing:** seek an all-input, one-query exact Fourier circuit with a fully counted number of arithmetic operations `o(N log N)` in a precisely identified model. Include all root preparation, data-dependent discovery, representation lookup, inverse transforms and memory/bit costs. Test for hidden Cooley–Tukey and sparse-pruned fallback.
2. **Further compiler sharing:** the remaining dominant fixed-s cost is independent Gaussian elimination on nearby Hankel polynomials and interpolation nodes. Investigate symbolic determinant reuse, displacement-rank structured elimination, and exact batch-inversion variants. Count every modular multiplication and inversion; savings in *inversion count alone* may worsen the current unit-cost field-op proxy.
3. **Completeness characterization:** quantify exceptional structured families where the resultant is zero, the Hankel gcd is nonlinear, or some of the 2s+2 probes vanish. Design secondary complete candidate enumerators with a charged bound instead of declaring success on generic inputs.
4. **Find parameter-free dense families of positive dimension scaling with N**, or prove an explicit dimension/cost tradeoff. Our present fixed-s sparse-rational union occupies a measure-zero subset of complex N-space for N large; dense entries are not generic structure.
5. **Word/bit model:** count modular-root construction, field inversions, hashed BSGS memory, precision or coefficient growth. The exact finite-field model is not an automatically stable complex FFT.
6. **Independent proof audit of Phase 21:** check the universal `U(w)=w^bV(w+w^-1)` factorization with machine algebra or formal proof. Reproducible tests are only evidence for an unreviewed mathematical lemma, not formal verification.

Strict acceptance for solving the original user challenge: a *general-input exact* DFT algorithm with a reproducible `o(N log N)` bound in its declared model. Phase 23 has not met it.
