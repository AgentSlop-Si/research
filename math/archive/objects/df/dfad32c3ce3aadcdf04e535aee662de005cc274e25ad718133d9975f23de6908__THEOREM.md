# Phase 39 — Two independently tunable Green couplings: exact multi-mask exclusion

**Date:** 10 October 2026. **Status:** New restricted algebraic certificate, verified exactly. **No new general-input `o(N log N)` Fourier algorithm or integer multiplication bit bound.**

## 1. Continuation from Phase 38

The existing Phase 38 model allowed one scalar pencil parameter and two completely free internal diagonal masks. We preserve its four fixed outer Green summands and two fixed middle Green resolvents, then introduce a **third, independently ordered middle Green operator** with an independently variable coefficient:

\[
M_N(\lambda,\mu,D_1,D_2,P_1,P_2)
=A D_1P_1\bigl(G_2+\lambda G_3+\mu G_6\bigr)P_2D_2C.
\]

Here `G_s = (I - alpha_s W_s)^(-1)`, where `W_s` is the directed cyclic permutation of a Hamiltonian ordering. Exactly as in Phase 38,

- `A=G0+((1+i)/3)*G1`, `C=G4+((1-i)/4)*G5`;
- `alpha_0..alpha_5 = ((1+i)/4, (2+i)/7, (1+2i)/8, (1-i)/5, (2+i)/9, (1+i)/6)`;
- `G6` uses the additional deterministic cycle in `phase39_bivariate.build`, with `alpha_6=(1+3i)/11`;
- all seven *couplings inside* the individual Green resolvents are fixed; **only** the two coefficients `lambda, mu` vary, independently in the complex field;
- `D1,D2` may be **any invertible complex diagonal matrices**, and `P1,P2` **any permutations**, not merely phase or cyclic gauges.

For `N >= 8`, the seven cycle orders are distinct, and at the checked small sizes they are all non-affine. For `N=4`, there are only `3! = 6` distinct Hamiltonian cycles starting at zero; `G6` uses a previously used order but a different Green coupling. Do **not** describe the `N=4` instance as seven distinct orders.

The target `F_N` is the unnormalized DFT with `F[j,k]=zeta_N^(jk)`; applying `A^{-1}` and `C^{-1}` reduces Fourier equality to

\[
T=A^{-1}F_NC^{-1}=D_1P_1B(x)P_2D_2,\qquad
B(x)=x_0G_2+x_1G_3+x_2G_6,\quad x=(1,\lambda,\mu).
\]

Homogenizing permits all nonzero projective `x=(x0:x1:x2)`, which is a *stronger* exclusion target than finite `lambda,mu` alone.

## 2. A fundamental defect in anchored bivariate tests

Take an ordered pair of middle rows `r0 != r1`, and select one anchor column `c0`. The two expressions

\[
\ell_0(x)=B_{r_0 c_0}(x),\qquad
\ell_1(x)=B_{r_1 c_0}(x)
\]

are homogeneous linear forms in **three** unknowns. Over `C` they have a common nonzero solution `x`, irrespective of the Green coefficients. Explicitly, when their coefficient vectors are linearly independent, their cross product is a projective common zero.

Every projective cross-ratio equation involving the same anchor column contains at least one of these two forms in each term; consequently **every such anchored equation vanishes at this spurious point**. An anchored-only bivariate Macaulay test therefore cannot give a full-rank homogeneous certificate at any degree. This is a test-design problem, not a valid Fourier parameter point: at the spurious point, the anchor Green entries vanish and invertible diagonal masks cannot fit the nonzero target anchor entries.

Phase 39 replaces those equations with overlapping **unanchored** `2 x 2` minors.

## 3. New polynomial invariants: no unknown masks or permutations

Fix two target rows `(0,1)` and four distinct target columns `(0,1,2,3)`. For a proposed mapping to middle rows `r0,r1` and columns `c0,c1,c2,c3`, define, for each `0 <= a < b <= 3`,

\[
q_{ab}(x) =
T_{0a}T_{1b}\,B_{r_1c_a}(x)B_{r_0c_b}(x)
-T_{0b}T_{1a}\,B_{r_1c_b}(x)B_{r_0c_a}(x).
\]

**Lemma 39A.** Any exact masked-and-permuted factorization forces `q_ab(1,lambda,mu)=0` for **all** pairs `a,b`. These are homogeneous quadratics in three parameters. The arbitrary diagonal row and column gauges cancel as common multiplicative factors. The required equations are polynomial—there is no division by any possibly zero middle entry.

If a subcollection of quadratics `q_j` has no nonzero common root over an algebraic closure, the entire proposed factorization is impossible for **every** complex `lambda,mu` and every pair of internal masks for that mapping.

### 3.1. Smaller-degree certificate: four equations suffice

Choose four overlapping minors with column pairs `(0,1), (0,2), (0,3), (1,2)`. Multiply each quadratic by each of `x0,x1,x2`, and form the `12 x 10` coefficient matrix `K_3`, with columns corresponding to the ten homogeneous cubic monomials.

**Theorem 39B.** If `rank(K_3)=10` over the coefficient field, the four quadratics have no nonzero projective common zero. Proof: their linear multiples then span **all** cubic polynomials. At a common projective root, every cubic monomial would vanish. But if some `x_k != 0`, the cubic `x_k^3` is nonzero, contradiction.

This is a simple *sufficient* certificate. A deficient matrix does **not** prove that a genuine Fourier factorization exists.

### 3.2. Minimum number of equations in the tested witness

Choose only three **non-anchored, connected** minors, with pairs `(0,1), (0,2), (1,3)`. Multiply each by all six quadratic monomials, producing an `18 x 15` matrix `K_4` spanning quartic forms.

**Theorem 39C.** If `rank(K_4)=15`, those **three** quadratic equations have no common nonzero projective solution. The same monomial-vanishing proof applies in degree four. Notice that the older three-equation anchored **star** `(0,1),(0,2),(0,3)` cannot have full rank because of the spurious point from Section 2.

The three-equation construction is optimal in equation count for the *generic* two-parameter projective problem: rank-one `2 x 3` matrices have codimension two; a generic projective two-plane cannot be excluded by only those two independent rank-one conditions. This is a standard Segre-variety dimension fact, not a new general complexity theorem.

## 5. Constructive and negative controls

1. A planted target of the form `T_plant=diag(u) P1 B(1,lambda,mu) P2 diag(v)` must have projective common root `(1:lambda:mu)`. It produces `rank(K_3)=9` and `rank(K_4)=14` in the recorded controls. Both the Python implementation and an independent native C++ verifier fail to declare that planted control impossible.
2. The true Fourier reference passes both exact Weyl identities, `FZ=DF` and `FD=Z^{-1}F`, in the same finite field. The sampled seven-Green circuit violates both identities.
3. The **online seven-Green executor** agrees exactly with independently formed dense matrices, with arbitrary nontrivial row and column permutations, tunable `lambda,mu`, and nontrivial input/output masks.
4. An explicit tiny rational three-generator `2 x 4` pencil (`generic_control.py`) has full `K_3`, `K_4` ranks. Hence full rank is a Zariski-open generic property in the **unrestricted** pencil/matrix-entry parameter space. This does **not** imply a uniform all-`N` theorem for the constrained Green-resolvent parameterization.

## 6. Complexity and limits

- For a **given** set of cycle orders, coefficients, masks, permutations, and scalar couplings, the candidate applies `A`, the three-term middle sum and `C` with **seven cyclic Green recurrences**, two diagonal passes and two permutation passes: `O(N)` *field arithmetic* per vector. Each individual Green recurrence is exact over its specified field, and precomputation of fixed scalar inverses and cycle orders is linear.
- **No choice** of the freely varied two middle couplings, two masks or two permutations succeeds for our certified fixed family at `N=4,8,16`.
- Building `T=A^-1 F C^-1` in the proof uses a **dense `O(N^3)` reference procedure** and is **not** part of a successful online FFT implementation. The fully exhaustive mapping audit costs `O(N^6)` fixed-size field tests. `O(N)` candidate application time is therefore **not** a proof of a faster DFT.
- Arithmetic complexity over special finite fields is different from general arbitrary-precision integer bit complexity. Even a hypothetical exact algebraic DFT factorization would need a separate precision, coefficient generation, preprocessing and data movement analysis.
- The theorem fixes the seven cycle orders, the outer Green stage parameters and all denominators. Independently tuning those, adding a fourth middle Green component, or using additional noncommuting stages falls outside its scope.
