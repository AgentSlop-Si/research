# Prior-art boundary and attribution

- T.-Y. Chien and S. Waldron, *A Characterization of Projective Unitary Equivalence of Finite Frames and Applications*, **SIAM Journal on Discrete Mathematics 30(2), 2016**, 976–994, https://doi.org/10.1137/15M1042140 . General Gram matrices, diagonal gauge equivalence, Bargmann/cycle invariants and rank-one recovery are established subjects. Phase 22's quadratic DFT-gate is a specialized certificate, not a claim to invent Gram invariants.
- The reflection-parity splitting of the DFT into cosine and sine parts, and exact diagonalization by real orthogonal bases, are classical real Fourier transform ideas. The contribution here is the explicit `F=H Q C`, its project-specific linear-cost sparse intertwiner `CZ=RC`, and the calibration/validation package—not claiming invention of cosine or sine transforms.
- The two-coordinate unitary-gate entropy lower bound belongs to existing FFT lower-bound literature, including N. Ailon's analysis. The project already developed this in Phase 16; the bound here is applied to the explicit residual `C`.
- J. Morgenstern, *Note on a Lower Bound on the Linear Complexity of the Fast Fourier Transform*, JACM 20(2), 1973, https://doi.org/10.1145/321752.321761 . Restricted bounded-coefficient linear lower bounds do not automatically rule out nonunitary Green solvers with unbounded internal coefficients.
- Recent exact DFT research claims sub-`N log N` arithmetic in more permissive models; see https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/lean/docs/130.md . Phase 22 does not compete with or reproduce those results, and is independent of the CrocSwap/Swapnil-jain integer-multiplication approach.

**No independent priority or peer-review claim.** Project-specific results should be checked for publication-grade novelty before external dissemination.
