# Exact certificate — four-column compatibility and resultant nonvanishing

## Proven matrix-rank necessity

Let \(C\) be a globally separated-generator matrix with arbitrary diagonal, \(D=QCP^{-1}\) another such matrix, \(P=(7,0,6,3,4,1,2,5)\), \(Q\) any invertible 8×8 matrix. Let \(Y_1,Y_2,Y_5\) be the first-five-row images of the three-dimensional column-generation spaces of \(C\), and \(Y_4\) the first-four-row image of the analogous space for input column 4. Define \(\eta\) as the signed 3×3 minor normal to \(Y_4\) and

\[
K=\left[\begin{array}{ccc}
Y_1&-Y_2&0\\Y_1&0&-Y_5\\\eta^T(Y_1)_{0:3}&0&0
\end{array}\right].
\]

All three selected output columns of \(D\) are collinear above row 5, and the fourth output column shares the same direction above row 4. If a selected projected column vanishes, its (nonzero) column decomposition directly gives a kernel vector; otherwise, rescale the three decompositions to a common nonzero vector and apply \(\eta^T\). Therefore \(\operatorname{rank}K<9\) in all cases. A nonzero 9×9 minor is an exact incompatibility witness. This lemma is independent of Fourier roots and covers zero projected columns.

## Polynomial normalization

On the earlier regular Fourier chart, equations \(E_3=a t+b\beta+c=0\) and \(E_4=d t+e\beta+f=0\) give \(\Delta=ae-bd\), \(T=-ce+bf\), \(V=-af+cd\), hence \(t=T/\Delta,\beta=V/\Delta\). Set \(s=h_0\), \(M=(\zeta-1)T\). The exact generator rescalings \(\widehat U=sU\), \(\widehat L=ML\) are given entirely by integral cyclotomic polynomials:

\[
\widehat U=(H_0,\ldots,H_6,0),\quad \widehat L_0=0,\quad\widehat L_7=M,
\]
\[
\widehat L_k=(\zeta-1)[\Delta+V(H_k-s)]
 +[T-\Delta+V(s-1)](\zeta^{-k}-1),\quad1\le k\le6.
\]

Thus every entry in the rescaled gate is in \(\mathbb Z[\zeta][s,h_1,h_2,h_6]\). The selected 9×9 minor is an actual polynomial; there are **no hidden rational coefficients**, and no division in its formal construction.

## Exact sparse polynomial factorization

The chosen row set is \(I=(0,1,2,5,6,7,8,9,10)\). Direct Laplace expansion along columns 4–6 (1-based) gives \(\widehat D=-D_B D_H\), where \(D_B\) is the 3×3 determinant of the first three rows of the second input-column basis and \(D_H\) is the remaining 6×6 determinant. ## Certificate over a prime ideal implies characteristic-zero exclusion

The ring map \(\mathbb Z[z]/(z^4+1)\to\mathbb F_{17}\), \(z\mapsto9\), is valid because \(9^4\equiv-1\pmod{17}\). Specialize \((s,h_1,h_2)=(1,0,0)\) and write \(x=h_6\). Then

\[
W(x)\equiv 10+14x+16x^2=16(x-2)(x-12)\pmod{17}.
\]

The independently computed polynomial gate determinant gives \(\widehat D(2)=3\), \(\widehat D(12)=2\) modulo 17, hence \(\gcd(W,\widehat D)=1\) in \(\mathbb F_{17}[x]\). Independently computing the univariate resultant gives

\[
\operatorname{Res}_x(W,\widehat D)\equiv6\not\equiv0\pmod{17}.
\]

Since the integral resultant polynomial specializes to this nonzero field element, it cannot vanish in \(\mathbb Z[z]/(z^4+1)\), nor in \(\mathbb C\). Therefore no common complex zero of the two necessary equations exists on the stated slice. The regular factorization chart has \(s\Delta T\ne0\); the two modular roots satisfy \(\Delta,T\ne0\), but the *characteristic-zero* deduction requires no assumption that a complex root reduces to one of the modular roots—it follows from the nonzero resultant itself.

## Explicit elimination to a proper exceptional set

The 9×9 minor factorization gives two shorter resultants in the three base parameters:

\[
R_B=\operatorname{Res}_{h_6}(W,D_B),\qquad
R_H=\operatorname{Res}_{h_6}(W,D_H).
\]

These were expanded exactly and exported in `exact_nonzero_resultants.json`:

| Resultant | Terms | Degree | Reduction at (1,0,0) mod (17,z-9) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| \(R_B\) | 37 | 5 | 16 |
| \(R_H\) | 476 | 15 | 11 |

Both are nonzero over \(\mathbb C\). Every regular factorization must satisfy \(R_B R_H=0\). The cubic \(W\) has degree at most two in \(h_6\). Its leading coefficient \(W_2\) is nonzero linear and does not divide \(W_1\), as witnessed by \(W_2(1,0,10)=0\), \(W_1(1,0,10)=2\pmod{17}\) with invertible leading coefficient. Therefore even the base where \(W\equiv0\) has dimension at most one, giving the overall regular-chart upper bound \(\dim\le2\).

## What the certificate explicitly does not establish

The polynomial obstruction is necessary but not sufficient; it does not prove emptiness of all regular complex parameters, nor handle the exceptional branches \(s=0\), \(\Delta=0\), \(T=0\) or arbitrary quasiseparable generators/permutations. It yields no FFT complexity improvement.
