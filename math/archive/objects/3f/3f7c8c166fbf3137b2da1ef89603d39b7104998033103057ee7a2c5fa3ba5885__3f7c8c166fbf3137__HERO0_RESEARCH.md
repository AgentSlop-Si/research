# HERO #0 — A Bessel–Fourier cycle connecting the two FFT research lineages

**Checkpoint:** 2026-10-09  
**Research target:** Full length-`N` Fourier transform of a *fresh arbitrary input*, in total sequential `O(N)` or `O(N log log N)` cost, including input/output materialization and all setup.  
**Current outcome:** An exact identity making *all Bessel-weighted frequency channels* obey a common operator recurrence, a finite-field Cauchy-matrix certificate, and a restricted impossibility bound for a single sparse-inverse Green shortcut. **No universal `O(N)` or `O(N log log N)` FFT has been proved or implemented.**

## 1. Previous research acknowledged and reused

- **Phase 23 nonunitary/real Green research.** A Fourier-compatible covariance `CZ=RC` gives a linear-work resolvent for inputs that are sums of a constant number of geometric progressions. Its adaptive exact NTT implementation recognizes special inputs, not general fresh inputs. The inherited archive's 24 Python tests pass independently in this session.
- **The parallel all-length v11 dyadic-chirp/Bessel research.** The nearest-grid correction contains `R=Theta(log N/log log N)` Bessel orders. A fused input-side Bessel block summary is already computed in `O(N)` work, but each of `R` orders still needs a length-`K` FFT and a length-`Q≈N` Green/prefix decoding pass. Its 13 tests pass independently in this session. The v11 shrinking-error result is **approximate and randomized**, not an exact DFT result.
- **Earlier Phase 8–23 negative gates:** finite leaf caching, input collisions, exact sparse recurrence recognizers, bounded-rank Green inverse compositions, and small fixed-stage circuit searches do not establish worst-case `O(N)` for arbitrary inputs. Do not relabel those as a new theorem.

This checkpoint adds a bridge **between** the Green-operator project and the Bessel-channel project, not a reformulation of a generic radix-2 FFT as a claimed speedup.

## 2. The exact shared cycle

Let `K>=2`, and use the unitary DFT `U_K` with entries

`U_K[k,b] = exp(-2*pi*i*k*b/K)/sqrt(K)`.

Let `G=(G_0,...,G_(K-1))` be completely arbitrary complex values, independent of `r`. Set `lambda=pi/K` and define the Bessel-weighted channel

`Y_r = U_K diag(J_r(lambda*b): 0<=b<K) G`, for `r>=0`.

Define the fixed, data-independent operator

`H_K = U_K diag(0,1,1/2,...,1/(K-1)) U_K^{-1}`.

**Theorem 1 (exact cycle):** For all complex `G` and all integers `r>=1`,

```
Y_(r+1) = (2r/lambda) H_K Y_r - Y_(r-1)
          + 1_(r=1) * (G_0/sqrt(K)) * ones_K.         (1)
```

**Proof.** For each `b>0`, the classical Bessel order recurrence is

`J_(r+1)(lambda*b) = 2r/(lambda*b) J_r(lambda*b) - J_(r-1)(lambda*b)`.

Multiplication by `G_b` and application of `U_K` give the first two terms of (1). At `b=0`, `J_0(0)=1`, `J_r(0)=0` for `r>=1`. The `H_K` multiplier is zero there. For `r=1` the candidate RHS consequently has an unwanted `-G_0` at coordinate `b=0`; it is corrected by adding `U_K(G_0 e_0)=G_0*ones_K/sqrt(K)`. For `r>1` all zero-coordinate terms already match. No assumption on the data distribution or sparsity is used. QED.

**The useful structural observation:** The complete `R`-channel family follows *one unchanged operator* `H_K` and two previous channel states. The recurring rule is genuinely independent of the original fresh `G` once the two initial channels are obtained. This is a reusable computation schedule, **not** yet an inexpensive implementation of its operator.

## 3. The exact kernel behind the cycle

Set `A_K = U_K diag(0,1,...,K-1) U_K^{-1}` and `P_0=ones_K*ones_K^*/K` (the projector onto the constant Fourier mode). Then

`H_K+P_0 = (A_K+P_0)^{-1}`.                                    (2)

Write `zeta=exp(-2*pi*i/K)`, `a_i=zeta^i`. The entries are

```
A_K[i,i] = (K-1)/2,
A_K[i,j] = a_j/(a_i-a_j)          for i != j.          (3)
```

**Proof of (3).** Since `A_K[i,j]=(1/K)*sum_(b=0)^(K-1) b*zeta^((i-j)*b)`, at `i=j` this is `(K-1)/2`. At `i!=j`, let `q=zeta^(i-j)` so `q^K=1 != q`. The finite weighted geometric sum obeys `sum b*q^b=-K/(1-q)`. Therefore `A_K[i,j]=-1/(1-a_i/a_j)=a_j/(a_i-a_j)`.

For two **disjoint** index sets `I,J` of equal cardinality `t`, the off-diagonal submatrix is a Cauchy matrix scaled on columns. The Cauchy determinant identity implies

`rank(A_K[I,J]) = t`                                                (4)

because the `a_i` and `a_j` are pairwise distinct and no cross-denominator vanishes. This holds over `C`, and over any finite field with a primitive `K`th root of unity and characteristic not dividing `K`.

## 4. A restricted obstruction, not a universal FFT lower bound

**Theorem 2 (row-sparse plus low-rank Cauchy obstruction):** If `A_K=S+E`, every row of `S` has at most `s` nonzeros, and `rank(E)<=r`, then

```
K <= (s+2)(r+1)-1.                                          (5)
```

**Proof.** Assume instead `K>=(s+2)(r+1)`. Pick an arbitrary set `I` of `r+1` row indices. The union of support columns of the selected rows of `S` has at most `s(r+1)` members. Exclude those columns and `I` itself. At least `K-(s+1)(r+1)>=r+1` columns remain; choose `J` of size `r+1`. Then `I,J` are disjoint and `S[I,J]=0`. Thus `E[I,J]=A_K[I,J]`. But by (4), this submatrix has rank `r+1`, contradicting `rank(E)<=r`. QED.

**Corollary (single corrected Green inverse).** If the inverse of the cycle's invertible operator is a row-`s`-sparse matrix plus a rank-`r` update,

`(H_K+P_0)^(-1) = B+E`,  `max_row_nnz(B)<=s`, `rank(E)<=r`,

then `A_K=B+(E-P_0)` with `rank(E-P_0)<=r+1`. Theorem 2 gives

```
K <= (s+2)(r+2)-1.                                          (6)
```

For **constant `s`** this forces **`r=Omega(K)`**. Consequently one tridiagonal or bounded-width inverse **with only a constant-size Woodbury correction** cannot be the long-sought general `O(K)` executor for `H_K`.

**Scope:** (5) and (6) concern **one specific sparse + low-rank representation**. They do not rule out O(K) circuits for this operator with a fundamentally different architecture, a noncommuting network of Green operators, a sum of independently ordered Green solves, or nonlinear preprocessing. Nor do they prove a lower bound on arbitrary FFT algorithms. Related Fourier full-cut rank and sparse-inverse limitations were already investigated in the project's earlier audits; novelty of this particular formulation has not been established.

## 5. Complexity ledger — what is actually saved, and what is not

In the inherited v11 all-length approximation, let

```
M = Theta(log N),
K = Theta(N/log N),
R = Theta(log N/log log N),
Q = Theta(N).
```

| Component | Current charged work | Work if a valid linear `H_K` is found |
|---|---:|---:|
| Fresh input scan and fused block summary | `O(N)` | `O(N)` |
| First two `K`-point Fourier channels | `O(K log K)=O(N)` | unchanged |
| Remaining `R` Bessel channels | `O(R K log K)` by separate FFTs | `O(R K)=O(N/log log N)` via (1) |
| Each order's length-`Q` gauged Green scan and output accumulation | **`O(RN)`** | **still `O(RN)`** |
| Entire v11 shrinking-error procedure | **`O(N log N/log log N)`** | **still `O(N log N/log log N)`** unless the scans are also fused |

The recurrence already works as a mathematical identity, but current `apply_H_oracle` uses **two FFTs**, taking `O(K log K)` time, **not** `O(K)`. It is an independent numerical verifier, not a sub-FFT implementation. Even a breakthrough `O(K)` kernel would only eliminate one of the two `R` bottlenecks of v11. And the v11 target is approximate, not an exact solution to arbitrary-input FFT.

## 6. Precision warning: the natural forward cycle is unstable

The Bessel recurrence subtracts two large terms to obtain an often tiny `J_(r+1)(z)` when `z=pi*b/K` is small. At `b=1`, the forward relative amplification grows rapidly with order and `K`. Accordingly the straightforward double-precision verifier's *absolute output error relative to the input l2 norm* increases from `~6.9e-12` at `K=8` to `~7.7e-8` at `K=128` for only 6 orders, with all base channel FFTs computed independently.

A direct-matrix verification using `mpmath` at 65 decimal digits checks the same identity at `K=8,16,32`, with observed max discrepancies `2.98e-61`, `5.44e-60`, and `2.46e-58` respectively. These are empirical oracle checks for small sizes, not an infinite-`K` numerical stability theorem. A stable backward recurrence/Clenshaw scheme, adaptive precision, or a different basis would be needed for a certified practical implementation.

## 7. A secondary all-length algebraic gate

In the dyadic nearest-grid lift, `Q=2^ceil(log2 N)` and `theta_k = Q*k/N - round(Q*k/N)`. For odd `N`, `gcd(N,Q)=1`, so the fractional parts `{Q*k/N mod 1: 0<=k<N}` consist of **N distinct residues**. Thus the all-length `theta_k` cannot be compressed into a constant-size finite list of *exactly equal* nearest-grid offsets for arbitrary odd `N`.

This does **not** rule out symbolic computation, approximating the offsets, or another nontrivial source of shared work. It closes only the simplistic exact-offset caching shortcut.

## 8. The sharply defined next construction gate

The next claim that would advance the *project's own* path, rather than re-run previous bounded-rank frame searches, is one of the following:

1. **Green cycle operator gate:** Provide an explicit uniformly generated, stable, arbitrary-vector `H_K` executor in `O(K)` total operations with fully charged preparation. It cannot be a single fixed-bandwidth sparse inverse plus `O(1)`-rank update by (6); test independently ordered noncommuting Green sums or an entirely different structured factorization instead.
2. **Joint decoding gate:** Find a shared recurrence allowing all `R` length-`Q` gauged Green scans and their final frequency-dependent Chebyshev combination to be computed together in `O(N log log N)` or `O(N)` work, not `O(RN)`. Any exact coefficients/rounding/covariance have to remain valid.
3. **Exact general FFT gate:** Ultimately bypass the approximation contract and prove an arbitrary fresh exact DFT algorithm with `O(N)` or `O(N log log N)` uniformly charged operations, for all lengths and including a provable cost for coefficient generation; compare in the **same model** as the latest known exact-complex-arithmetic bounds.

For all gates: a passing small-`K` identity is necessary evidence, not proof of the asymptotic executor; keep independent random inputs, worst-case tests, an explicit recurrence/circuit bound, and bit/numerical precision distinct.

## 9. Reproducibility and how to read the tests

`cycle_bridge.py` executes:
- Finite-field exact `A_K` entry identities for `K=8,16,32,64` (`5440` exact matrix entries).
- Cauchy full-rank checks for disjoint minors and row-sparse/low-rank obstruction spot checks on random sparse matrices.
- Seeded random-complex Bessel channel recurrence comparisons with NumPy's FFT oracle for `K=8,16,32,64,128`.
- High-precision direct Fourier/Bessel matrix verification via `mpmath`.
- Exact odd-grid residue-count checks for seven odd sizes.

Run `python cycle_bridge.py` after `python -m pip install -r requirements.txt`. Results are saved to `results.json` and are reproducible from a fixed random seed. The numerical oracle *does not* implement the desired O(N) algorithm and makes no performance claim against optimized FFT libraries.

**Final research status:** We have **a real all-input Bessel-channel operator cycle**, **a Cauchy rank obstruction for an overly simple Green realization**, and **two precisely isolated remaining high-work steps**. That is actionable progress; **an unconditional asymptotic FFT breakthrough remains open**.
