# Exact fusion of noncommuting q-Weyl corrections

9 October 2026. This package supplies exact reusable fusion primitives for
the actual boundary-corrected q-Weyl operator C. It demonstrates that many
rank-one corrections can combine into a **full-rank, O(N)-work map** when
their weights have a short Fourier description. It also implements a more
general nested-state fusion, without materializing a full output per term.

These are restricted operator families, not an exact sub-N-log-N Fourier
transform. Classical Fourier orthogonality, pruned FFTs, transposition, and
the previously established q-Weyl similarity provide the ingredients. The
contribution here is their checked combination into explicit operators and
fully charged evaluators in this project; no mathematical priority is claimed.

## Common exact model

Let N=2^L>=2 and supply a primitive Nth root q in a field of characteristic
not dividing N. Define

\[
f_j=\prod_{a=1}^j(1-q^a),\quad t_j=1/f_j,\quad
d_j=(-1)^j q^{j(j-1)/2}/f_j,\quad
T=H_q(A),\quad Q=\operatorname{diag}(q^i),
\]

where A is the lower shift. All t_j,d_j used here are nonzero. T is lower
Toeplitz with kernel t and T^-1 has kernel d. The earlier audited operator
has C=TBT^-1, B_(i,i+1)=q-q^(-i), including the cyclic corner in C. Put
g_j=product_(a=0)^(j-1)(q-q^(-a)); then g_j^-1=d_j and g_(N-1)=N/q.

Only the supplied Nth root is required by any implemented route. Root
construction and exact field representation have their own bit costs, outside
the unit field-operation model. Every implemented preparation, constant
inversion, online field operation, and full-output write is accounted for.
No floating-point accuracy or native wall-clock improvement is claimed.
The `full_output_writes` metadata counts writes to the final returned vector;
temporary zero initialization, leaf arrays, copies and intermediate writes
also occur and are included in the stated word-work and storage bounds.

## Rank-one orbit: a fused map of any rank up to N

The highest power has the outer-product form

\[
C^{N-1}=a b^T,\qquad a_i=t_i,\qquad
b_k=g_{N-1}d_{N-1-k}.
\]

For supplied coefficients c_0,...,c_(s-1), 1<=s<=N, consider

\[
M_c=\sum_{j=0}^{s-1}c_j Q^j C^{N-1}Q^j.
\]

The order is intentional: there is Q^j on both sides, not its inverse.
Q and C do not commute. With F_(i,k)=q^(ik), the exact identity is

\[
M_c=\operatorname{diag}(a)\,
F[:,0:s]\operatorname{diag}(c)F[0:s,:]\,
\operatorname{diag}(b).
\]

Indeed its (i,k) entry is a_i b_k sum_j c_j q^(j(i+k)). The two partial
Fourier maps are transposes, not conjugate transposes, and neither has an
inverse-transform normalization. Equivalently pad c by zeros to length N
and use diag(a) F diag(c) F diag(b). Because the diagonal scalings and F
are invertible, the rank is exactly the number of nonzero c_j. Thus the
family does not remain trapped in a common rank-one range.

`rank_one_orbit.py` computes the first R Fourier coefficients of b*x,
where R is the least power of two >=s, multiplies the first s by c, sets
the other R-s to zero, and applies the transposed pruned graph. The final
diagonal a produces all N outputs. For the partial transform, recurse on
even and odd inputs until length R, use N/R ordinary R-point FFTs, then
combine only the first R frequencies. The transposed graph reverses these
combination operations and interleaves the leaf outputs. Its leaf transforms
are again F_R because F_R is symmetric.

The total cost is **O(N(1+log s)) including O(N) preparation**, with O(N)
live storage. At s=N it is exactly the ordinary two-FFT implementation of
this full-rank family. It does not improve a full arbitrary Fourier transform.

### Short Fourier descriptions remove both transforms

Suppose instead the input description of all N weights is a list of t pairs
(a,gamma_a), meaning

\[
c_j=\sum_{a\in S}\gamma_a q^{aj}\quad(0\le j<N).
\]

The list is supplied explicitly. Its t entries are read, checked for integer
mode indices, and stored; no free FFT or N-entry weight table is assumed.
Repeated mode indices are allowed, and their redundant work remains charged.
The word-index bound uses O(log N)-bit mode indices; parsing or reducing
arbitrarily large supplied integers has its separate bit cost. Discovering
a short description from an arbitrary N-entry weight vector is not included
or assumed to be free.
Root orthogonality gives

\[
\boxed{(M_c x)_i=N a_i\sum_{a\in S}
\gamma_a b_{(-i-a)\bmod N}x_{(-i-a)\bmod N}.}
\]

This is a sum of t weighted antidiagonal cyclic permutations, evaluated
coordinate by coordinate in **O(N+tN+t)** total work and O(N+t) storage.
It uses no transforms and materializes only the final N-vector. For one
mode, preparation absorbs the complete permutation weights and the online
map takes exactly N field multiplications.

The uniform case c_j=1 is

\[
\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}Q^j C^{N-1}Q^j
=N\operatorname{diag}(a)R_-\operatorname{diag}(b),
\qquad (R_-x)_i=x_{-i\bmod N}.
\]

All factors are invertible, so this is full rank despite being a sum of N
rank-one terms. Two- and three-mode examples are also full rank whenever
all described c_j are nonzero. The audit explicitly checks this for
c_j=1+3q^j and c_j=1+3q^j+9q^(2j) in both test fields and cyclotomic fields.
The claim is conditional in arbitrary other fields: coefficient cancellation
can reduce rank, without affecting correctness of the evaluator.

### Exact rank-one route counts

Let R=2^ceil(log2 s) and ell=log2 R. Preparation for the pruned route uses
8N+L-4 multiplications, N-1 subtractions, N-1 negations, and one inversion
of the constant 2. This includes full earlier q-factorial preparation,
a duplicated root table, and all outer-product coefficients.

| Route | Online multiplications | Online additions | Online subtractions |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Pruned orbit with s supplied weights | N ell+4N-2R+s | N ell+N-R | N ell |
| One described Fourier mode | N | 0 | 0 |
| t>=2 described Fourier modes | (t+2)N | (t-1)N | 0 |

The one-mode preparation adds 3N multiplications to the common preparation;
the t>=2 mode preparation adds N. Copying the mode description costs O(t)
word operations but no field arithmetic. The factorized t-mode route does
not store tN coefficient matrices. Input scaling, permutation addressing,
accumulation, and all N output writes are included in its O(tN) work.

For N=64, the counted pruned online totals are 318, 508, 695, 1216, and
1344 field operations for s=1,2,3,32,64 respectively. The full-rank one-,
two-, and three-mode maps use 64, 320, and 448 online field operations,
with preparation totals 833, 705, and 705 respectively. These totals count
each field-operation type as one for reporting; they are not runtime
benchmarks or comparisons between different target operators.

## General nested-state fusion

`fused_modulated.py` implements the larger family

\[
\mathcal M x=\sum_{j=0}^{s-1}Q^j L_jQ^j x,
\qquad L_j=C^{N-r}p_j(C),\quad \deg p_j<r.
\]

r is a power of two and m=next_power_of_two(r+s-1) must satisfy m<=N/2.
Unlike unmodulated polynomial tails, these terms do not merely collapse
by adding their polynomial coefficients. The useful existing conjugacy is

\[
D=T^{-1}QT=(I-A)Q,\qquad
(Dz)_i=q^i z_i-q^{i-1}z_{i-1}.
\]

It follows directly from Q T=(I-A)T Q, which is the finite q-difference
identity with A^N=0. Every term is T D^j times a compact head map times
D^j T^-1. The implementation performs:

1. One projected inverse convolution to obtain the last m coordinates of
   T^-1 x, using the previously audited window-spectrum bank.
2. At step j, take the last r coordinates of this shrinking suffix. Scale
   by g_(N-r+k), apply the upper triangular Toeplitz kernel p_j, and scale
   by d_i, producing an r-entry head u_j. Advance the suffix under D and
   discard its first entry; all retained values have known predecessors.
3. Combine the heads by reverse Horner, u_0+D(u_1+D(...)). Each D pass
   increases the head support by only one. The combined head has length
   r+s-1, rather than s separately materialized N-vectors.
4. Decode this one combined head through T using the shared window bank,
   returning all N outputs.

The input suffix is long enough because each D update consumes only one
extra predecessor. The decoded prefix is long enough because D is lower
bidiagonal. The result has rank at most r+s-1; this is a constructive
common-state bound, not a rank assumption that justifies discarding data.

This route costs O(N log(m+1)+s[m+r log(r+1)]) online. Cold preparation is
O(N log(m+1)+sr log(r+1)); storage is O(N+sr), including all stored heads
and prepared small kernels. The specialized rank-one route above improves
the s*m portion using classical partial Fourier evaluation.

### General route ledger and same-target comparator

Put ell=log2(2r), h=log2(2m),

\[
V=2(N+m)h+2sr\ell,\quad
I=(s-1)(m-1)-(s-1)(s-2)/2,\quad
O=(s-1)r+(s-1)(s-2)/2.
\]

The exact online counts are:

- multiplications: V+6N+2m+6sr+2(I+O);
- additions: V+2N-2m+(s-1)r;
- subtractions: V+I+O-(s-1);
- negations: s-1.

Cold preparation is the previous tail preparation at window m, plus
N+sr*ell multiplications, log2(m/r)+sr*ell additions and sr*ell
subtractions. The base's unused zero-polynomial spectrum is conservatively
included. There are no additional inversions. All word-index, padding,
copying, and output traffic obey the stated bounds. Metadata describes
logical array operations, not every Python allocation or interpreter action.

The independent comparator computes the same modulated aggregate one
complete tail at a time, while sharing its constant window banks across
terms. If P_r and A_r denote the earlier tail preparation/online ledgers,
its preparation is P_r plus N-1+(s-1)r*ell multiplications and
(s-1)r*ell each of additions/subtractions. Online it is s*A_r plus
3N(s-1) multiplications and N(s-1) additions, paying for input modulation,
output modulation and accumulating every complete term.

For N=64,r=4,s=4, the fused route takes 5281 field operations including
cold preparation, versus 10161 for that same-target independent comparator.
This demonstrates a charged reuse improvement. It is not evidence of
improvement over a best general Fourier algorithm.

## Why this still does not compute H_q(C)

The scalar-weighted rank-one orbit family has the rigid entry form

\[
(M_c)_{i,k}=a_i b_k h_{(i+k)\bmod N}.
\]

For the actual H=H_q(C), put P=diag(f_i) and
Gamma_j=(-1)^j q^(j(j-1)/2). The previously proved identity
H=P^-1 F Gamma^-1 T^-1 gives

\[
H_{i,N-1}=\Gamma_{N-1}^{-1}t_i q^{-i}.
\]

Its last column therefore forces h to one Fourier character if H belonged
to the orbit family. That would force rank one, contradicting the
invertibility of H for N>=2. Thus **even arbitrary N weights cannot make
this scalar-weighted orbit family equal H**.

The identity-base loophole is also excluded for N>=4: H-alpha*I is outside
this orbit family for every scalar alpha. The last column fixes h away
from one cyclic index. The next-last normalized column is

\[
A q^{-2i}+B q^{-i},\quad
A=\frac{\Gamma_{N-2}^{-1}}{g_{N-1}d_1}\ne0,\quad
B=\frac{\Gamma_{N-1}^{-1}}{g_{N-1}}.
\]

At i=1 and i=N-1, neither the changed diagonal nor the exceptional cyclic
index participates. Matching h would require A q^(-i)=B(q-1) for two
distinct roots q^(-i), which is impossible. N=2 is excluded from this
stronger statement; H-I does belong to the family there.

This is a precise closure test for one chosen family, not an obstruction
to all graph fusion, varying correction kernels, a different base map, or
other modulations. Indeed the full-rank O(N) mode examples explicitly show
that a count of rank-one corrections alone cannot impose N separate
full-vector passes. Recovering the complete H still requires a larger
family with a comparably cheap shared evaluator.

## Verification and prior work

Run `python run_audit.py` and `python run_orbit_audit.py` in this directory.
The reports in `results/` record:

- generic fusion: 76 configurations, 2134 full basis vectors and 76 mixed
  vectors, checked against sparse-C Horner and the independent full-output
  tail comparator;
- pruned rank-one fusion: 63 configurations, 1300 full basis vectors and
  63 mixed vectors, with both partial-transform orientations independently
  checked and the outer product compared against actual C^(N-1);
- described-mode fusion: 60 configurations, 1064 full basis vectors and
  60 mixed vectors, including exact verification that every displayed
  two-/three-mode example is full rank;
- agreement with every preparation and online count formula.

Tests cover primes 65537 and 998244353 at N=2,4,...,64, plus exact
cyclotomic fields at N=2,4,8. Independent root-order checks are in the
audit; preparation treats root validity as a supplied precondition. Mode
index validation rejects fractional and boolean indices. The finite tests
check the programs; the identities above establish their all-N contracts.

Pruned Fourier evaluation and its dual are established techniques. The
primary [FFTW discussion of pruned FFTs](https://fftw.org/pruned.html)
describes O(N log K) evaluation of the first K outputs and the reversed
steps for K nonzero inputs. It cites H. V. Sorensen and C. S. Burrus,
*Efficient computation of the DFT with only a subset of input or output
points*, IEEE Transactions on Signal Processing 41(3), 1184-1200 (1993).
The permutation formula is a direct application of classical roots-of-unity
orthogonality. The nested-state route reuses the previous q-factorial and
Toeplitz-window primitives; it does not treat their preparation as free.
