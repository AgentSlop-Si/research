# Independent review of the active-bank certificates

Date: 2026-10-09 UTC. Full constructor proofs are preserved in `constructor/`. This note records independent mathematical checks and their scope.

## Spectral certificate and normalization

For `F_N=P B_J Q`, put `C0=PQ`, `K=C0^{-1}F_N`, and `a=m|J|`. The active bank uses unnormalized `F_m`; inactive blocks are identity. Then `K=Q^{-1}B_J Q`. Since `F_m²=m J_m` and `F_m⁴=m² I`,

\[
\Pi=(K^4-I)/(m^2-1)
\]

is conjugate to the active coordinate selector whenever `m²-1!=0` in the field. It is idempotent of rank a, and `(K-I)(I-Pi)=0`. The guard also makes `F_m-I` invertible, proving `rank(F_N-C0)=a`. For even m, `tr K²=N+a` and `tr K⁴=N+(m²-1)a`; in positive characteristic these traces are field residues, not integer rank encodings.

The certificate is necessary only. Our exact control over `F_17`, with `m=2` and `K=diag(6,7,1,1)`, passes the rank-two projector and inactive-complement conditions, but `tr K²=2`, whereas the actual one-block F2 bank requires 6. Thus the projector alone does not even enforce the Fourier eigenvalue profile. The determinant pencil in the constructor report retains all multiplicities but still does not produce an admissible paired-Green P or Q.

Normalized Fourier blocks instead satisfy fourth power identity, so the selector contrast disappears. An exact normalized F2 control verifies this. Absorbing bank normalization into P would change C0 and cannot be done while treating the old no-bank product as fixed. If `m²=1` in the field the displayed projector is undefined; no characteristic-zero rank conclusion is transferred through that singular denominator.

## Scalar no-bank products

The scalar-C0 impossibility is correct in characteristic zero for proper dyadic block size `m<N`. If `C0=cI`, `K⁴=N²c^{-4}I` is scalar, excluding a mixed bank because its fourth power has two distinct eigenvalues. An all-active bank forces `c⁴=(N/m)²`, while `tr K²=2Nc^{-2}=2N` forces `c²=1`, contradicting `N/m>1`. An all-inactive bank would require the Fourier matrix itself to be scalar. The conclusion is limited to this scalar no-bank subclass; arbitrary C0 remains open.

## Active-set separator

For output rows I, input columns J0, and bank interface sets A,C, split the product into its principal interface term and two exterior terms. The latter have ranks at most `rank P[I,A^c]` and `rank Q[C^c,J0]`. An inactive bank block contributes `h_b=|A_b intersect C_b|`; an active block contributes at most `min(|A_b|,|C_b|)`. Therefore the constructor's weighted covering inequality is valid with weight

`w_b=min(|A_b|,|C_b|)-h_b`.

Each weight is between zero and `m/2`. A full-rank Fourier target block with width W therefore forces `a>=2(W-r_P-r_Q-sum h_b)`. In particular, a balanced disjoint interface with sublinear exterior leakage is inconsistent with a fixed inactive fraction. This result does not exclude full activation or linear-leakage routes.

## Bank-boundary expansion

With endpoints preserving each bank block, every bank-prefix cut is preserved by the endpoints. The inverse complementary-rank identity bounds each Green cross block by its number of incoming cycle edges. Rank subadditivity applies to sums and products, while the bank contributes zero across its boundary. Hence the sum of the eight incoming edge counts must be at least `min(tm,N-tm)`.

Summing over bank boundaries is correct: each full directed cycle has equal numbers entering and leaving each prefix cut, so twice its incoming-cut sum is its total absolute displacement of bank labels. For even q the target lower sums to `m q²/4`, giving total displacement at least `Nq/2`, and average displacement at least `q/16` over eight cycles. Arbitrary endpoint permutations can supply their own routing and are excluded from this particular corollary.

The stronger alternating-bank restriction for natural and bit-reversed natural cycles is independently proved in `STRIPED_CUT_REVIEW.md`.

## Inverse erasure and complete Weyl certificate

The inverse-erasure inequality is valid: if `H_i^{-1}=A_i+E_i`, the row support of the all-A product is at most `d=product s_i`, and telescoping costs rank at most `a+sum r_i`, including the erased bank. To prove the Fourier-versus-row-sparse bound directly, take `floor(N/(d+1))` consecutive rows. Their sparse supports cover at most d times this many columns, leaving at least that many unused columns. The resulting Fourier block is square Vandermonde and full rank. This yields the stated lower bound without a principal-pivot assumption.

The bank-aware Weyl equations are also complete when combined with the seed. Set `A=P^{-1}DP` and `T=QSQ^{-1}`. Then `A B_J=B_J T` is equivalent to `D(PB_JQ)=(PB_JQ)S`. Its columns obey `M_(j+1)=D M_j`; adding `M e0=ones` forces every Fourier entry. Without the seed, covariance permits arbitrary row scaling, so covariance alone is insufficient. The block equations and Boolean active selectors must share the same P,Q and cannot be solved independently block by block.

These identities define exact rejection/verification targets. Computing dense inverses, projectors or determinant pencils is not a supplied linear-time parameter generator. The independently authored checker tests abstract bank similarities and an explicit false positive; those targets are not represented as new paired-Green Fourier constructions.
