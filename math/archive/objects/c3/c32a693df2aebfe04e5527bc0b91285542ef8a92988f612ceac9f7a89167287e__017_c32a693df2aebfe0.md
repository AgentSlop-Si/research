# Two-round symbolic sharing: exact negative result

Research pass: 8 October 2026

## Result

The tested sharing idea does not improve ordinary two-round synthetic butterflies. For the smallest instance (length t = 4, polynomial degree r = 2), exhaustive enumeration of 6,561 pair partitions finds one optimal pair-partition assignment: 16 additions/subtractions, exactly the staged baseline. Ten exact configurations, covering 8 to 256 real scalar inputs, give the same conclusion. This is not a uniqueness claim about all optimal circuits.

There is also a uniform, restricted lower-bound argument: two genuine consecutive butterfly layers, with any signed permutation between them and disjoint first-layer pairs meeting in each second-layer butterfly, require at least 2N additions in the cancellation-free signed-sum grammar defined below. The ordinary layers attain 2N. This is not a lower bound for general linear circuits, Fourier transforms, or algorithms allowing cancellation and more general coefficients.

No improved recursive exponent, novel FFT algorithm, or evidence that a breakthrough is close follows from this experiment.

## Exact object and baseline

Work over R_r = Q[y]/(y^r + 1); the same coefficient identity extends to real or complex inputs. Multiplication by a power of y is a signed coefficient permutation. The normalized butterfly maps (a,b) to ((a+b)/2,(a-b)/2). Two consecutive decimation-in-frequency rounds are expanded on all N = tr scalar coefficients. The imaginary components of complex inputs are independent identical copies, so scalar counts double for complex coefficients.

The source specification is the pinned OpenAI transform construction [1]. The implementation checks its entire untruncated transform against the defining Fourier sum, including bit-reversed stored order. Each two-round fragment is then checked as an exact linear operator, with normalization 1/4.

The baseline is the ordinary staged butterfly circuit, with N first-layer and N second-layer additions. Both compared exact circuits defer their common normalization to the output; this prevents counting deferred scaling as a sharing improvement. The dense 3N-addition expansion is not the baseline.

The local grammar permits binary additions/subtractions with disjoint input supports, fanout and sharing of equal signed sums, free global sign equivalence for the lower bound, and a final common 1/4 scaling. It permits arbitrary pairing and arbitrary depth of useful subexpressions. It excludes cancellation, intermediate coefficients other than +/-1, nonlinear operations, lookup tables, and transformations of the frame compiler.

## Why the restricted lower bound holds

Every output has four distinct input coordinates with coefficients +/-1 before normalization. The unnormalized transform rows are mutually orthogonal because the layers are Hadamard butterflies and signed permutations.

1. A useful two-input signed sum occurs in at most two outputs. If both inputs come from one original butterfly pair, that relative-sign form is one first-layer value, which feeds one second-layer butterfly and hence two outputs. If they come from two original pairs A and B, every containing output has exactly support A union B. Normalize the selected two signs to a fixed orientation. Pairwise orthogonality would require the remaining two signs of every two such rows to be opposite. At most two rows can satisfy that condition.
2. A useful three-input signed sum occurs in at most one output. Any two containing outputs would have the same two original pairs and the same four-input support. Agreement on three signs leaves their dot product nonzero, even after global sign normalization.
3. Final outputs are distinct up to sign by orthogonality. A cancellation-free four-leaf binary tree has three additions. Across N outputs, only pair nodes can share, and each can be used at most twice. Equivalently, if k outputs use a three-plus-one split, at least k triple nodes and ceil((2N-k)/2) pair nodes are required, in addition to N final nodes. Their sum is at least 2N.

All support and sign hypotheses are checked exhaustively for every tested matrix. The proof applies uniformly to the stated two-layer topology; the finite tests do not establish global optimality outside this grammar. The proof is an elementary argument supplied with this experiment, with no priority or publication-novelty claim.

## Finite results

The tested (t, r, starting stage) configurations are:

- (4,2,0), (4,4,0), (4,8,0)
- (8,4,0), (8,4,1), (8,8,0)
- (16,8,0), (16,8,1), (16,8,2), (16,16,0)

The respective baseline/optimal addition counts are 16, 32, 64, 64, 64, 128, 256, 256, 256, and 512. Every case has maximum pair incidence two and maximum triple incidence one.

The smallest case enumerates all three partitions into pairs for each of eight outputs, hence 3^8 = 6,561 assignments. Counts of assignments by number of distinct pair nodes are: 8:1, 10:32, 12:384, 14:2,048, 16:4,096. Eight final output additions are charged separately. The sole optimal pair partition reproduces the original first-layer pairing. Larger cases use the exact incidence certificate rather than falsely claiming an exhaustive general circuit search.

## Dirty scratch: emitted word, not an assumption

The cancellation-free DAG is lifted into an invertible rational mixer L, with one physical role per outgoing edge except the first-input/output pivot identification at each addition. It has c+q = 3N scratch roles. Additive copies and signed shears operate on arbitrary initial scratch; no used scratch is assumed zero.

With V injecting the source into input roles and J extracting output roles, the complete chronological word is:

L; subtract J/4 from target; L inverse; add V source; L; add J/4 to target; L inverse; subtract V source.

Its exact map is (x, target, scratch) -> (x, target + Mx/4, scratch), where M is the unnormalized two-round matrix. The full emitted word is replayed on every symbolic source, target and dirty-scratch basis column. This is an additive-output implementation, not the source framework's signed-role permutation interface. This is the standard transparent-computation idea related to the already published shared-computation construction [2], not a new restoration technique.

For the smallest instance, both baseline and candidate have:

- 8 real inputs and 8 target accumulators
- 24 scratch roles; full 40-by-40 state operator checked
- 32 mixer shears; four mixer traversals
- 160 total shears, including source copies, injections and all cleanup
- 16 quarter-scaled target updates, each charging a dyadic shift as well as an addition; no general scalar multiplications
- 320 scalar operand reads and 160 destination writes for those shears
- 160 instruction fetches, each carrying operation/address data

Across the suite these counts scale as 3N scratch, 5N total roles and 20N shears. No separate negation gates occur in the emitted tested words. Signs are carried in signed shears. This lifting is a verified implementation and comparison convention, not a claim of optimal dirty-workspace compilation.

## Routing, preparation and precision

In the ordinary read-only-input arithmetic DAG, 2N additions entail 4N operand reads and 2N result writes; final scaling adds N reads and N writes. Thus the common charged arithmetic/scaling traffic is 8N scalar accesses. Signed twiddles are compiled into operand references for both competitors. A separately materialized twiddle would incur one coefficient read and one write per moved coefficient; exact per-case counts are retained in summary.json. The smallest instance has one nonidentity two-coefficient record twiddle, adding two reads and two writes if materialized.

The lifted implementation charges every data shear and all restoration, but uses random-access register addressing. Each address requires O(log N) bits and each emitted instruction must be read. Circuit generation, exhaustive search, certificate writing, and descriptor construction are preparation work; none is advertised as free or as a uniform fast transform algorithm. Fixed-tape layout conversion, scans, routing and source-framework descriptors are not implemented. No fixed-tape speedup is inferred from these access counts.

Exact dyadic evaluation of two normalized rounds needs two additional fractional bits when input components lie on the 2^-p grid. The replay also records the maximum induced row l1 norm over every intermediate dirty-lift state: 14 in all ten cases, with coefficient denominators at most four. If all initial source, target and scratch components have magnitude at most B, every intermediate component has magnitude at most 14B. This gives an explicit local precision bound; unrestricted arbitrary scratch still has exact restoration, but its storage cost depends on its input magnitude and precision. No rounding occurs during the full-state proof.

## Rounded interfaces are different

Exact matrix equality cannot justify moving truncation across a layer. At p = 1, take real constant-polynomial inputs (1, 1/2, 1/2, 0), with other coefficients zero. Each input lies in the unit disk. Two rounds with toward-zero truncation to the half-integer grid at each boundary return all zeros. Evaluating both rounds exactly and truncating once returns DC coefficient 1/2.

Thus Q_p(L2 Q_p(L1 x)) differs from Q_p(L2 L1 x). A fused algorithm could still be a valid approximation with a fresh error proof, but would not be bit-for-bit identical to the rounded interface. Each exact normalized synthetic round is contractive, so a once-rounded two-round map has the elementary one-truncation error bound; the staged implementation has two possible truncation contributions. This local observation does not establish compatibility with the full recursive precision argument.

## Why no recurrence claim is available

The CrocSwap recursive certificate is based on physical roles and frame-edge child ranks, not the number of scalar additions. In weighted form, for dimension m, role count W and n_t children of rank t, a proposed saving a requires the strict inequality

Psi(a) = sum_t (n_t/W) (t/m)^(1-a) < 1.

This experiment supplies neither the rational projector/common-frame assignments, all-role endpoint identities nor a resulting rank histogram n_t for its dirty lift. Consequently Psi is not defined for this candidate and no source moment test was evaluated; it would be incorrect to report that the moment test failed. It also does not supply the source-framework tape-layout, uniform compilation, complete precision or final assembly obligations. Those are the precise missing interface. The scalar network reproduces its baseline cost anyway; there is no candidate improvement to amplify.

## Reproduce

Requirements: Python 3.11 or later; standard library only. No external repository, package installation, network, randomized test, or numerical tolerance is needed.

Run from this directory:

    python3 experiment.py
    python3 verify_results.py
    python3 -m unittest -v test_experiment.py

The experiment regenerates results/summary.json and ten full-state certificates. verify_results.py is an independently written emitted-word and incidence replayer and does not import experiment.py. Seven unit tests include deliberate missing-cleanup and wrong-injection-sign faults, forbidden cancellation, and the rounded counterexample. All checks passed in this pass. These are exact arithmetic and finite proof checks, not a formally verified theorem prover or reproduction of the complete upstream framework.
