# Inverse support closes the internal-shuffle scan question

9 October 2026. This extends the newly supplied
`fft_wide_global_scan_rank_research_2026-10-09(2).zip` within its precise
ordinary scan architecture. The earlier cut-rank invariant could be defeated
by arbitrary intermediate permutations. Inverse support still gives a
logarithmic pass lower bound under every such permutation.

**Main result.** For dyadic N>=2, any exact factorization of F_N into ordinary
one-state weighted forward/backward scans, diagonal matrices, and arbitrary
permutations anywhere in the word needs at least `1+log2(N)` scan factors.
An explicit construction here uses `2log2(N)` scans. Thus the optimal number
of scans in this architecture is Theta(log N), even with unrestricted internal
shuffles and unbounded complex coefficients.

This is an elementary inverse-support/fan-in argument, not a novelty claim
for sparse matrix products. It is **not a general FFT arithmetic lower bound**.
The construction is the ordinary radix-two FFT expressed in this architecture,
not a new faster algorithm. Its purpose is to match the lower bound in order
and make the full computational model checkable.

## The inverse-support proof

An ordinary forward scan has

    y_0=b_0*x_0,
    y_i=a_i*y_(i-1)+b_i*x_i,  1<=i<N.

Its determinant is the product of the b_i. Since the target Fourier matrix
is invertible and all factors are square N-by-N matrices, every factor in an
exact product must be invertible. In particular all b_i and all diagonal
scalings are nonzero. Solving for the input gives

    x_0=y_0/b_0,
    x_i=(y_i-a_i*y_(i-1))/b_i.

The scan inverse therefore has at most two nonzeros per row, and its first
row has exactly one. A backward scan has the analogous upper-bidiagonal
inverse, with a one-supported last row. Zero a_i do not invalidate the bound.

Let rho(A) be the maximum number of nonzeros in one row. For every pair of
compatible matrices,

    rho(A B) <= rho(A)*rho(B).

Each product row is a combination of at most rho(A) rows of B, each supported
on at most rho(B) columns. Cancellations can only decrease the union support.
Permutation and invertible diagonal factors have rho=1. Thus the inverse of
any word containing t scans has maximum row support at most 2^t, regardless
of all intermediate layouts.

Every entry of the Fourier inverse is nonzero:

    F_N^(-1)[j,k] = q^(-jk)/N.

It follows immediately that t>=ceil(log2 N). For ordinary, noncyclic scans
one can sharpen this by one: the leftmost scan inverse in the inverse word
has a one-supported boundary row. Its preceding inverse permutation/diagonal
factors only scale and reorder that row. Multiplying by the remaining t-1
scan inverses gives at least one final row with support at most 2^(t-1).
Since every Fourier-inverse row has N nonzeros,

    t >= 1+ceil(log2 N).

The maximum-support argument also applies to invertible cyclic scans whose
inverses are cyclic bidiagonal. The extra +1 does not follow for those scans,
since they need not have a one-supported boundary row.

More generally, if an invertible stage has inverse row support at most d_i,
then any such factorization of F_N satisfies `product_i d_i >= N`. Uniformly
bounded d_i prohibit o(log N) stages. This statement depends on inverse row
support, not on forward density, coefficient magnitudes or a fixed index cut.

## Matching-order construction with actual elementary scans

A two-coordinate butterfly with nonzero twiddle w factors as

    [[1,w],[1,-w]] = [[1,0],[1,1]] * [[1,w],[0,-2w]].

The upper factor is a backward scan with coefficients
`b_even=1, b_odd=-2w, a_even=-1/2`, resetting the recurrence between pairs.
The lower factor is a forward scan with `b=1, a_odd=1`, again resetting
between pairs. All scalar coefficients depend only on the supplied root/N.

After the ordinary bit-reversal permutation, route each radix-two layer's
paired coordinates next to one another, apply the backward and forward
scans, and scatter back. This gives two literal full-array scans per layer,
hence 2log2(N) scans. Every output agrees with the positive-sign DFT.
The program generates all permutations and coefficients, reads/writes all
stage arrays, and returns every scalar output; no dense matrix application
is hidden in a named stage.

The deliberately literal scalar loop charges even zero/identity scales.
For N>=2 it uses exactly

    N+3 + log2(N)*(13N/2-4)

field operations. This includes root preparation/validation, the constants
-2 and -1/2, per-pair twiddle coefficient generation, and both scans.
Per layer there are 2N routing copies, 2N scan-result writes and 4N scan
coefficient entries. All are O(N) additional word work. The asymptotic cost
is Theta(N log N), and the constants are worse than ordinary butterflies.
Live workspace is O(N); the diagnostic summary retains only O(log N) records.

## Scope: what is and is not excluded

The result closes the o(log N)-pass possibility for **products** of the
archive's N-dimensional scans, diagonals and arbitrary internal permutations.
If each scan is executed as the specified full-array Theta(N)-work pass,
the total work is Omega(N log N). A pass-count bound alone is not a general
gate lower bound for a different implementation that skips whole degenerate
passes or combines their arithmetic in a new way.

It does not cover additive branches, independent feedthrough outputs,
larger-state scan primitives, arbitrary use of auxiliary registers, or
general O(N)-work operators with dense inverses. For example I+u*v^T can
have a dense inverse and still be applied by a dot product and a rank-one
update in O(N) work; inverse sparsity is a property that must be established
for a proposed stage, not a consequence of its linear evaluation cost.
The proof also does not constrain our adaptive, restricted-input recurrence
evaluators: those do not factor the full Fourier matrix on all fresh inputs.

## Reproduce and provenance

Run `python3 run_audit.py`, using standard-library Python. The audit checks
136 complete Fourier basis vectors, 36 mixed full outputs, 206 independent
scan/inverse matrix identities and 72 products with random internal shuffles.
It includes two prime fields and small characteristic-zero cyclotomic fields.
Every scan-factor FFT output is checked against a separately formed Fourier
matrix. Every emitted field count is matched to the loop formula above.
Finite checks corroborate the implementation; the support proof is all-size.

`exact_fields.py` is the self-contained exact arithmetic helper copied from
the earlier finite-boundary checkpoint. The attached scan-rank source is
preserved separately without modifications or rerunning its old experiments.
No source result is treated as a general N-log-N FFT lower bound.
