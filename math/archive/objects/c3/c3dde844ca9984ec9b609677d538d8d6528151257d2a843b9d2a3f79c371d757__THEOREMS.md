# HERO #0 — Phase 12: Fourier–Green obstruction by permutation order and Schur-positive minors

**Date:** 10 October 2026  
**Status:** New *project-level* exact deductions, fully written proofs, executable certificates and negative controls. **Not an all-input fast FFT.** Novelty against the complete mathematical literature is **not established**. The 1D Green/quasiseparable fact, Erdős–Szekeres theorem, Schur polynomial positivity and Chebotarëv theorem are classical.

## Computational problem and grammar

Let `F_N=(exp(2π i jk/N))_{0<=j,k<N}` be the **unnormalized** complex Fourier matrix. The normalized version has the same ranks and the results remain unchanged under multiplication by a nonzero scalar.

A **weighted-permutation Green resolvent** is

\[
G=(I-P)^{-1}, \qquad P_{i,\pi(i)}=w_i,
\]

where `pi` is **any** permutation (any number of cycles), `w_i` are arbitrary complex numbers (zero allowed), and each permutation-cycle weight product is unequal to 1, so the inverse exists. Each such `G` applies to any input in `O(N)` field operations by solving one scalar first-order cyclic recurrence per cycle, including input-independent per-cycle preparation. Invertibility is essential: a singular `I-P` has no resolvent.

We study the **one-layer** grammar

\[
F_N=\sum_{j=1}^r G_j+E+S,\qquad
\operatorname{rank}(E)\le t,\quad
\max_i|\operatorname{supp}(S_{i,:})|\le s,
\]

with fixed input-independent coefficients. All permutations, weights, orderings and the operators `E,S` are unrestricted except for their stated ranks/supports. The results **do not apply to products of Green-sum layers**, arbitrary graph operators, input-adaptive algorithms, arbitrary arithmetic circuits, the original FFT, or exact integer-multiplication bounds.

## Lemma 1 — rank-one crosscuts for arbitrary weighted permutations

For a single cycle `v_0,...,v_{h-1}`, where `P[v_i,v_{i+1 mod h}]=w_{v_i}`, set `c=prod_i w_{v_i}`. If `1-c != 0`, then

\[
G=\frac{I+P+\cdots+P^{h-1}}{1-c}.
\]

For row position `i` and column position `j`, the unique nonzero contributing power follows the forward directed path from `v_i` to `v_j`. If `i<j`, its numerator is `w[v_i] ... w[v_{j-1}]`, which factors through **any** fixed cut between the two groups. If `i>j`, the wrapped path factors into `w[v_i] ... w[v_{h-1}]` times `w[v_0] ... w[v_{j-1}]`. These factorizations work even when some weights vanish. Thus, whenever all chosen row positions precede all chosen column positions (or vice versa), the crosscut matrix is an outer product and has rank at most 1.

For an arbitrary permutation `pi`, list its disjoint cycles consecutively and order each cycle as above. The inverse is block-diagonal between distinct cycles. For **globally separated** row and column index sets, at most one cycle intersects both sides: all other cross-cycle entries are zero. Consequently the entire selected crosscut still has rank at most one.

This is precisely a permutational **two-sided quasiseparability-of-order-one** property, and it is fully independent of the numeric weights.

## Lemma 2 — common separated rectangles

Let `r` arbitrary orderings of the same set of `B` labels be given, and let `m>=2`. Repeated application of the classical Erdős–Szekeres monotone-subsequence theorem gives a common subsequence of length at least `L` which is increasing or decreasing in each ordering, provided

\[
B>(L-1)^{2^{r-1}}.
\]

Choose `L=2m` and take the first `m` labels as row set `R` and the last `m` as column set `C`. Then they are separated in **every** ordering (orientation may vary). Hence on `R x C` the sum of `r` Green resolvents has rank at most `r`; adding a rank-`t` correction gives rank at most `r+t`.

To allow an additional correction `S` with at most `s` nonzeros per row, take instead `L=m(s+2)`. Keep the first `m` row labels and `m(s+1)` tail candidates. The rows collectively forbid at most `ms` columns, so at least `m` tail candidates have `S[R,C]=0`. The same separated-crosscut rank bound holds.

The construction requires **no knowledge of the Green weights**. It is algorithmically reproducible using longest-increasing/decreasing-subsequence extraction.

## Lemma 3 — small-arc Fourier full minors, all lengths

Fix `2<=m<=B`. If

\[
\boxed{N>2(m-1)(B-m)(B-1),}
\]

then **every** `m x m` minor of the top-left `B x B` submatrix `F_N[0:B,0:B]` is nonzero over the complex numbers.

**Proof.** Take arbitrary strictly increasing row labels `a_0<...<a_{m-1}` and column labels `c_0<...<c_{m-1}` from `0,...,B-1`. Set `z_j=exp(2π i c_j/N)`. Factor the column-wise unit-modulus terms `z_j^{a_0}`. The remaining generalized Vandermonde determinant factors as

\[
\det[z_j^{a_i-a_0}]_{i,j}
=\det[z_j^i]_{i,j}\,s_\lambda(z_0,\ldots,z_{m-1}),
\]

where `s_lambda` is the Schur polynomial for the partition

\[
\lambda=(a_{m-1}-a_0-(m-1),\ldots,a_1-a_0-1,0).
\]

Its coefficients in monomial form are **nonnegative integers**, by the semistandard-tableaux formula. It is nonzero and homogeneous of degree

\[
 d=|\lambda|=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}(a_i-a_0-i)\le(m-1)(B-m).
\]

All `z_j` lie on a short arc of angular width at most `2π(B-1)/N`. Each monomial in `s_lambda` has degree exactly `d`, so the arguments of all its positive-coefficient monomial contributions fit into an arc of width at most

\[
 \Delta\le \frac{2\pi d(B-1)}N<\pi.
\]

After rotating by the midpoint of that arc, **every term has strictly positive real part**, so the Schur polynomial cannot vanish. The ordinary Vandermonde factor also cannot vanish because the `z_j` are distinct (since `N>B-1`). QED.

In the case `m=3,B=26`, the maximum degree is `d=2*(26-3)=46` and the largest column span is `25`, giving the explicit threshold

\[
\boxed{N>2\cdot46\cdot25=2300.}
\]

For three selected rows whose differences from the first are `0<a<b`, a quantitative check is

\[
 |\det(F_N[R,C])|\ \ge
 \bigl|\prod_{i<j}(z_j-z_i)\bigr|
 \frac{ab(b-a)}2\cos\!\left(\frac{\pi(a+b-3)(c_2-c_0)}{N}\right)>0
\]

under the small-arc condition. `small_arc_gate.py` checks this independent lower bound numerically at 80-digit precision; **the theorem is the exact symbolic argument, not a consequence of the tests**.

## Theorem A — one sum of two Green resolvents is impossible for all N>=2301

For every integer `N>=2301`, for every pair of invertible weighted permutation matrices `I-P_1,I-P_2`, with **arbitrary independent permutation cycle decompositions** and arbitrary complex weights,

\[
\boxed{F_N\ne(I-P_1)^{-1}+(I-P_2)^{-1}.}
\]

**Proof.** Restrict to common index set `{0,...,25}`. The two induced Green permutations define two orderings of those 26 indices. By Lemma 2 there exist 3 distinct rows and 3 distinct columns separated in both orders. Lemma 1 bounds the sum's rank on this crosscut by 2. Lemma 3 says the corresponding 3x3 Fourier submatrix has rank 3. Contradiction. QED.

The same holds with arbitrary scalar coefficients multiplying each Green resolver, and with an additional arbitrary **diagonal matrix**, because the selected row and column sets are disjoint and a diagonal correction vanishes on their crosscut.

### An independent exact certificate at N=4096

At `N=4096`, `Phi_4096(x)=x^2048+1`. For any 3x3 minor from indices `{0,...,25}`, the formal determinant `P(x)=det(x^(row_i*column_j))` is a nonzero polynomial: its highest exponent is achieved uniquely by matching the sorted rows and columns (strict rearrangement inequality). Its degree is at most `3*25^2=1875<2048=deg(Phi_4096)`. Hence it cannot vanish at a primitive 4096th root of unity. **This proves all those minors nonzero by a second, independent, purely algebraic argument** at this specific length.

The file `witness_n4096.json` gives a fully exact GF(65537) sample with both Green crosscut ranks 1, their sum rank 2, and Fourier crosscut rank 3; it is supporting evidence, not a substitute for the complex-number theorem.

## Theorem B — general finite-sum and correction budget, all sufficiently large lengths

Let `r>=1`, `t,s>=0` be integers, and define

\[
 m=r+t+1,\qquad
 B=\big(m(s+2)-1\big)^{2^{r-1}}+1,\qquad
 N_0=2(m-1)(B-m)(B-1).
\]

For **every** `N>N_0`, it is impossible to represent `F_N` as the sum of `r` arbitrary weighted-permutation Green resolvents, plus any rank-at-most-`t` matrix and any row-degree-at-most-`s` matrix.

**Proof.** Apply Lemma 2 to the first `B` coordinate labels and avoid the sparse correction's supports, extracting an `m x m` separated rectangle. The resolvent sum plus low-rank correction has crosscut rank at most `r+t=m-1`, while the sparse correction is identically zero there. By Lemma 3 the Fourier minor has rank exactly `m`. Contradiction. QED.

Selected thresholds:

| Green resolvents `r` | extra rank `t` | sparse row degree `s` | All lengths excluded once `N` exceeds |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2 | 0 | 0 | **2300** |
| 2 | 0 | 1 | **15872** |
| 2 | 1 | 0 | **13524** |
| 3 | 0 | 0 | **34,545,588** |

**Consequence for one-shot sums.** With fixed correction budgets `t,s=O(1)`, the number `r` of resolvent summands must be `Omega(log log N)` as `N->infinity`. This is a *restricted representation-complexity lower bound*, **not** a general FFT runtime lower bound: computing a sum could hypothetically share work across its terms, and products or nonpermutation graph layers evade the grammar.

## Theorem C — stronger small-length exclusions from prime factors

If `N` has **any prime divisor `p>=29`**, Theorem A already holds at that `N`, even when `N<=2300`.

Write `N=m p^v` with `gcd(m,p)=1`. Choose `a=m p^((v-1)/2)` for odd `v`, or `a=m p^(v/2-1)` for even `v`. The `p` coordinates `S={0,a,...,(p-1)a}` satisfy

\[
F_N[ia,ja]=\begin{cases}
\zeta_p^{mij},&v\ \text{odd},\\
\zeta_{p^2}^{mij},&v\ \text{even}.
\end{cases}
\]

Every 3x3 minor of these `p x p` matrices is nonzero over `C`, as follows. Normalize its three row/column labels to `[0,u,v]` and `[0,s,t]` with `u,v,s,t` distinct in the appropriate nonzero pairs modulo `p`. The normalized determinant is a 6-term sum of roots of unity,

\[
\zeta^{us+vt}+\zeta^{ut}+\zeta^{vs}
-\zeta^{us}-\zeta^{vt}-\zeta^{ut+vs},
\]

where the exponents are multiplied by the unit `m` in the actual submatrix. The positive term `ut` has no matching negative exponent modulo `p` or `p^2`: the three possible differences are nonzero products of nonzero differences modulo `p`. Thus the determinant is a **nonzero formal polynomial with at most six nonzero coefficients** reduced to exponents modulo the root order.

For `p>=7`, such a polynomial cannot be a nonzero multiple of `Phi_p=1+x+...+x^(p-1)`, since the degree-`<p` multiple would require `p` nonzero coefficients. Likewise it cannot be a multiple of `Phi_(p^2)=1+x^p+...+x^((p-1)p)`, since any degree-`<p^2` multiple is `R(x) Phi_(p^2)(x)` with `deg(R)<p`, and consequently has at least `p` nonzero coefficients. Thus the 3x3 minor is nonzero over `C`.

Restrict the two Green resolvents to `S`, inherit their cycle-concatenated orders, and use the 26-element Erdős–Szekeres construction within these `p` indices. The rank contradiction is unchanged. This proves the assertion.

Examples tested with prime divisor 29: `N=29,58,841,1682,24389,48778` (the last through exact minor checks). The root order is `29` for odd valuation and `841` for even valuation.

This prime-factor theorem is useful **below the universal 2301 threshold**, and relies on elementary cyclotomic arguments rather than the stronger all-minors Chebotarëv result. For a general sum of `r` resolvents at prime length `p`, the classical Chebotarëv theorem provides all `(r+t+1)`-minors and yields the sharper **prime-only** sufficient exclusion `p>(m(s+2)-1)^(2^(r-1))`.

## Negative control — why products remain open

Let `T1=G1+G2` and `T2=G3+G4`, with independent arbitrary weighted permutations. The one-layer crosscut rank argument for `T1` does not extend to `T1*T2`. The exact finite-field sample in `witness_product_escape.json` at `N=29` has a selected 3x3 crosscut of `T1` with rank 1, but the **same crosscut of `T1*T2` has rank 3**. This is a positive demonstration of escaping the obstruction, **not a solution of `T1*T2=F_N`**.

A Phase 13 search should focus on exact polynomial/rational compatibility for products of two independently permuted Green-sum layers, possibly through `F_N = (A^-1+B^-1)(C^-1+D^-1)`, with all setup and scalar operations charged. A precise algebraic gate is provided in `two_layer_polynomial_gate.py`:

\[
A F_N D=(A+B)(CB)^{-1}(C+D),
\]

which is equivalent (when the Green inverses exist) to the `2N x 2N` **polynomial block matrix**

\[
\begin{pmatrix}CB & C+D\\ A+B & AF_ND\end{pmatrix}
\]

having rank exactly `N`. This equivalence was checked independently against the direct four-Green product in `GF(65537)` at `N=4,8,16`. All random instances **failed** to factor the Fourier transform, as expected. This is a starting exact search condition, not evidence that a uniform factorization exists. The model and target must remain fixed: fresh arbitrary inputs, all Fourier outputs, and a complete verified family of factorizations rather than a few finite examples.
