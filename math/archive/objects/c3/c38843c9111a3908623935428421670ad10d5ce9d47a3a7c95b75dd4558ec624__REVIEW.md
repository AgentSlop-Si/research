## Material findings

1. **Automatic relation rebuild still pays for a full backlog copy.** Source S02, `relation_view.py` lines 203–209 and 417–426, creates the list slice before testing whether to rebuild. The advertised pre-scan avoidance of a long history does not hold for this implementation. Its cached basis can also alias mutable caller data (58–65), while view projections are copied (313); external mutation can silently desynchronize views from the state.
2. **Finite-element validation is insufficient for the floating surrogate guarantee.** S04, `sparse_surrogate.py` lines 124–130, classifies a nonzero vector as zero when the naive L2 calculation underflows. A fresh NumPy arithmetic check on `[1e-200,0]` gives norm zero, whereas a stable norm is `1e-200`; the resulting zero transform would violate the stated epsilon=1/2 mathematical error tolerance. Large finite values also risk overflowing norms. Explicit sample counts bypass epsilon/delta validation, and dense fallback is selected after drawing/coalescing the sample population when the sparse bypass is disabled (191–204).
3. **The reference NTT's root-power bill omits validation.** S16 lines 8–9 execute both root construction and the primitivity check, but lines 19–22 count only the first. With N=16, six power calls occur while five are reported. This does not change its asymptotic class, but the literal cold arithmetic accounting is incomplete.
4. **Affine fallback totals and stage totals disagree.** S60 lines 146–156 add fallback work to `field_operations` without adding a fallback stage. The independent driver checks the fallback total but omits the stages-sum assertion on that route (S61,125–127). S63's composed wrapper does correctly add the fallback stage.
5. **The general Bareiss helper has a dormant pivot-sign defect.** S30 lines 42–56 return +1 for the 3x3 identity with its first two rows exchanged; the determinant is -1. A permutation-form determinant and a transparent static recurrence trace establish the discrepancy without executing that source. Its specific positive moment-matrix cases need no row swap, so this does not invalidate those saved cases.
6. **Several claimed/public guards are narrower than they sound.** S23's header says complete finite differences, but lines 74–89 sample at most 20/30 coordinates. Its int64 dot bound is documented rather than enforced. S49 uses uint32_t shifts despite unchecked n, so n>=32 is outside a safe supported domain. S83 stores the three-times derivative-cost test as a boolean but prints unconditional success; the archived flags happen to all be true.
7. **The spectral verifier's generic overflow checks need strengthening, but the actual witness is sound.** S53 guards individual dot products, while its guard's own absolute-value/sum operations and later absolute row sums require separate protection for arbitrary malformed certificates. Independent Python-integer reconstruction of the actual saved witness confirms all 32,896 upper-triangle entries, minimum diagonal 16,706,411 and minimum strict dominance margin 4,681,152. The stronger absolute-row-sum bound is 353,260,058,664,960, safely below 2^63. This is an API-hardening issue, not a rejection of the saved N=256 spectral certificate.

These are static findings or clearly labeled fresh independent checks. No current pass of any uploaded test suite is claimed.

## Mathematical and model boundaries retained

- **Known relations and selected bins:** dynamic/projection views assume a supplied basis relation and cached or prepared spectral projections. Registration, relation discovery, old spectra and full-output materialization cannot be silently omitted from an arbitrary-input transform comparison.
- **Approximation versus exactness:** importance sampling, frequency-shift medians, global-chirp medians and bounded-word decoder integration have global-additive probabilistic error. Epsilon times L1, L1/sqrt(N), or L2 is not relative coefficient error or epsilon times L2/sqrt(N). Growing collisions can worsen error at the natural coefficient scale. Fixed seeds and a few empirical trials do not establish universal probability statements.
- **Restricted exact fast paths:** affine-cycle and composed low-defect evaluators can accept dense structured inputs, but require fixed small defect/filter budgets; supplied continuous parameters are not discovered for free. Their fallback remains an ordinary FFT plus the attempted recognition bill. Exact field-operation counts do not include the representation/bit cost of arbitrary cyclotomic numbers.
- **Generic dimension and graph capacity:** nonzero Jacobian minors establish generic dominance/local dimension in a separated-generator model, not realizability of the special Fourier matrix. The N=256 expander certificate defeats one moving-partition rank test, not every network cut or coefficient constraint. Local H16 topology enumeration is one deletion plus at most two rewires, not all 31-call schedules.
- **Rank lifting:** nonzero modular cyclotomic minors give characteristic-zero lower bounds. Vanishing modular determinants alone do not give complex upper bounds; explicit kernels or separate exact arguments are needed. Small cyclotomic controls and analytic identities must remain separately attributed.
- **Nonlinear reductions:** unitary entropy, smooth width bottlenecks, Boolean feature banks, degree-bounded gauges and tangent extraction each have their own assumptions. Boolean-only correctness and equality merely as a finite-field function do not imply the global polynomial identity required by derivative arguments; S85 contains the relevant counterexamples.
- **Costs:** grouped macros, fixed kernels, packed integers and lookup tables cannot be charged as unit work without their stated model. Direct q-Pascal evaluation remains quadratic. One big-integer multiply has long operands. Eager cold table cost, output writing/rescaling, root preparation, sampler work and control/index overhead remain part of end-to-end accounting. Lazy tables are expressly not cold-asymptotic evidence.
