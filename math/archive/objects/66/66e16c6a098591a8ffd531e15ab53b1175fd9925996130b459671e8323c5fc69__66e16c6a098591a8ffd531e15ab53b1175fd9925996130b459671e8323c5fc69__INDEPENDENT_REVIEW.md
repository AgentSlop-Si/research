# Independent review of the exact decimation evaluator

Reviewed `README.md`, `decimation.py`, `fixed_kernel.py`, `operators.py`, the
field backend in `prior_exact_arithmetic.py`, `run_audit.py`, and its saved
`results/decimation_audit.json`. The implementation and stated asymptotic
costs are consistent for the declared exact-field contract. No correctness
or accounting issue remains within that contract. This is a subquadratic
operator evaluator, **not an improvement over the available O(N log N)
complete-operator algorithm**.

## Algebra and finite boundaries

The gauge, signed wraparound, and subsequent normalized split are compatible.
In a level of size n=N/r, with h=n/2, the diagonal satisfies
`D J_- = q^r J_+ D`, including the corner: its last diagonal entry is
`q^(-r(h-1)) = -q^r`. Consequently the normalized upper-right block is
`p_r(J_+) p_r(q^r J_+) diag(q^(-2ri)) = D_(2r)`.

Each inverse-factor denominator is nonzero. For nonleaf levels h is even,
and `q^h` has order 2r. Thus `1+q^(a h)=0` would require `a=r mod 2r`,
which is excluded by `0<=a<r`. The two backward scans in `inverse_factor`
solve the full signed cyclic recurrence; they do not discard a boundary
equation. The n=2 case is handled directly.

The three recursive calls implement the block polynomial exactly:

\[
a=E(D)x,\quad b=O(D)y,\quad c=(E+O)(D)(x+y),
\qquad (a+Db,c-a-b)=(E(D)x+DO(D)y,O(D)x+E(D)y).
\]

This checks both mixed terms. Coefficient additions, the extra D action,
and both basis maps are included in the implementation's online counts.
It applies to the polynomial padded to length N, including the full H_q.

## Online and preparation costs

Let a level have dimension n and degree r. Define its family-action gate
count by

\[
F(n,r)=n+2n\min(n,r).
\]

For a nonleaf node put h=n/2. The forward and inverse minus-block counts are

\[
B(h,r)=h+\begin{cases}r(2h+1),&r\le h,\\2h^2,&r>h,\end{cases}
\quad
I(h,r)=h+\begin{cases}r(4h-2),&r\le h,\\2h^2,&r>h.\end{cases}
\]

Including every counted add, subtract, multiply, and negate, the recursive
ledger is

\[
T(2,r)=F(2,r)+6,
\quad T(n,r)=3T(h,2r)+11h+B(h,r)+I(h,r)+F(h,2r).
\]

The outer two gauges add 2N gates. These independently derived recurrences
match all 13 saved small-case ledgers and all four larger ledgers exactly.
The nonrecursive work at depth d is Theta(min(N,N^2/4^d)) per node. Summing
over the 3^d nodes, including the leaves, gives precisely the advertised
Theta(N^(1+log_2(3)/2)) online bound. The switch near d=(log_2 N)/2,
not the leaves, determines this exponent.

Preparation invokes the linear q-factorial preparation once. Each depth
stores one shared plan, with O(r) explicitly charged denominator inversions.
Constructing the optional cyclic and inverse negacyclic kernels uses factor
scans costing O(rn)=O(N) per depth. It does not use dense matrix inversion,
an uncharged lookup table, or a transform. There are O(log N) depths, so
preparation is O(N log N). An independent direct formula for this preparation
ledger also matched all 17 saved cases. Root finding is outside the algorithm
because a primitive q is expressly supplied. Inversion is a counted unit
exact-field operation, not a claim about bit cost.

For one fresh application, preparation plus online work remains
Theta(N^(1+log_2(3)/2)); warm reuse is unnecessary for that bound.

## Why three calls still use linear live storage

The three child calls run sequentially. Each parent retains only a constant
number of arrays of its own dimension: its inputs, split coefficients and
vectors, the coefficient/vector sums, and completed child results. At most
one child is active. Thus the workspace satisfies

\[
W(n)\le W(n/2)+c n=O(n),
\]

rather than a three-child simultaneous-storage recurrence. The completed
subtrees and their internal arrays are not retained. `nodes` stores one
integer count per depth, not a record for every recursive node.

For immutable plans, each depth has O(n+r) entries, including its optional
dense kernels. Both geometric sums satisfy `sum n < 2N` and `sum r < N`.
Global powers, gauges, the polynomial, and all N outputs add O(N) entries.
Preparation's temporary factor vectors and evaluation's list-copy/index
work remain within the same storage and work bounds.

**Storage means O(N) live abstract field elements plus word indices.** It
does not mean O(N) bytes for the explicit cyclotomic backend, whose single
field element is itself a tuple of N/2 rationals. Rational bit lengths and
floating-point conditioning are not analyzed. This qualification is already
consistent with the README's exact-field model and should accompany any
standalone summary of the storage result.

## Audit scope and call-path check

The evaluator's call path consists of q-factorial preparation, scalar
factor/inverse-factor scans, direct cyclic products, diagonal maps, and the
three-way polynomial recursion. No ordinary FFT is called. The helper file
contains an unrelated `compile_radix2` definition, but the evaluator does not
invoke it; the reported zero-FFT count is supported by inspection rather than
only by its literal counter field.

The existing exact 13-case functional audit was inspected, not rerun. The
new focused check independently recomputed the preparation and online scalar
recurrences and matched all 13 small and four larger saved ledgers. The
all-size conclusions above follow from the identities and loop/space bounds,
not extrapolation from those finite certificates. Shape and primitive-root
validity remain caller preconditions; in particular, the public wrapper's
`zip` is not a complete validation mechanism for an oversized input vector.

The useful next question is whether the special H_q coefficients permit a
proved reduction or sharing of the three distinct child actions. The current
algorithm supplies no such sharing, and its result should not be presented
as a lower bound against other decompositions.
