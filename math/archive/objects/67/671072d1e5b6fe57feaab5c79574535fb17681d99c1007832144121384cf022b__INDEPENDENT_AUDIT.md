## 2. Degeneration, residual guards, and monicity

Let `d` be odd, `b` odd with `1≤b<d`, `A≥1`, and `n=Ad+b`. Let `ω` be primitive of order `d`. Put

`F(z)=z^d+1`, `G(z)=H_b(z;ω)=(z+1)U(z)`, `g=U(−1)=G′(−1)`.

The Gaussian symmetry makes `G` reciprocal, so odd `b` gives the factor `z+1`. Both `G` and `U` are monic. The exact q-Lucas specialization is

`H_n(z;ω)=F(z)^A G(z)=F(z)^A(z+1)U(z)`.

The two residual guards are

`Δ=Disc(U)≠0`, `R=Res(F,U)≠0`.

They imply that `U` has simple roots and none is a root of `F`. In particular `g≠0`. Thus precisely one `F` cluster, at `−1`, has multiplicity `A+1`; the other `d−1` clusters have multiplicity `A`; and there are `b−1` simple residual roots. Counting gives `Ad+1+b−1=n`, as required.

Neither residual guard may be omitted. `Disc(U)≠0` alone permits a residual root at a cluster. The product `Res(F,U)` includes `g`, so a separate `g≠0` assumption would be redundant under the full guards.

## 3. Independent derivation of the overlap polynomial

Work formally in characteristic zero with

`q=ω(1+ε²)^(1/d)`, `z=−1+εx`, `y=−√d x`.

In particular `q^d−1=ε²` exactly. Split the logarithm of the formal generating function

`Σ H_n(z;q)t^n/(q;q)_n = exp(Σ_(k≥1) (1+z^k)t^k/[k(1−q^k)])`

into terms divisible by `d` and the regular remainder. With `u=t^d`, the leading singular coefficient of degree `u^a` is

`ε^(−a)d^(−a/2)He_a(y)/a!`.

The order bound behind this assertion is the same as in the accepted separated-cluster derivation: only the linear and quadratic singular logarithm terms attain the full pole weight. Higher singular terms have strictly smaller weight. All regular coefficients are power series without negative ε powers.

At the overlapping point the regular coefficient of `t^b` vanishes at ε=0. Its first term is

`εx g/(ω;ω)_b`.

The next possible regular degree contributing to `t^(Ad+b)` is `b+d`. At `z=−1` its coefficient is

`B=1/(ω²;ω²)_((b+d)/2)`.

To verify this without an analytic product assumption, the product of the two formal q-exponential series at arguments `t` and `−t` is the formal series with even coefficient `1/(q²;q²)_m`. The first removed divisible-by-d even logarithm term occurs at degree `2d`; since `b+d<2d`, it does not change the coefficient under consideration. The denominator is nonzero because `(b+d)/2<d` and `d` is odd.

Only these two regular degrees contribute at order `ε^(A+1)`: the first has an extra ε factor, and the second uses singular index `A−1`. Degrees `b+jd` with `j≥2` have total order at least `ε^(A+2)`.

Since

`(q;q)_n = (−1)^A A! d^A (ω;ω)_b ε^(2A)(1+O(ε²))`,

putting

`W=(ω;ω)_b/(ω²;ω²)_((b+d)/2)`, `v=dW`, `τ=v/g`

gives the exact leading identity

`H_n(−1+εx;q)=ε^(A+1)(−1)^(A+1)d^((A−1)/2)g J_(A+1)(−√d x;τ)+O(ε^(A+2))`,

where

`J_(A+1)(y;τ)=y He_A(y)−Aτ He_(A−1)(y)`.

`J` is monic of degree `A+1`. The coefficient of `x^(A+1)` after the displayed substitution is `g d^A`, exactly the leading coefficient of the unperturbed local factor `F(z)^A(z+1)U(z)`. There is no missing normalization or square-root sign.

The finite series checks are even stronger than evaluating the model at a few x values: they compare every coefficient below the predicted order and every coefficient at that order. They cover all clusters in

`(d,A,b)=(3,1,1),(3,2,1),(3,3,1),(3,4,1),(7,1,3),(7,2,3)`.

There are 26 cluster checks in total. Each passes.

## 4. Exact ordinary-discriminant order, coefficient, and sign

Assume also `Disc(J)≠0`. Hermite polynomials have ordinary discriminants

`P_A=Disc(He_A)=∏_(j=1)^A j^j`.

Consequently each cluster has a full set of distinct first-order ε branches, and the residual branches remain simple. The discriminant order in ε is twice

`L=(d−1)A(A−1)/2 + A(A+1)/2 = dA(A−1)/2+A`.

The exact coefficient is obtained by multiplying squared root differences:

- Within ordinary clusters: `P_A^(d−1)` and scaling contribution `d^(−(d−1)A(A−1)/2)`.
- Within the overlap cluster: `Disc(J)` and scaling contribution `d^(−A(A+1)/2)`.
- Between distinct clusters: `(Disc F)^(A²) d^(2A)`. The extra factor follows from `∏_(α≠−1)(−1−α)=F′(−1)=d`.
- Between clusters and residual roots: `R^(2A) g²`.
- Among residual roots: `Δ`.

All residual and cluster polynomials used here are monic. The powers of cluster roots from local scaling multiply to one: excluding `−1`, their product is one, and all relevant exponents are even. Since

`Disc(z^d+1)=(−1)^((d−1)/2)d^d`,

the result is

`D_n(q(ε))=ε^(2L)(C(ω)+O(ε²))`,

`C(ω)=(−1)^(A(d−1)/2) d^[dA(A+1)/2+A] P_A^(d−1) Disc(J) Δ R^(2A) g²`.

The order is exact under the three stated nonvanishing guards. The final remainder is even because `D_n(q(ε))` is even in ε. The sign is genuinely the ordinary-discriminant sign and cannot be replaced by an absolute value.

## 5. Reciprocal parameter identity at half degree

Now let `d=4r+3`, `b=2r+1`, `Q=ω²`, and `m=2r+1`. Define

`a_j=(ω;Q)_j/(Q;Q)_j`, `M=(ω;Q)_m`.

Differentiating the Rogers–Szegő three-term recurrence at `z=−1` gives, by induction,

`g=(Q;Q)_r Σ_(j=0)^r a_j`.

Cancellation of the complete nonzero root product `d=∏_(k=1)^(d−1)(1−ω^k)` in the definition of `v` gives

`v=(Q;Q)_r M`.

Every denominator and every factor of `v` is nonzero in characteristic zero; for reduction modulo a prime `p∤d`, all are units at every primitive-d residue root.

Because `ω=Q^(−m)`, the finite q-binomial identity gives

`Σ_(j=0)^m a_j=(Q^(−m);Q)_m=M`.

Writing a star for substitution `ω↦ω^−1`, direct factor inversion and complementary Gaussian symmetry give

`a_j*=ω^j a_j`, `a_(m−j)=(M/M*)a_j*`.

The complementary pairing partitions the full sum into the two equal-length ranges `0,…,r` and `r+1,…,m`. Dividing by `M` proves the denominator-safe identity

`g/v + g*/v* = 1`.

Whenever both g values are nonzero, this is precisely

`1/τ + 1/τ* = 1`, equivalently `τ*=τ/(τ−1)`.

This proof works for composite odd d as well as prime d. It is not inferred from the five fixed-base checks. The checks independently verify its cross-multiplied form at all five bases.

## 6. The characteristic-p good-factor lemma

Let `p≡3 (mod4)` be prime, `A=(p−1)/2`, and work over an algebraic closure of characteristic p. Suppose `τ,τ*` are nonzero and satisfy `1/τ+1/τ*=1`. Then at least one of `J(y;τ)` and `J(y;τ*)` is squarefree.

Here are the denominator and zero-root details.

1. `A` is odd and `A<p`. Thus `He_A(0)=0`, while `He_(A−1)(0)≠0` modulo p. Since `τ≠0`, zero is not a root of `J(y;τ)`. The same holds for `τ*`.
2. The reciprocal identity and the finiteness/nonzeroness of both parameters imply `τ≠1` and `τ*≠1`.
3. `Disc(He_A)=P_A` is a p-unit. Therefore `U_0=He_A` has no common root with `U_0′`. At any root of `J=yU_0−τU_0′`, the value `U_0` is nonzero, so logarithmic differentiation is legitimate.
4. At a multiple root y, the Hermite differential equation yields

   `U_0′(y)/U_0(y)=y/τ`,

   `y²=−τ(1+Aτ)/(1−τ)`.

   In characteristic p, `A=−1/2`; replacing τ by `τ*=τ/(τ−1)` negates this square. Both roots are nonzero. As A is odd, J is even, so if both J polynomials have a multiple root we may choose them as y and iy, where `i²=−1`.

Now set `V_0(y)=i^(−A)He_A(iy)`, a monic degree-A polynomial. The two differential equations are

`U_0″−yU_0′+AU_0=0`, `V_0″+yV_0′−AV_0=0`.

For

`W_0=U_0′V_0−U_0V_0′−yU_0V_0`,

one obtains `W_0′=−(2A+1)U_0V_0=0`. Its degree is p, its leading coefficient is −1, and its constant term is zero. A degree-at-most-p polynomial with zero derivative has only constant and y^p terms, hence

`W_0(y)=−y^p`.

At the hypothesized pair of multiple roots,

`U_0′/U_0=y/τ`, `V_0′/V_0=−y/τ*`,

so `W_0/(U_0V_0)=y(1/τ+1/τ*−1)=0`. This contradicts `y≠0`. The lemma follows.

Fresh characteristic controls at `p=3,7,11,19,23` check the complete Wronskian identity and, independently, the gcd of the parameter discriminant with its reciprocal pullback. Each gcd is exactly the factor τ, the excluded zero parameter. These are controls of the uniform proof, not its basis. No extrapolation to other congruence classes or all A is made.

## 7. Paired residual guard and exact cyclotomic bridge

For `b=(d−1)/2`, define integral polynomials

`Γ_d(q)=Disc_z U_b(z;q) · Res_z(z^d+1,U_b(z;q))`,

where `U_b=H_b/(z+1)` is monic over `Z[q]`. Let

`B_d=rem_(Φ_d)[Γ_d(q)Γ_d(q^(d−1))]`,

and let c_d be the positive coefficient content when this canonical remainder is nonzero.

For `p∤d`, the algebra `F_p[q]/(Φ_d)` is a product of fields. If `p∤c_d`, at least one component has nonzero B_d. In that component, both residual guard values `Γ_d(ω)` and `Γ_d(ω^−1)` are nonzero. Consequently both g values are units; all Pochhammer factors in v are units; and both τ values are defined, finite, and nonzero. The lemma supplies at least one simple J in this pair. At the corresponding root/component the complete coefficient C is a p-unit: d, P_A, Δ, R, g, and Disc(J) are all units.

It is enough that one component works. There is no claim that B_d or C is a unit in every component. The paired residual guard is deliberately stronger than an unpaired guard because the Hermite argument may select either member of the inversion pair.

The cyclotomic step must be done over the integers before reduction. A p-unit value of C implies C is nonzero in characteristic zero. Galois conjugacy then gives exact characteristic-zero multiplicity L at every primitive dth root, so

`D_n(q)=Φ_d(q)^L E_(n,d)(q)`, with `E_(n,d)∈Z[q]`.

The integral quotient exists by successive exact division by the monic cyclotomic polynomial. With `Q_d=(q^d−1)/Φ_d`, the leading coefficient identity is

`E_(n,d)(ω)=Q_d(ω)^L C(ω)`.

For `p∤d`, Q_d is a unit at every primitive-d residue root. If `D_n(ζ_dp)=0`, irreducibility and coprimality of `Φ_dp` and `Φ_d` force `Φ_dp|E_(n,d)` over the integers. The exact identity

`Φ_dp(q)Φ_d(q)=Φ_d(q^p)`

reduces to

`Φ_dp(q)≡Φ_d(q)^(p−1) (mod p)`.

Thus E would vanish at every primitive-d residue component, contradicting the p-unit component already obtained. All cancellation occurs in the polynomial domain before evaluation; there is no division by zero at a root.

No comparison of L with p−1 is needed. In particular, arbitrarily large L is harmless after the complete integer division. Reducing an undivided discriminant or only a short Taylor approximation would not establish this implication.

## 9. Scalar-bank corollary and boundaries

All five d values and the prime p are `3 mod4`, so `N=dp≡1 mod4`. In the previously accepted model, the bank uses scalar coefficients, the same natural N-cycle P, first-order resolvents with distinct nonzero nodes and `λ^N≠1`, and one free diagonal. An arbitrary diagonal does not enlarge this common-cycle circulant model.

The shifted Hankel rank gives the lower bound `m=(N−1)/2`. The unique annihilator is `T_m=(-1)^mH_m(−z)`. Its constant term excludes zero nodes; the accepted resonance theorem gives the cycle guard when `N≡1 mod4`; and the squarefreeness proved above supplies m distinct legal nodes. Thus m is attained. The discriminants of T_m and H_m agree because the root differences acquire an even total sign.

This conclusion is not an unrestricted full-inverse minimum, does not change the cycle ordering, and does not permit confluent or higher-order resolvents. It does not imply that every composite order works, nor that the analogous first leading coefficient proves arbitrary repeated ramification. When A reaches p, the Hermite factors need no longer be units. No assertion about all future fixed-base contents follows from the five exact rows.
