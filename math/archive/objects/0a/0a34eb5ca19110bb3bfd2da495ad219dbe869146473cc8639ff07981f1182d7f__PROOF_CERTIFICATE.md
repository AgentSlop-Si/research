## Conventions and models

Let `N >= 2`, let `K` be a field of characteristic not dividing `2N`, and suppose `omega` is a primitive `N`th root of unity in `K`. Define

\[
S e_j=e_{j+1\bmod N},\qquad T e_j=\omega^j e_j,
\qquad TS=\omega ST.
\]

Write `W_{a,b}=S^a T^b`. These `N^2` matrices form a basis of `Mat_N(K)` with twisted product

\[
W_{a,b}W_{c,d}=\omega^{bc}W_{a+c,b+d},
\]

where indices are modulo `N`. We use the **unnormalized negative-exponent** Fourier matrix

\[
F_-=[\omega^{-ij}]_{0\le i,j<N},\quad
F_-^{-1}=\frac1N F_+,\quad F_+=[\omega^{ij}]_{i,j}.
\]

Define `supp_W(A)` as the number of nonzero coefficients in the unique expansion of `A` into `W_{a,b}`. Computation cost means scalar field operations, including preparation and final explicit output; modular inversion is one field operation in the finite-field test ledger, not one machine instruction or one bit operation.

## Theorem 1 — exact Fourier Weyl support

For the inverse Fourier matrix,

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{supp}_W(F_-^{-1}) =
\begin{cases}N^2,&N\text{ odd},\\N^2/2,&N\text{ even}.\end{cases}}
\]

More precisely, the coefficient of `W_{a,b}` in `F_-^{-1}` is

\[
 c_{a,b}=\frac{1}{N^2}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}
     \omega^{j^2+(a-b)j} =\frac{G_N(a-b)}{N^2}.
\]

**Proof.** Weyl matrices are orthogonal for the trace pairing, so the coefficient of `W_{a,b}` in `F_+ / N` is

\[
\frac{1}{N^2}\operatorname{tr}(T^{-b} S^{-a}F_+)
=\frac1{N^2}\sum_j \omega^{-bj}\omega^{(j+a)j}.
\]

Define `G_N(d)=sum_j omega^(j^2+d*j)` and its inverse-phase companion `G_N^#(d)=sum_j omega^(-j^2-d*j)`. Character orthogonality gives

\[
 G_N(d)G_N^\#(d)
=N\sum_{h:\,N\mid2h}\omega^{h^2+dh}.
\]

If `N` is odd this equals `N`, so every `G_N(d)` is nonzero. If `N` is even it equals

\[
N\bigl(1+(-1)^{N/2+d}\bigr).
\]

Moreover replacing `j` by `j+N/2` shows

\[
G_N(d)=(-1)^{N/2+d}G_N(d).
\]

Hence exactly half of `d` values give zero: for `4|N`, `G_N(d)` is nonzero precisely when `d` is even; for `N=2 mod 4`, precisely when `d` is odd. Each `d=a-b` occurs at exactly `N` Weyl indices. QED.

The argument works over `C` and over finite fields containing the specified primitive root, provided the field characteristic does not divide `2N`. The quadratic Gauss-sum identities are classical; this is an exact *support audit* in our Phase 15 operator basis.

## Theorem 2 — mixed Green chains must have logarithmic depth

Suppose an invertible matrix `M` is given by a literal product

\[
 M=W_0 G_1W_1G_2\cdots G_LW_L,
\]

where each `W_j` is a scalar multiple of a **Weyl monomial**, and each `G_j^{-1}` is a linear combination of at most `q_j` Weyl monomials. The latter monomials can point in **arbitrary and different Weyl directions at every stage**. In particular, the magnetic Green inverse

\[
 G_j^{-1}=I-\alpha_jS^{s_j}-\beta_jT^{b_j}
\]

has `q_j <= 3`, for arbitrary stride/modulation indices.

Then

\[
 \boxed{\prod_{j=1}^L q_j\;\ge\;
        \operatorname{supp}_W(M^{-1}).}
\]

In particular, if `M=F_-` and `q_j<=3`,

\[
\boxed{L\ge\left\lceil\log_3 K_N\right\rceil,\qquad
K_N=\begin{cases}N^2,&N\text{ odd},\\N^2/2,&N\text{ even}.\end{cases}}
\]

**Proof.** Inversion reverses the product. Every inverse Green factor contributes at most `q_j` Weyl monomials. Multiplying two Weyl monomials yields another single Weyl monomial, times a nonzero scalar. Distributing the factors therefore generates at most `prod q_j` terms; cancellations can reduce, never increase, the number of nonzero basis coefficients. Weyl monomial gauges preserve support cardinality by translating/permuting the indices. Apply Theorem 1. QED.

This statement is **stable under replacing the Weyl coordinate generators by any other invertible monomial Weyl frame**, because the induced invertible map on `Z_N^2` only permutes basis atoms up to scalars. It does not allow arbitrary dense basis changes for free.

**Crucial limitation:** This is a necessary **literal stage-count** condition for a specified *product* grammar. It is NOT an `Omega(N log N)` time lower bound, because multiple algebraic stages may sometimes be fused into one linear-work executor (Theorem 5). Sums of branches, arbitrary diagonal gauges, or stages whose inverse is not Weyl-sparse are not covered.

## Theorem 3 — an extra obstruction for exact chirp-convolution Green factorizations

Now let `N=2^m >= 4`, and let `zeta` be a primitive `2N`th root, with `omega=zeta^2`. Define the circular chirp-convolution matrix

\[
 C_{i,j}=\zeta^{(i-j)^2},
\]

which is well-defined modulo `N` because `N` is even. With `D=diag(zeta^(j^2))`, the exact Bluestein identity is

\[
 \boxed{C=DF_-D,\qquad C^{-1}=\frac1N D^{-1}F_+D^{-1},}
\]

and the inverse convolution kernel is

\[
\boxed{q_t=(C^{-1})_{t,0}=\frac1N\zeta^{-t^2}\ne0\quad\text{for every }t.}
\]

Suppose

\[
 C=c\prod_{j=1}^{L}(I-\alpha_jS^{s_j})^{-1}
\]

with all inverses defined. **Even with arbitrary nonzero scalar parameters and arbitrary nonzero shifts `s_j`**, this implies

\[
 \boxed{L\ge m+1.}
\]

**Proof.** Its inverse is a scalar times a product of `L` binomials in cyclic shift `S`, hence has at most `2^L` nonzero shift coefficients. Since `C^{-1}` has `N=2^m` nonzero shift coefficients, `L>=m`.

Assume for contradiction `L=m`. The `2^m` subset sums of shifts `s_j` must be pairwise distinct modulo `N`, because otherwise the product cannot have `N` distinct nonzero coefficients. Thus subset sums are a bijection to `Z_N`.

For each `t=0,...,m-1`, apply the additive character of order `2^(t+1)` to all subset sums. Their sum vanishes, while the factored sum equals

\[
 \prod_{j=1}^m\bigl(1+\exp(2\pi i s_j/2^{t+1})\bigr).
\]

Thus at least one shift has `2`-adic valuation exactly `t`. Since there are `m` shifts, the valuations must be `0,1,...,m-1` exactly once each. In particular, select `s_u` odd and `s_v` divisible by `2` but not `4`. Their four subset sums `0,s_u,s_v,s_u+s_v` are distinct and have coefficients of the form `c, c*(-alpha_u), c*(-alpha_v), c*alpha_u*alpha_v` (with an irrelevant common scalar). They must satisfy the parallelogram identity

\[
 q_0q_{s_u+s_v}=q_{s_u}q_{s_v}.
\]

But the actual chirp kernel satisfies

\[
\frac{q_0q_{s_u+s_v}}{q_{s_u}q_{s_v}}
=\zeta^{-2s_us_v}\ne1,
\]

since `2N` cannot divide `2s_us_v` when `v_2(s_us_v)=1<m`. This contradiction rules out `L=m`. QED.

The same obstruction survives inserting **Weyl-monomial shift/modulation gauges** between pure-shift Green factors: these gauges normalize the circulant algebra and can be pushed to the ends, modifying scalar parameters. A net modulation cannot equal the nonzero circulant `C`; a net shift merely translates the chirp kernel, and its parallelogram ratio is unchanged. Arbitrary diagonal gauges are **not** covered.

The theorem does **not** state that `m+1` stages suffice. It is a lower bound, not an existence result.

## Theorem 4 — full binary-cut rank of the inverse chirp kernel

Still let `N=2^m`, `m>=2`. Reshape the *single length-N vector* `q_t=zeta^(-t^2)/N` by writing `t=u+2^a v` for `1<=a<m`, `0<=u<2^a`, `0<=v<2^(m-a)`. Then

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}(q_{u+2^av})_{u,v}
=2^{\min(a,m-a)}.}
\]

**Proof.** Expand the quadratic exponent:

\[
q_{u+2^a v}=
 \underbrace{\frac1N\zeta^{-u^2}}_{\text{nonzero row factor}}
 \underbrace{\zeta^{-2^{2a}v^2}}_{\text{nonzero column factor}}
 \underbrace{(\omega^{-2^av})^u}_{\text{Vandermonde}}.
\]

The nodes `omega^(-2^a v)` are all distinct for `v=0,...,2^(m-a)-1`. The last matrix therefore has rank equal to the minimum of its row and column counts. Nonzero diagonal scalings do not change rank. QED.

For balanced cuts, the required separated-tensor rank grows as `sqrt(N)` (up to floor/ceiling). But **generating this very kernel vector costs only `O(N)`** using

\[
q_{j+1}=q_j\zeta^{-(2j+1)},\qquad
\rho_{j+1}=\rho_j\zeta^{-2},
\]

with `rho_j=zeta^(-(2j+1))`. Tensor rank is not an arithmetic-time lower bound for generating an explicit vector, nor for computing an arbitrary transform.

## Theorem 5 — a positive multiscale Union identity

For `N=2^m`, `alpha^N!=1`,

\[
\boxed{
\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}\bigl(I+\alpha^{2^j}S^{2^j}\bigr)
=(1-\alpha^N)(I-\alpha S)^{-1}.
}
\]

**Proof.** Apply `prod_{j=0}^{m-1}(1+z^(2^j))=(1-z^(2^m))/(1-z)` to `z=alpha*S`, where `S^N=I`. QED.

The left has `m` dyadic stages, each individually `O(N)`. The right has a single charged cyclic Green solve, which uses `O(N)` field operations and writes all `N` outputs. Hence an apparent `m`-stage operator can be **jointly evaluated in linear time**. This is classical algebra, but it is an essential *positive control* demonstrating why the stage lower bounds in Theorems 2–3 cannot be promoted to unrestricted time lower bounds.

On `N=65,536`, the exact finite-field audit counted `2,097,167` scalar operations and `1,048,576` intermediate writes for literal stage-by-stage evaluation, versus `393,214` scalar operations and `131,072` writes for preparation plus fused execution. Every output agreed; the full cyclic recurrence and twelve separately computed coefficients were independently verified. **Both algorithms compute this Green-derived operator, not `F_-`.**
