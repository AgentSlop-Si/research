# Review of the decoder's integer trigonometric construction

The decoder implements trigonometric table preparation with integer arithmetic, rather than charging a transcendental oracle at unit cost. The following independently verifies its conservative bound.

Put S=2^P and R=2^(P+guard). In an arctangent series at 1/q, each retained fixed-point floor incurs error below 1/R. When the first term floors to zero, the alternating-series remainder is also below 1/R. If M terms were retained, the approximation error is at most (M+1)/R. For q>=2, M<=(P+guard+1)/2. Applying Machin's identity gives pre-round error at most

    [16(M5+1) + 4(M239+1)] / R.

With `guard=bit_length(128(P+1)^2)`, this is below 1/(2S) for P>=8. The final nearest-integer rounding adds at most 1/(2S), so the returned pi constant has error below 1/S.

The centered angle is computed as the nearest fixed-point value of `2*pi_fixed*a/Q`, where |a|<=Q/2. Its error is below 3/(2S), and its absolute value is below 4. A complex Taylor recurrence step rounds each coordinate with error at most 1/(2S), hence its complex local error is below 1/S. The sum of propagated errors through degree d is bounded by

    d exp(4)/S < 55 d/S.

Using 64d/S is therefore conservative. The Taylor remainder along the imaginary axis is bounded by `4^(d+1)/(d+1)!`; the exponential in its integral remainder has modulus one. The degree-selection loop bounds this by 2^(-bits-4). Finally,

    P = bits + bit_length(256(d+1)) + 4

leaves enough margin for the angle error, Taylor rounding, and remainder together to be below the advertised complex error `2^(-bits)`.

For fixed requested accuracy, d and P are constants. There are only O(log N) table angles. Even if precision grows modestly for a conservative implementation, polylogarithmic-in-log-N preparation remains negligible compared with the charged sublinear enumeration of the main table. Growing accuracy requirements as a function of N are outside the stated fixed-accuracy theorem.

The implementation folds the four sign phases into the block-start grid, which is a power of two divisible by four. Thus its descriptor alphabet is `Q0*Q1`, saving the explicit factor four used in the generic derivation. The negative slope and start angle implement the ordinary negative-exponent DFT convention. Nearest, rather than floor, phase quantization gives its smaller displayed phase error `pi/Q0 + pi(B-1)/Q1`.

`review_decoder.py` checks three fully constructed eager tables against independently evaluated sample expansions, including a two-atom key, maximum-index phase, multiple packed words, and a partial final block. It also performs 1,024 direct trigonometric comparisons at four precisions. These numerical checks complement the proof; binary64 comparisons are not used as a certified transcendental oracle.
