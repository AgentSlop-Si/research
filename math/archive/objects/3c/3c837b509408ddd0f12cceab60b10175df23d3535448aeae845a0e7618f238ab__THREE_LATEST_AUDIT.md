## 2. Exact branch: a real characteristic-zero advance, still a restricted chart

The fixed permutation remains `(7,0,6,3,4,1,2,5)`, and the stages are globally separated upper/lower rank-one-generator matrices. This is not the full class of all order-one quasiseparable matrices or all possible Fourier factorizations.

The new 11-by-9 matrix couples projected columns 1,2,5 of the inverse-stage parametrization and a cofactor annihilator from column 4. Its rank must be at most eight for an intended invertible separated factorization. The source's standalone statement needs the invertibility/nonzero selected-column hypotheses made explicit. The delicate zero fourth-output-column case is valid: its nonzero coefficient triple lies in the kernel of the 4-by-3 projected basis, making that matrix rank at most two and its entire cofactor vector zero. Thus no additional nonzero fourth-column multiplier is required.

On the regular structural chart `h0 Delta T != 0`, the selected 9-by-9 polynomial minor is exactly

`Dhat = -DB DH`,

with DB a nine-term quadratic, DH a 105-term sextic, and Dhat a 250-term degree-eight polynomial. Fresh reconstruction matches all 924 cyclotomic coefficient vectors in the source's Delta,T,V,M,W,minor and resultant exports. It also derives the full determinant directly, rather than only testing evaluations of a supplied polynomial.

The 31-term cubic W and Dhat have resultant nonzero on `h0=1,h1=h2=0`: its reduction under `zeta8 -> 9` modulo 17 is 6. Because this is a valid integral coefficient reduction of the exact resultant, it proves no common complex root on that regular slice. This is stronger than a finite-field search with no solutions, whose absence would not by itself imply complex infeasibility.

The two eliminated resultants have 37 and 476 terms and are nonzero. They place the structural base inside a proper hypersurface union; the necessary four-variable h-locus has dimension at most two. It is not proved empty. The inverse-stage full parameters and every excluded denominator/corner branch must remain tracked separately; neither the dimension statement nor the minor replaces the full residual equations or the complete lift.

An independent refinement resolves the source's possible identically-zero-W fiber more tightly. After eliminating its nonzero linear leading coefficient, the remaining two coefficients have a nonzero degree-six elimination resultant and a constant nonzero quadratic leading coefficient. There are at most 12 base points where W vanishes identically in h6. Their free-h6 union therefore has dimension at most one. This does not reduce the overall dimension-two bound or solve the regular system.

## 3. Phase19 and Phase20: stronger tests and sharper restricted barriers

### Certification improves; evaluation cost does not disappear

The full identities `MZ=DM` and `MD=Z^-1M` force `M=cF` for every integer length. A scalar calibration fixes F. They are stronger than the earlier symmetrized pair, which admits `F(aI+bJ)`. The Weyl Frobenius residual gap is exactly `2 sin(pi/N)`, so an unscaled small residual becomes a weaker accuracy test as N grows.

For a fixed linear map over GF(p), one calibration call and three oracle calls per random vector give a valid `1+3h`-call verifier. With residual-stack rank rho, its exact false-acceptance probability is `p^(-h rho)`, bounded by `p^-h` if any residual is nonzero. A stateful/nonlinear/adaptive oracle is outside this theorem. Root setup, field size, candidate preprocessing and candidate evaluation cost are still charged. Finite-field passes are not characteristic-zero identity certificates.

Projective cross-ratios exclude all outer monomial gauges when one ratio is not an Nth root. A two-column subgroup histogram is a stronger necessary test. It is still insufficient even when checked for every pair: Sylvester Hadamard matrices at powers of two pass all those histograms while failing full Fourier monomial equivalence.

The exact full anchored-ratio criterion extends to every N. The independent recognizer uses a primitive exponent that is a unit modulo N, unlike the source implementation's dyadic odd-exponent shortcut. It reconstructs arbitrary scaled/permuted Fourier controls at 18 lengths through 32, including mixed-prime composites. Full explicit-matrix recognition costs at least reading its N-squared entries; it is not a fast Fourier evaluator.

### Independent strengthening: one sparse inverse row already matters

If one row of M inverse has at most s<N nonzeros, then

`||M-F||_2 >= sqrt(1-s/N)`.

For b that row, `bM=e_i`, `||bF||=||b||`, and `|(bF)_i|<=sqrt(s/N)||b||`. Completing the square in `||e_i-bF||^2/||b||^2` proves the bound without any condition-number hypothesis. It strengthens the source's valid but weaker delta/(1+delta) estimate.

A pure product of sparse-denominator inverses has inverse support at most the product of its individual row-support bounds. With bound s per stage, error is at least `sqrt(max(0,1-s^t/N))`, tending to one when `s^t=o(N)`. Sums of inverses need not have a sparse total inverse, so this does not rule out the surviving Green-sum architecture.

### Independent strengthening: stationary commutator error tends to one

A constant-coefficient path denominator has commutator rank at most two with the common cyclic shift Z. A t-stage product of q-term Green sums in that same frame has rank at most 2qt. The exact singular values of `[F,Z]` give the stronger consequence, for r<N,

`rank([M,Z])<=r => ||M-F||_2 >= cos((2 floor(r/2)+1)pi/(2N))`.

Thus for the even rank budget 2qt the floor is `cos((2qt+1)pi/(2N))`, tending to one for qt=o(N). This improves the source's limit 1/sqrt(2). Exact equality requires 2qt>=N for even N and at least N-1 for odd N. Independent path permutations or arbitrary diagonal masks cannot be added without a new commutator budget.

### Other correct boundaries retained

- Nested Phase19 proves a uniform inverse one-norm estimate `(b/a^2)[1+2w(b/a)^2]` for complex half-bandwidth-w matrices with singular values in [a,b]. It gives a gain/conditioning obstruction for stable bounded-amplitude Green products, even with different path orders. It is a call/stage budget, not an unrestricted shared-graph scalar-operation lower bound.
- The simultaneous middle-monomial signature gate tests fixed outside factors, not all parameter choices.
- Scalar rational functions of one Harper operator separated by one arbitrary middle monomial still cannot equal Fourier in the stated 4-divisible lengths. More middle masks or noncommuting factors remain outside that argument.
- A real core cannot become Fourier by arbitrary outer complex monomial gauges for N>=3. For `F=AQB` with real invertible A,B and no additional complex factors, Q must have the exact real/purely-imaginary axis counts. Dense real mixers can attain this classification; no fast application is supplied.

## 4. All-length approximation: proof accepted, implementation caveats repaired separately

The v10 near-grid identity pads to Q, the next power of two, and writes each target Fourier row as a dyadic row times a bounded residual phase. Bessel/Chebyshev truncation supplies a separable rank R approximation. Crucially, reusing the same random state for all rank terms recombines them into one weighted input per target, preserving the v9 variance bound without an R factor:

`variance <= (M/N)(1+tau_R)^2 ||x||_2^2`.

The fixed-input simultaneous-output probability argument is valid. It is not a uniform successful random operator for every input and it is not exact recovery. The finite-rank residual phase introduces deterministic bias; dense arbitrary input has no promised quantization gap enabling conversion to exact values.

The Bessel proof is valid in the exact-coefficient model, but the claimed fully charged implementation is incomplete. The prototype invokes a special-function routine without coefficient-generation cost or numerical certification, stores all R Chebyshev arrays using Theta(RN) workspace, uses non-certified floating nearest-grid indices, and omits the N=1 direct case. A naive forward Bessel order recurrence is numerically unstable and cannot silently fill the preparation gap. A literal failure target N^(-a) is vacuous at a=0; use c N^(-a), 0<c<1, for a nontrivial fixed confidence.

The independent all-state covariance controls through Q=32 agree to numerical precision. They validate the analytical composition locally, not a production finite-precision implementation. Details and precise counterexamples: `lift_audit/AUDIT_REPORT.md`.

### A separately proved rational-Taylor variant closes coefficient preparation

`taylor_extension/CHARGED_TAYLOR_PRECISION_EXTENSION.md` replaces the special-function coefficients with streamed powers and factorial recurrences. A finite rational Machin series constructs rho and a certified eta with `|rho-pi|<=eta`, in charged O(log(1/eta)) rational operations. The residual polynomial obeys the sharper real-phase tail

`tau_R <= eta + |rho|^R/R!`.

Exact integer quotient/remainder indices and O(N) streamed recurrences charge all rank coefficients and input weights. Reusing the random state gives the same covariance theorem. For a fixed number of random rows, work is O(RN+log(1/eta)) arithmetic operations and O(N) arithmetic storage, under the inherited certified dyadic-row and root contract.

For fixed energy-relative error, R is constant and arithmetic work is O(N). For polynomially shrinking error, R=Theta(log N/log log N) is sufficient. The explicit deterministic rounding ledger includes weighted-input, coefficient, row, cancellation, summation, normalizer and quantization-radius errors. It is a theorem for the specified variant and contract; it does not certify the uploaded NumPy/Bessel binary64 implementation. Rational and floating word bit costs, exponent range and root generation are not suppressed.

The new all-length lift does not automatically preserve the earlier sparse-spectrum exact recovery promise: multiplying an input by the rank weights can make its Fourier spectrum dense. No exact all-length corollary follows by combining the two statements without a further proof.

## 5. Exact bridge: complete chirp banks and a broader phase-aware obstruction

### Completing the chirp bank restores exactness and the ordinary work bill

For a fixed chirp parameter a, take beta_h=beta_0+hK, h=0,...,M-1. Within each contiguous length-M block these are the M Fourier characters. The scaled block operators P_h/M are pairwise orthogonal projections, and

`(1/M) sum_h P_h = I`.

Thus averaging all M exact row operators gives exactly F. A partial bank has only K linear measurements per row; fewer than M rows cannot be injective on arbitrary dense inputs. Nonlinear postprocessing does not reconstruct information absent from the fixed linear transcript. The precise adaptive and randomized restrictions are stated in the bridge report; access to fresh raw input beyond the transcript is outside that obstruction.

This is a valid exact completion but not a faster transform. The straightforward full-output row evaluation costs O(NM+N log K). For the useful approximation setting M=Theta(log N), it is Theta(N log N). Sharing the local Fourier analysis across the bank recovers the familiar N log M plus N log K factorization scale. No lower bound against every possible shared evaluator is inferred from the cost of those known schedules.

### Exact near-grid correction has high rank at odd lengths

The exact residual matrix is `H[k,n]=exp(-2 pi i n theta_k/Q)`, where theta_k is the centered residual of kQ/N. There are exactly d=N/gcd(N,Q) distinct residuals. They produce d distinct scalar bases, so H is a Vandermonde matrix with repeated rows and exact rank d. Any exact separated correction therefore needs at least d terms. At every odd N>1, the dyadic Q has gcd(N,Q)=1, so the exact rank is N.

This rules out promoting the short approximate separable correction to exactness at those lengths. Completing every beta bank computes the truncated center exactly and leaves its deterministic approximation bias. The rank bound does not exclude a different nonseparable correction or an unrestricted exact algorithm.

### Linear masks and affine-conjugated paths can be charged together

The independently reviewed extension permits stages that are sums of

`c D^a P B^-1 P^-1 D^b`,

where B is an invertible constant-coefficient path tridiagonal, a,b are integers, and P is an affine coordinate permutation. Each summand may have its own affine conjugation and linear phases. This is broader than a common-frame unmasked stationary product, while still excluding arbitrary diagonals, arbitrary path permutations and independent left/right affine factors.

Let T(A)=Z^-1 A Z. A common-frame inverse has displacement rank at most two, or at most four after an arbitrary affine conjugation. A linear phase mask changes this into a twisted relation `T(A)-lambda A`, with lambda an Nth root, without increasing the budget. Rank budgets add along a product branch.

Expand a t-stage product into b branches; let d<=b be the number of distinct total twist phases and r_nu the individual branch defect ranks. With p the polynomial having those distinct phases as roots,

`rank(p(T)M) <= d sum_nu r_nu`.

For Fourier, `p(T)F=h(Z^-1D)F`, with h a nonzero degree-d polynomial. The weighted cyclic shift is cyclic, so the nullity of h is at most d. Consequently exact equality requires

`N <= d(1+sum_nu r_nu) <= d(1+rho t b) <= b+rho t b^2`,

where rho=2 in the common path frame or rho=4 for affine-conjugated paths. If each stage has at most a fixed q>=2 summands, this forces t=Omega(log N), even with arbitrary conditioning. A single-branch circuit has the stronger bound t>=(N-1)/rho. With one common phase and even N, the Fourier twisted defect has full rank N; its explicit spectrum also supplies the sharpened cosine approximation floor.

The affine qualifier is essential. A bare affine multiplier can have nearly full displacement rank and cannot be inserted as free wiring. The review supplies an explicit N=9 counterexample. Also, an expanded-branch/stage inequality does not establish N log N scalar work under arbitrary shared-state evaluation.

## 6. How to use the combined checkpoint

Keep the exact and approximate tracks separate in conclusions but share their verified algebra and cost accounting. The exact F8 branch now has a stronger saturated necessary target and a genuine excluded complex slice; its remaining parameter variety and denominator branches are open. Phase20 supplies inexpensive falsification of fixed candidate circuits, with sharp conditioning-free barriers for specified subclasses. The all-length work supplies a controlled approximate bridge and a coefficient-generation repair, not an exact transform.

The next exact candidate needs all of the following: identities for arbitrary fresh inputs and a scalable length family, a complete treatment of chart exceptions, a fully charged evaluator including setup and outputs, and a precision/bit model whenever a practical claim is made. Dense support, phase correctness at one small size, a small number of formal stages, or a good approximate error bound establishes only part of that target.

No publication-priority claim is made. Existing classical identities and externally reported algorithms are not superseded by these project-specific checks.
