# Independent review: necessary resolvent gates for forced odd-chirp B

Date: 2026-10-09 UTC. Verdict: the inverse-support, complementary-cut, circular-displacement, and inherited inverse-defect gates are correct within their stated representation and endpoint scopes. They neither exclude arbitrary independent paired-Green sums nor supply a cheap positive realization of B.

## 1. Target and finite-field full-row argument

For odd N>=3 over a field containing a primitive Nth root and of characteristic not dividing 2N, use the previously reviewed chirp A[j,j]=omega^(h j^2), h=(N+1)/2, and B=(A+F^(-1))^(-1), with the separate guard 1+N G!=0. Its inverse is exactly X=A+F^(-1).

Every off-diagonal X entry is nonzero. Over C every diagonal is also nonzero because its two summands have different magnitudes, one and 1/N.

The finite-field proof correctly claims only that at least one diagonal survives. If all vanished, the zeroth entry would imply N=-1 in the field, and the first would imply omega^(h+1)=1. Primitiveness then forces N to divide 3, so N=3. The relation N=-1 would force characteristic two, excluded by hypothesis. Thus at least one inverse row has all N entries nonzero.

The source's F_521,N=5,omega=104 example independently reproduces c=47 and diagonal [418,24,0,0,24]. The two zeros are real field cancellations; the maximum inverse row support remains five. No all-entry finite-field density claim is permitted.

## 2. Pure-product and more general sparse-denominator bounds

For a single invertible Green factor G=L(I-W)^(-1)R with monomial endpoints and weighted-permutation W, G^(-1)=R^(-1)(I-W)L^(-1) has at most two nonzeros per row. This survives independent endpoint permutations, different cycle orders, and zero weights. Row support is submultiplicative, so a product of L such factors representing the target must satisfy

2^L>=N, hence L>=ceil(log2 N).

The full-row witness makes the exact N threshold valid even in the finite-field cancellation cases. If the inverse denominator is I minus a sum of g_l weighted permutations, the corresponding necessary bound is product_l(g_l+1)>=N.

This is a bound on the specified direct factorization, not on arbitrary arithmetic algorithms for the same operator. It does not apply automatically to products of Green sums, auxiliary-state elimination, or a reorganized circuit merely because these also involve Green maps. At least logarithmically many separately applied scan factors would entail that many passes, but no universal work lower bound is claimed.

## 3. Exact complementary-cut rank identity

For any invertible matrix T and coordinate set I with complement Ic,

rank T[I,Ic]=rank T^(-1)[I,Ic].

Let Vc be the coordinate subspace on Ic. The two restricted nullspaces have dimensions dim(Vc intersect T^(-1)Vc) and dim(Vc intersect TVc), and T maps the first intersection bijectively onto the second. This proves the equality without requiring an invertible principal subblock.

Applying it to B, the diagonal A contributes nothing across a complementary cut, giving

rank B[I,Ic]=rank F^(-1)[I,Ic]

for every I. If I is a cyclic contiguous interval of m=floor(N/2) positions, factoring out its starting phase leaves a consecutive-row Vandermonde rectangle on distinct roots. Its rank is m.

Noncontiguous sets need not have maximum cut rank, particularly at composite lengths. An independent N=9 control with I={0,3,6} has cut rank two for both B and F^(-1), below the possible three. This confirms the exact inheritance statement while preventing an erroneous all-cuts-full-rank generalization.

## 4. Crossing budgets and endpoint scope

For row-action W[i,pi(i)]=w_i, the crossing rectangle of I-W is a partial matching. The inverse-cut identity therefore bounds the corresponding Green crossing rank by

e_pi(I)=#{i in I: pi(i) in Ic}.

Zero weights may reduce the actual rank. Diagonal endpoints do not increase it, even if those endpoints are singular. Endpoints preserving the tested cut also preserve the relevant bound. Arbitrary monomial endpoints can route across the cut themselves; their crossing ranks must be included as additional terms, rather than silently omitted.

The two standard inequalities

rank(U+V)[I,Ic]<=rank U[I,Ic]+rank V[I,Ic],
rank(UV)[I,Ic]<=rank U[I,Ic]+rank V[I,Ic]

hold by rank subadditivity and splitting the intermediate coordinates. Consequently a sum/product expression with r diagonal-ended Green occurrences must supply sum_s e_pi_s(I)>=m on every cyclic half interval. More general endpoint routing adds its own budget. Counting expression occurrences is not a DAG-sharing work lower bound.

Two diagonal-ended natural-cycle terms have only two crossings per half interval and are excluded for odd N>=7. Conversely the jump pi(i)=i+m is Hamiltonian, because gcd(m,N)=1, and crosses every half interval m times. It passes this capacity gate as a single term but is excluded by the inverse-support bound. Passing a crossing test supplies no phase correctness.

## 5. Circular-displacement identity

For N=2m+1, fix an edge from pi(i) to i. Among all N cyclic intervals of length m, the number containing i but not pi(i) is exactly the shorter circular distance dist_N(i,pi(i)). One way to count it is to subtract the number of intervals containing both endpoints from the m intervals containing i; for distance delta<=m the overlap count is m-delta.

Summing each necessary half-cut budget over all interval starts gives

sum_s sum_i dist_N(i,pi_s(i))>=N m.

Thus the mean circular displacement over rN listed cycle edges is at least m/r. For two diagonal-ended terms the mean must be at least (N-1)/4. This rejects bounded local routing at growing N under those endpoint restrictions; it does not reject arbitrary global orders. Zero-weight edges are counted as potential capacity, so the condition remains necessary but can be loose.

## 6. Additive Green identity and singular branches

For individually invertible branch maps with sparse inverses Q0,Q1,

B=Q0^(-1)+Q1^(-1) iff Q0 B Q1=Q0+Q1.

The order is correct. If the target B is invertible, then Q0+Q1 is invertible and B^(-1)=Q1(Q0+Q1)^(-1)Q0. This specifies the remaining coefficient-synthesis problem; a dense inverse used to inspect it is not free construction work.

A singular endpoint-scaled branch need not admit Q_s at all. The inverse parametrization cannot simply delete such a branch from a broader additive class. The source explicitly retains those branches under the cut tests, which remain valid. No unrestricted two-Green exclusion is inferred from the identity.

## 7. Inherited inverse-defect extension

The earlier reviewed Fourier-rigidity lemma gives rank(E)>=floor(N/(d+1)) when F^(-1)=S+E and S has maximum row degree d. Its consecutive-row Vandermonde proof is field-algebraic and permits singular S.

If B^(-1)=S+E, subtracting the known diagonal A makes F^(-1)=(S-A)+E, and S-A has row degree at most d+1. Hence

rank(E)>=floor(N/(d+2)).

For inverse factors G_i^(-1)=S_i+E_i, telescoping with full inverse factors to the left and sparse factors to the right gives an error of rank at most sum_i r_i from baseline S_1...S_L, whose row degree is at most D=product_i d_i. Thus

sum_i r_i>=floor(N/(D+2)).

The product order and rank argument are correct for noncommuting factors. Monomial endpoints preserve the required support/rank descriptions; arbitrary dense endpoints do not. This is correctly labeled an application of the inherited lemma, not a new general Fourier lower bound. It requires actual inverse decompositions and does not certify that finding those decompositions is cheap.

## 8. Independent exact controls

The independently authored standard-library checker passes seven target cases, including odd primes and composites and the prescribed cancellation example. It verifies 65 cyclic half cuts, 195 Green inverse/cut identities, corresponding singular-endpoint cut controls, and 21 circular-displacement sums.

It also checks 18 pure-product support controls with arbitrary monomial endpoints and correct inverse-factor order, six noncommuting full-prefix/sparse-suffix defect telescopes, and 28 target inverse-defect inequalities for row degrees zero through three. The separate N=9 noncontiguous-cut control prevents an overstrong rank claim.

No uploaded or constructor code was executed or imported. Dense matrices are finite proof controls only. All checks and an isolated copied replay pass; source snapshot, code, proof and results are sealed separately from prior reviews.

## 9. Accepted consequence

The fixed chirp B cannot have a bounded-length pure sparse-denominator Green product, and any candidate additive representation must provide substantial cut capacity and satisfy the exact coefficient equations. These are necessary rejection/design tests. General independently ordered paired-Green sums remain open, and no cheap B or faster FFT has been constructed here.
