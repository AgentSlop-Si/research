## Mathematical scope

Forms a quartic lower condition and three upper resultants in eight variables, then seeks a nonzero 4×4 Jacobian minor over GF(p). This is evidence for algebraic independence of the specialized four polynomials. Lifting to Q(zeta8), as the returned status claims, also requires that these are verified reductions of the intended exact characteristic-zero polynomials at an admissible prime/root.

## Guards and acceptance

The default root is supplied by primitive_root(p,8); a caller-provided root and prime are not checked. There are no chart-denominator or equation-zero checks on the random witness point. Those are unnecessary for polynomial independence, but mean the point is not a feasible factorization witness or a smooth point on the common zero set.

## Tests and certificate limits

Lines 23–38 require rank four twice, recompute a pivot determinant, and assert it is nonzero. At most trials random points and up to binomial(8,4)=70 column sets are examined; failure raises ValueError. The regression checks a deterministic minor value and selected columns, not a standalone externally supplied certificate validator.

## Cost and implementation

Fixed n=8 polynomial construction dominates; Jacobian evaluation scales with generated monomial counts. Numeric row reduction uses int64 and inherits prime-size overflow restrictions. Algebraic independence neither proves the four-equation zero locus has codimension four everywhere nor proves any infeasibility.
