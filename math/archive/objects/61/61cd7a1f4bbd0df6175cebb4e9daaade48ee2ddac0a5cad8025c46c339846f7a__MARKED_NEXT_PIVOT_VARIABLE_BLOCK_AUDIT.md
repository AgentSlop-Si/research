# Independent audit: next-pivot support reduction and variable-bit frontier work

## Decision

**Accept both bounded construction milestones.** The next parity pivot has the stated weighted-tree/transfer decomposition and exact support reduction. The variable-bit elimination has the stated Schur and packet identities, with an exact finite-horizon frontier count and a tight work bound for its particular fully materialized frontier evaluator.

Neither milestone provides the remaining coupled inverse on polynomial unknowns or a closed recursive Fourier algorithm. In particular, setting b near log₂log₂N already makes the proposed frontier evaluator cost Θ(N log N). The useful advances are explicit exact representations and correctly charged bounded operations, together with a precise account of the still-unsolved inverse.

Two new independent scalar controls sharpen the guard boundary:

- Next pivot: N=32, GF(65537), z=2 has reference-tree guard δ=0 but det Z=25365. The coupled pivot is invertible despite failure of that reference inverse.
- Variable packet: N=4, GF(17), b=2, z=8 has individual branch determinants 3 and 14, while the combined packet determinant is zero. The full matrix I−zQ has determinant 7. Neither separate branch guards nor full-system invertibility license that eliminated chart.

The checks include 35 next-pivot cases with 30 guarded inverse/marked identities, 10 complete reduced-operator jets through N=256, 56 variable-bit jets through N=128, 196 basis-input checks, 87 guarded Schur/packet/marked solves, and 329 exact integer frontier-count cases. All controls and the relocated replay pass.

## 2. Shared conventions and field guards

Let N=2^n and let ω be a supplied primitive Nth root in a field, with N invertible. The underlying directed operator is

    Q e_s = e_(2s mod N) + ω^s e_(2s+1 mod N).

A one-edge is weighted by the root power belonging to its actual source state. Root powers must be prepared once or generated by a charged recurrence; arbitrary per-edge exponentiation is not a free scalar operation. The formal variable z is independent of the field data. Formal inverses with constant term I exist without evaluating a scalar pivot.

Finite tests use explicitly verified root order. The fixed small prime fields are test fixtures, not a root-finding or primality algorithm. Scalar inversions are charged separately when translated to bit operations. No complex numerical stability, coefficient-size, or floating-point claim follows from these finite-field checks.

## 3. Next-pivot decomposition: accepted with exact orientation

For N≥16, put l=N/4 and k=N/8. The preceding odd pivot is D=I_l−zB₁, where

    B₁ e_j = ω^(4j+3) e_(2j+1 mod l).

Its unique root has weight ω⁻¹, hence det D=q=1−zω⁻¹. The odd restriction of the next Schur block is exactly

    Z = I_k − z²T − z³ U D⁻¹ V,

with

    T e_s = ω^(16s+10) e_(4s+2 mod k),
    V e_s = ω^(8s+5) e_(4s+2 mod l),
    U e_j = ω^(8j+6) e_(2j+1 mod k).

Here A₀e_s=e_(2s mod N), J e_j=e_(2j+1), and (Cx)_j=ω^j(x_j−x_(j+N/2)). Our oracle constructs C K J directly from the finite polynomial K=Σ_(t<n) z^t(A₀^t−e₀1ᵀ), forms the first Schur block, and extracts its odd rows/columns. Thus the verification is not merely a comparison of two copies of the claimed T,U,V formulas. All entries and phases agree.

The map of T is s↦4s+2. Since 3 is invertible modulo the power-of-two k, it has a unique fixed point s*=−2/3 mod k. Subtracting s* makes every iteration multiplication by 4, so every vertex reaches the root in at most ceil(log₂k/2) steps. Therefore A=I−z²T is a one-cycle-root tree resolvent with

    det A = δ = 1−z² ω^(16s*+10).

At a scalar z, its tree solver requires δ≠0. This has no bearing on formal existence around zero, where δ has constant coefficient one.

## 4. Rank and exact row support

Repeatedly applying the affine map of B₁ gives

    U B₁^a V e_s
      = ω^((48·2^a−8)s + 36·2^a−9−a)
        e_(2^(a+3)s + 3·2^(a+1)−1 mod k).

This is independently checked for every entry through a=n+1 in the finite cases. At a=0, UV maps s to 8s+5 with nonzero weight ω^(40s+27), so its rank is max(k/8,1). A minor with nonzero constant term proves the same lower bound for rank(U(I−zB₁)⁻¹V) over the rational-function field. It applies generically after specialization, not at every scalar. In particular the entire correction z³U(I−zB₁)⁻¹V vanishes at z=0.

The union of reachable rows has one element at k=2, and k/4+1 elements at k≥4. For k=2^r≥4, the residue classes for a=0,...,r−3 are disjoint and contribute k/4−1 positions. The last two positions are k/2−1 and k−1; later powers remain at k−1. The small k=4 case has no earlier residue classes and still gives those two positions. Thus the upper rank bound is k/4+1=N/32+1 for N≥32.

This is an exact image-support theorem for the specified reference A. It does not optimize rank over all possible reference trees, prove the exact generic rank at every size, or lower-bound arbitrary arithmetic work. The support union, rather than the rank lower bound alone, supplies the smaller coordinate interface.

## 5. Support Woodbury reduction and markers

Let E inject into the sorted reachable-row set S, and W=Eᵀ U D⁻¹V. Then Z=A−z³EW. On the chart qδ≠0,

    R_small = I − z³ W A⁻¹ E,
    Z⁻¹ = A⁻¹ + z³ A⁻¹ E R_small⁻¹ W A⁻¹,
    det Z = δ det R_small.

These follow by multiplying the proposed inverse by Z, or by eliminating the auxiliary coordinate in the corresponding 2×2 block system. No approximation or bounded-rank assumption is used.

For arbitrary constant or formal vectors x,h, the bilinear identity is

    hᵀZ⁻¹x = hᵀA⁻¹x
      + z³ (hᵀA⁻¹E) R_small⁻¹ (W A⁻¹x).

The identity remains algebraically valid on polynomial unknowns. Its inexpensive constant-input evaluator does not supply a cheap algorithm for those polynomial-vector products or for R_small⁻¹.

The explicit δ=0 control above is material: at N=32, GF(65537), z=2, q=49154 and det Z=25365. The rational coupled pivot exists and is invertible; only the chosen A⁻¹ chart fails. Another control at N=16, GF(257), z=2 has δ=0 and det Z=247. Three q=0 controls are correctly left unevaluated through D⁻¹. A failed chart is not a theorem of Fourier impossibility.

## 6. Constant-input next-pivot jet: charged finite horizon

For a constant vector v on S, the degree-n reduced-operator jet is

    R_small(z)v = v
      − Σ_(a,b≥0, 3+2a+b≤n)
          z^(3+2a+b) Eᵀ U B₁^b V T^a E v.

After T^a and V, the structural support has size at most max(k/(2·4^a),1). Each subsequent B₁ step halves the noncollapsed support. Thus the intermediate state count obeys

    Σ_(3+2a+b≤n) max(k/(2·4^a·2^b),1)
      ≤ 4k/3 + O(n²).

Generating the T prefixes, routing V and U, and accumulating contributions have the same order bound. Because n²=O(N), one constant-input prefix has O(N) field/address work and sparse coefficient storage. Roots, input initialization, row routing and final coefficient/marker accumulation are included.

This requires an actual sparse representation. Writing every zero into a dense (n+1)×|S| coefficient rectangle would cost Θ(N log N); that is not the claimed output representation. Our checker implements reusable stamped state arrays, emits sparse (degree,row,value) contributions, and combines them by two deterministic radix-N passes. Keys are below N², so the sort/merge costs O(N+number of contributions), without assuming unit-time hash tables or reallocating a length-N workspace at each transition. The checker’s dense comparison oracle and diagnostic assertions are deliberately outside that production schedule.

All coefficients, not just each transition separately, are compared with independent full-vector tree-power convolution through degree n. Ten checks cover N=16,...,256 in two fields and preserve a nontrivial arbitrary constant marker. The test logs record exact state visits, sparse slots and contribution counts.

The state still carries ambient tree workspaces of dimensions N/8 and N/4. A reduction of the retained coordinate count does not prove these workspaces shrink proportionally at the next stage. Neither this jet action nor the support dimension proves recursive closure.

## 7. Variable-bit Schur elimination: accepted

For 1≤b≤n, retain R={s:2^b divides s} and eliminate E=Rᶜ. Write D_E=I−zQ_EE. Then

    S_b = I_R − zQ_RR − z²Q_RE D_E⁻¹ Q_ER,
    S_b y_R = x_R + zQ_RE D_E⁻¹x_E,
    y_E = D_E⁻¹(x_E + zQ_ER y_R).

Substituting these equations into the full system proves the elimination. Multiplying by a left marker preserves

    hᵀy = h_EᵀD_E⁻¹x_E
      + (h_Rᵀ + z h_EᵀD_E⁻¹Q_ER)y_R.

D_E(0)=I, so formal elimination is always defined. At a scalar parameter, both the eliminated and retained coupled pivots must exist. The full matrix may be invertible even when one block chart fails.

The retained direct operator Q_RR is indeed the unweighted doubling tree on N/2^b coordinates. The difficult term is the coupled first-return operator, not that isolated tree.

## 8. Exact finite-horizon frontier count

Initialize every eliminated coordinate, including any whose particular input amplitude happens to be zero. At level L, the structural support of Q_EE^L on arbitrary initial input consists exactly of N-bit states whose suffix of length

    d_L = min(n,b+L)

contains no run of b zeros. The reason is a direct path condition: the initial state and the L subsequent states must each avoid the retained suffix 0^b. Reading backward from the final state converts those constraints into consecutive b-bit windows in that suffix. Unspecified initial bits can be chosen as ones. For L≤n there is at most one length-L shift-register path between a specified start/end pair; its phase is nonzero, so this structural count does not assume cancellation-free sums of multiple same-entry paths beyond the stated horizon.

Let A_b(t) count length-t binary words avoiding 0^b. Then A_b(t)=2^t for t<b and A_b(t)=Σ_(j=1)^b A_b(t−j) thereafter. Consequently

    F_(N,b,L) = 2^(n−d_L) A_b(d_L),
    S(N,b) = Σ_(L=0)^n F_(N,b,L)
           = N Σ_(t=b)^(n−1) A_b(t)/2^t + (b+1)A_b(n).

These are exact potential coefficient-slot counts, not counts of free bit words alone. The checker independently computes A_b via a trailing-zero finite-state automaton and also enumerates the actual suffix predicate at all finite jet sizes. It compares the entire weighted vector at every level with dense powers, including all 196 selected basis inputs across two fields.

The checker’s real frontier branch uses a shared N-entry root table, two reusable scalar arrays, visit stamps and active-index lists. It performs at most two outgoing edge checks per active state, charges every retained weighted multiplication and duplicate merge, and clears only newly visited targets. Full jets store S slots; marker contractions cost O(S). There is no implicit Θ(N) reset at each degree. Its ledger records root preparation, scalar updates, target initialization, marker operations and storage.

## 9. Exact work bounds and edge cases

The word generating function is

    Σ_(t≥0) A_b(t)u^t
      = (1+u+...+u^(b−1))/(1−u−...−u^b).

At u=1/2 the sum is 2^(b+1)−2. The probabilities A_b(t)/2^t are nonincreasing, and d_L≥L. Therefore

    S(N,b) ≤ N min(n+1, 2^(b+1)−2).

For a lower bound, the suffix at level L has at most L+1 candidate forbidden windows. If L+1≤2^(b−1), the union bound leaves at least half of all N states. Summing those initial levels gives

    S(N,b) ≥ (N/4) min(n+1,2^b).

Thus the full-frontier evaluator has Θ(N min(n+1,2^b)) work because it explicitly processes/stores those structural slots. This is not a lower bound for other circuits, a single marked coefficient, or the DFT.

Important exact boundaries:

- b=1: S=N, including N=2.
- b=n: S=(n+1)(N−1), although the total allowed word count through degree n is only 2N−2.
- b=floor(log₂n), n≥2: 2^b is within a constant factor of n, so this evaluator costs Θ(Nn).

All formula manipulations and both bounds pass 329 independent exact integer cases, including count-only parameters through n=512. Those huge-N cases are integer computations, not executions of exponentially large transforms. Counts use O(log N)-bit words up to constant factors; the O(nb) dynamic program is within O(N) for b≤n. No probabilistic estimate substitutes for the finite-horizon formula.

## 10. Packet formulation and its coupled guard

Eliminating the intermediate trailing-zero classes leaves the ones-ending interface of dimension m=N/2. Its packet operator is

    P_b(z)=Σ_(a=0)^(b−1) z^(a+1) T_a,
    T_a e_j = ω^(2^a(2j+1))
                e_(2^(a+1)j+2^a mod m).

This follows directly from appending a zeros and then one, with the one-edge weight at the correct source state. Let r=N/2^b and

    W_b e_j = ω^(2^b j) e_(2^b j mod m),
    V_b e_j = e_(2j+1 mod r).

The retained operator is exactly

    S_b = I_r − zA_(0,r)
          − z^(b+1) V_b (I_m−P_b)⁻¹ W_b.

The internal zero-only chains have no cycles and their eliminated diagonal is I. Hence their determinant contribution is one: det D_E = det(I_m−P_b). Our checks verify this determinant equality in addition to the claimed operator and marked identities. The interface inside the unresolved packet inverse is N/2, regardless of the smaller final retained dimension.

For b≥2, the T₀ and T₁ maps compose in the two orders to 8j+5 and 8j+6. Their distinct nonzero routes prevent a naive commuting-tree simplification. The first-return language permits arbitrarily many alternating packets; a two-run lemma cannot simply be repeated without a new invariant.

The separate-guard counterexample in the decision paragraph is fully explicit. At N=4, GF(17), primitive ω=13, b=2, z=8, the matrices I−zT₀ and I−z²T₁ have determinants 3 and 14. Yet det(I−P₂)=det D_E=0, while det(I−zQ)=7. Independent examples also occur at N=8, GF(97), b=3, z=84, and N=16, GF(97), b=2, z=15. Conversely, N=8, GF(257), z=2 gives nonsingular D_E but a singular retained/full operator for all three possible b. Each required guard is tested independently.

## 11. Verification strength and cost caveats

The constructor verifiers are useful bounded algebraic oracles. They call `pow(omega,s,p)` at many individual transitions, use dictionaries, build dense matrices and perform dense inversions. Those programs do not themselves implement all details of the claimed linear/table-driven schedule. This is a verifier-versus-algorithm distinction: the reports explicitly describe the shared root table and stamped frontier program, which our independent implementation realizes. The primitive descriptions plus the proofs justify the stated field/word-work bound, not the constructor verifier’s runtime or its edge count in isolation.

The next-pivot checker additionally verifies every weighted transfer coefficient, complete reduced-operator jets, all inverse entries and preserved bilinear values. The variable-bit checker checks direct Schur elimination against the independently built packet representation, dense full-system recovery, all marker identities, suffix-language support and exact finite-horizon counts. Scalar chart failures are recorded rather than silently skipped as successes.

The accepted outcome is a constant-input jet operator. Formal right-hand sides generated during recursive elimination generally contain polynomial coefficients. Feeding them into the same program introduces coefficient products/convolutions and repeated packet interactions. No supplied analysis charges and closes those operations, no fast retained inverse is constructed, and neither report supplies T(N)=T(N/2^b)+O(N). No general FFT advance, unrestricted lower bound or historical novelty claim is approved by this audit.
