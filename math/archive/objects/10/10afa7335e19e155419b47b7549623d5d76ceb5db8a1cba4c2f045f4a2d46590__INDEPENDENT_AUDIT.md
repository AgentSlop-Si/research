# Independent audit: ramified content extension

## Verdict

**Accept the conditional ramified-content valuation theorem, the reciprocal determinant reduction, and all three new fixed-base computations. No substantive mathematical correction is required.**

For positive `s≡1 (mod4)`, prime `p≡1 (mod4)`, and `c_s≠0` with `p∤s c_s`, the submitted argument proves

`c_(sp)≠0` and `v_p(c_(sp))=floor(3s(p−1)/8)`.

In particular, for every prime `ℓ≡1 (mod4)`, it proves

`v_ℓ(c_ℓ)=floor(3(ℓ−1)/8)`.

Fresh exact arithmetic reproduces every submitted coefficient of the ordinary discriminant, cluster resultant, their product, contracted discriminant, endpoint factors, and reduced multiplication determinant for `d=17,21,25`. In particular,

`c_17=17^6`, `c_21=3^11·7^9`, `c_25=5^17`.

**The uniform assertion that no prime outside `d` divides `c_d` remains unproved.** Neither the valuation theorem nor the fixed-base computations establish that assertion. The source correctly retains this stopping boundary.

Two small clarifications are useful, but do not change the theorem: state `d=4r+1≥5` when defining the two removed cluster factors; and make the choice of the good upper prime explicit via `ζ_(sp)↦ω̄` in a residue field. The latter detail is supplied in §4 below.

## 2. Reciprocal identities, signs, and exact division

Let `d=4r+1≥5`, `H=H_(2r)`, and `H(z)=z^r P(z+z^−1)`, with `P` monic of degree `r`. Let `C_d` be defined by

`(z^d+1)/(z+1)=z^(2r)C_d(z+z^−1)`.

Factoring `P` into linear factors and `H` into reciprocal monic quadratics gives the exact ordinary-discriminant identity

`Disc(H)=P(2)P(−2)(Disc P)^2`.

The quadratic factors have discriminants `a_i²−4`; their pairwise resultants are `(a_i−a_j)^2`. Thus no discriminant sign has been lost. Pairing the cluster nodes `−q^j` and `−q^(−j)` gives

`R_d=H(−1)Res(C_d,P)^2`.

The leftover node is exactly `−1`; the powers introduced by the two reciprocal nodes multiply to one. Since `P(2)=H(1)` and `P(−2)=(-1)^r H(−1)`, their product is exactly

`D_(2r)R_d=(-1)^r H(1)[H(−1)Disc(P)Res(C_d,P)]²`.

This is an identity of ordinary discriminants and ordinary resultants. It neither uses nor substitutes a q-difference discriminant.

The roots `η_j=−q^j−q^(−j)`, `1≤j≤2r`, are distinct in every characteristic not dividing `d`, including characteristic two. Equality of two sums `a+a^−1=b+b^−1` implies `(a−b)(ab−1)=0`; the chosen index range has no duplicate reciprocal pair.

The quotient

`C_d^*=C_d/[(u−η_1)(u−η_(2r))]`

is integral. One may either use its explicit product of the other linear factors in `O_d[u]`, or exact successive monic division. No division by two or by a difference of roots occurs. At `d=5`, this quotient is `1`; the formula is not intended at `d=1`.

For `M_d=Res(P,(u−2)P'C_d^*)`, the exact sign is

`M_d=(-1)^(r+r(r−1)/2) H(1)Disc(P)Res(C_d^*,P)`.

Indeed `Res(P,u−2)=(-1)^rP(2)`, `Res(P,P')=(-1)^(r(r−1)/2)Disc(P)`, and swapping `P` and `C_d^*` contributes `(-1)^(r(2r−2))=1`. Its determinant interpretation follows because `P` is monic and multiplication is on the free rank-`r` quotient module.

Every one of these exact identities, with its sign, was checked in each of the three new bases.

## 3. The unit claims and unramified content criterion

The three classical special values used in the source are correct:

- `H_(2r)(−1;q)=∏_(j=1)^r(1−q^(2j−1))`;
- `H_(2r)(−q;q)=H_(2r)(−1;q)`;
- `H_n(t;t²)=∏_(j=1)^n(1+t^j)`.

They are located in Vinroot's primary paper, equations (2.3), (2.5), and (2.6), with the recurrence in (1.1): [primary paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1011.0984). The identities can also be obtained by the recurrence, so no convergence or positivity premise enters the argument.

At any prime above a rational `p∤d`, reduction preserves the exact order `d` of a primitive `d`th root. Consequently all factors in the alternating product are nonzero. Powers of roots of unity are units, so the second identity makes `P(η_1)` a unit. For `t=−q^(2r+1)`, one has `t²=q`, and `t` is the reciprocal of the cluster node indexed by `2r`. In odd characteristic its order is `2d`; in characteristic two its order is `d`. None of the factors `1+t^j`, `1≤j≤2r`, vanish. Hence `P(η_(2r))` is a unit as well.

These statements hold at every prime away from `d`, establishing the claimed units in `O_d[1/d]`.

Now `O_d/pO_d` is a product of fields because `p∤d`. In each field component, the product `D_(2r)R_d` is zero exactly when `H(1)Disc(P)Res(C_d^*,P)` is zero, and thus exactly when `M_d` is zero. Squaring and the removed unit factors do not change these componentwise zero sets. Therefore

`p|c_d ⇔ B_d∈pO_d ⇔ M_d∈pO_d`.

The middle equivalence uses the canonical power basis, not a norm. It says the element vanishes in every component; it does not demand a unit in every component. Thus the source correctly distinguishes an existential good factor from an everywhere-good condition. The argument also works at `p=2`.

The proposed Galois-orbit Bézout identity would suffice for the missing global support assertion, but no such identity has been proved. This audit does not replace that missing step with finite tests.

## 4. Conditional valuation: integral division before specialization

Let `s≡1 (mod4)` be positive and `p≡1 (mod4)` prime with `c_s≠0` and `p∤s c_s`. Put

`A=(p−1)/2`, `b=(s−1)/2`, `d=sp`, `n=As+b`, `K=sA(A−1)/2`.

Write `G=H_b(z;ω)`, with `ω` primitive of order `s`, and `Δ=Disc G`, `R=Res(z^s+1,G)`. Since the canonical product remainder is not zero modulo `p`, there is at least one residue factor where both `Δ` and `R` are nonzero. In particular both are nonzero in characteristic zero. All their Galois conjugates are nonzero too. This validates the separated-cluster formula at every primitive `s`th root.

For `q=ω(1+ε²)^(1/s)`, the accepted discriminant calculation has exact order `K` in `Φ_s`, and the quotient value is

`(D_n/Φ_s^K)(ω)=Q_s(ω)^K C`,

`C=(-1)^(A(s−1)/2)s^(sA(A+1)/2)P_A^s ΔR^(2A)`,

where `Q_s=(q^s−1)/Φ_s` and `P_A=∏_(j=1)^A j^j`.

The half-integer-looking cluster scalings introduce no ambiguity here: the stated coefficient is in the cyclotomic field and has the exact integral exponents shown. At the chosen good residue factor, `C` and `Q_s(ω)` are units because `A<p`, `p∤s`, and `ΔR` is nonzero.

For `V_j(q)=H_n(−q^j;q)`, the cluster coordinate satisfies `x=O(ε)`. As `A` is even, the Hermite constant term is nonzero and gives

`V_j(q)=(q^s−1)^(A/2) κ G(−ω^j)+O((q^s−1)^(A/2+1))`,

`κ=(-s)^(A/2)(A−1)!!`.

The potentially weaker intermediate `O(ε^(A+1))` improves to `O(ε^(A+2))` because the specialized left side is an even formal series in `ε`; `A` is even as well. The sign follows directly from `He_A(0)=(-1)^(A/2)(A−1)!!`. Since `R≠0`, every `G(−ω^j)` is nonzero.

Galois conjugacy therefore gives exact order `A/2` at every root of `Φ_s` for each integer-polynomial `V_j`. Successive division by the monic integer polynomial `Φ_s` proves that every quotient belongs to `Z[q]`. This step is performed before reduction or evaluation at a higher cyclotomic root.

Let `J_d=∏_(j=0)^(d−1)V_j`. Each cluster index modulo `s` occurs `p` times, so

`(J_d/Φ_s^(dA/2))(ω)=Q_s(ω)^(dA/2)κ^dR^p`.

Combining the two integral quotients gives

`D_nJ_d=Φ_s^L E`, `E∈Z[q]`,

`L=K+dA/2=3sA²/2`.

The value `E(ω)` is a unit at the selected good residue factor. There is no denominator whose p-adic valuation has been ignored, and no inequality such as `L<p−1` is needed.

## 5. All upper primes versus one good upper prime

At `ζ=ζ_(sp)`, the values `−ζ^j` are exactly the roots of `z^(sp)+1`. Hence

`B_(sp)(ζ)=D_n(ζ)J_d(ζ)=Φ_s(ζ)^L E(ζ)`.

For every prime `𝔓|p` in `O_(sp)`, normalize `v_𝔓` to take value one on a uniformizer. Since `p∤s`, the ramification index is `p−1`. The element `ζ^s` is a primitive pth root; its difference from one has valuation one at every such prime. Reduction of `ζ` has exact order `s`, so `Q_s(ζ)` is a unit. It follows that

`v_𝔓(Φ_s(ζ))=1` for every `𝔓|p`.

Because `E(ζ)` is integral, the exact polynomial factorization proves

`v_𝔓(B_(sp)(ζ))≥L` for every `𝔓|p`.

This uniform lower bound does not rely on every lower residue factor being good.

For equality, let `ω̄` be the primitive `s`th root in the chosen good residue field `k`. The identity

`Φ_(sp) mod p=Φ_s^(p−1)`

ensures that `ζ↦ω̄` defines a surjective map `Z[ζ]→k`. Its kernel is a prime above `p`, and the image of `E(ζ)` is precisely the known nonzero `E(ω̄)`. Thus `E(ζ)` is a unit at that prime and the valuation equals `L` there. This also proves `B_(sp)≠0`.

This explicit residue-field construction avoids an otherwise harmless root-embedding choice. If one instead fixes the conventional lower generator `ζ^p`, the reduction changes by a Frobenius power; integer-polynomial evaluation preserves nonvanishing under that change.

Finally, `O_(sp)=Z[ζ]` has the canonical power basis. Therefore coefficient content satisfies

`v_p(c_(sp))=max{k:B_(sp)(ζ)∈p^kO_(sp)}`

`=floor(min_(𝔓|p) v_𝔓(B_(sp)(ζ))/(p−1))`

`=floor(L/(p−1))=floor(3s(p−1)/8)`.

The minimum and the floor are indispensable. A valuation at one prime alone would not justify a rational-content lower bound; the global integral quotient is what supplies the lower bound everywhere. Likewise a norm would weight and add valuations, and would not answer this content question.

The needed integral-basis and ramification statements are supported by Sharifi's Propositions 3.1.20–3.1.21, with the prime-power uniformizer in Lemma 3.1.13: [author's notes](https://math.ucla.edu/~sharifi/notes/algnum-ch03.html).

## 6. Prime specialization and exact local controls

Taking `s=1` and `c_1=1` gives the prime-base formula with no exception among primes `ℓ≡1 (mod4)`.

For `ℓ=4r+1`, the exact uniformizer exponents are

- ordinary discriminant: `r(2r−1)`;
- cluster resultant: `ℓr`;
- product: `6r²`.

Dividing by the ramification index `4r` and taking floors gives the source's separate contents:

`v_ℓ(content D)=floor((ℓ−3)/8)`,

`v_ℓ(content R)=r`,

`v_ℓ(c_ℓ)=floor(3r/2)`.

Their floors are not additive. In particular, `ℓ=17` gives separate exponents `1` and `4` but product exponent `6`; this is a correct ramification effect.

## 7. Complete fixed-base calculations and family exceptions

The fresh calculations match all six full arrays in each source base file: `B`, ordinary `D`, cluster `R`, `Disc P`, `H(1)`, and `P(−2)`. They also match every coefficient of each reduced determinant. The determinant contents are

`content(M_17)=17²`,

These need not have the same ramified exponents as `c_d`; the reduction theorem asserts only the unramified divisibility equivalence. The reconstructed arrays and all comparisons are saved in `REBUILT_17.json`, `REBUILT_21.json`, and `REBUILT_25.json`.

The accepted sufficient criterion therefore proves squarefreeness for

- `N=17p`, every odd prime `p≠17`;
- `N=21p`, every odd prime `p∉{3,7}`;
- `N=25p`, every odd prime `p≠5`.

The respective excluded orders are `289`, `63` and `147`, and `125`. None is supplied by these corollaries. For the subfamilies with `p≡1 (mod4)`, the `21p` exclusion is vacuous, since neither `3` nor `7` is `1 mod4`; the other two exclusions remain.

The inherited resonance and restricted common-cycle scalar-resolvent theorem yields the half-degree minimum only in its already accepted model and with the required congruence. This audit establishes no unrestricted inverse minimum or faster transform algorithm.

## 8. Boundaries and final conclusion

The restrictions are essential to this proof:

- `p∤s` gives the simple lower reduction and ramification index `p−1`;
- `p∤c_s` gives the necessary unit at one lower residue factor;
- `p≡1 (mod4)` makes `A` even and the Hermite constant nonzero;
- full cyclotomic division in `Z[q]` must occur before upper-root evaluation;
- rational content is governed by the minimum over every prime above `p`.

No repeated-p ramification theorem follows when `p|s`. No equivalent formula for `p≡3 (mod4)` follows from a Hermite constant that vanishes. No claim that all unramified residue factors are good is needed or true.

The mathematical extension is sound within these boundaries. The exact ramified valuation is proved uniformly; the unramified obstruction is genuinely reduced to a smaller determinant; and the finite-base family corollaries are fully supported by exact data. The general extraneous-prime-support problem is still open in this work. Correctness here does not establish publication novelty or literature priority.
