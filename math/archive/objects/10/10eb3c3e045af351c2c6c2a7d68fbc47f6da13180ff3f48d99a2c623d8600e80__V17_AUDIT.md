## 1. Two-Green sparse zero-rectangle theorem

The theorem allows invertible A and B supported on their main diagonal plus one weighted permutation graph. If T=A^{-1}+B^{-1}, then A T B=A+B. Choose two rows I following one permutation edge of A; their candidate support R has size at most three. If A's permutation is identity, use any two distinct rows. The corresponding A+B support lies in H=I union sigma_A(I) union sigma_B(I), of size at most five.

For N>=11, its complement S has more than N/2 elements. The inverse permutation tau_B has an edge entirely in S. If that edge is not fixed, its two endpoints give two columns J; if fixed, take that endpoint and any other element of S. Then C=J union tau_B(J) has size at most three, while (A+B)[I,J]=0. This explicit fixed-point branch is important and is implemented correctly.

U=A[I,R] and V=B[C,J] each have rank two because A and B are invertible. For a full-spark target, H0=T[R,C] has rank min(|R|,|C|), with both support sizes in {2,3}. The double Sylvester inequality gives

rank(U H0 V) >= 4−max(|R|,|C|) >=1,

contradicting its required zero value. The guaranteed rank is at least one; higher ranks observed in random controls are not the universal assertion.

The algebra tolerates zero diagonal entries, zero edge weights and fixed points whenever the complete A,B are invertible. Its class is slightly broader than a normalized I−DP denominator, but this does not escape the separator proof: arbitrary diagonal entries do not add crossing rank in a concatenated cycle order.

At complex prime length, classical Chebotarev full spark covers arbitrary endpoint permutations and invertible diagonal endpoint scales. The argument does not silently assume the same property at composite lengths or over all finite fields.

## 2. Relationship to the independently verified cyclic separator

The approved Green review established that any invertible diagonal-plus-weighted-permutation matrix has inverse offdiagonal cut ranks at most one in the concatenation of its cycle orders, including zero weights and several cycles. Two such inverse terms have a common strictly separated block of size

m=ceil(floor(N/2)/2)

and rank at most two, with arbitrary independent term gauges. At prime N>=11, m>=3 and the corresponding Fourier block has rank m by full spark. Thus the same prime cutoff was already a consequence of those ingredients. The uploaded zero-rectangle proof is a useful alternative local certificate, not a newly open or newly solved growing-size family.

The complex norm variant of the separator works also at composite lengths, giving the earlier condition m²<=2N and excluding N=30,31 and every N>=34. The v17 prime proof does not replace that broader composite-length statement.

Neither argument extends automatically to products of Green sums, non-diagonal outer mixers, or inverse stages with several independent permutation graphs. Three or more fixed additive terms are not an asymptotic escape by themselves: the general fixed-term separator remains relevant and must be checked before proposing another bounded additive bank.

## 3. Uploaded semiseparable annihilator theorem

For separated generators a_i b_j below the diagonal and c_i e_j above, a vector annihilating the local b/e triples cancels both exterior tails. The row-side statement is its transpose. Nonzero adjacent generator minors ensure the three overlapping cross-product vectors have nonzero leading coordinates in staggered positions, hence are linearly independent on their five-coordinate union.

Any five forbidden points miss a consecutive five-coordinate interval once N>=30: otherwise every one of the six gaps, including the end gaps, has length at most four, forcing N<=5+6*4=29. Therefore the localized input and output banks give a zero three-by-three product. A full-spark five-by-five Fourier block cannot send a three-dimensional image into the two-dimensional kernel of the other bank, proving the source's prime N>=31 statement.

Its genericity premise is sufficient but not intrinsic to the rank mechanism. For a general quasiseparable order-one stage, the exterior restriction on any five-coordinate interval has rank at most two, so its localized kernel has dimension at least three even when cross products vanish. Choosing an actual kernel basis removes that degeneracy caveat. More decisively, the boundary theorem in the companion note removes the premise and lowers the prime threshold to 11.

Separated generators are not the full quasiseparable class. The difference and its explicit invertible three-by-three counterexample were already established in the three-stage review. The new boundary result covers the larger class.

## 4. Implementation, numerical and field qualifications

The source is primarily a dense verifier and optional optimizer, not a new fast evaluator. Its sparse-matrix inversions, Fourier matrices and dense matrix products have the corresponding dense setup and arithmetic costs. Linear application of a supplied Green or separated-generator stage does not synthesize a Fourier representation.

The witness routine validates permutations by sorting, costing O(N log N) comparisons. It also reconstructs set(S) inside a candidate loop, which can cost O(N²) in the worst case. A bitmap validation and one prebuilt membership set repair these specific witness-preparation costs; no uploaded code was changed. These costs do not undermine the mathematical obstruction.

Finite-field checks explicitly confirm the selected small Fourier blocks have full rank. Their success is finite evidence, not an all-prime-field analogue of Chebotarev's characteristic-zero theorem. Unsatisfied numerical searches at N=5,6,8 do not prove impossibility or exhaust all cycle orders and initializations. A floating N=4 residual does not supply exact parameters, an all-size identity or a precision contract.
