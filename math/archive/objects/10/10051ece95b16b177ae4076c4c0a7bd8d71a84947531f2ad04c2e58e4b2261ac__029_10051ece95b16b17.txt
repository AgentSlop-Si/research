INVERSE SUPPORT CLOSES THE PURE-SCAN / ARBITRARY-SHUFFLE GATE
Verified 2026-10-09

RESULT

Let N > 1. Any exact factorization of an N-point Fourier matrix into t
serial pure one-state forward/backward scans, arbitrary diagonal factors,
and arbitrary coordinate permutations, including permutations BETWEEN
scans, satisfies

    t >= 1 + ceil(log_2 N).

The simpler maximum-row-support argument already gives t >= ceil(log_2 N).
The extra 1 uses the singleton boundary row of the first inverse scan.

For N = 2^m over C, and more generally over a field containing a primitive
N-th root of unity, an explicit construction uses at most 2m pure scans
with interleaved permutations. Consequently the minimum pure-scan count
is Theta(log N) for dyadic lengths. The exact minimum between m+1 and 2m
is not determined here, except when those bounds coincide.

This is an architecture-specific factorization theorem, not a general
Fourier arithmetic-circuit lower bound. The method does not extend as
stated to general scalar-state filters with direct input feedthrough.
No literature-priority claim is made.


1. PRECISE MODEL AND FIELD ASSUMPTIONS

All factors act on the same N-dimensional vector space over a field K.
All coefficients and permutations are input-independent. A pure forward
scan is precisely

    y_0 = b_0 x_0,
    y_i = a_i y_(i-1) + b_i x_i,       1 <= i < N.

A pure backward scan is

    y_(N-1) = b_(N-1) x_(N-1),
    y_i = a_i y_(i+1) + b_i x_i,       0 <= i < N-1.

Zero a_i values are permitted and reset the recurrence. The boundary
state is zero. Only the current N-vector is passed to the next factor:
there are no additional channels, saved side inputs, branches, sums of
operators, or rectangular expansion/compression stages.

Between scans one may apply arbitrary diagonal matrices and arbitrary
permutation matrices. There is no bound on their coefficients or on
permutation distance. Initial and final permutations are also allowed.

For the Fourier specialization, K contains a primitive N-th root omega,
and N is nonzero in K. Define F_N[k,j] = omega^(kj), 0 <= k,j < N. Then

    F_N^(-1)[j,k] = (1/N) omega^(-jk),

so every entry of F_N^(-1) is nonzero. Over C these assumptions hold for
every positive integer N. For dyadic N > 1 the root assumption already
excludes characteristic 2. No norm, stability, bounded-coefficient, or
positivity assumption is used.


2. INVERTIBILITY FORCES EVERY SCAN'S INPUT COEFFICIENT TO BE NONZERO

A product of square N-by-N matrices over a field is invertible only if
every factor is invertible. Each scan is triangular with diagonal b_i,
so its determinant is the product of all b_i. Therefore a scan occurring
in a factorization of an invertible target must have b_i != 0 for all i.
Every intervening diagonal factor must likewise be nonsingular.

For a forward scan, solving its defining equations gives

    x_0 = y_0 / b_0,
    x_i = (y_i - a_i y_(i-1)) / b_i.

Its inverse is lower bidiagonal. A backward scan has an upper-bidiagonal
inverse, by the identical calculation with i+1. In either direction,
each inverse row has at most two nonzeros, and a boundary row has exactly
one nonzero.

Inverse diagonal and permutation factors have exactly one nonzero per
row and column. Products of these factors are nonsingular monomial
matrices; multiplying by one merely rescales/reorders coordinates.


3. SUPPORT PROOF, INCLUDING THE STRONGER BOUNDARY-ROW VERSION

Write r(A) for the maximum number of nonzeros in a row of A. For arbitrary
square matrices A and B,

    supp((AB)[i,:]) is contained in
    the union of supp(B[k,:]) over k in supp(A[i,:]).

Hence r(AB) <= r(A) r(B). Cancellation only decreases support, so it
cannot invalidate this upper bound. Reversing and inverting the original
factorization therefore gives

    r(F_N^(-1)) <= 2^t.

As r(F_N^(-1)) = N, this proves t >= ceil(log_2 N), regardless of how the
permutations are interleaved.

For the stronger statement, first observe that t = 0 gives a monomial
matrix, which cannot have an everywhere-nonzero inverse when N > 1.
Thus t >= 1. In the inverse product, ignore any leading monomial factor
and take the singleton boundary row of the leftmost inverse scan. There
is a corresponding row of the whole product: the leading monomial
factor only reorders and rescales rows. Immediately after that inverse
scan, this row has support size 1. Every subsequent inverse scan can
multiply its support size by at most 2; all intervening monomial factors
preserve support size. Consequently at least one row of the final
inverse product has at most

    2^(t-1)

nonzeros. Since every row of F_N^(-1) has N nonzeros,

    N <= 2^(t-1),
    t >= 1 + ceil(log_2 N).

This argument actually applies to any invertible target whose inverse
has no zero entries, not only Fourier matrices. More generally, if every
inverse row has at least q nonzeros and t >= 1, it gives
t >= 1 + ceil(log_2 q). The case N = 1 is exceptional and needs no scans.


4. EXPLICIT 2 log_2 N UPPER BOUND FOR DYADIC LENGTHS

A radix-2 Fourier butterfly with nonzero twiddle w is

    B(w) = [[1, w], [1, -w]].

It factors exactly as

    B(w) = L U(w),
    L    = [[1, 0], [1, 1]],
    U(w) = [[1, w], [0, -2w]].

Place the disjoint pairs of a butterfly layer at adjacent coordinates
(2q, 2q+1), using a permutation. Allow w to vary with the pair.

First implement the block-diagonal U layer with ONE backward scan:

    b_(2q)   = 1,       a_(2q)   = -1/2,
    b_(2q+1) = -2 w_q,  a_(2q+1) = 0.

Indeed, the odd output is -2 w_q x_(2q+1); the preceding even output is
x_(2q) - (1/2)(-2 w_q x_(2q+1)) = x_(2q) + w_q x_(2q+1).
The odd-coordinate resets prevent interaction between pairs.

Next implement the block-diagonal L layer with ONE forward scan:

    b_i = 1 for all i,
    a_(2q) = 0,    a_(2q+1) = 1.

The even output stays unchanged; the odd output becomes the sum of the
two inputs to this L layer. Thus the pair outputs are exactly

    x_(2q) + w_q x_(2q+1),
    x_(2q) - w_q x_(2q+1).

The radix-2 identity follows directly by splitting the DFT input into
even and odd indices. If N = 2n, let E_k and O_k be their n-point DFTs
with root omega^2. Then, for 0 <= k < n,

    (F_N x)_k     = E_k + omega^k O_k,
    (F_N x)_(k+n) = E_k - omega^k O_k.

Recursing gives log_2 N layers of disjoint B(w) butterflies. Permutations
make each layer's pairs adjacent; resets allow all pairs and subproblems
to use the same two full-vector scan passes. Therefore 2 log_2 N pure
scan passes suffice, with O(log N) additional permutations. This is a
realization of the classical radix-2 FFT in the stated primitive family,
not a faster FFT algorithm.


5. WHAT WORK LOWER BOUND DOES AND DOES NOT FOLLOW

If the implementation model charges Theta(N) work for every full scan
pass, the lower bound on t immediately implies Omega(N log N) work.
For dyadic lengths the construction above costs O(N log N), including
O(N) indexed movements per inter-layer permutation, so this particular
full-pass architecture has Theta(N log N) optimal work.

The scan-count theorem remains true if permutations are free. However,
the theorem alone does NOT establish a work bound for implementations
that fuse factors, skip degenerate coordinates, avoid materializing
intermediate full vectors, or use a different arithmetic circuit. One
must separately justify the claimed Theta(N) charge per counted pass.

It also does not apply to rectangular factors with extra registers,
nonlinear/adaptive primitives, parallel channels added together, general
state-space output maps, or an arbitrary O(N)-cost wide-stage family.
The all-length claim is the lower bound; the matching logarithmic upper
bound proved here is for dyadic lengths only.


6. GENERAL FEEDTHROUGH: A CONCRETE FAILURE OF THE SPARSE-INVERSE LEMMA

Consider the one-state causal filter, over C,

    h_(-1) = 0,
    h_i = h_(i-1) + x_i,
    y_i = h_i + x_i.

This uses a direct input feedthrough term in addition to its state. Let
Z be the nilpotent lower-shift matrix, Z[i,i-1] = 1. Its input/output
matrix is

    T = I + (I-Z)^(-1) = (2I-Z)(I-Z)^(-1).

It is invertible, but

    T^(-1) = (I-Z)(2I-Z)^(-1)
           = (1/2) I - sum_(k=1)^(N-1) 2^(-(k+1)) Z^k.

Every entry on or below the diagonal is nonzero. In particular the last
row of T^(-1) has N nonzeros after ONE such feedthrough pass. For N >= 3,
this directly contradicts the proposed per-factor two-nonzeros-per-row
invariant in the enlarged model. The same example is the sum of an
identity channel and a pure prefix-sum channel.

Therefore the present proof cannot establish the logarithmic Fourier
pass bound for general feedthrough filters or sums of scan channels.
This counterexample DOES NOT exhibit a constant-pass Fourier algorithm
in the enlarged class, and it does not disprove a possible Fourier
lower bound there by a different method. That larger question remains
unresolved by this note.

An output scaling y_i = c_i h_i without direct feedthrough is different:
it is simply a diagonal factor after the pure scan and is already
covered when the total map is invertible.


7. VERIFICATION, RESEARCH STATUS, AND REFERENCES

The uploaded RESEARCH.md, README.md, and NEXT_GATE.md were read as static
text. No uploaded program was imported or executed.

A separate, newly written exact arithmetic check over F_257 compared the
entire matrix produced by the two-scan-per-butterfly construction against
F_N for N = 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, and 64. All checks passed. Each individual
scan was also inverted by the stated local formula during those checks.
The feedthrough inverse formula and its dense final row were separately
checked at N = 7 over the same field. These finite checks corroborate
the algebra; the all-size statements rest on the proofs above.

This closes the uploaded NEXT_GATE's o(log N)-pass possibility for its
SERIAL PURE-SCAN family even after arbitrary internal shuffles. It does
not close that gate for every input-independent O(N)-cost wide stage.

Historical caution: bidiagonal Fourier factorizations were studied long
before this project. Paul D. Gader's 1989 paper discusses 2N-2-factor
bidiagonal factorizations and systolic DFT evaluation, with no reduction
in floating-point operation count relative to direct evaluation. The
present short support argument is not accompanied by a novelty claim.
