## Executive result

- **Phase33 accepted within its stated scopes:** the two-Green finite-instance exclusion, q² nonsingular-chart dimension bound, local tangent obstruction, partial sixth-column interpolation, and inherited additive all-length obstruction are valid.
- **v19 packet fusion and rational-scan rank gate accepted**, with full coefficient/preprocessing and numerator costs retained.
- **v19's proposed sparse high-treewidth direct-inverse escape is incorrect in the stated model.** If A^-1 is monomially Fourier, A itself is dense. Its graph has treewidth N-1, irrespective of primality or diagonal dominance. A constant-degree sparse denominator cannot be such a direct inverse.

No exact general-input sub-NlogN Fourier algorithm, unrestricted arithmetic lower bound, product-of-sums exclusion, or publication-priority claim follows.

## 1. Phase33: exact two-sided two-Green certificate

For `M=L1 G1 R1+L2 G2 R2`, assume the endpoint diagonals are invertible and the first three columns match Fourier. Absorb each R_s[0,0] into its left diagonal, so those two entries become one. Put the remaining right entries on columns one and two at `(a,b)` and `(c,d)`, all nonzero.

In an output row, let the Green responses be `(A,C,E)` and `(B,D,H)`, and the target entries `(1,f1,f2)`. The target must lie in the span of the two scaled responses, so

`det [[A,B,1],[aC,bD,f1],[cE,dH,f2]]=0`.

Expanding gives the row equation

`(CH,-DE,-f2 BC,f2 AD,f1 BE,-f1 AH) dot (ad,bc,a,b,c,d)=0`.

Thus full rank six of the N-by-six coefficient matrix forces the compound vector to vanish, contradicting the nonzero right entries. This argument does not divide by a response minor and includes singular interpolation charts. A rank-deficient certificate is only an inconclusive necessary condition.

The independent checker reconstructs the specified seeded cycle instances and both primes. All sixteen first-six-row determinants, at N8 through N1024, agree exactly with the supplied residues. The fixed couplings are 3 and 5; every inverse guard and primitive-root condition is checked. A nonzero reduction of a guarded cyclotomic determinant proves nonvanishing in characteristic zero. Therefore each specified pair is excluded even with arbitrary complex invertible two-sided diagonals. A nonzero rational determinant also excludes a nonempty open set of coupling values for that fixed pair; exceptional couplings and different pairs remain outside that conclusion.

The source's term “non-affine” here is checked for the successor map as an affine map modulo N. It is not the earlier theorem's different property of being a binary-affine conjugate of the counter. These notions must not be silently identified.

## 2. Phase33: q² chart, local test, and partial positive extension

For q fixed Green operators and q prescribed columns, normalize the q right entries on one selected column to one. This leaves `q(q-1)` right parameters on the other selected columns. On the open chart where every row response matrix E_i is invertible, all q left entries in that row are uniquely determined by `l_i=E_i^-1 f_i`. A further column introduces q right entries. Its attainable vector is therefore a rational map of at most `q²` parameters, with image closure dimension at most q².

When N>q², a generic extra target vector is excluded. A particular Fourier column might still lie on that image, and singular charts are not covered. Nonzero endpoint requirements further restrict the domain; they do not increase this dimension bound.

At the all-ones right-gauge base point, with B_i=E_i and `h_is=G_s[i,j*]`, differentiating `E_i l_i=f_i` yields

`d y_i/d r_(s,ell)=-(h_i^T B_i^-1)_ell B_i[ell,s] l_is`,

`d y_i/d r_(s,*)=h_is l_is`.

Independent exact checks for q5 confirm ranks 25 and 26 after adjoining the residual, at N32,64,128,256,512,1024,4096. This is a first-order correction failure at those points, not a global nonlinear exclusion.

Solving one five-by-five system for the sixth column's five right entries preserves all five whole matched columns and matches the extra column in the first five rows. The other N-5 entries disagree at every checked size N8 through N4096. All left gauges, these right gauges, and the required pivots are nonzero. The formulas define algebraic characteristic-zero coefficients; guarded nonzero modular residuals certify failure of the remaining entries there as well.

The main supplied audit JSON omitted N512 and N4096, while the prose described extra checks. This review independently supplies those missing controls. It does not infer a full Fourier identity from matched columns or from finite-field arithmetic alone.

Relative to Phase32, the new result genuinely permits input-side tuning. It does not reopen the earlier full-transform claims. Relative to the character-profile checkpoint, these are arbitrary-order additive gauges rather than the fixed natural/bit-reversal projected paths; their local closures are different constructions.

## 3. Inherited additive separation theorem and sharper error floor

For q cycle orders, choose equal groups from the first order's opposite halves. In each subsequent order, sort their union and split it in half. One pair of opposite intersections contains at least half of each current group's size. Restrict to those intersections; earlier separations persist. The result is two disjoint sets of size `m=floor(N/2^q)` separated in every order.

A cyclic Green response on a separated rectangle factors into a row power times a column power, with one constant wrap multiplier if needed. Both endpoint diagonal gauges preserve rank at most one. Hence an additive sum of q terms has rank at most q on that common rectangle.

For unit-normalized Fourier, its m-by-m block has squared Frobenius norm `m²/N` and operator norm at most one. Exact equality therefore requires `m²<=qN`. In particular, a sublogarithmic number of such additive terms is impossible. Products or more general shared-state circuits are not covered; only a separate term-by-term implementation automatically incurs q linear scans.

The stated error floor is also correct and slightly sharper than the earlier denominator-m bound. The first q squared singular values sum to at most q, and the other m-q are each at most the square of the next singular value. Thus, when m>q and m²>qN,

`||M-Fhat_N||_2² >= (m²/N-q)/(m-q)`.

This combines the block norm restriction with the best-rank-q approximation bound. It is not a bit-complexity theorem. The fresh checker constructs separated groups and checks every entry of the rank-one identities for q2 through q5, up to N8192.

## 4. v19 positive fusion identity and its precise cost

For a primitive b-th root xi, with b invertible in the field, the roots-of-unity filter proves the rational identity

`(1/b) sum_r xi^(-rs)(I-xi^r X)^-1 = X^s(I-X^b)^-1`, for 0<=s<b.

It follows first as a formal power series and then as a rational identity wherever the inverses exist. In fact the algebraic identity applies to any square X under those guards. For the claimed cheap evaluator, X is alpha times a permutation. Decomposing the permutation into cycles constructs P^b and P^s in O(N) indexing work; the right side then requires one stride-permutation Green solve, one permutation, and scalar scaling. Powers and inverse guards are charged. The usual O(N) scalar-operation statement assumes the index/power parameters have the usual bounded representation; more explicitly scalar exponentiation contributes O(log b) preparation, and exact bit costs remain separate.

For arbitrary packet weights c_r, define `c_hat_s=(1/b)sum_r c_r xi^(rs)`. Then

`sum_r c_r(I-alpha xi^r P)^-1 = b sum_s c_hat_s(alpha P)^s (I-(alpha P)^b)^-1`.

The positive sign in c_hat is correct for this formula. Only the character-weight case collapses the numerator to one shift. General weights require computing their transform and applying the numerator; a dense b-term numerator is not silently O(N), and a direct evaluation costs O(bN). The source's prototype packet timings are not reproduced or adopted as Fourier performance claims.

Twelve independent matrix packet cases verify every character channel and arbitrary-weight formula. This is a useful exact shared evaluator for the specified same-order packet, not an exact Fourier transform.

## 5. v19 rational-scan rank gate

Let `T=q(C)^-1 p(C)`, where C is one N-cycle, q_0 is nonzero, q has degree d, p has degree e, and q(C) is invertible. In the meaningful range e<N, put `m=floor((N-e)/2)`, choose columns `B={0,...,m-1}`, and rows `A={m+e,...,2m+e-1}`.

The forcing `p(C)e_j` vanishes on A without wrap. If d<m, every column restricted to A satisfies the same order-d homogeneous recurrence. Its first d entries determine the rest, since subsequent recurrence rows reference only earlier entries within A. Therefore `rank T[A,B]<=d`. For d=0 the block is zero. When e>=N the displayed lower bound is vacuous unless the polynomial is first reduced modulo the cycle relation.

At prime N over characteristic zero, every square Fourier minor is nonzero, so arbitrary endpoint monomial equivalence forces `d>=floor((N-e)/2)`. The classical prime-minor input is credited to [Tao's primary paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0308286), presenting Chebotarev's theorem; no new general Fourier lower bound or positive-characteristic full-spark claim is made.

For a packet of width b, d=b and e<=b-1 yield `b>=ceil(N/3)`. A character packet with prime N and b<N is simply a shifted Green on the Hamiltonian cycle P^b. Forty-eight fresh recurrence-rectangle controls pass. These are one-common-order representation statements, not exclusions of general multi-order products or shared evaluators.

## 6. Corrected separator lemma, without principal-block guards

Partition the indices into U,S,V and let A be invertible. Complementary-block nullity gives

`rank A^-1[U,V] = rank A[U union S,S union V]-|S|`.

For completeness, a vector b supported on V whose solution `x=A^-1 b` vanishes on U is equivalent, by invertibility, to a vector x supported on S union V in the kernel of the displayed complementary A block. The two kernel spaces are isomorphic. Equating their nullities proves the formula.

If A[U,V]=0, that complementary block has rank at most 2|S|, since all its entries lie in |S| columns or |S| rows. Hence

`rank A^-1[U,V]<=|S|`.

Neither A[U,U] invertibility nor A[V,U]=0 is required. More generally the same argument yields `rank A^-1[U,V]<=rank A[U,V]+|S|`. This is a reusable separator/near-separator identity. Four independent exact controls deliberately make the principal U block singular and retain nonzero reverse V-to-U entries; the stronger formula passes.

The source's guarded separator lemma is therefore true but unnecessarily restricted. Its balanced-component grouping argument also works: if |S|<N/4, every component of size at most N/2 is smaller than two thirds of N-|S|, permitting two unions each of size at least one third of N-|S|.

## 7. Decisive correction: the direct Fourier-inverse model is already dense

Suppose `A^-1=L F_N R`, with invertible monomial L,R. Then

`A=R^-1 F_N^-1 L^-1`.

Every entry of F_N^-1 is nonzero. Monomial multiplication only permutes and nontrivially scales rows and columns, so **every entry of A is nonzero**. Thus its undirected sparsity graph is complete and has treewidth N-1. This argument holds at every Fourier length, without prime full-spark, separators, stability, or diagonal-dominance assumptions.

For `A=I-sum_(r=1)^g alpha_r P_r`, each row has at most g+1 supported positions. Density therefore requires `g>=N-1`. A constant-degree high-treewidth expander denominator is not an unclosed possibility for this direct-inverse model. That proposed next target in v19 must be removed or reformulated with additional factors that genuinely change the model.

There is a second incompatibility with the source's strict-row-diagonal-dominance hypothesis. Such a monomial Fourier inverse has entry magnitudes `r_i c_j`, all factors positive. Strict dominance in row i requires `c_i>sum_(j!=i)c_j`, or c_i more than half their total. Two different rows already contradict this for N>=2. Thus the source's stable direct-inverse theorem is true but has no nontrivial examples in its stated class.

This correction does not rule out sparse factors in a product, Schur-complement constructions, augmented-state realizations with nontrivial input/output maps, or implicit dense denominators with separate structure. It closes only the literal sparse N-by-N denominator whose inverse itself is monomially Fourier. Four exact monomial-Fourier inverse controls confirm all N² entries are nonzero, while the all-N conclusion follows from the proof above.
