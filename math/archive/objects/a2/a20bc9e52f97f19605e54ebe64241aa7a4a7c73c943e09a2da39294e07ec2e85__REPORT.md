Date: 9 October 2026

## Conclusion

The archive's restricted-model dimension results pass independent verification. The all-size upper-bound proof is valid, all 34 recorded finite-field Jacobian ranks reproduce, and all six saved full-row Jacobian determinants reproduce exactly. These establish the stated complex dimensions for N = 8, 16, 32. They do not establish saturation for arbitrary N or a Fourier factorization.

The audit additionally verifies gauge completeness at every saved subthreshold depth, and gives a simple proof extending full dimension to every larger depth for these same three sizes. Neither extension proves an FFT speedup.

## Model and verified results

A stage is the separated-generator matrix

    S[i,j] = L[i] V[j]   when i > j
           = d[i]        when i = j
           = U[i] W[j]   when i < j.

The product is S[t-1] P ... P S[0], with the same binary bit-reversal permutation P at every boundary for the numerical witnesses. The all-size upper bound allows arbitrary fixed boundary permutations.

## Review of the upper bound

The dimension of one stage is 5N-6 for N >= 3. To make the parameter count rigorous, consider the lower-triangular part on the dense open set of nonzero active generators. Its active coordinates are L[1], ..., L[N-1] and V[0], ..., V[N-2]. If two such parameter pairs give the same entries, comparison in column 0 fixes all L coordinates up to one common scale; comparison in row N-1 fixes every V coordinate by the inverse scale. Thus there is exactly one scale redundancy, giving lower-part dimension 2N-3. The upper part has the same dimension, independently of the N diagonal entries. The stage image closure is irreducible because it is the closure of a polynomial image of an affine space.

Invertible stages are a nonempty dense open subset: the identity is a stage, so the determinant polynomial is not identically zero. For a tuple of t invertible stages, introduce t-1 invertible diagonal matrices D[j] at the boundaries. In one-based notation, replace stage S[j] by

    D[j] S[j] P[j-1] D[j-1]^(-1) P[j-1]^(-1),

omitting nonexistent boundary factors. Left and right diagonal scaling preserve the stage form. Neighboring factors cancel in the total product, and the action is free: a fixed first invertible stage forces D[1] = I, followed inductively by all remaining D[j] = I. Each fiber therefore contains an orbit of dimension (t-1)N on the dense invertible locus. The image dimension is bounded by

    t(5N-6) - (t-1)N = N + t(4N-6),

and also by the ambient dimension N^2. Passing to the closure does not enlarge this beyond the image of the dense locus. This validates Theorem A, including exceptional parameter tuples.

A generic matrix consequently needs at least ceil((N^2-N)/(4N-6)) stages. If 4 divides N and N >= 4, this is N/4+1: the unrounded quotient is N/4 + (N/2)/(4N-6), strictly between N/4 and N/4+1. Allowing intermediate or endpoint permutations gives only finitely many image closures, so it does not evade the generic dimension obstruction.

This reasoning counts matrices and their actual product-preserving symmetries. It does not subtract gauge parameters from a raw generator count without accounting for the six local redundancies per stage.

## Why the finite-field certificates prove complex statements

All product entries and Jacobian entries are integer polynomials in the stage generators. A minor evaluated at the saved integer parameters is an integer. Its nonzero residue modulo either verified prime proves that integer is nonzero. Therefore the complex Jacobian has rank at least the corresponding modular rank at the same integer parameter point. The complex image-dimension upper bound supplies the reverse inequality.

One prime already suffices for each lower bound; the second is an independent arithmetic consistency check. No numerical tolerance or probabilistic rank estimate enters this argument. At full row rank the complex map is locally surjective at the witness and is dominant. Dominance does not assert that every specified matrix, including a Fourier matrix, occurs in the image.

For gauges there is a separate logical point: an equality JG = 0 observed only modulo a prime would not by itself prove the equality over the integers. Here the inclusion follows identically by differentiating the actual local scaling and cross-stage diagonal actions. The modular computations then certify independence of those exact gauge vectors and, together with the saturated Jacobian bound, determine the complex kernel dimension.

## Gauge checks and bounded extensions

At every saved depth with N + t(4N-6) < N^2, the gauge matrix has rank 6t + (t-1)N, exactly equal to the Jacobian nullity. The six local directions per stage consist of four inactive coordinates and the lower and upper generator-pair scalings. The remaining directions are matched row and column scalings across the permutation boundaries.

The archive singles out (N,t) = (8,2), (16,4), and (32,8), with nullities 20, 72, and 272. The additional audit checks establish the same completeness statement at all earlier saved depths for those three lengths. This is a finite collection of exact sample-point statements; it is not an all-N theorem.

At full threshold, the nullity, gauge rank, and remaining kernel dimension are:

| N | t | Jacobian nullity | Gauge rank | Additional kernel dimension |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8 | 3 | 56 | 34 | 22 |
| 16 | 5 | 144 | 94 | 50 |
| 32 | 9 | 416 | 310 | 106 |

There is also an immediate exact extension in depth. The identity matrix belongs to the stage family. Appending it as a new leftmost stage sends an existing output M to P M. Left multiplication by P is invertible, so full dimension persists. Repeating this proves full dimension for every t >= N/4+1 when N is 8, 16, or 32. Combined with the verified smaller depths, the dimension formula therefore holds for every t >= 1 at these three N values. This deduction adds no evidence for other N values. Because bit reversal is involutive, appending two identity stages even preserves the original output exactly.

## Identity shuffles and arithmetic scope

The identity-shuffle obstruction is valid. Across any cut, the upper-right block of AB is A_LL B_LR + A_LR B_RR, so its rank is at most the sum of the corresponding off-diagonal rank bounds; the lower-left block has the analogous property. Products of t order-one stages in the same ordering have quasiseparable rank at most t. A generic matrix has maximum such rank floor(N/2), so identity shuffles cannot be dominant when t < floor(N/2).

The full-charge scan count also checks: each stage uses N diagonal products, 2N lower-scan products, 2N upper-scan products, and 4N additions. At the demonstrated full-dimensional thresholds, the counts are:

| N | t | Multiplications | Additions | Total arithmetic | Permutation reads |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8 | 3 | 120 | 96 | 216 | 16 |
| 16 | 5 | 400 | 320 | 720 | 64 |
| 32 | 9 | 1440 | 1152 | 2592 | 256 |

These are counts for the unoptimized reference executor and saved generic witnesses. The symbolic threshold family has quadratic total arithmetic, and the witness matrices have not been shown to be Fourier matrices. A low-dimensional representable family can still contain a highly structured Fourier point; generic dimension obstruction alone is not a Fourier circuit lower bound.

## Reproducibility and limits

The supplement contains its own source, copied JSON witness inputs, the complete checks, and provenance. It imports no source code from the uploaded archive and does not execute the uploaded Python, C++, runner, or nested prior-checkpoint archive. The uploaded source was read as text solely to identify the stated model, coordinate order, and claims.

The independent Jacobian reconstruction uses the rank-one derivative u v^T for each local coordinate, then forms the global derivative (A u)(v^T B). The source checker instead materializes each local derivative matrix and propagates matrix products. This audit independently implements finite-field elimination in NumPy and cross-checks that eliminator with exact scalar-integer determinant expansion on small matrices.

Every modular matrix operand is reduced before multiplication. The largest relevant dot-product bound is 1440 * 65536^2 = 6,184,752,906,240, below signed-int64 capacity. Elimination updates involve only a single product of two reduced residues. Thus NumPy integer arithmetic cannot overflow within these audited configurations.

The original archive's prior checkpoint, external literature claims, and reported identity-shuffle sample ranks were not independently reproduced in this audit. The identity-shuffle theorem was checked directly as above. No general bit-reversal saturation theorem, Fourier factorization, literature-priority claim, or unrestricted Fourier arithmetic lower bound is certified here.
