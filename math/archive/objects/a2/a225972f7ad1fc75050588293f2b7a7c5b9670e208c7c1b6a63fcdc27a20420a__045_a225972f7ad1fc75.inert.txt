# A low-rank/induced-1-condition gate for Fourier factorizations

Independent verification of the candidate argument, 9 October 2026.

## Result and scope

Let F be the normalized N-by-N discrete Fourier matrix over the complex numbers. Suppose an exact factorization of F contains:

- t invertible Green factors, each having induced-1 condition number at most 7;
- B Householder factors, or more generally B factors H with rank(H-I) at most one;
- any number of unitary monomial factors (permutations and unit-modulus diagonal phases), and nonzero global scalar factors.

The order and choice of all these factors are arbitrary. Then

    7^t (B+1)^2 >= N.                                      (A)

Consequently t+B >= ceil(log_7 N). In the particular architecture with one reflection per Green stage, B=t and

    t log 7 + 2 log(t+1) >= log N,                          (B)

so t is Omega(log N). Here and below logarithms without a base are natural.

This closes the sublogarithmic-stage possibility for this bounded-induced-1 Green plus rank-one wrapper family, even though the reflections themselves may have growing induced-1 condition number. It is not a general Fourier-transform arithmetic lower bound, and does not settle the user's broader exact FFT target. No literature-priority or novelty claim is made.

The proof is actually valid with any flat unitary matrix in place of F. It uses no special number-theoretic property of Fourier entries.

## 1. Sparse-kernel norm lemma

For a vector x, let ||x||_p denote its usual complex l_p norm. For a matrix A, ||A||_1 is the induced l_1 norm, equivalently the largest absolute column sum. Define kappa_1(A)=||A||_1 ||A^-1||_1 when A is invertible.

**Lemma.** Let U be an N-by-N unitary matrix with |U_ij|=1/sqrt(N). If rank(U-A)<=r<N, with r a nonnegative integer, then

    ||A||_1 >= sqrt(N)/(r+1).                              (1)

A need not be invertible for this assertion.

**Proof.** Put s=r+1. Select any set S of s input coordinates. The restriction of U-A to vectors supported on S has rank at most r<s. Therefore it has a nonzero kernel vector x, supported on S, with Ax=Ux.

Flatness and the triangle inequality give, for every output coordinate i,

    |(Ux)_i| <= ||x||_1/sqrt(N)
              <= sqrt(s/N) ||x||_2.

Unitarity gives ||Ux||_2=||x||_2. For any vector y,

    ||y||_2^2 <= ||y||_infinity ||y||_1.

Thus

    ||Ux||_1 >= sqrt(N/s) ||x||_2.

Finally, ||x||_1<=sqrt(s)||x||_2, so

    ||A||_1 >= ||Ax||_1/||x||_1
             = ||Ux||_1/||x||_1
             >= sqrt(N)/s.

All denominators are nonzero since x is nonzero and U is invertible. This proves (1). The argument is valid for complex x: only absolute values, standard complex vector norms, and unitarity are used.

## 2. Passing to the inverse

Now assume A is invertible. The identity

    U^-1 - A^-1 = U^-1 (A-U) A^-1                        (2)

shows that rank(U^-1-A^-1)=rank(U-A). Both multipliers in (2) are invertible. No transpose, adjoint, or reversed product is substituted for an inverse in this identity.

Since U^-1=U* is again flat unitary, applying the lemma to U^-1 and A^-1 yields

    ||A^-1||_1 >= sqrt(N)/(r+1).

Multiplication with (1) gives

    kappa_1(A) >= N/(r+1)^2.                              (3)

Combining this with the universal inequality kappa_1(A)>=1 gives the optional stronger statement max(1,N/(r+1)^2).

**Large rank bounds.** If only rank(U-A)<=R is known for an integer R>=N, (1) cannot be asserted using R: for instance, A=delta I can have arbitrarily small norm. Nevertheless

    kappa_1(A) >= N/(R+1)^2                              (4)

remains true, trivially, because its right side is below 1. Therefore (4) holds for every nonnegative integer rank budget R, using (3) when R<N and kappa_1(A)>=1 otherwise. This distinction prevents applying the sparse-kernel step outside its range.

For a nonzero scaled Fourier target alpha F, normalize A to A/alpha. Its condition number is unchanged. The resulting condition-number theorem is identical; the two individual norm lower bounds acquire reciprocal factors |alpha| and 1/|alpha|.

## 3. Deleting the rank-one factors

Write the full factorization in its actual order as

    F = A_L A_(L-1) ... A_1.

At every Householder/rank-one position replace A_j=H_j by C_j=I, and retain every other factor: C_j=A_j. Define

    G = C_L C_(L-1) ... C_1.

Every remaining Green, monomial, and nonzero scalar factor is invertible, so G is invertible. The exact telescoping identity is

    F-G = sum over j of
          A_L ... A_(j+1) (A_j-C_j) C_(j-1) ... C_1.     (5)

The terms at unchanged positions vanish. Each of the B remaining terms has rank at most one, regardless of the surrounding factors, because multiplication cannot increase rank. Therefore

    rank(F-G) <= B.                                       (6)

The number B is the number of rank-one factors, not a claim that the sum in (5) has rank exactly B. Cancellations can make it smaller and only strengthen the conclusion.

For a complex Householder reflection, H=I-2uu*/(u*u) with u nonzero, rank(H-I)=1 and H is invertible. The uploaded real rational reflection is the corresponding real special case.

## 4. Bounding the retained product

Induced norms are submultiplicative, hence so is their condition number:

    kappa_1(A B) <= kappa_1(A) kappa_1(B).

A unitary monomial Q has ||Q||_1=||Q^-1||_1=1. A nonzero global scalar matrix alpha I has condition number 1. The retained product therefore satisfies

    kappa_1(G) <= 7^t.                                    (7)

By (6) and the condition-number theorem, including its large-rank case,

    kappa_1(G) >= N/(B+1)^2.                              (8)

Combining (7) and (8) proves (A).

**Why arbitrary diagonal gains are excluded.** If a diagonal factor has entries of unequal absolute magnitude, its induced-1 condition number is the ratio of largest to smallest magnitude, not necessarily 1. Such factors cannot be silently included in (7). If their condition-number product is K, the same proof instead yields

    7^t K (B+1)^2 >= N.

A nonunit magnitude common to every diagonal entry is a permitted global scalar and has K=1.

## 5. Verifying the Green constant 7

The relevant bare primitive is

    T = tridiag(-1,3,-1),
    B0 = P T P^-1,
    M = B0^-1 + (1/20) T^-1,

where P is a permutation. This notation B0 distinguishes the matrix from the number B of rank-one factors.

We have ||T||_1<=5 and ||T^-1||_1<=1. For the latter bound, write T=3I-E with ||E||_1<=2 and apply the convergent Neumann series. Permutation similarity gives the same two bounds for B0. Therefore

    ||M||_1 <= 1+1/20 = 21/20.

Preserving multiplication order,

    M = B0^-1 [I+(1/20) B0 T^-1],
    M^-1 = [I+(1/20) B0 T^-1]^-1 B0.

The perturbation has induced-1 norm at most 1/4. Another Neumann-series bound gives

    ||M^-1||_1 <= 5/(1-1/4) = 20/3.

Hence kappa_1(M)<=7. Also kappa_1(M^-1)=kappa_1(M), so inverse Green stages are covered. Unitary monomial factors and global scalars before or after a bare Green stage can be separated without changing its condition-number bound.

The improvement kappa_2(M)<5 in the earlier inverse extension is compatible with this argument but is not used here. Confusing kappa_2 with kappa_1 would invalidate the gate; the needed kappa_1<=7 bound has been verified directly above.

## 6. Stage and explicit-pass consequences

For every nonnegative integer B, B+1<=2^B. Thus

    (B+1)^2 <= 4^B <= 7^B.

Together with (A), this yields N<=7^(t+B), so

    t+B >= ceil(log_7 N).                                (9)

For B=t, (B) is the sharper architecture-specific constraint. In particular, log(t+1)<=t implies the simple uniform bound

    t >= log N/(log 7+2).

A sharper asymptotic expression follows by setting L=log_7 N. If t>=L it is immediate; otherwise t<L and (B) gives

    t >= L - 2 log_7(L+1).

Thus the lower bound is log_7 N-O(log log N) in the one-reflection-per-Green architecture.

If every Green and every reflection is executed as a separately charged full-array stage with Omega(N) cost, (9) forces Omega(N log N) work in that explicit-stage implementation model. This is the model relevant to applying the dense Green and dense Householder primitives one after another.

A factor-count lower bound alone does not forbid a different algorithm that fuses factors, exploits extra algebra, or evaluates their product with fewer operations than separately applying them. Nor does it charge a sparse-support rank-one factor as a full-array pass merely because its rank is one. Those issues require a separate execution-cost argument. The dense uploaded reflection does have full support.

## 7. Independent sanity checks and provenance

`independent_checks.py` is newly authored for this verification. It imports no uploaded code. It checks:

- Exact four-point complex Fourier rank, sparse-kernel witness, and inverse identities, including non-real perturbations and rank budgets 0 through 3. The associated scalar norm comparisons are floating-point checks of the exact matrices.
- Deterministic numerical instances at several Fourier lengths, low-rank perturbations, sparse-kernel witnesses, both norm inequalities, and the condition-number inequality.
- The telescoping identity and rank budget for independently generated products of Green factors, complex Householders, unitary monomials, and global scalars; and the retained-product bound.
- The invalid large-rank individual-norm extension, by an explicit scaled-identity example.

The recorded finite checks are sanity checks only. Sections 1 through 6 provide the all-N proof. No uploaded executable source is run, and no numerical check is used to infer the theorem.

Context read during verification: the earlier standalone inverse extension and summary in `materials_1155`. Their earlier warning that the bare-product gate did not cover reflections is superseded, for this specific low-rank-wrapper architecture, by the argument above. Arbitrary diagonal gains remain outside the constant-7-only version.
