# A classical cut-rank proof subsumes the adjacent-anchor certificate

Date: 2026-10-10. Status: analytical derivation, independently checked against the stipulated support maps. This preserves the accepted impossibility conclusion and substantially downgrades its claim to methodological novelty.

## Stronger statement

Let k be any field containing a primitive eighth root zeta. Let F[r,s]=zeta^(rs), for 0<=r,s<8. Put S={0,1,2,3}, and write S^c={4,5,6,7}. Let

- pi=(1,2,3,4,5,6,7,0), in map notation;
- sigma=(2,7,5,0,6,1,3,4), in map notation;
- P_tau e_j=e_tau(j).

For any invertible Q0=diag(a)+diag(b)P_pi and Q1=diag(c)+diag(d)P_sigma, and any invertible matrix B that is block diagonal for S|S^c,

    F != (Q0^(-1)+Q1^(-1)) B.

Arbitrary zero edge coefficients are allowed. This proof does not even need nonzero diagonal coefficients, beyond invertibility of each Q. Existence of a primitive eighth root already rules out characteristic two.

In particular B may be any nonsingular diagonal scaling A K_adj C of the adjacent-pair anchor. Thus the statement subsumes the accepted entire adjacent-anchor diagonal-orbit obstruction, including every zero-pivot and zero-edge boundary. It covers a larger first-factor class, but it is an immediate application of an established rank principle, not evidence of a new lower-bound method.

## Three-line obstruction

For every invertible Q and every index set S,

    rank Q^(-1)[S^c,S] = rank Q[S^c,S].                 (1)

The only pi edge from S to S^c is 3->4. The sigma edges from S to S^c are 1->7 and 2->5. Hence

    rank (Q0^(-1)+Q1^(-1))[S^c,S] <= 1+2 = 3.        (2)

Multiplication on the right by B preserves this rank, because the S-column block is multiplied only by its invertible B[S,S]. But F[S^c,S] is a Vandermonde matrix with nodes zeta^4,zeta^5,zeta^6,zeta^7, so

    det F[S^c,S] = product_(4<=i<j<=7)(zeta^j-zeta^i) != 0.

Its rank is 4, contradicting (2).

Equivalently, for the audited normal form M=F T K_adj with T=diag(1,t0,1,t1,1,t2,1,t3), all t nonzero,

    rank M[S^c,S] = rank F[S^c,S] = 4,

because T and K_adj preserve S. This is incompatible with M being the sum of the two prescribed inverse branches. The harmless overall factor 1/2 does not change rank.

## Proof of the inverse-block identity, including singular principal blocks

Let E be the coordinate subspace supported on S. The kernel of the map Q[S^c,S] consists of v in E for which Qv also lies in E. The invertible map v -> Qv bijects this kernel with the kernel of Q^(-1)[S^c,S]. Both maps have domain dimension |S|. Their nullities, hence ranks, agree. No inverse of Q[S,S] or Q[S^c,S^c] is used, so no principal-block or pivot assumption is hidden.

## Priority consequence

The inverse-block rank identity and its sum/inversion applications are established: see Chandrasekaran, Epperly and Govindarajan, *Graph-Induced Rank Structures and their Representations*, Propositions 2.1-2.3, https://arxiv.org/html/1911.05858. The application here is derived explicitly above. The source is not being claimed to state this particular F8 support pair.

Therefore the extensive accepted unit-ideal and boundary certificates remain correct redundant proofs of this particular exclusion. Their discovery effort, exactness, and independent verification do not establish historical novelty or significance beyond a simple classical cut argument. A claim that this example needs a new algebraic obstruction is unsupported.

    det F[4:8,0:4] = -4+4z-4z^2,

and agrees with the Vandermonde product. This finite check supplements, rather than replaces, the all-field Vandermonde proof. No supplied program was run.
