# Certified shrinking-error Fourier approximation and periodic fusion

## Validated improvement

The uploaded shrinking-error checkpoint's asymptotic balance is correct. Its
prototype, however, only generated requested table rows, repacked scalar lanes
online, and did not certify transcendental setup or word-sized intermediates.
Those experiments alone did not instantiate its cold-preprocessing claim.
The existing `guarded_decoder.py` closes those implementation gaps: it can
enumerate every row, stores already packed rows, sums only bounded words, and
generates phasors by certified integer Machin/Taylor arithmetic. Its block size
and precision parameters already support the new growing schedule.

For every fresh Gaussian-integer input with O(log N)-bit coordinates, fixed
integer c>=1 and fixed 0<gamma<1, the combined construction has the following
two fully charged upper bounds. All N output approximations are explicitly
written; all tables are built cold.

| Word operations, including setup | Additive normalized error factor eta_N |
|---|---|
| O(N) | O(log log N / sqrt(log N)) |
| O(N log N / log log N) | O((log log N)^(3/2) / log N) |

With failure probability at most N^-c, the approximate unnormalized DFT array
`Fhat` satisfies

    max_m |Fhat_m/sqrt(N) - (U_N x)_m|
        <= eta_N L_box(x)/sqrt(N).

The actual machine outputs are Gaussian-integer numerators and a common
integer denominator representing every coordinate of Fhat. The normalization
by sqrt(N) in this inequality is mathematical; **an exact irrational scalar
is not assumed to fit in a machine word**. Ordinary fixed-point unitary output
conversion needs a separately charged rounding budget. This result is an
approximation theorem, not an exact FFT or conventional small relative error
guarantee for each Fourier bin. No novelty or practical crossover is claimed.

## Explicit integer/rational schedule

Let `L=ceil(log2 N)` and `ell=ceil(log2 L)`. No input is zero-padded or altered.
Choose a rational time factor `1<=tau<=L`, with a bounded-word representation,
and set

    eta = ell / ceil_sqrt(ceil(tau L)),
    A = 3+(c+1)L,
    K0 = ceil(64 A / eta^2),
    B = K0.

Thus `eta=Theta(ell/sqrt(tau L))` and
`K0=B=Theta(tau L^2/ell^2)`. Allocate sampling error eta/4 and sampling
failure `N^-c/2`. Since

    ln(4N/(N^-c/2)) <= log2(8N^(c+1)) <= A,

the safe complex Hoeffding union bound is satisfied by K0. Allocate the other
half of the failure probability to the existing rejection-capped exact
integer sampler. Its binary searches and capped random draws take poly(L)
work, in addition to its complete O(N) scan, so no new sampler is needed.

Choose powers of two

    Q0 >= 64/eta,
    Q1 >= B Q0,
    H >= 32/eta,

using the least such powers (and Q0>=4). Choose
`b=max(8,ceil(log2(32/eta)))` phasor error bits. A descriptor packs its start
phase and slope into `p=log2 Q0+log2 Q1` bits; the quarter-turn atom sign is
folded exactly into the start phase. Let

    g = floor(gamma (L-1)/p),
    G = ceil(K0/g),
    K = Gg.

The use of L-1 is deliberate: `L-1<=log2 N`, even for nondyadic N. Hence the
complete table has exactly `2^(pg)<=N^gamma` rows. A planner must not replace
g=0 by g=1 and then continue claiming this inequality.

For sufficiently large N, g>=1, B<=N and all stated word widths hold. The
finite remaining region is handled by a directly evaluated certified sum.
Since c and gamma are fixed and the error schedule is specified, this finite
base case can be absorbed in the asymptotic constant. It can nevertheless be
enormous. The research prototype exposes base-regime status rather than
pretending finite complete-table tests have reached the asymptotic schedule.

## Deterministic error remains certified while precision grows

Nearest-bin descriptors have complex trajectory error at most

    pi/Q0 + pi(B-1)/Q1.

Each table entry averages g quantized phasors, rounds each coordinate to
step 1/H, and clips to [-1,1]. Certified phasor error adds at most 2^-b and
coordinate rounding adds at most `1/(sqrt(2)H)`. Averaging equal-size groups
adds no error. The rational upper bound used by the planner is

    e_decoder <= 4/Q0 + 4(B-1)/Q1 + 2^-b + 3/(4H) <= eta/4.

Here pi<4 and 1/sqrt(2)<3/4; no floating approximation is used to validate
the budget. Sampling plus decoding therefore has error at most eta/2, leaving
slack if an explicitly specified output-conversion step is later added.

The old fixed-accuracy phasor construction also works at this increasing
precision. It chooses d with

    4^(d+1)/(d+1)! <= 2^(-b-4),
    P = b + bit_length(256(d+1)) + 4.

Its previously proved Machin and Taylor error is at most 2^-b. In the present
schedule, `b=O(ell)`, `d=O(ell)`, and `P=O(ell)` uniformly for tau<=L. Thus
the scalar precision is **not** treated as a constant: the complete phasor
grid has poly(L) entries and is generated in poly(L) word operations. Its
products occupy O(ell) bits (the parameter-construction intermediates also
fit O(L) bits); there is no growing-precision arithmetic oracle.

## Guarded summation and exact rounded-block cost

Use `r=ceil(log2(2HG+1))` bits per offset lane, `w=Theta(L)` machine bits,
`a=floor(w/r)` lanes per word and `W=ceil(B/a)` words per coordinate of a row.
Every stored lane is in [0,2H]. Summing G rows never reaches 2^r in a field,
so there are no carries between lanes or beyond the word. Every output lane
is extracted once and its offset GH is subtracted once.

For the general time factor,

    p = Theta(ell),             g = Theta(L/ell),
    G = Theta(tau L/ell),       r = Theta(ell),
    W = Theta(tau L/ell).

There are `R=ceil(N/B)` blocks. Charging the final partial block, the complete
online cost is

    O(N + poly(L) + RK + RG + RGW).

Since B<=N, `R<=2N/B`; the terms become respectively O(N), o(N), O(N),
O(N ell/L), and O(N tau). This explicitly includes all key construction,
row reads, packed accumulation, final unpacking, output writes and block
control. In particular it does not hide a scalar re-packing of B lanes for
every group at query time.

All complete table rows are built before decoding. One row costs
O(gB) word operations and O(W) words; hence setup time is
`N^gamma poly(L)=o(N)` and table memory is also o(N). The phasor grid,
sample list, fixed-width keys and current block occupy poly(L) additional
words, and the explicitly written outputs occupy O(N).

The inherited model permits unit-cost bounded-word integer division and
remainder, alongside arithmetic, shifts, masks, comparisons and direct
addressing. Inputs must have a common fixed-point denominator or be Gaussian
integers; arbitrary floating exponents or arbitrary real scalars are not
silently covered. Input bit size and failure exponent are absorbed in a
sufficient fixed constant in w. The existing periodic fusion reports its
additional conservative width for residual fitting and output rescaling.

## General time/error frontier

The same proof yields, uniformly for rational `1<=tau<=L`,

    T = O(N tau),
    eta_N = O(ell/sqrt(tau L)).

Choosing tau=1 gives the linear-time vanishing-error result. Choosing
tau=L/ell gives the uploaded O(N L/ell) point. More generally, for a
computable growing h with `h->infinity` and `1<=h<=L`, choose tau=L/h:

    T = O(N L/h) = o(N log N),
    eta_N = O(ell sqrt(h)/L).

The required rational parameters must remain bounded-word computable, as
they do for the displayed logarithmic/polynomial choices. This is an
approximate time/error frontier, not a conclusion about exact DFT complexity.

## Direct compatibility with the exact periodic control variate

The existing `control_variate.py` fits q= gcd(N,4) residue means, choosing
each real/imaginary mean or zero by an exact residual-mass comparison. With
n=N/q it constructs integer rho satisfying

    F x = F g + F rho/n,
    L_box(rho)/n <= L_box(x).

The short spectrum Fg uses only Gaussian-integer quarter rotations. Feed
rho through the growing-accuracy sampler/decoder with the same schedule.
The complete fused output then has error at most

    eta_N L_box(rho)/(n sqrt(N)),

with unchanged time bounds. The q quarter-root frequencies are overwritten
by their exact raw-sum values; zero residual returns a fully exact spectrum.
All fitting, certification and rescaling remain O(N) word operations.

For a near-periodic comparison g_star, exact means give
`L_box(rho)/n<=2L_box(x-g_star)`. For every input, Cauchy-Schwarz also gives
`L_box(x)/sqrt(N)<=sqrt(2)||x||_2`, so the linear-time bound vanishes relative
to total input energy. It can still greatly exceed the magnitude of an
individual cancellation-heavy Fourier coefficient.

## What the planner's cold bounds mean

`parameter_schedule.py` validates exact rational inequalities without creating
N or an exponential table. It reports the row-count exponent pg and coarse
logarithmic bounds on cold work/memory. The constants are conservative loop
bounds, not fitted timings:

* A row decodes g descriptors, forms B groups of g phasor accesses/additions,
  rounds and clips two coordinates, and packs two output fields per lane.
  Fewer than 128 elementary arithmetic, memory, comparison and loop operations
  per `(g+1)(B+1)` suffice for a direct RAM implementation; the planner uses 512.
* Each phasor takes O(d) bounded-word operations, each arctangent setup takes
  O(P) terms, and the degree search with direct factorial construction costs
  O(P^2). Here d<P. The bound `128(P+1)^2(Q1+1)` safely covers the grid and
  its setup. It also dominates its storage. The predictor is an upper bound
  for this explicit algorithm, not a count of Python interpreter instructions.

For general N with the indicated ceiling L, a cold logarithmic bound at most
L-1 certifies that bound is at most N. The planner distinguishes this finite
certificate from the asymptotic o(N) proof. Its c=0 option is a boundary test
with a vacuous failure bound of one; high-probability claims use c>=1.

The finite audit enumerates full tables at deliberately tractable parameters,
including g=2, B larger than log2 N, multiple words per row and partial output
blocks. Separate high-precision Decimal checks test growing integer-generated
phasor precision. These tests support the concrete implementation; they do
not make its asymptotic precomputation practical or establish a measured
speed advantage over an FFT library.
