SHARED-DENOMINATOR BOSTAN–MORI TREE: EXACT SPECTRAL BRIDGE AND COST
2026-10-09

Result
------
This pass does not produce an o(N log k) exact algorithm. It isolates two
separate amplitude-dependent maps that denominator reuse does not pay for:
(1) extension of a numerator's L-point spectrum to the missing coset of a
2L-point spectrum, and (2) terminal rational-series decoding. The spectral
extension is the familiar FFT-doubling operation, not a novel algorithm.
An exact full-rank aliasing identity explains why simply commuting its
twiddles through denominator multipliers does not make it disappear.
These are limitations of this proposed implementation, not lower bounds
on arbitrary algorithms or on joint processing of its constrained inputs.

1. Model and residue tree
-------------------------
Use exact arithmetic over C (or a field of characteristic not 2 supporting
the required auxiliary FFT roots). Input is distinct nonzero z_s with
z_s^N=1 and arbitrary a_s, s=1,...,k. We want f_0,...,f_{N-1}, where
f_m=sum_s a_s z_s^m. All support-dependent preprocessing is charged.

At depth ell and residue 0<=r<2^ell, define

  F_{ell,r}(t) = sum_{m>=0} f_{r+2^ell m} t^m
               = P_{ell,r}(t)/Q_ell(t),
  Q_ell(t) = product_s (1-z_s^(2^ell)t),
  P_{ell,r}(t) = sum_s a_s z_s^r Q_ell(t)/(1-z_s^(2^ell)t).

Thus deg P<k and deg Q=k. These representations remain valid if roots
collide; cancellation can reduce their degrees. In particular, for odd
prime N, squaring never identifies distinct Nth roots, so the common
reduced denominator has degree k (assuming the corresponding amplitudes
are nonzero). Powers-of-two N can have useful aliasing, but no arbitrary-
support guarantee of early aliasing follows.

Given a node P=P_{ell,r}, Q=Q_ell, put U(t)=P(t)Q(-t). Then

  Q_{ell+1}(t^2)=Q(t)Q(-t),
  P_{ell+1,r}(t^2)=[U(t)+U(-t)]/2,
  t P_{ell+1,r+2^ell}(t^2)=[U(t)-U(-t)]/2.

The denominator is common to every node of a level. Its polynomial and
FFT data can therefore be computed once per level. The numerators differ.

2. Exact spectral branch formulas
---------------------------------
Let L be a power of two with k<=L<2k (the k=1 case is immediate). Let alpha
be a primitive 2Lth root and omega=alpha^2. Store each degree-<L numerator
p by x_r=p(omega^r), 0<=r<L. To branch, one needs p on all 2L roots.

If that 2L spectrum is available, then for j=0,...,L-1,

  p_even(omega^j)
    = [p(alpha^j)Q(-alpha^j)+p(-alpha^j)Q(alpha^j)]/2,
  p_odd(omega^j)
    = [p(alpha^j)Q(-alpha^j)-p(-alpha^j)Q(alpha^j)]/(2 alpha^j).

Both child spectra together cost O(L) arithmetic given the parent 2L
spectrum and the common Q values. This already shares the work between
siblings. However, each child has only an L-point spectrum. Its next
branch needs its own missing coset, unless a further cross-node identity
is supplied.

3. The missing-coset operator E
-------------------------------
Let F have entries F_{r,j}=omega^(rj), and let

  F_odd = F diag(alpha^j),
  E = F_odd F^{-1} = F diag(alpha^j) F^{-1}.

Then y=E x gives y_r=p(alpha omega^r), the missing half of the 2L spectrum.
Interleave x and y to get the complete 2L spectrum. Explicitly,

  E_{r,s}=(1/L) sum_{j=0}^{L-1}(alpha omega^(r-s))^j
         = 2/[L(1-alpha omega^(r-s))].

E is a fixed dense circulant/Cauchy matrix, independent of the support,
amplitudes and tree level. There are no zero entries. Also

  E^2=F diag(omega^j) F^{-1},

which is the cyclic permutation x_r -> x_{r+1 mod L}. This attractive
identity alone is insufficient: denominator pointwise multipliers lie
between successive extensions, and the next section gives their exact
commutation defect.

The standard application of E is inverse DFT, coefficient twist, forward
DFT: 2 Fcost(L)+O(L)=O(L log L). Precomputing E's entries or its convolution
kernel FFT eliminates only support-independent setup; it does not execute
E x for a new x. If coefficients are also already available, FFT doubling
needs only the twisted forward transform, Fcost(L)+O(L). Maintaining those
coefficients across the numerator products still has to be charged.

4. Exact aliasing/commutation defect
------------------------------------
For a degree-d polynomial q with d<=L and nonzero leading coefficient, set

  D0=diag(q(omega^r)),
  D1=diag(q(alpha omega^r)),
  H_q(p)=floor(p(t)q(t)/t^L), for deg p<L.

Here floor means retain coefficients of degrees >=L and shift down by L.
Identify H_q with its L-by-L coefficient matrix, padding its output by zeros.
Then the exact matrix identity is

  E D0 - D1 E = 2 F_odd H_q F^{-1}.

Proof. Write pq=A+t^L B with deg A<L and deg B<d. On the even grid t^L=1,
so the sampled product represents A+B. Extending that aliased polynomial
gives (A+B)(alpha omega^r). True multiplication on the odd grid gives
(A-B)(alpha omega^r), because t^L=-1. Their difference is 2B on that grid.

Moreover rank(H_q)=d. Its d output coefficients depend triangularly on
the top d input coefficients, with nonzero diagonal q_d, after ordering
from highest degree downward. Therefore

  rank(E D0-D1 E)=d.

For the BM multiplier q=Q_ell or Q_ell(-t), d=k. On the full degree-<L
space the defect is rank k, and at L=k it is full rank L. In that state
space, moving E past the pointwise Q multiplier and canceling pairs using
E^2 requires the indicated rank-k correction; a permutation/twiddle-only
commutation would omit it. Rank/density does not establish a superlinear
operation lower bound.

IMPORTANT RESTRICTION: actual BM numerators have degree<k, not arbitrary
degree<L. Restricted to this input space, the defect for degree-k q has
rank max(0,2k-L), by the same triangular argument. Thus its restricted
rank need not be Theta(k) for a padded transform. Taking L=k when k is a
power of two gives the clean full-rank case with no padding. Choosing
L>=2k even removes this one product's alias defect entirely on actual
numerators. This does not by itself remove the missing-grid issue: a
parent 2L grid yields an L grid for each child. To get those children on
a 2L grid by the same direct pointwise parity formulas, the parent must
instead be known on a 4L grid. In particular, evaluation of a child at
alpha omega^r requires its parent's product at square roots of that
point, in a further half-twisted coset. No cheap restricted or batched
implementation, and no universal obstruction to one, is inferred from
the ambient rank calculation.

5. What constrained batching may still exploit
---------------------------------------------
Numerators across a level are emphatically not independent arbitrary
vectors. Their coefficients and spectra satisfy

  P_{ell,r}(t)=sum_s a_s z_s^r Q_ell(t)/(1-z_s^(2^ell)t).

All of them are generated by the same k amplitudes; their matrix has rank
at most k. The standalone E-application cost therefore cannot be summed
as an unconditional lower bound on joint processing. A successful new
algorithm could fuse E applications, denominator multipliers, and final
decoding on precisely this family. The proof above only invalidates the
simple commutation/cancellation shortcut. It does not exclude such fusion.

Likewise, carrying a very oversampled numerator spectrum permits several
successive parity steps without extending it, but the grid halves after
each step. After h levels it needs an initial 2^h L grid. Standard pruned
FFT construction of that grid, plus the required parity network, does not
by itself give a better asymptotic cost. A claim that it does must charge
generation of the oversampled amplitude-dependent data, not just reuse
of Q or of twiddle tables.

6. Terminal decoding is a separate bottleneck
--------------------------------------------
Choose D=ceil(log_2(N/L)), assuming N>=L. There are 2^D=Theta(N/L)
residue leaves, each requesting at most L coefficients. All leaf
denominators are the same Q_D. Let

  h(t)=Q_D(t)^(-1) mod t^L,
  T_h = the lower-triangular Toeplitz map p -> (h p) mod t^L.

For a leaf numerator spectrum x, its requested coefficient block is

  T_h F^{-1} x,

with unused final entries discarded. Precomputing h, its multiplication
kernel FFT, or even the complete fixed matrix is allowed but does not
apply this terminal map. The standard per-leaf cost is O(M(L)+Fcost(L))
=O(L log L). Leaf residues must finally be interleaved into m order, O(N).

Thus speeding up E alone does NOT prove o(N log k) for this construction.
Continuing the untruncated degree-k tree to scalar leaves instead uses
Theta(N) nodes of dimension Theta(k), giving at least Theta(Nk) work in
the explicit dense-vector implementation, even if E cost were linear.
Shrinking/truncating dimensions below k does not magically fix this:
straightforward parity processing still has Theta(N) explicit data per
full level over Theta(log k) tail levels, before any transform costs.
Truncation from a spectrum is itself not a free pointwise operation.

7. Honest sufficient condition and full cost
--------------------------------------------
Let tau(L) be the cost of a support-independent E application and let
sigma(L) be the cost of the fixed terminal map T_h F^{-1} for the required
denominator family. Suppose their setup and the common denominator setup
cost Cpre(k,log N), and that all transforms/roots/initial data are charged.
The node-by-node spectral BM scheme then costs

  O(Cpre(k,log N) + (N/L)[tau(L)+sigma(L)+L]).

Standard algorithms have tau,sigma=O(L log L), so this is O(N log k)
plus polynomial-in-log-N preprocessing when k=Theta(log N).

A sufficient improvement would be

  Cpre=o(N log k), and tau(L)+sigma(L)=o(L log L),

uniformly for the relevant fresh supports. Polynomial(k,log N) setup
satisfies the first condition for k=Theta(log N). A fast E algorithm alone
does not satisfy the second if sigma retains its ordinary FFT cost.
Alternatively, a joint algorithm for the constrained node family can
replace the separate tau/sigma bounds if it proves the aggregate
amplitude-dependent work, including terminal output, is o(N log k).

Initial Q_0 and P_0 can be built by a product/sum tree in
O(M(k) log k). Each common denominator Q_{ell+1} follows by one Graeffe
product and even extraction in O(M(k)); its spectra need O(M(k)). Across
O(log N) levels this is O(M(k)(log N+log k)), apart from acquiring the
given/auxiliary roots and the setup of any proposed faster fixed maps.
These costs are small here but cannot be silently omitted.

8. Prior art / source boundary
------------------------------
The BM parity formulas and FFT-doubling bridge are established methods:

Alin Bostan and Ryuhei Mori, A Simple and Fast Algorithm for Computing the
N-th Term of a Linearly Recurrent Sequence (2020), sections 4.3 and 4.4,
Algorithm 10 (UP). With only an n-point DFT as input, UP uses inverse DFT,
twist, forward DFT and costs 2 E(n)+n in that paper's notation.
https://specfun.inria.fr/bostan/BoMo20.pdf#page=24

Joris van der Hoeven, Optimizing the half-gcd algorithm, section 2.1:
FFT doubling can cost F(n/2)+O(n) when the polynomial's coefficients and
its half-length transform are both available; the polynomial coefficients
are used to form the twisted remainder before the additional transform.
https://www.texmacs.org/joris/gcd/gcd.pdf#page=3

The explicit Cauchy entries and alias-commutator rank proof above are
direct derivations used to audit this candidate, not claims of novelty.
Independent exact finite-field tests checked the commutator
rank on 310 choices over mod 97 (L=1,2,4,8,16, all 1<=k<=L),
but the algebraic proof, rather than testing, establishes the identity.
