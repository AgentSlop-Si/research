## Direct comparisons for A

1. **Vandebril and Van Barel, A short note on the nullity theorem, TW397 (2004).** Read §3, especially Corollary 1, PDF p.7: rank A^(-1)[α,β]=rank A[β^c,α^c]+|α|+|β|−n. This is exactly the complementary-rank ingredient, with swapped complements. The displayed source uses real matrices; the accepted audit supplies the elementary field-general kernel proof. No new inverse-nullity result should be claimed. https://www.cs.kuleuven.be/publicaties/rapporten/tw/TW397.pdf

2. **Chandrasekaran, Epperly and Govindarajan, Graph-Induced Rank Structures and their Representations, arXiv:1911.05858v3.** Read Propositions 2.1–2.3 and Example 2.4, PDF pp.5–8. Inverse off-diagonal ranks and common-graph addition/product closure are explicit. The paper counts boundary edges; the candidate proof uses vertex separators. Its arrowhead example has a high-degree support graph but an alternative rank-one line-graph structure. Thus high degree does not certify separation from the old ordered class. These inspected propositions do not give the full independently routed flat-unitary sum bound. https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.05858

3. **Dvořák and Norin, Treewidth of graphs with balanced separations, arXiv:1408.3869.** Read introduction, PDF pp.1–2. The paper recalls the classical weighted separator consequence of treewidth and credits Robertson–Seymour. Its two-side balance convention is 2/3; the candidate's half-balanced component statement is separately proved by a weighted bag-tree centroid. Do not attribute the precise half-component convention to a statement using a different definition. https://arxiv.org/pdf/1408.3869

4. **Fomin, Lokshtanov, Pilipczuk, Saurabh and Wrochna, Fully polynomial-time parameterized computations for graphs and matrices of low treewidth, arXiv:1511.01379v1.** Read Theorem 1.3, introduction, and the description of §4.3. Given a decomposition of the bipartite support graph, it obtains generalized LU and rank/determinant in O(k³(n+m)) operations and solves subsequent systems in O(k²(n+m)). This establishes algorithmic relevance of the matrix class. Its stated result is an upper bound for solving systems, not a lower bound for independently routed sums, nor a lower bound for shared branch evaluation. https://arxiv.org/pdf/1511.01379

5. **Inherited FFT_SEPARATION_RANK_THEORY/THEOREM.md.** Read the complete actual theorem at the path hashed in INPUTS.sha256. Independent order pairs, simultaneous halving, all-N complex rank comparison and spectral error are explicit. Bare-cycle concatenation is the elementary consequence checked in the accepted audit and source ledger. This internal antecedent must be credited even if public-literature priority is unresolved. It does not itself prove that all attached-tree inverses have or lack uniformly bounded minimal ordered separation rank.

The gain-graph/frame-matrix and generalized-network comparisons were read in routed_branch_priority_0441. Their original sources were not freshly opened in this pass, so this report does not label them independently reinspected. Their established structure is consistent with the above attribution boundary.

## Direct comparisons for B

6. **Atakishiyeva, Atakishiyev and Koornwinder, On a q-extension of Mehta's eigenvectors of the finite Fourier transform for q a root of unity, arXiv:0811.4100.** Read §2, especially (2.8), and the q→1 passage surrounding (3.8). Through the continuous-q-Hermite/Rogers–Szegő normalization, (2.8) is the exact root-of-unity factorization. It fixes q at the root. That equality alone does not determine the first nonzero coefficient when q moves off the root and z enters a shared cluster. The candidate needs that additional coefficient extraction. https://arxiv.org/pdf/0811.4100

7. **Sawa and Uchida, Discriminants of classical quasi-orthogonal polynomials, with combinatorial and number-theoretic applications, arXiv:1802.00605v1.** Read Theorem 4.8 and formula (4.13), PDF pp.17–18. It gives the ordinary discriminant of L_m^(α)+cL_(m−1)^(α). The project's exact parity map turns J into this form at α=±1/2, followed by a quadratic pullback. Thus Disc(J) by itself is not the central originality candidate. The map and discriminant pullback were read in PRIMARY_REDUCTIONS.md; no finite normalization checker was rerun. https://arxiv.org/pdf/1802.00605v1

8. **Gómez-Ullate, Grandati and Milson, Durfee rectangles and pseudo-Wronskian equivalences for Hermite polynomials, arXiv:1612.05514.** Read Definition 3.1 and Theorem 3.1 (15), PDF pp.8–9. The translated-Maya-diagram equivalence supplies the hook-Wronskian identification used in the accepted reduction. This does not itself assert the candidate reciprocal Rogers–Szegő residue application. https://arxiv.org/pdf/1612.05514

9. **Grosu and Grosu, The irreducibility of some Wronskian Hermite polynomials, arXiv:2007.00065v1.** Read Theorem 8, PDF p.4, including its odd-modulus and degree-Vandermonde coprimality hypotheses. For the hook degree sequence {1,…,A,2A+1}, reduction modulo p=2A+1 gives the zero partition and the known congruence K_A=y^p. The p≡3 mod4 restriction belongs to the subsequent zero-root exclusion, not this congruence. The exact hook map is inherited from the inspected project reduction, not newly proved here. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.00065v1

10. **Ismail and Witte, Discriminants and Functional Equations for Polynomials Orthogonal on the Unit Circle, arXiv:math/0012259v2.** Read definitions (1.7)–(1.9) and §5 formulas (5.15)–(5.18), PDF p.24. Its explicit Rogers–Szegő product is for D(H_n,q), obtained using D_q. It cannot be substituted for the ordinary derivative discriminant D_n(q) in the candidate. This exact mismatch limits the product formula's relevance; it is not proof that all ordinary-discriminant consequences of that paper have been exhausted. https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0012259

11. **Spiridonov and Zhedanov, Zeros and orthogonality of the Askey–Wilson polynomials for q a root of unity, arXiv:q-alg/9605034v2.** Read the introduction, §2 statement setup and continuous-q-Hermite limit in §5. The authors explicitly warn that complex recurrence coefficients need not give simple zeros. Their terminal-degree node analysis fixes q to the root and therefore does not, by itself, furnish the candidate off-root, high-degree, shared-cluster coefficient. No claim is made that all consequences of every parameter specialization have been excluded. https://arxiv.org/pdf/q-alg/9605034

12. **Åhag, Czyż and Lundow, Quartic Fourier–Laplace limits for (p,q)-Rogers–Szegő polynomials, arXiv:2607.09150.** A new nearby search hit: read the introduction and Theorem A. It takes growing even n with positive real parameters approaching one and obtains a quartic Fourier–Laplace limit and scaled-zero convergence near w=1. This is a different regime from fixed degree data at a fixed nontrivial odd root, shared-root Hermite splitting and exact cyclotomic discriminant coefficients. Its stated theorem does not absorb the candidate application. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.09150
