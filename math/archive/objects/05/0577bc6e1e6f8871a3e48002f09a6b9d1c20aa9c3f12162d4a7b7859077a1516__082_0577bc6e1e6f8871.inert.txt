# An exact two-stage obstruction for globally overlapping quasiseparable FFTs

**Date:** 2026-10-09. **Status:** A new proof derived in the continuation of the supplied research, with independently executable exact finite-field witnesses. This is **not** a proven general FFT lower bound or a claim of a faster FFT. The derivation may be related to known communication-rank/structured-matrix arguments; mathematical priority is **not asserted**.

## Scope and definitions

Let `N` be any positive integer, and write `m=floor(sqrt(N))`.

Call an `N x N` matrix `S` **r-quasiseparable in the natural order** if for all `k=1,...,N-1`,

    rank S[0:k, k:N] <= r,   rank S[k:N, 0:k] <= r.

All ranks below are algebraic, over a field containing a primitive `N`-th root of unity `w`, in particular over the complex numbers. The unnormalized Fourier matrix is `F_N[i,j]=w^(i*j)` for `0<=i,j<N`.

### Theorem (two-stage overlap obstruction; arbitrary intermediate permutation)

Suppose `F_N=A P B`, where `P` is an arbitrary invertible `N x N` monomial matrix (a permutation with arbitrary nonzero coordinatewise weights) and `A` and `B` are `r_A`- and `r_B`-quasiseparable matrices in the natural order. Then necessarily

    floor(sqrt(N)) <= r_A + 2*r_B + 1.

Because `F_N^T=F_N`, transposing gives another necessary condition:

    floor(sqrt(N)) <= 2*r_A + r_B + 1.

For equal ranks `r_A=r_B=r`:

    r >= (floor(sqrt(N))-1)/3.

In particular, a two-stage constant-channel construction cannot realize `F_N` for unbounded `N`, with *any* intermediate monomial shuffle. No positivity, normality, bounded-norm assumption or invertibility of `A,B` is needed.

This directly rules out **two rank-2 quasiseparable Green-type stages** at `N>=64`. It does not rule out three or more stages, different non-quasiseparable O(N) transforms, or a sub-`N log N` FFT in another construction.

## Proof (every step explicit)

1. Fix the first `m` output rows `I={0,...,m-1}`. Since `N>=m^2`, choose `m` mutually disjoint **contiguous input-column intervals** `J_0,...,J_{m-1}`, each of length `m`; e.g. `J_t={tm,...,tm+m-1}`.

2. Define `pi` by `(P B)[k,j] = d_k B[pi(k),j]` with every `d_k != 0`. The nonzero factors `d_k` can be absorbed into the outer-product summands without changing any rank estimates. Since `pi` is a permutation, the set `pi(I)` has exactly `m` elements. Among `m` disjoint `J_t` there is one, call it `J=[s,s+m)`, with

       c = #{k in I : pi(k) in J} <= 1.

   Otherwise each of the `m` intervals would contain at least two of the `m` marked indices, which is impossible. (The leftover `N-m^2` indices only make this easier.)

3. Now take the `m x m` submatrix `M=(A P B)[I,J]`. Partition the middle summation index `k` into four disjoint classes:

   - `k >= m`: in `A[I, {m,...,N-1}]`, which has rank at most `r_A` by the upper cut condition. Its contribution to `M` has rank at most `r_A`, regardless of `B` or `P`.
   - `k<m` and `pi(k)<s`: the rows `B[pi(k),J]` are all from the upper off-diagonal block across the cut `s`, so this contribution has rank at most `r_B`.
   - `k<m` and `pi(k)>=s+m`: those rows of `B` come from the lower off-diagonal block across cut `s+m`, again rank at most `r_B`.
   - `k<m` and `pi(k) in J`: there are at most `c<=1` such indices. The contribution is a sum of at most `c` outer products, hence rank at most `c`.

4. Matrix-rank subadditivity gives the **exact minor invariant**:

       rank M <= r_A + 2*r_B + c <= r_A + 2*r_B + 1.

5. But the corresponding Fourier minor **always has rank m**. Its columns are distinct Fourier frequencies indexed by `J`, and its rows are `i=0,...,m-1`. Thus

       F_N[I,J][i,j] = (w^(s+j))^i,  0<=i,j<m.

   This is a Vandermonde matrix in the distinct scalars `w^s,...,w^(s+m-1)`. Its determinant is the nonzero product

       prod_{0<=a<b<m} (w^(s+b)-w^(s+a)).

   Hence `rank(F_N[I,J])=m`.

6. If `F_N=A P B`, the ranks must be equal. Combining steps 4 and 5 gives

       m <= r_A + 2*r_B + 1.

7. Transpose the alleged identity. Because `F_N=F_N^T`, `P^T` is still monomial and `A^T,B^T` are quasiseparable of their respective orders, applying the same proof gives `m<=r_B+2*r_A+1`.

**QED.**

## Connection to supplied Green-stage research

- The supplied `S=T^(-1)+(T+I)^(-1)` is 2-quasiseparable since each inverse of a tridiagonal path matrix has rank-one strict off-diagonal blocks; sums have rank at most two.
- Its `O(N)` solver work, globally dense inverse and bounded 1-condition do not affect this theorem: it is a pure exact-rank obstruction. Any two such stages, even with arbitrary intermediate `P`, fail for every `N>=64`.
- Earlier numerical near-fit for `N=8, t=2,r=2` is not contradicted: the lower bound is not yet active there.
- The result is **not** a proof that all products of `o(log N)` quasiseparable stages fail: proving or constructing the `t>=3` family is still open in this project.

## Fully charged work and caveats

For `r` independent upper/lower generator channels, a conventional stage executor costs `O(rN)` arithmetic and requires `O(rN)` generator coefficients. This theorem forces `r=Omega(sqrt(N))` for **two** stages, but it does **not** independently prove an `Omega(N sqrt N)` operation lower bound: highly structured generators could admit a different faster executor. It does prove that keeping a constant number of channels in the two-stage model cannot yield a full DFT at all sufficiently large sizes.

It also does not include arbitrary uncharged input/output permutations changing both external orderings; the Fourier minor is stated in the original natural row/column labeling. External permutations must be specified and accounted separately if the model is extended.

## Independent reproducibility

`minor_certificate.py` tests the four rank-contribution classes and the Fourier Vandermonde minor exactly modulo `998244353`, using a primitive `N`-th root when `N` divides `p-1`. These are *finite witnesses*, not an enumeration of all permutations. The all-permutation statement follows from the proof, not testing.
