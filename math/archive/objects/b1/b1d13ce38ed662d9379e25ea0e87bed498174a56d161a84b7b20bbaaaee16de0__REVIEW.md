## Balanced seed equivalence

Let u be the all-ones vector, A invertible, and B invertible bi-regular with Bu=b u and u^T B=b u^T. Necessarily b!=0. If F=A D B, the first Fourier row gives A[0,i]D[i,i]=1/b. Thus every first-row entry of A is nonzero and D is forced, up to the scalar b. Applying the factorization to u gives

    A (1/A[0,*])=N e0,

where reciprocation is entrywise. Inverting A gives A[0,i](A^(-1))[i,0]=1/N. These are exactly the diagonal entries of A^(-1)e0 e0^T A. Conversely, if the reciprocal-row identity holds, D=diag(1/A[0,*]) and B=D^(-1)A^(-1)F produce a bi-regular B with sum one. This is a genuine equivalence, but the constructed B can hide a dense Fourier action and gives no application-cost bound.

An arbitrary middle permutation P can be absorbed into the regular right factor, since it fixes u on both sides. A unitary A has the required property exactly when its first row is flat. Scaling the Fourier target does not change the leverage condition. An outer Green resolvent with a two-sparse inverse first column cannot satisfy it at N>2; zero first-row entries are separately disqualifying.

## Universal weak-coupling obstruction

For A=c(I+E), ||E||_2<=delta<1, let R=(I+E)^(-1). Its row-zero inverse identity yields

    (1+E00)R00-1=-sum_(j!=0) E0j Rj0.

Cauchy–Schwarz bounds the right side by delta^2/(1-delta), because the off-zero part of R e0 equals that of -R E e0. If delta<1/2, the modulus of A00(A^(-1))00 exceeds1/2, incompatible with1/N for every N>=2.

For A=sum_s c_s(I-alpha_s W_s)^(-1), c_s positive real and W_s permutation matrices, division by c=sum c_s and the operator Neumann bound give delta<=rho/(1-rho) when |alpha_s|<=rho<1/3. This works for any number of terms and arbitrary complex phases in alpha_s. Positive real combination weights, scalar couplings and bi-regularity are real hypotheses; arbitrary endpoint masks or row-varying edge weights do not automatically satisfy them. The source's 1/4,1/5 example gives delta<=7/24 and the exact leverage modulus bound359/408.

## First-cross calibration and cost

For invertible bi-regular A,B with sums a,b and nonzero first-row/first-column signatures r,s, F's first row forces D_i=1/(b r_i). Its first column then requires (Ps)_i/b=r_i/a. Thus matching the two normalized multisets is necessary and sufficient for matching the complete first Fourier row and column, not for full Fourier equality. In particular B=A^T always gives the first-cross construction A diag(1/(a A[0,*]))A^T.

Computing signatures and applying a fixed number of Green scans costs O(N) field operations after preparation. Matching arbitrary exact complex signatures by sorting requires an explicitly available representation/comparison model; the stated O(N log N) comparison bound or expected O(N) exact hashing is not an unconditional complex-field arithmetic primitive. A moment mismatch gives a linear-work rejection only; matching moments or a finite fingerprint is not a deterministic multiset-equality proof. The complete reciprocal-row gate itself uses one transpose and one forward application, with guarded entrywise divisions. All phase/weight preparation and storage remain charged.

Our independent finite controls verify the genuine N4 Fourier-positive example in two fields, and guarded N8/N16 first-cross examples that fail the full Fourier identity. The source's larger reported scale runs were not rerun or promoted to full-transform evidence.

## Fixed N8/N16 integer-polynomial certificates

The recorded arrays are Hamiltonian traversal orders, not direct successor maps; this distinction matters because some array positions equal their labels. For each specified pair, the numerator C(z) has entries z^distance0+z^distance1, with distances in the source's predecessor-row convention. The scalar denominator1-z^N cancels from the seed leverage equation. Therefore

    H_i=N C[0,i] cofactor(C,0,i)-det C

must vanish for every i in any admissible invertible factorization. Cofactor(0,i), rather than cofactor(i,0), is the correct inverse-column orientation.

Every entry has coefficient l1-norm at most two. The Leibniz expansion gives determinant l1-norm at most N!2^N; multiplying the corresponding cofactor-entry product by N gives the same bound. Thus every H_i coefficient has absolute value at most2 N!2^N and degree at mostN(N-1). The Mersenne prime2^127-1 is greater than twice this bound at both sizes, so modular interpolation followed by centered lifting uniquely recovers the actual integer coefficients.

The reviewer independently evaluates at shifted nodes -N(N-1),...,0, performs shifted Newton interpolation, checks the prime with Lucas–Lehmer arithmetic, and recomputes integer determinant/cofactor values at -3,2,3,5. All three full coefficient arrays match the source exactly. An independently implemented primitive-polynomial Euclidean algorithm gives degrees

    N8: H0,H1,det C,gcd =54,54,54,46;
    N16: H0,H1,det C,gcd =240,240,240,209.

The source GCD contents are16 and4, respectively. Exact integer division confirms those full source GCDs divide det C over Z, not merely modulo a prime. Therefore any complex common zero of H0,H1 makes det C zero. At z^N=1 the Green factors are undefined; otherwise det C=0 makes A singular, also impossible in a Fourier factorization. This is a valid all-complex-common-coupling exclusion for the two specified cycle pairs only. Their permutation rank differences are6 and14, independently checked.

The certificates do not exclude distinct independent couplings, arbitrary cycle pairs, extra internal masks, irregular right factors or independent outer/branch endpoint gauges. Polynomial extraction is offline dense research work; it is not part of a linear-time online FFT.
