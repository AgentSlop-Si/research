# Results summary — October 9, 2026

## What was proved

For N=2m and binary fresh inputs x=(u,1-u), a decoder that receives only r odd Fourier frequencies and returns all N Fourier frequencies requires Lipschitz constant at least

    (2^floor(N/(4r))-1)/N.

This holds regardless of the decoder's nonlinear algebraic degree or internal arithmetic cost, **provided its only varying data input is those r Fourier scalar values**. To avoid exponential instability, a polynomial-condition decoder needs r=Omega(N/log N); r=N/4 suffices for this family by standard real conjugate symmetry, but evaluating that many frequencies isn't free.

The same subfamily requires roughly N/(4r) fractional bits per representative (minus logarithmic integer-magnitude allowance) merely to avoid forced uniform-grid collisions.

## Audit scope

The proof is symbolic and independent of the finite computations. Tests independently verify the Fourier orbit identities in a prime field, numerical full-spectrum separation, high-precision candidate gaps, combinatorial packing/precision bounds, and a stable conjugation positive control. No universal arithmetic gate savings were found or claimed.

**No new exact O(N) DFT algorithm or complexity lower bound for arbitrary fresh exact complex vectors.**
