# Independent review: an explicit eight-order MGG cycle bank

Review date: 9 October 2026 UTC. This review independently reconstructs the proof and uses a separately authored exact checker. No uploaded or constructor code is executed.

## Verdict and exact scope

**Accepted:** for every dyadic `N >= 2^17`, the construction below gives eight explicitly enumerable Hamiltonian cycle orders on `N` vertices. For every set `S` of `N/2` vertices, three copies of the bank have aggregate incoming-edge capacity strictly greater than `N/2`. Constructing and enumerating the eight orders takes `O(N)` word operations and `O(N)` words of storage. The rectangular construction really is a quotient of the square MGG graph, and both stated cycle-count formulas and the improved splice-loss bound are correct.

This is a **necessary-capacity escape only**. More precisely, it defeats the **common-subset complementary edge-count obstruction**, with the same balanced subset at all stages. It does not certify arbitrary multi-stage separators whose intermediate cut sets may differ. It does not supply Fourier coefficients, show that the upper-bound capacity is attained by a sum or product, prove an exact Fourier factorization, provide a recursively compatible bank, establish a fast coefficient generator, or break an FFT runtime bound. It does not claim to settle unbalanced cuts or rank arguments stronger than the specified additive incoming-edge accounting.

The MGG spectral theorem is used as a cited mathematical premise, not re-proved by finite tests or independently re-run in Isabelle. Everything from that theorem to the capacity claim is proved below.

## 1. The eight labeled permutation covers

Let `m=2^r`, with `r>=1`. Use either the square vertex set `Z_m x Z_m` (`N=m^2`) or the rectangle `Z_(2m) x Z_m` (`N=2m^2`). Put `a=m` or `2m`, respectively, and `b=m`.

For `c in {0,1}` and `epsilon in {+1,-1}`, define

- `H_(c,epsilon)(x,y) = (x+epsilon(2y+c) mod a, y)`;
- `V_(c,epsilon)(x,y) = (x, y+epsilon(2x+c) mod b)`.

Each is a permutation; changing the sign gives its inverse. The bank has eight labels even where small moduli make two labeled edges coincide. Parallel edges and loops are retained until each cover is spliced. The spectral theorem is a theorem about this labeled multigraph, so silently deduplicating edges would be incorrect.

### External spectral input and normalization

The official AFP theory defines exactly these eight shears on `Z_n x Z_n` and proves `mgg_numerical_radius_aux`: the mean-zero, unnormalized adjacency quadratic form has absolute value at most `5 sqrt(2)` times the squared norm. Its normalized theorem `mgg_numerical_radius` divides by the degree eight. Source: [AFP, Expander_Graphs_MGG](https://isa-afp.org/browser_info/current/AFP/Expander_Graphs/Expander_Graphs_MGG.html), checked 9 October 2026 UTC. Relevant definitions/theorems are `mgg_graph_step`, `out_deg`, `mgg_numerical_radius_aux`, and `mgg_numerical_radius`.

Let `A` be the unnormalized adjacency matrix and `1` the constant vector. Then `A1=8*1`, `A` is symmetric, and for every real mean-zero vector `f`,

`|f^T A f| <= 5 sqrt(2) ||f||^2`.

This fixes the factor of eight and the directed-edge convention needed below.

## 2. The rectangular quotient is valid

Start from the square MGG graph on `Z_(2m) x Z_(2m)`. Project by

`q(x,y) = (x, y mod m)`.

The fibers are `{(x,y),(x,y+m)}`. Horizontal shears descend because increasing `y` by `m` changes `2y+c` by `2m`, zero modulo the horizontal modulus. Vertical shears descend because reduction modulo `m` commutes with adding `epsilon(2x+c)`. Thus every labeled cover, including its inverse, descends to exactly the rectangular formula in Section 1. No edge averaging or non-permutation map is introduced.

For a function `f` on the rectangle let `Uf=f o q`. Every fiber has two points, so

`||Uf||^2 = 2||f||^2`, `A_square U = U A_rectangle`,

and a mean-zero `f` has mean-zero pullback. Consequently

`f^T A_rectangle f = (Uf)^T A_square(Uf)/2`,

which transfers the same bound `5 sqrt(2)` to the rectangle. Equivalently `U/sqrt(2)` is an isometric intertwiner. This avoids relying on an informal interlacing assertion for an unspecified quotient.

## 3. Incoming half-cut capacity before splicing

For a set `S` of cardinality `N/2`, let `e(S)` count labeled directed edges with tail in `S^c` and head in `S`. Take

`f = 1_S - (1/2)1`.

Then `f` has mean zero and `||f||^2=N/4`. Since each vertex has eight outgoing and incoming labeled edges,

`e(S) = 8|S| - 1_S^T A 1_S`

`     = 2N - f^T A f`

`     >= (8-5 sqrt(2))N/4`.

Write `alpha=(8-5 sqrt(2))/4`. This counts incoming edges once. Counting both crossing orientations and then forgetting the factor two would give an incorrect threshold.

## 4. Exact cover-cycle counts

A translation by `d` on `Z_t` has `gcd(t,d)` cycles, including `gcd(t,0)=t`. For `m=2^r`, grouping residues by their two-adic valuation gives

`sum_(z=0)^(m-1) gcd(m,z) = m(r+2)/2`.

Indeed zero contributes `m`; for each valuation `v=0,...,r-1`, there are `m/2^(v+1)` residues contributing `2^v`, hence `m/2` per valuation. In addition,

`sum_(z=0)^(m-1) gcd(m,2z) = m(r+1)`.

One can derive the latter by reducing `z` modulo `m/2`, or use the same valuation argument. Sign does not change the gcd.

For **each sign separately**, the square counts are

| Cover | Number of cycles |
|---|---:|
| H0 | m(r+1) |
| H1 | m |
| V0 | m(r+1) |
| V1 | m |

The odd translations in H1 and V1 each form one cycle in their row or column. Summing over both signs gives

`C_square = 4m(r+2)`.

For **each sign separately**, the rectangular counts are

| Cover | Number of cycles |
|---|---:|
| H0 | m(r+2) |
| H1 | m |
| V0 | 2m(r+1) |
| V1 | 2m |

Here H0 uses `sum_y gcd(2m,2y)=2 sum_y gcd(m,y)`, while V0 sums the square vertical count twice as `x` ranges over `2m` values. Therefore

`C_rectangle = 2m(3r+7)`.

These formulas apply for `r>=1`; no logarithmic formula for the degenerate `m=1` case is needed for the theorem.

## 5. Deterministic Hamiltonian splicing and sharp loss accounting

For a cover permutation `p` having `c` cycles, choose one representative `a_j` from each cycle. For definiteness, label vertex `(x,y)` by `ay+x`, scan these labels increasingly, and choose the first unvisited vertex of each cycle. Let `b_j=p(a_j)`. If `c>1`, replace precisely the selected edges by

`p'(a_j)=b_((j+1) mod c)`.

All other edges remain unchanged. Following the new edges visits each old cycle, then the next, so `p'` is one Hamiltonian cycle. The selected tails and selected heads are unchanged as multisets. Since they came from distinct cycles, exactly `c` edges change. If `c=1`, leave the cover unchanged, costing zero changed edges.

For any set `S`, suppose `a` selected tails lie outside `S` and `b` selected heads lie inside. Under any matching of the `c` selected tails with the `c` selected heads, the number of incoming selected edges lies in

`[max(0,a+b-c), min(a,b)]`.

The old matching and the new matching both lie in this interval. The incoming-edge count can therefore decrease by at most

`min(a,b)-max(0,a+b-c)`

`= min(a,b,c-a,c-b) <= floor(c/2)`.

This argument depends on preserving the selected head counts, not merely on changing at most `c` edges. It proves the same bound for every cut simultaneously and does not assume that the splice was chosen after the cut.

For `m>=2`, all eight covers have at least two cycles and all the counts above are even. The aggregate loss bounds are consequently

`L_square = C_square/2 = 2m(r+2)`,

`L_rectangle = C_rectangle/2 = m(3r+7)`.

The spliced bank's half-cut incoming count `K(S)` thus satisfies

`K(S) >= alpha N - L`.

## 6. All dyadic N >= 2^17, proved without floating-point arithmetic

Three copies of this bank have additive capacity `3K(S)`. The exact sufficient inequality is

`3(alpha N-L)>N/2`, equivalently `(22-15 sqrt(2))N > 12L`.

A simple rational certificate suffices: `(17/12)^2>2`, so

`sqrt(2)<17/12`, `alpha>11/48`.

It follows that `3K(S)-N/2 > 3N/16-3L`, which is positive whenever `L/N<1/16`.

For odd exponent `N=2^(2r+1)`, the first required size is `r=8`, `N=2^17`. At that size

`L/N=(3r+7)/2^(r+1)=31/512<1/16`,

and `3N/16-3L=768>0`. The normalized loss strictly decreases with `r`, since `3r+10<2(3r+7)` for `r>=0`.

For even exponent `N=2^(2r)`, the first required size is `r=9`, `N=2^18`. There

`L/N=2(r+2)/2^r=22/512<1/16`,

and `3N/16-3L=15360>0`. This normalized loss also strictly decreases, since `r+3<2(r+2)`.

These two parity cases prove the statement for every dyadic `N>=2^17`. The threshold is sufficient for this analytic bound, not claimed optimal for the actual spliced graphs. In fact the exact bound is not yet positive at `N=2^16`; this does not prove that the finite construction fails there.

## 7. Enumeration, operation model, and what is charged

The checker gives executable pseudocode for construction using only the definitions above. For each of eight labels:

1. Generate its successor array by the modular formula.
2. Visit each vertex once to identify cover-cycle representatives.
3. Redirect the representatives' successors as specified.
4. Follow the resulting cycle once to enumerate a linear order and its wrap edge.

Each scan has length at most `N`; the number of labels is fixed. All successor-array writes, visits, splices, and order writes are charged. This is `O(N)` operations on a word RAM with `Theta(log N)`-bit indices, and `O(N)` words of working/output storage. Moduli are powers of two, so modular reduction can be done by a mask. This is not an assertion of `O(N)` bit complexity: explicitly writing `8N` indices already takes `Theta(N log N)` bits.

If used in a weighted cyclic Green bank, each inverse is meaningful only when its total cycle-weight product is not one. Applying a supplied valid weighted Hamiltonian Green and reordering its entries is linear work, but producing coefficients and proving a Fourier identity are separate obligations. Eight cheap enumerations do not furnish those missing coefficients.

## 8. Why the conclusion is only a necessary-cut escape

For an invertible matrix `B`, the complementary off-diagonal blocks of `B` and `B^-1` have equal rank. Apply this to `B=I-D_w P_pi`: its off-diagonal block is a weighted partial matching, so the corresponding Green cross-rank is at most the incoming-edge count of `pi`. Zero weights can only lower the rank. A sum of eight Green terms therefore has an upper bound given by the bank's incoming count, and a product of three banks has the additive bound `3K(S)` (with arbitrary intervening cut-preserving diagonal operators).

The graph construction makes that **upper-bound budget** greater than the maximum `N/2` rank of a balanced block, for every balanced `S`. Therefore that budget alone cannot exclude a candidate. This argument uses one common `S` in every layer; a general multi-stage separator can use different intermediate cut sets and is not certified by this theorem. A large rank upper bound never proves that a specific matrix, coefficient assignment, sum, or product reaches that rank. Cancellations, coefficient identities, endpoint routing, recursive reuse, and actual arithmetic/bit costs remain to be checked in any eventual construction.

This uses genuinely redesigned cycle orders. It does not reopen the already excluded fixed natural-plus-bit-reversal family.
