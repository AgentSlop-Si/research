# A residual-certified finite-word Green/median bridge

Independent bounded synthesis, 9 October 2026.

## Result and scope

The concrete addition is a **rounded, linear-work Green bridge with a posteriori source and target error certificates**, composed with the already proved shrinking-error decoder and the already implemented optimal periodic median. The certificates use cheap residual scans rather than assuming that exact Green coefficients fit a machine word. This closes a specific mathematical representation gap in the supplied conditional bridge. It is not an exact arbitrary-input FFT, a practical speed benchmark, or an established novelty claim.

The independently written implementation here covers the finite-word source filter, root construction, median head, rank-two target inverse and their certificates. Its Fourier/decoder calls are clearly marked **test-only oracles**. It does not implement the prior decoder's full growing-parameter schedule, which the earlier audits also identified as unfinished. Consequently this is an implemented bridge component and a compositional word-RAM theorem, not an end-to-end optimized linear-time FFT executable.

## 1. Convention and prior ingredients

Let N>=4 be dyadic, q=exp(2πi/N), and F[k,j]=q^(kj), **unnormalized**. Inputs have integer real and imaginary components of magnitude at most X<=N^c for fixed c. Put

    T=tridiag(-1,3,-1), S=T^-1+(T+I)^-1,
    H=FTF^-1, K=FSF^-1, B=K^-1.

The previously audited formulas are

    (Hv)_k=(3-q^k-q^-k)v_k + average_l(q^l v_l) + q^-k average_l(v_l),
    B=H/2+I/4-(H+I/2)^-1/8,
    ||B||∞ <= R=73/18,  ||(H+I/2)^-1||∞ <= 22/9.

All norms in these three formulas are ordinary complex induced norms. Let M(z)=sum_j(|Re z_j|+|Im z_j|), and m∞(z)=max_j(|Re z_j|+|Im z_j|); these are convenient computable upper bounds, not substitutions for the complex norms in the established theorem.

The decoder interface needed below is already proved in the audited v4 material: for bounded-word integer/common-scale r it returns z with

    ||z-Fr||∞ <= η M(r)

on its success event, with η=O(loglog N/sqrt(log N)) in O(N) word operations including preparation and explicit outputs. Other audited tradeoffs can be substituted, but the O(N) point is the one retained here.

## 2. Certify the rounded source from its residual

Choose Q=2^p, δ=1/Q. Compute rounded Thomas solutions z3,z4 on the δ grid to Tz3=x and (T+I)z4=x. Define u=z3+z4. Compute, **exactly on the dyadic grid**, the two tridiagonal residuals

    r3=x-Tz3,  r4=x-(T+I)z4,
    E=M(r3)+(1/2)M(r4).

Then

    M(u-Sx) <= E.                                      (1)

Indeed z3-T^-1x=-T^-1r3 and z4-(T+I)^-1x=-(T+I)^-1r4. The inverses are real; their induced 1-norms are at most 1 and 1/2 by the Neumann series, respectively. Apply the bounds separately to real and imaginary parts. This proof is valid for any proposed z3,z4; numerical stability is not an assumption needed for certification.

The actual proposed solver is also uniformly well behaved. With a=3 or 4, its exact Thomas coefficient recurrence is α_i=1/(a-α_(i-1)), α_(-1)=0. Exact α_i<1/2. At δ<=1/64, rounded coefficients remain below 1/2, and coefficient errors contract by at most 1/4 apart from one rounding error. The forward β_i=α_i(x_i+β_(i-1)) and backward z_i=β_i+α_i z_(i+1) recurrences contract by at most 1/2. Thus componentwise errors are O(δ(X+1)) with an absolute, N-independent constant, and

    E=O(N δ(X+1)).                                     (2)

There is no exact rational Green matrix or exponentially long denominator in this executor.

## 3. The exact periodic head and decoder

Choose the componentwise medians of u on each residue class modulo 4, using deterministic median-of-medians. The resulting 4-periodic vector h is on the same δ grid and

    r=u-h,   M(r)=min_{4-periodic h'} M(u-h').

Its transform Fh is computed exactly: only bins 0,N/4,N/2,3N/4 can be nonzero, with quarter rotations and integer sums. No root approximation enters this head transform. Feed the integer vector Qr to the existing decoder and scale its output by 1/Q. Form Fh+z, then round it once to the δ grid, obtaining v. With ε_v=2δ (a deliberately conservative complex-coordinate rounding bound),

    ||v-FSx||∞ <= η M(r)+E+ε_v.                        (3)

This equation is the important interface: source error is paid in full unnormalized Fourier units. A zero measured residual r does **not** imply exact original Fourier outputs, because E and later target errors can be nonzero.

## 4. Certify the rounded target from a second residual

Build a dyadic root table qtilde_k with certified complex absolute errors at most ρ. Define Htilde by the displayed H formula, replacing q^k by qtilde_k and q^-k by its complex conjugate. The identity qtilde_k^(-1)=conjugate(qtilde_k) is neither needed nor assumed. Directly,

    ||H-Htilde||∞ <= 4ρ.                               (4)

There are 2ρ from the diagonal and ρ from each averaged term.

Produce any dyadic approximate solution zt to (H+I/2)zt=v. The implementation uses the explicit diagonal-plus-rank-two formula below. Now compute

    rhat=v-(Htilde+I/2)zt

**exactly as a dyadic/common-denominator vector** and return

    y=Htilde v/2+v/4-zt/8.

No final rounding is needed: the output numerator and common denominator NQ² times a fixed constant still use O(log N) bits when p=O(log N). If a caller requires an extra output rounding, its coordinate error must be added.

A fully computable target certificate is

    τ = 2ρ m∞(v) + (11/36)[m∞(rhat)+4ρ m∞(zt)].        (5)

It guarantees ||y-Bv||∞<=τ. To prove this, the true solve residual is at most m∞(rhat)+4ρm∞(zt); applying the inverse norm 22/9 and the factor 1/8 gives 11/36. The first term pays (Htilde-H)v/2. Again, even a poor approximate solve remains honestly certified.

### Explicit rounded target solve

For exact roots define g_k=1/(7/2-2Re q^k), t=average(g_k), and

    rplus =[1+(11/2)t]/2,
    rminus=[1+(3/2)t]/2.

The 2x2 correction matrix is [[a,t],[t,a]], with eigenvalues rplus,rminus. Its inverse is applied using sums and differences. Given

    b0=average(q^k g_k v_k), b1=average(g_k v_k),
    cplus=(b0+b1)/(2rplus), cminus=(b0-b1)/(2rminus),
    a0=cplus+cminus, a1=cplus-cminus,

the exact solve is zt_k=g_k(v_k-a0-q^-k a1). The implementation rounds each indicated scalar arithmetic operation and uses qtilde. It is not assumed to be the exact inverse of Htilde: (5) measures that discrepancy.

At ρ<=1/8 and sufficiently small δ, g denominators exceed 1 and rplus,rminus exceed 1/4 even after rounding. All coefficient maps and their derivatives are uniformly bounded; the two reductions are averages, so per-summand rounding errors do not accumulate a factor N after division. Therefore its solve residual and output perturbations are O((ρ+δ)(m∞(v)+1)), with absolute constants. This proves that the computable certificate is small, not merely formally valid, at the chosen precision.

## 5. Final certificate and precision schedule

Combining (3), (5) and ||B||∞<=73/18 gives the central formula

    ||y-Fx||∞ <= (73/18)[η M(r)+E+ε_v]+τ.              (6)

This is a deterministic implication on the decoder success event; it does not add a new probabilistic failure.

The root builder below supplies ρ=8Nδ. On the decoder success event η<=1 eventually and m∞(v)=O(N(X+1)), because the source, median and residual masses are all polynomially bounded. The added error floor is consequently

    (73/18)(E+ε_v)+τ = O(N² δ(X+1)).                   (7)

For example p=ceil((c+6)log2 N)+20 makes (7) O(N^-4), with p=O(log N). The exact displayed certificate (6), not the asymptotic constant hidden in (7), is what the executable reports. This is an **additive** accuracy floor. It must not be folded into a residual-only claim when r=0.

For normalized Fourier outputs divide (6) by sqrt(N), and separately pay any rounding required to materialize that division. The implementation and checks deliberately return unnormalized rational outputs, avoiding that extra representation issue.

## 6. Certified root preparation in bounded words

The independent code uses only integer arithmetic to prepare the root. At guard scale G=Q*2^(bit_length(p+1)+20), compute π via Machin's identity 16 arctan(1/5)-4 arctan(1/239). Each alternating term is divided directly to scale G; stop when its next magnitude is below 1/G. Integer powers stop at O(G) times a polynomial in p, so they have O(p) bits. The sum of all truncation/rounding errors is at most 40(p+1)/G.

Round 2π/N to that scale, and evaluate sin and cos by p+11 alternating terms with integer fixed-point recurrences. For N>=4 the argument is below 2. The term recurrence multipliers contract after the first cosine term; the accumulated term-rounding and argument errors are bounded by 100(p+11)/G. The alternating remainders are smaller than 1/(1024Q) for p>=16. The guard margin makes each component's pre-final-round error less than 1/(100Q); rounding to Q then gives complex root error below δ.

Generate the table by repeated complex multiplication rounded to scale Q. Each step contributes complex rounding error less than δ, the multiplier magnitude is at most 1+δ, and a discrete geometric bound gives error below 8Nδ when Nδ<=1/64. The root table's actual errors are also compared with a separate 100-digit oracle in the checks; the all-N certificate comes from the preceding inequalities, not from that numerical comparison.

Preparing the single seed root takes O(p)=O(log N) word operations; generating and writing the complete N-entry root table takes O(N) additional word operations. Constant-many-word products can instead be accommodated in one word by choosing a sufficiently large constant in W=Θ(log N), as in the existing decoder model. The model includes integer multiply/divide and sufficient constant-factor word width; it is not a physical 64-bit, fixed-range claim.

## 7. Complete cost, including checks

With fixed input exponent c, fixed failure exponent, and the above precision schedule:

- seed-root/constant preparation: O(log N) word operations;
- generation and writing of the complete N-entry root table: O(N);
- two coefficient recurrences and two source solves: O(N);
- two exact tridiagonal residual scans: O(N);
- deterministic median fitting, residual writing and exact head transform: O(N);
- prior growing-parameter decoder, including its table generation/sampling/output: O(N);
- two target averages, 2x2 correction, N output coordinates: O(N);
- two exact Htilde applications and certificate scans: O(N);
- all data movement and output writes: O(N).

Storage is O(N) words. Intermediate numerator/denominator lengths are O(p+log N+log(X+1))=O(log N). In particular exact certificate computations use common dyadic denominators and N, not general expanding rational eliminations. The Python checker uses Fraction to make those certificates legible and auditable; fixed common-denominator integer arithmetic realizes the same bounded-word operations.

Thus, conditional on the previously established complete decoder interface, the whole mathematical pipeline is O(N) word work. The checked-in program is intentionally **not** an end-to-end decoder implementation: its direct DFT oracle is quadratic and excluded from that runtime claim.

A no-worse option runs the original median-residual route too, with half the failure budget assigned to each decoder, and selects the smaller full certificate. Two O(N) calls remain O(N); the union bound pays the total failure budget. This does not manufacture a universal improvement: the Green route helps only when the measured residual reduction outweighs R and its rounding floor.

## 8. Independent tests and an informative family

The script imports no uploaded executables. It contains a new integer fixed-point executor and exact rational certificate checker. Numerical DFTs and exact rational reference solves are used only to verify it.

The near-structured family is built for testing by rounding 2^20 S^-1 1 to integer inputs. Every input is freshly supplied and dense; no previous transform is reused. This is deliberately constructed structure, not evidence about typical arbitrary inputs.

At N=128, p=64 and illustrative decoder allowance η=1/16:

- original optimal periodic residual mass: 1,128,508;
- Green-filtered optimal periodic residual mass: about 13.49352;
- source residual certificate: 4.82e-12 or smaller;
- target certificate: about 2.10e-8;
- complete transformed certificate: about 3.42023;
- original median-only certificate: 70,531.75;
- observed error with a deliberately injected admissible decoder error: about 1.15002.

These numbers demonstrate the new bridge's conditional value and the size of its paid rounding floor. They do not benchmark the decoder, prove a typical-data speedup, or supply exact arbitrary-input outputs. The η in this finite sanity example is an interface parameter, not a claim that the asymptotic decoder attains that constant at N=128.

The final results file also isolates the target executor with no decoder error, so the target certificate is tested independently rather than concealed behind a much larger sampling allowance.
