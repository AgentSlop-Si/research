## Independent proof

From U=S4 P3 S3 P2 S2 P1 S1 and invertibility of U, every square S_j is invertible. Multiplication gives P3^−1 S4^−1 U S1^−1 P1^−1=S3 P2 S2. This step does not divide by quasiseparable generators and does not require their nonzero entries.

For a two-sided quasiseparable order-one stage S and partition A, the cross-block rank is at most the number d(A) of changes in its natural-order indicator. Separate lower and upper triangular contributions into runs, each crossing a natural cut of rank at most one. This argument remains valid with zero propagation or generator coefficients. In the middle product, transport the partition through p2, and use the off-diagonal block product formula to add ranks. Consequently rank H[R_m,C_m]≤d(g_m)+d(g_m[p2]).

## Constructive use and limits

This is a rigorous rejection region for fixed-layout coefficient searches: an exact candidate must push at least one outer inverse sufficiently far from every diagonal-unitary baseline, or pay the stated combined deviation. It also rejects a normalized approximation claim near identity/phase-only outer inverses quantitatively. Thus a search initialized with tiny outer-inverse perturbations cannot be mistaken for a route to an exact factorization merely by freely tuning the two middle stages.

It does not prove that general quasiseparable outer inverses cannot realize the needed deviation while remaining O(N)-applicable. It gives no all-N family, no general circuit lower bound, no scalar-operation saving, and no positive Fourier factorization. The rank/trace/approximation tools are classical; the new verified content here is the explicit finite-layout certificate. Publication novelty is unassessed.
