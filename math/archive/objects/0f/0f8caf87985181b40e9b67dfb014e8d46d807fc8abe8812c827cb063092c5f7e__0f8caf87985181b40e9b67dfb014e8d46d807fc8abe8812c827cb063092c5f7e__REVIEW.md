# Independent phase-grid compatibility review

9 October 2026 UTC. This supplements the sealed rational-pencil review. No constructor program was run or imported.

## Approved conclusion

The specified phase-valued two-stage pencil cannot equal the full 16-point Fourier matrix, for any four complex stage couplings and any invertible complex middle monomial. This is an exact characteristic-zero exclusion at N=16. It does not establish an all-size theorem, exclude arbitrary phase masks or additional stages, or provide a faster Fourier algorithm.

## Exact candidate

Let zeta be a primitive sixteenth root, M=4, N=16, and use row-major zero-boundary shifts. Define

    G_q = (I - D_(q^u) L_t - L_u)^(-1),
    R_s e_j = e_((bitreverse_4(j)+s) mod 16).

The stage pencils are

    H1 = alpha G_zeta + beta R_11 G_(zeta^-1) R_11^-1,
    H2 = gamma G_(zeta^-1) + delta R_7 G_zeta R_7^-1.

The target is F16=H2 T H1 with arbitrary middle monomial T. The notation G_(zeta^-1) means replacing q by its inverse, not taking the inverse of G_q. All component denominators are unit lower triangular before routing. Thus their invertibility and O(N) given-parameter application are unambiguous.

## Sparse-left-row implication

The exact left row is

    H2[1,:] = gamma(e0+e1)
              + delta[(1+zeta)e7 + zeta e15 + e9 + e1].

It has support contained in {0,1,7,9,15}. Multiplication by any middle monomial preserves a support bound of five. Therefore Fourier row 1 must belong to the span of at most five rows of H1. Any subset of fewer than five can be extended to a five-row subset, so it is sufficient to exclude all binomial(16,5)=4,368 subsets.

This is an exact support fact from the grid and permutation, not an inference from floating point or modular nonzero patterns. The earlier rational-field degree bound is inconclusive here because the fixed row matrices already have entries in Q(zeta16).

## Homogeneous minors and complete projective coverage

For a five-row subset S and six columns C, form the augmented 6-by-6 matrix with the selected five H1 rows followed by Fourier row 1. Its determinant is a homogeneous degree-five polynomial

    P_C(alpha,beta) in Z[zeta16][alpha,beta].

If the Fourier row belongs to the selected row span, every such determinant vanishes. Two selected minors with nonzero homogeneous resultant therefore exclude the entire projective line of couplings. No invertibility of H1, no division by alpha or beta, and no coefficient-ratio chart is needed.

The supplied witnesses retain the fixed degree five when constructing each 10-by-10 Sylvester determinant. This is essential: silently dropping affine degree could lose the point alpha=0. In the independently checked certificates, 2,972 pairs have only the second leading coefficient nonzero, 120 have only the first nonzero, and 118 have both nonzero. No pair drops both leading terms. The full homogeneous determinant handles all of these cases together.

## Why the modular certificate proves a complex obstruction

There is a ring homomorphism

    Z[zeta16] -> F97,  zeta16 -> 8,

because 8^8=-1 mod97 and Phi16(z)=z^8+1. A determinant/resultant commutes with this specialization. If the resulting residue is nonzero, the original resultant cannot be zero in Z[zeta16] or in Q(zeta16). Under the complex embedding its value remains nonzero. Therefore the homogeneous minors have no common projective zero over C.

This is a lift of a nonzero algebraic certificate, not an inference from enumerating F97-rational parameter values. The separate exploratory point probe is not needed for the proof and was not treated as characteristic-zero evidence.

Missing-coordinate witnesses require greater care: a modular zero alone would not prove an exact zero. The independent audit reconstructs every entry as an exact signed integer coefficient vector modulo z^8+1 by enumerating lattice paths. Every claimed missing coordinate is zero in both exact component matrices. Fourier entries are nonzero, so those witnesses exclude every coupling directly.

## Independent checks

All 4,368 subsets occur exactly once, with no omissions or duplicates:

- 1,158 exact structural missing-coordinate witnesses.
- 3,210 pairs of nonzero homogeneous-resultant witnesses.
- 6,420 minor polynomials independently reconstructed and matched.
- All 3,210 stored resultant residues independently reconstructed and nonzero.

Independence of methods:

- Green entries: direct enumeration of weighted lattice paths, rather than the producer's forward recurrence.
- Routing: direct permutation-image assignment with bitwise reversal.
- Minor coefficients: determinant multilinearity over 32 choices of component rows, rather than interpolation at six scalar values.
- Resultants: fraction-free integer Bareiss determinants of the fixed-degree Sylvester matrix, followed by reduction modulo 97, rather than modular Gaussian elimination.
- Controls include common projective infinity, a common finite root, nonzero endpoint resultant, and exact structural versus modular support comparisons.

These tests validate the stated polynomial certificate over characteristic zero. They do not turn numerical or finite-field search failure into a general impossibility theorem.

## Application and synthesis ledger

Each G_q application is a forward scan using prepared phases q^u. The masks have O(N) entries, and the roots and powers can be generated explicitly once supplied q is fixed. Each route, diagonal, sum, and fixed number of stages costs O(N) in the arithmetic/index model. This remains a viable cost description of the primitives. The exact target fails, so the family supplies no full Fourier evaluator at this size. An eventual construction still needs exact arbitrary-input equality, an all-size parameter pattern, explicit permutation and coefficient synthesis, and its full charged ledger.
