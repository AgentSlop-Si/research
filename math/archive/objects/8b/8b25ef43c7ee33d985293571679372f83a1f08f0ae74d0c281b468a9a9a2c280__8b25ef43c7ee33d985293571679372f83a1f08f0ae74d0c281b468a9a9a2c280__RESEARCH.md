# Fresh Boolean FFT — spectral completion and cubic obstruction

**Date:** 2026-10-09. **Status:** rigorous general quadratic rigidity lemma, independently reproducible finite cubic non-completion certificates, exact small characteristic-zero Boolean identity, and negative complexity conclusion. **Not a new O(N) FFT, not a stronger version of OpenAI #130.**

## 1. Why this is a distinct next experiment

The continuing target is **fresh input, full exact length-N Fourier transform, one processor, all work charged**, ultimately O(N). This experiment does not revisit cached updates, hash collisions, bounded-degree time-domain annihilator filters, chirp recurrences, Galois-output descriptors, CrocSwap rank networks, or Boolean overlap counts.

The unresolved Boolean-specific mechanism is `x_j^2=x_j`. With the unnormalized cyclic DFT `X_k = sum_j x_j zeta^(kj)`, this implies the exact *spectral idempotence* equation

`sum_a X_a X_(k-a mod N) = N X_k` for every k.

Can its nonlinear, global cross-frequency products compute missing bins more cheaply than the usual FFT? We investigate rather than assume. The convolution theorem and idempotent-group-algebra interpretation are classical; see [Fourier transform of Boolean functions](https://mathoverflow.net/questions/11595/characterization-of-boolean-valued-functions-on-the-discrete-cube-based-on-its-f), [Nicholson (1971)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022000071800144), and [DFT over rings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_Fourier_transform_over_a_ring). This paper does not claim novelty for those ingredients.

Inputs are fresh binary `x in {0,1}^N`. For the structural impossibility proofs, scalars are complex and `zeta` is a primitive Nth complex root. Finite checks use a prime `p=998244353` with the required Nth root; a nonzero algebraic determinant modulo this prime gives a *characteristic-zero* certificate, not merely a heuristic.

## 2. A tempting positive identity, and why it is inadequate

For `k != 0`, split out `a=0` and `a=k` in spectral idempotence:

`(N-2X_0) X_k = sum_(a not in {0,k}) X_a X_(k-a)`.

When `X_0 != N/2` this *exactly* recovers one missing Fourier coefficient from the other `N-1` coefficients in O(N) scalar operations. All operands on the right are indeed known. On uniformly random binary inputs, the singular fraction is `C(N,N/2)/2^N ~ sqrt(2/(pi N))`, which tends to zero.

But this **is not a fresh-input asymptotic FFT acceleration**: the algorithm already requires all other `N-1` Fourier values. Even a directly evaluated missing Fourier bin costs only O(N) operations. For real binary inputs `X_(N-k)=conj(X_k)`, so when the conjugate bin is available, recovery is easier and standard. At balanced Hamming weight `N/2`, the division fails entirely; these inputs are legitimate worst cases. The code explicitly tests the failure instead of silently dividing by zero.

## 3. General all-N theorem: quadratic spectral completion is impossible

**Quadratic rigidity theorem.** Let `N>=2` and let `S` be any proper subset of Fourier frequency indices. There exists no complex polynomial `P((X_s)_(s in S))` of total degree at most two that equals an *omitted* Fourier bin `X_k`, `k not in S`, for every binary `x`.

**Proof.** Let `A` be the `|S| x N` submatrix of the complex Fourier matrix comprising source rows. A quadratic polynomial in `y=A x` has the form

`c + b^T A x + x^T A^T C A x`

with `C` chosen symmetric (characteristic zero). Evaluating this expression at `0`, every `e_i`, and every `e_i+e_j` with `i!=j` and comparing to the linear target shows `c=0` and every off-diagonal entry of `M=A^T C A` is zero. Thus M is diagonal.

If a diagonal entry of M were nonzero, `e_i` would lie in the column space of M, hence the column space of `A^T`, i.e. the span of the source Fourier rows. Yet the expansion of every `e_i` in the complete Fourier basis has **all N coefficients nonzero**: its transform consists of roots of unity. It therefore cannot belong to the span of a proper subset of Fourier rows. Hence M=0. The remaining linear form is a combination of source Fourier rows, and cannot equal the omitted Fourier row `k` by basis independence. Contradiction. QED.

This is a theorem about **complex-polynomial combinations of already evaluated Fourier values**, not arbitrary circuits acting on the original input. The theorem does not include `conj` as a primitive: ordinary real-input conjugate symmetry is a semilinear operation and is cheaper. Nor does it cover rational expressions, branching, degree>=3 or a global new FFT computation.

### Degree-d character-sum necessary condition

If `P` has degree at most `d`, evaluating it on one-hot inputs `e_i` shows the sequence `P((zeta^(si))_s) - P(0)` lies in the span of Fourier characters whose indices are sums of at most `d` source indices. Thus the desired missing index must belong to the bounded sumset `S ∪ (S+S) ∪ ... ∪ dS` (with `0` included for constant terms). This is necessary, not sufficient. A source subgroup such as all even indices cannot generate any odd target at **any** polynomial degree. The code audits this inexpensive pre-rejection condition.

## 4. Why checking only equivariant polynomial monomials is complete

A cyclic shift `x_j -> x_(j-t)` multiplies Fourier bin `X_s` by `zeta^(st)`. If a polynomial P of degree <=d in the known bins equals `X_k` on every binary x, average it over all N shifts with Fourier weight k:

`P_k(y)=N^(-1) sum_(t=0)^(N-1) zeta^(-kt) P((zeta^(st) y_s)_(s in S)).`

Then `P_k(X_S(x))=X_k(x)` for all binary x; only monomials with frequency-index sum `k mod N` remain. These are **all** candidates in `graded_certificate.py`; omitting other monomials loses no possible degree-d solution.

Also, after substituting the linear Fourier forms into P, the result is a polynomial in the input coordinates of total degree at most d. Its unique multilinear reduction on the Boolean cube also has degree <=d. Therefore verifying a candidate on binary inputs of Hamming weight <=d is enough for *complete* cube correctness via Boolean Möbius inversion. Conversely any contradiction on those inputs proves failure on the full cube.

## 5. Certified no-cubic result for omitted conjugate pairs

To exclude the trivial real-input conjugation shortcut, the **source excludes both modes 1 and N-1**, while the target is `X_1`. For `N = 8,16,32`, the experiment enumerated all degree-1, 2, 3 equivariant monomials in the remaining modes, and found a set of `M+1` binary inputs of Hamming weight <=3 whose `(M+1)x(M+1)` matrix `[candidate evaluations | target evaluations]` has nonzero determinant in F_998244353.

| N | Known frequencies | Candidate graded monomials M | Witness input rows M+1 | Determinant mod p |
|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| 8 | 6 | 8 | 9 | 244742411 |
| 16 | 14 | 40 | 41 | 897006988 |
| 32 | 30 | 168 | 169 | 712284710 |

**Why this proves the same claim over C:** the evaluation matrix is defined over the cyclotomic integer ring `Z[zeta_N]`. Because the prime splits at N and `p-1` is divisible by N, there is a ring homomorphism `Z[zeta_N] -> F_p` mapping `zeta_N` to the selected primitive modular root. If the determinant were zero in characteristic zero, all such specializations would vanish. The independent checker produces nonzero residues, so the determinant is nonzero algebraically. Consequently the target column is independent from all M candidate columns **over C**, regardless of what complex constants the candidate polynomial might use.

This is a **finite-length impossibility certificate** for degree<=3 completion under the stated interface. It is not an asymptotic lower bound and does not forbid a different algorithm with direct time-domain access. The certificate input supports and exact determinant residues are in `audit_results.json`. The separate `independent_certify.py` reconstructs its own matrix and its own Gaussian elimination without importing the synthesis modules.

## 6. A sharpness control: one missing bin can have a cubic formula

If we leave bin `X_7` out of an 8-point binary FFT but keep bins `X_0,...,X_6`, exhaustive modular synthesis discovers a cubic formula. Write

`Q = X_1 X_6 + X_2 X_5 + X_3 X_4`, and

`R = X_1^2 X_5 + X_1 X_3^2 + X_2^2 X_3`.

Then the following **exact characteristic-zero Boolean identity** holds:

`4 X_7 = -(X_0 - 4) Q - R - 2 X_1 X_2 X_4`.

The independent `audit.py` verifies it for **all 256 binary vectors** exactly in `Z[z]/(z^4+1)`, a characteristic-zero cyclotomic ring. The formula corresponds to 10 nonzero monomial terms and costs 33 elementary scalar arithmetic operations in a simple naive evaluation, *after all seven known Fourier bins already exist*. By comparison a directly evaluated 8-term bin has O(8) operations; on real input `X_7=conj(X_1)` is even cheaper. **This example does not save FFT work.** It is a control demonstrating that higher-degree Boolean-only identities can genuinely exist, so the quadratic rigidity theorem cannot be extended to all degrees by the same proof.

## 7. Large ambiguity in half-spectrum completion

Let `N=2m` and choose any fresh binary `u in {0,1}^m`. Set `x=(u,1-u)`. The even output bins are *identical* for all 2^m choices:

`X_0=m`, `X_2=X_4=...=X_(N-2)=0`.

The odd outputs are

`X_(2t+1) = sum_(j=0)^(m-1) (2u_j-1) zeta_N^j zeta_m^(tj)`, i.e. a length-m Fourier transform of an arbitrary independent sign vector after fixed modulation.

Thus **no function at all**, nonlinear or otherwise, can recover the odd spectrum from the even spectrum on every binary input. The 8-point and 16-point ambiguity cases are explicitly verified and include 16 and 256 distinct inputs sharing the same even spectrum, respectively. This is an information-ambiguity result, not an arithmetic lower bound for computing the odd spectrum from the original input.

## 8. Why we rejected a tempting Frobenius variant before coding it

For base-field-valued inputs over a characteristic-p field, the Fourier spectrum satisfies `X_(pk)=X_k^p`. When p is fixed and N grows, Frobenius orbits can grow, apparently yielding O(N) *unit-cost extension-field* operations for all outputs in certain cyclotomic models. This is **known prior art**: cyclotomic FFTs and the 2026 arXiv paper *Cyclotomic finite-field Fourier spectra: Galois descent, native subfields, and residual coding* (https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20062) explicitly study the same orbit consistency. We did **not** relabel it a new invention or repeat its implementation.

For example, a primitive 2^t-th root over F3 requires extension degree `2^(t-2)=N/4` for t>=3. Each explicit output field element then requires Θ(N) base-field digits, and all N fully expanded outputs occupy Θ(N²) base-field digits. Counting an entire growing-width field element as one unit-cost scalar changes the computational model; it cannot prove an O(N) bit-algorithm or exact complex FFT. This is the same symbolic/expansion trap already recorded in the Galois-orbit checkpoint.

## 9. Conclusion and next gate

**New within this project:** (i) a clean all-N quadratic impossibility theorem for polynomial completion from a proper FFT subset, (ii) independently checkable degree-3 non-completion certificates at N=8,16,32 when a conjugate pair is omitted, (iii) an exact cubic small positive control with uncompetitive gate cost, and (iv) an explicit 2^(N/2)-fold spectral ambiguity family.

**Nothing here proves a novel O(N) fresh exact FFT**, and the project claims no publication priority for its component algebraic identities. Spectral self-convolution is established, and the finite certificates restrict only one candidate algorithmic class. A constant-degree spectral completion algorithm might start working at larger N, use degree>=4, employ rational/semilinear operations, or exploit direct access to input samples; none is excluded here.

The next serious candidate should generate a **growing set of *missing conjugate pairs*** without recomputing them and with **charged amortized < FFT** work. A proposed degree-4 or higher formula must first pass the same shift-equivariant low-weight certificate gate, avoid singular denominators for every binary input, and show savings after computing its source spectrum. If it only recovers one output with Ω(N) arithmetic, it does not beat the full FFT. Do not restart hash-consing, short annihilator sparsification, chirp-recurrence arguments, matrix-description compression, or CrocSwap network retuning.
