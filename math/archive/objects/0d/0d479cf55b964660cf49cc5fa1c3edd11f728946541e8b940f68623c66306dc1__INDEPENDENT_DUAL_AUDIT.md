# Independent dual-support and eight-coordinate audit

9 October 2026. Uploaded programs were read as source/data only, never run or imported. The companion checker was authored independently, uses only the Python standard library, and reads the original archive directly.

## Verdict

The new reductions are correct on their explicitly localized charts. Every coefficient in the submitted 20-polynomial export matches an independent reconstruction over Z[z]/(z^4+1). The three-character recurrence and eight-coordinate rational reconstruction follow from the Fourier-support argument, not from numerical fitting.

This is a smaller exact feasibility target for a restricted two-stage architecture. It neither solves that target nor supplies a new exact Fourier algorithm or a Fourier lower bound. No unbounded solving was attempted.

The audit adds three useful concrete artifacts:

1. Explicit factored saturation data for the 20-polynomial chart, including its scaled matrix, output anchors and inverse-corner factors.
2. A source-independent 3,750-node exact rational circuit specifying the twelve remaining F8 equations in eight variables, together with 29 required nonzero factors. This removes dependence on executing an uploaded evaluator.
3. An exact characteristic-zero exceptional partial candidate with S1=0, invertible C and nonzero B and A corners. Its construction is symbolic over Q(zeta8), with a small mod-97 certificate proving the required denominators and anchors are nonzero. It is explicitly not a full factorization.

## 2. Independent exact coefficient reconstruction

Use coordinates (t,V1,...,V6,h0,...,h6,beta,gamma), z^4=-1, and D=t(z-1). Put

T_k=(z-1)(1-beta h0+beta h_k)+(t-1+beta(h0-1))(z^-k-1),

q_i=z^i-gamma,

S_k=sum_(i<k) q_i h_i,

Rnum_k=sum_(k<i<7) q_i T_i+q_7 D,

N_k=-(D q_k+V_k Rnum_k).

Then L_k=T_k/D and eta_k=N_k/(D S_k). The normalized physical matrix has diagonal one, lower entries L_i V_j, upper entries h_i eta_j, endpoint L_7=1, eta_7=1, C70=t and C07=h0.

Define Cbar=C diag(D,D S1,...,D S6,D), and Bhat=F Cbar P^-1. Columnwise clearing is important: different B columns carry different S factors. The checker constructs Cbar from these definitions, forms its Fourier product and all thirty anchored B minors, and reduces every coefficient by z^4=-1.

Results:

- Exactly the ten stated first-row/first-column anchored minors are identically zero.
- The stronger six linear first-column support equations and six first-row annihilation equations also vanish formally.
- The other twenty polynomials match every submitted monomial and every integer cyclotomic coefficient.
- Maximum degree 9, 8,437 terms and 2,858 monomials all match.
- The coefficient-file SHA-256 is 54ebeee0c41301dbf494670aea5c6eaffb05bbe503af95f0077aa21a750ac1f3.

Independent exact finite-field evaluations supplement this formal comparison: 144 fresh cases over GF(17), GF(97), GF(257), using all four primitive-eighth-root embeddings in each field and allowing zero-valued interior coordinates. Each case compares all 64 scaled matrix entries and all twenty remaining polynomials with direct matrix calculations. Of these cases, 109 satisfy all final determinant/anchor requirements. The nonregular cases are retained as controls, not treated as solutions.

Both t and h0 are required nonzero. Removing their maximal common monomial factors from each equation yields an equivalent localized list with degree distribution:

## 3. Explicit localization of the twenty-polynomial system

Let

RL_k=D-T_k V_k,

QU_k=D S_k-h_k N_k, for k=1,...,6.

These obey RL_k/D=1-L_k V_k and QU_k/(D S_k)=1-h_k eta_k. A concrete sufficient and necessary product for the stated regular patch is

g20 = t h0 (product S_k) det(Cbar) Bhat70 Bhat07 (product RL_k) (product QU_k).

All factors are polynomial over Q(z). Since z-1 is a nonzero field constant, t!=0 already ensures D!=0. On this patch:

- det(Cbar)!=0 is equivalent to det(C)!=0, since det(Cbar)=D^8(product S_k)det(C);
- Bhat70!=0 and Bhat07!=0 are equivalent to the corresponding physical B pivots;
- the RL and QU factors are exactly the inverse-corner conditions.

The last statement uses the independently established all-size separated-matrix identities

adj(C)_(n-1,0)=-C_(n-1,0) product_(k=1)^(n-2)(1-L_k V_k),

adj(C)_(0,n-1)=-C_(0,n-1) product_(k=1)^(n-2)(1-h_k eta_k).

The first formula follows by subtracting successive suitably scaled rows in the corner minor; transposition gives the second. They extend polynomially even at vanishing generators. For invertible C, division by det(C) gives A's two corners. Nonzero corners, together with inversion's preservation of crossing ranks, reconstruct the global separated chart for A.

`explicit_dual_saturation.json` exports the entries of Cbar, both Bhat pivots, D, all S, RL and QU polynomials. The determinant and product remain factored; no unsupported claim about their minimal degree is made. Saturating by g20, or adjoining u*g20-1=0, is necessary before using the raw twenty polynomials as the advertised regular feasibility problem. The headline degree nine does not include such a reciprocal equation.

## 4. Proof of the all-n recurrence and eight-coordinate chart

Assume n>=6, characteristic not dividing n, z primitive of order n, and permutation prefix (n-1,0,n-2). The rest of the permutation may be arbitrary. Set xi=z^-1. Assume the normalized global separated C chart and all subsequently specified nonzero conditions.

### First lower column and first upper row

For beta=B_(n-1,1)/B_(n-1,0), the first-column identities force

C[k,0]-beta C[k,n-1]=a+b xi^k.

The k=0 and k=n-1 endpoint values determine

b=(t-1+beta(h0-1))/(z-1), a=1-beta h0-b,

L_k=(a+b xi^k+beta h_k)/t.

For gamma1=B_(1,n-1)/B_(0,n-1), put q1_r=z^r-gamma1. Each relevant physical interior column obeys

q1_k+eta_k S1_k+V_k R1_k=0,

where S1_k=sum_(r<k)q1_r h_r and R1_k=sum_(r>k)q1_r L_r. If S1_k!=0 this solves eta_k. These steps leave 2n parameters and (n-3)(n-4) anchored equations. At n=8 these are 16 parameters and twenty equations.

### Second lower column and invertibility

Let rho=B_(n-1,2)/B_(n-1,0), alpha=eta_(n-2)-rho, and v=C[:,n-2]-rho C[:,n-1]. Fourier support in frequencies 0,1,2 implies v_k is a linear combination of 1,xi^k,xi^(2k). For k=0,...,n-3, v_k=alpha h_k.

Alpha cannot vanish for invertible C. If it did, v would be supported only at the last two time indices. Its Fourier transform has the form z^(r(n-2))(a+b z^r), which has at most one zero among distinct frequencies unless a=b=0. The required vanished frequencies 3,...,n-1 include at least two. Thus v=0, making two C columns dependent. This is a proof using consecutive final time positions; it does not invoke an unrestricted uncertainty principle for composite n.

Consequently h0,...,h_(n-3) satisfy

h_(k+3)=(1+xi+xi^2)h_(k+2)-(xi+xi^2+xi^3)h_(k+1)+xi^3 h_k,

for k=0,...,n-6. These are n-5 independent linear recurrence identities. Their first three values determine the sequence; the actual h_(n-2) is still free. Let h* denote extrapolation through n-1.

Writing eta_(n-2)=a_k+b_k V_(n-2), with a_k=-q1_k/S1_k and b_k=-R1_k/S1_k, the final two support conditions form the stated 2-by-2 system for V_(n-2),rho. Its nonzero determinant makes that solution unique. The checker instead uses a generic modular linear solver, and obtains h* by three-point Vandermonde interpolation, providing an independent implementation of the source's Cramer/recurrence route.

### Second upper row

With q2_r=z^(2r)-gamma2, the second row imposes a second linear equation for eta_k,V_k, k=1,...,n-3. Its determinant is, up to order/sign,

Delta_k=R2_k S1_k-R1_k S2_k.

If Delta_k!=0, solving yields

V_k=(q1_k S2_k-q2_k S1_k)/Delta_k.

Thus the eight free coordinates are (t,h0,h1,h2,h_(n-2),beta,gamma1,gamma2). Conversely these formulas make all selected support equations true whenever defined, hence all 4n-14 selected anchored identities vanish. There remain

(n-2)(n-3)-(4n-14)=(n-4)(n-5)

anchored identities. At n=8, there are twelve, comprising six later lower identities and six later upper identities.

This is a coordinate theorem on the stated open locus. No claim about the zero-denominator branches or arbitrary permutations follows. The eight numbers describe a partially constrained candidate stage, not the arbitrary FFT input and not a full Fourier transform.

## 5. Exact eight-variable target and additional checks

`onion_eight_variable_exact_circuit.json` is a portable mathematical arithmetic-circuit specification over Q(z), z^4=-1. Its ordered nodes use integers, root powers, inputs, addition, multiplication, negation and inverse. It explicitly supplies:

- all entries of C and B;
- all twelve remaining residuals;
- all eighteen identically satisfied residuals;
- 29 nonzero factors: t,h0, six S1, five Delta, the boundary determinant, det(C), two B pivots and twelve local inverse-corner gaps;
- alpha as a diagnostic. It need not be separately saturated once det(C)!=0 is enforced.

Every inverse operand must be nonzero as well. A polynomial solver can add an auxiliary variable for each inverse and its multiplication equation, or expand numerators and saturate by denominators and guards. Solutions may lie in the algebraic closure of Q(z); restricting parameter values to Q(z) would be an extra unsupported constraint. An exclusion certificate for this circuit still concerns only this open chart.

The circuit has 3,750 nodes as encoded. This is a specification size for the fixed n=8 feasibility problem, not an optimized transform operation count. It agrees with direct independent matrix reconstruction in 89 fresh valid evaluations over three fields and in the source's fixed reference nonsolution. The exact twelve reference residuals all match. The reference has det(C)=10859, B70=4178, B07=7594, A70=4671 and A07=6329 modulo 12289, so it is a genuine regular partial candidate and a nonsolution.

The broader independent test covers n=6,7,8,9,10,12,16,32,64 over suitable fields and fresh legal permutations. There are 104 defined candidate points, 92 meeting all final nonzero conditions. Ten are singular, and two other nonsingular candidates fail an endpoint requirement. Every case satisfies the selected identities and fails at least one remaining identity. These misses are expected for unconstrained random points; they are not an impossibility certificate. The observed singular cases additionally confirm that rational reconstruction alone is not a replacement for det(C)!=0.

## 6. Exceptional branches really exist in characteristic zero

Both submitted finite-field S1=0 witnesses were independently reconstructed. They have invertible C, nonzero B and A corners, the first ten identities zero and all twenty remaining identities nonzero. Their displayed corner values match exactly.

The audit strengthens this with an exact characteristic-zero construction. Work in K=Q[z]/(z^4+1). Choose

t=2, beta=3, gamma1=1, (h0,...,h6)=(1,2,3,4,5,6,7),

(V2,...,V6)=(1,2,3,4,5), eta1=7.

Define L by the first-column formula. For q_i=z^i-1 define S and R as above. Because h0=1 and q0=0, S1=0 identically. Set V1=-q1/R1. For k=2,...,6, set eta_k=-(q_k+V_k R_k)/S_k. This solves the entire first-row family: at k=1 the coefficient of eta1 vanishes, and the chosen V1 solves the remaining equation. The first-column family holds by the definition of L.

All these formulas are well defined in K, and C, B, A have the required regular anchors. Here is a small exact nonvanishing certificate using reduction z->64 modulo 97 (64 has order eight):

- R1=86;
- (S2,...,S6)=(29,92,94,84,82);
- det(C)=19;
- (B70,B07)=(92,90);
- (A70,A07)=(15,33).

The other construction denominators t and z-1 also have nonzero residues. Therefore every rational expression is defined in the localized integer cyclotomic ring admitting this reduction. A quantity with nonzero residue cannot be zero in K. This is a characteristic-zero nonvanishing proof, not a claim that an arbitrary modular root lifts. The identities hold identically by the explicit rational construction. For example, lower_3_2 reduces to 31, so it is nonzero in K; indeed all twenty remaining residuals have nonzero residues.

The full certificate is `characteristic_zero_exception_certificate.json`. This demonstrates that the S1 restriction omits genuine invertible regular *partial candidates* over characteristic zero. It does not demonstrate a full Fourier solution on the omitted branch, and does not by itself refute a future theorem that all full solutions happen to avoid that branch. It does show why generic-chart exclusion alone cannot silently become a full-model exclusion.

## 7. What would settle the next gate

For the regular F8 chart: produce a verified algebraic solution satisfying every guard, or an exact unit-ideal/ideal-membership certificate after correct saturation. For a claim covering the whole separated-stage model, additionally exhaust the S=0, Delta=0, boundary-determinant=0, zero-corner and zero-diagonal branches, or solve/exclude the complete quadratic lift. A finite-field miss, equation count, random nonsolution or a singular raw-polynomial root does not settle either question.

If a valid factorization is eventually found, verify all Fourier entries and separately establish an all-length construction with fully charged arithmetic, coefficients, routing and storage. Nothing in the current coordinate count supplies that cost theorem.
