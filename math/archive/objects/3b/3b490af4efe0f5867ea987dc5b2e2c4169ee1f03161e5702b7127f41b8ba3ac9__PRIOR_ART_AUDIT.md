# Prior-art audit — all-length lift

The nearest-grid correction is part of the established NUFFT literature.

1. C. Anderson and M. D. Dahleh, **Rapid Computation of the Discrete Fourier Transform**, SIAM Journal on Scientific Computing 17(4), 1996, DOI `10.1137/0917059`. The paper treats unrestricted/nonequispaced transforms using local Taylor expansions and FFTs.
2. D. Ruiz-Antolin and A. Townsend, **A Nonuniform Fast Fourier Transform Based on Low Rank Approximation**, SIAM Journal on Scientific Computing 40(1), 2018, DOI `10.1137/17M1134822`, arXiv `1701.04492`. The paper expresses a NUDFT as a short sum of diagonally scaled DFTs; it discusses a Taylor construction and more stable Chebyshev constructions.
3. NIST DLMF §10.12 records the classical Jacobi--Anger expansions used here.

Therefore this bundle does **not** claim invention of low-rank near-grid Fourier correction, Taylor NUFFT, Chebyshev correction, or Jacobi--Anger expansion.

The distinct project claim being tested is the covariance-preserving composition with v9:

- one random unit-step chirp projection is reused across all expansion terms;
- for each target output, the term sum becomes one v9 estimator on a single modulated input;
- the sharp dyadic covariance bound survives with factor `(M/N)(1+tau_R)^2`;
- robust aggregation is performed only after reconstructing the arbitrary-length outputs;
- total storage remains `O(N)`.

A limited literature search did not locate this exact composition. That is not a proof of novelty.
