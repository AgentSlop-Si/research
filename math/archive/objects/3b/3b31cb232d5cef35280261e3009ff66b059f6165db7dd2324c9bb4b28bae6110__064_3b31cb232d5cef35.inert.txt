# A growing stage-count obstruction for the fixed-strength phase-12 family

Independent proof check, 9 October 2026. This is a scoped factorization
obstruction, not a general Fourier-algorithm lower bound or a priority claim.

## Result and scope

Let a word contain t phase-12 stages S=V C_a(A1) C_a(A2), or their
inverses, interleaved with arbitrary unitary monomial matrices. The two path
orders and the tree placement may change at every stage. Use the attachment's
fixed strengths a=1/32 and e=1/512; the tree Cayley has strength 2. If this word
equals a flat N-by-N unitary (including normalized Fourier), then

    t >= log(15 N / 16) / [log(6144) + 604 log(20)].

For N>1, t=0 is impossible. Thus t=Omega(log N). The displayed constant is
deliberately very loose. Unlike the attachment's five-stage gate, this bound
grows with N. It needs neither random expansion nor bounded induced 1-norm.

If each full-array stage is executed separately and charged Theta(N), this
implies Omega(N log N) work for that execution architecture. It does NOT
exclude algebraic fusion of an entire word, N-dependent unbounded strengths,
different nonlocal primitives, general circuits, arbitrary diagonal gains,
or an approximation task instead of exact Fourier.

## 1. Uniform absolutely convergent sparse expansion

For any Hermitian adjacency A in this construction, each row has at most
3 nonzeros and ||A||2<=3. Signed block copies of the tree adjacency obey the
same bounds. For |a|<=2 set

    E = I - (I+a^2 A^2)/37,
    H = (2/37)(I+i a A),
    rho = 36/37.

All eigenvalues of E lie in [0,rho], hence ||E||2<=rho. E has at most
10 nonzeros per row; H has at most 4. Since

    (I-i a A)^-1 = (I+i a A)(I+a^2 A^2)^-1,

the Cayley transform has the exact operator-norm convergent expansion

    C_a(A) = -I + sum_(k>=0) H E^k.                    (1)

The identity also holds for a replaced by -a, covering inverse Cayleys.
Use z=73/72>1. Then rho*z=73/74 and ||H||2<=14/37, so the sum of the
weighted norms of the listed terms is at most

    1 + sum_(k>=0) ||H||2 rho^k z^k <= 1+28 = 29.    (2)

The weight of the term -I is one; the weight of H E^k is z^k. We do not
assume the terms commute across different factors.

## 2. The apparently dense rotation also has this expansion

Write J for the permutation swapping the two halves and

    Abar = diag(-A_T, A_T),
    R = J C_2(Abar) = [[0,B],[B*,0]],
    V = c I + i s R,
    c = 1/sqrt(1+e^2),  s = e/sqrt(1+e^2), e=1/512.

Inserting (1), V has precisely three branch types: cI; -i s J; and
i s J H E^k, k>=0. Its weighted absolute norm sum is at most

    c+s*29 <= 1+29/512.

V^-1 changes the sign of s and has the same bound. A complete S or S^-1
therefore has weighted absolute norm sum at most

    K = 29^2(1+29/512) < 1024.                       (3)

The order of factors in S^-1 is reversed but this argument is unchanged.
Intervening unitary monomials have norm one and one nonzero per row.

## 3. Global total-degree truncation controls the error without log t

Expand the entire t-stage word using (1). Each term chooses one of at most
12 branch patterns per stage (2 path branches times 2 path branches times
3 rotation branches). It contains at most 3t geometric indices. Its degree
is the SUM of those indices; identity branches have degree zero.

Absolute convergence and norm submultiplicativity give

    sum_terms z^degree ||term||2 <= K^t < 1024^t.

Let G_L be the sum of all terms of total degree at most L. Then

    ||word-G_L||2 <= 1024^t z^(-L).                 (4)

This truncates the WHOLE expansion by total degree, rather than assigning
error 1/t to every stage. This distinction avoids an unnecessary log t loss.
For t>=1 choose L=604t. Directly, 1024*(72/73)^604<1/4, so (4) is <1/4.

## 4. The union of supports is still only exponential in t

For a fixed branch pattern, the number of nonnegative tuples of at most
3t indices with sum<=L is at most binom(L+3t,3t)<=2^(L+3t).
Zero-padding the unused indices makes this inequality valid even when some
branches are identities. There are at most 12^t branch patterns.

One term has at most 3t factors H, each with row support at most 4, and
total L or fewer factors E, each with row support at most 10. Monomials do
not increase the maximum row support. Multiplication multiplies the maximum
row-support bound. Summing all terms only unions their supports. Thus every
row of G_L has at most

    12^t * 2^(L+3t) * 4^(3t) * 10^L
      = 6144^t * 20^L
      = [6144 * 20^604]^t                            (5)

nonzero positions. The bound can exceed N; that is harmless. This explicitly
counts the supports of ALL retained terms, not only one selected term.

## 5. Fourier flatness forces almost full support

If F is a flat unitary and a row of G has at most s nonzeros, then on at
least N-s coordinates that row of F-G has squared magnitude 1/N. Therefore

    ||F-G||2 >= ||e_i^*(F-G)||2 >= sqrt(1-s/N)

whenever s<=N. Hence ||F-G||2<1/4 requires s>15N/16 for every row. Combining
this with (4)-(5) gives the stated stage-count inequality. A scalar multiple
of a flat unitary is covered when its modulus is one, as necessarily holds
for a product of the allowed unitary factors. A separate overall nonzero
normalization can be divided out first.

## Why this does not contradict the attachment

The phase-12 stage may simultaneously be exactly unitary, have robust
Omega(N) balanced-cut singular values in every order, have an O(N) inverse,
and have polynomially growing induced 1-condition. Those properties do not
make it Fourier-flat. This proof controls spectral-norm approximation by
row-sparse matrices, a property not captured by those earlier proxies.

The argument uses classical sparse polynomial/resolvent expansion and
submultiplicativity. No claim about novelty in the literature is made.

## 6. Fusion with the earlier rank-one Householder obstruction

The bound survives adding B unitary factors of the form I+u v* (in
particular Householders), at arbitrary positions in the word. Replacing
those B factors by I changes the product by a matrix R of rank at most B,
by telescoping one factor at a time. The remaining product W is unitary and
contains only t phase-12 stages and monomials. Thus F=W+R.

Let G be the sparse approximation to W above, with error delta<1/4 and
maximum row support d=[6144*20^604]^t. If B<N, choose k=B+1 rows I. Let
J exclude the union of G's support on those rows; at most d*k columns are
excluded. On I-by-J, G is zero, hence

    F[I,J] = R[I,J] + (W-G)[I,J].                    (6)

The first term has rank at most k-1 and the second has norm at most delta.
On the other hand, flatness and orthonormality of the selected rows give

    F[I,J] F[I,J]* = I_k - F[I,J^c] F[I,J^c]*,
    ||F[I,J^c]||2^2 <= ||F[I,J^c]||F^2 <= d*k^2/N.

Consequently the k-th singular value of F[I,J] is at least
sqrt(1-d*k^2/N) when the expression is positive. Equation (6), or the
best rank-(k-1) approximation bound, makes that singular value at most
delta. Therefore

    d*(B+1)^2 >= (1-delta^2)*N > 15N/16.             (7)

If d*k^2/N>=1, (7) is already automatic. This also covers any dimensional
edge case where J has fewer than k columns. If B>=N the desired overall
factor-count consequence is immediate. For t=0 the remaining product is
itself a monomial, so take d=1, delta=0 without approximation.

Taking logs of (7) and using log(B+1)<=B yields, for N large enough,

    t+B >= log(15N/16) / [log(6144)+604 log(20)]
         = Omega(log N).

This is an approximate-sparse-plus-low-rank bridge to the earlier
Householder gate. It does not charge a sparse rank-one update as a full
array pass automatically; an Omega(N log N) execution statement requires
that each counted phase-12/Householder factor is actually evaluated as a
separately charged Theta(N)-work pass. Algebraic fusion remains outside.
