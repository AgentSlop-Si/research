# Phase 30 — four/five Green generators, all-minor Fourier phase certificates, and the projective dimension threshold

**Date:** 10 October 2026. **Status:** Reproducible finite-length nonfactorization theorems for explicitly seeded families; general geometric gate-size theorem. **No new `o(N log N)` FFT or integer multiplier.**

## 1. Precisely what is being studied

Let \(N\in\{4,8,16\}\) and \(F_N=[\zeta_N^{ij}]_{0\le i,j<N}\), the **unnormalized** DFT. (A missing global \(1/\sqrt N\) can always be absorbed into a nonzero diagonal mask.) The outer invertible Green sums \(A,C\) are **exactly** those supplied by `phase29_two_parameter.build_family(N, seed)`, and

\[
 T_N=A^{-1}F_NC^{-1}.
\]

All \(T_N\) entries in these instances are nonzero, as verified during input generation. The three inherited Green components \(G_0,G_1,G_2\) are as in Phase 29. The new fourth component is

\[
 G_3=L_3(I-\tfrac1{11}W_3)^{-1}R_3,
\]

where \(W_3\) is an independently seeded Hamiltonian-cycle permutation and \(L_3,R_3\) are diagonal with powers of \(\zeta_N\); the exact seed convention is implemented in `phase30_four_parameter.py`. Every \(G_k\) is a dense matrix with an **O(N)-arithmetic application algorithm**, using one cyclic recurrence and two diagonal passes.

The middle pencil is

\[
 B(\lambda,\mu,\rho)=G_0+\lambda G_1+\mu G_2+\rho G_3.
\]

The feasibility question is whether

\[
 F_N=A D_1P_1 B(\lambda,\mu,\rho)P_2D_2 C
\]

for any complex coefficients \(\lambda,\mu,\rho\), **any two permutations** \(P_1,P_2\), and **any nonzero complex diagonal** matrices \(D_1,D_2\). Invertible outer matrices are fixed and non-tunable. This is NOT the assertion that all possible Green factorizations are impossible.

For the projective analysis, homogenize with \(\nu\):

\[
 B_h(\lambda,\mu,\rho,\nu)=\nu G_0+\lambda G_1+\mu G_2+\rho G_3.
\]

A Fourier factorization requires a nonzero projective point with \(\nu\ne0\), and we prove the stronger statement that **no** nonzero projective solution is possible for the checked, precisely seeded families and lengths.

## 2. Why the old anchored cross-ratio gate is insufficient

Phase 29 used the four anchored equations on a chosen \(3\times3\) submatrix, involving the source's first selected row and column. With four independent homogeneous parameters, the three linear conditions

\[
 B_{r_0,c_0}=B_{r_0,c_1}=B_{r_0,c_2}=0
\]

always admit a nonzero projective solution over an algebraically closed field. Such a parameter vector satisfies every anchored cross-ratio equation **trivially**, but cannot represent a Fourier matrix under invertible diagonal scalings.

Indeed, the tested Phase 30 anchored-moment Macaulay matrices on \(3\times3\) windows have rank 54 out of 56 at degree 5. These are **spurious zeros caused by a vanishing anchor**, not valid Fourier factorizations.

## 3. The stronger all-\(2\times2\)-minors gate

Fix any ordered triples of distinct source rows \(r_0,r_1,r_2\) and source columns \(c_0,c_1,c_2\). All complete input/output permutations restrict to such a pair of triples. Use target rows and columns 0,1,2 and form

\[
 E_{ij}=\frac{B_h[r_i,c_j]}{T_N[i,j]},\qquad 0\le i,j<3.
\]

If \(T_N=D_LP_LB_hP_RD_R\), then \(E_{ij}=u_iv_j\), with every \(u_i,v_j\ne0\). Thus all **nine** \(2\times2\) minors of \(E\) must vanish. Clearing the nonzero target denominators gives nine homogeneous quadratic equations in \(\lambda,\mu,\rho,\nu\):

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 0={}&T_{i,j'}T_{i',j}\, B_{r_i,c_j}B_{r_{i'},c_{j'}}\\
 &-T_{i,j}T_{i',j'}\,B_{r_i,c_{j'}}B_{r_{i'},c_j},
 \end{aligned}
\]

for \(0\le i<i'<3\), \(0\le j<j'<3\). These constraints have **no privileged anchor**. They can still have extraneous rank-one matrices with zero entries, but a *full-degree homogeneous certificate* rules out all projective zeros, including extraneous ones.

Multiply the nine quadrics by each of the four degree-one monomials. Arrange their coefficients in the 20-dimensional vector space of **homogeneous cubics in four variables**, whose dimension is

\[
 {3+4-1\choose4-1}=20.
\]

This produces the \(36\times20\) exact **Macaulay matrix** \(\mathcal M(r,c)\). If it has rank 20, its row space equals the space of every homogeneous degree-three polynomial in four variables. Hence the ideal generated by the nine quadrics contains \(\lambda^3,\mu^3,\rho^3,\nu^3\), so the only common affine zero is the origin; **there is no projective zero over any algebraically closed extension**. This yields a necessary-condition certificate, not a numerical fit.

### Lifting finite-field certificates to complex algebra

All coefficients are rational functions of \(\zeta_N\), with rational denominators nonzero in the selected finite fields. Since \(N\mid(p-1)\), the cyclotomic relation admits a homomorphic specialization \(\zeta_N\mapsto\omega\in\mathbb F_p\), where \(\omega\) is a primitive \(N\)th root of unity. A nonzero determinant after specialization cannot arise from an identically zero element of \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_N)\). Thus **rank 20 in finite-field arithmetic proves nonzero rank 20 in characteristic zero** for the same symbolic matrices.

We never assert the reverse implication, and no argument relies on numerical floating-point fitting.

## 5. A general projective-dimension barrier for these certificates

The projectivized rank-one \(m\times m\) matrices form the Segre variety

\[
 \Sigma_m=\mathbb P^{m-1}\times\mathbb P^{m-1}\subset\mathbb P^{m^2-1},
\]

of dimension \(2m-2\), codimension \((m-1)^2\), and defined by all 2-by-2 minors. A pencil spanning \(q\) linearly independent matrix components yields a projective \(\mathbb P^{q-1}\) inside \(\mathbb P^{m^2-1}\) (after entrywise normalization by the nonzero target). The projective dimension theorem says that two projective varieties whose dimensions sum to at least the ambient dimension must intersect over an algebraically closed field. Consequently if

\[
 q-1+(2m-2)\ge m^2-1,
\]

that is, if

\[
 \boxed{q\ge(m-1)^2+1},
\]

**every** such \(q\)-component projective span intersects the rank-one locus. Therefore **no all-minors projective-emptiness certificate on that window can exist** once \(q\ge(m-1)^2+1\). This is a limitation of the *certificate*, not proof of an admissible Fourier factorization: intersection points may have zero entries or zero homogenizing coordinate.

Conversely, \(q\le(m-1)^2\) is only the dimension range where **generic** avoidance is possible; it is not a universal nonfactorization theorem.

| q independent Green components | Smallest \(m\) whose \(m\times m\) minors can generically certify emptiness |
|---:|---:|
| 3–4 | 3 |
| 5–9 | 4 |
| 10–16 | 5 |

**Five-term control:** Add an independently ordered fifth Green operator with denominator \(I-W/13\). For selected 3-by-3 windows at N=8, the degree-3,4,5 Macaulay quotient dimensions were **6,6,6**. This is consistent with the degree-six Segre intersection for a generic \(\mathbb P^4\subset\mathbb P^8\). Replacing the witness by 4-by-4 minors restores full degree-three rank 35/35 for all 576 complete permutation pairs at N=4, with **100 sampled** mappings each at N=8 and N=16. The two larger five-term audits are NOT exhaustive.

The Segre/dimension argument is classical algebraic geometry. Our contribution within this research chain is its targeted synthesis interpretation and the exact fixed-family certificates, **not** a claim to have discovered the Segre variety or projective intersection theorem.

## 6. Complexity and honest limits

- One four-Green pencil application: four O(N) cyclic solves, diagonal scalings and additions, hence O(N) **field operations**, given the chosen cycle orders and coefficients.
- A successful complete \(A\)-middle-\(C\) realization would also need to count the outer Green applications, coordinate operations, construction of all parameters, precision and bit costs. **No such realization has been found here.**
- The offline dense inversions, Macaulay rank computations, and exhaustive search are expensive *proof techniques*; they are not a claimed online Fourier algorithm.
- All finite-size exclusions apply to the exact deterministic seeds and fixed outer factors, not to arbitrary non-affine Green networks and not to all lengths.
- Nothing here establishes \(o(N\log N)\) for arbitrary FFT inputs or a faster integer-multiplication upper bound.

## 7. What Phase 31 should actually investigate

1. Five or more genuinely independent, non-affine Green terms; test **4x4** and larger full-minor Fourier windows, not anchored 3x3 projective equations.
2. If a projective rank-one window survives, verify all entries are nonzero and the homogenizing coefficient is admissible, reconstruct both diagonal/permutation masks, and validate **both** exact Weyl identities over a field before numerical tests.
3. Seek reusable parameter laws **uniform in growing N**, and count total Green arithmetic, permutations, offline synthesis, and coefficient/bit precision. Finite exclusions are useful filters, not a complexity breakthrough.
4. Avoid returning to input collision caches, superficial density/commutator rank, or recasting standard radix-2 FFT stages as a supposedly faster algorithm.
