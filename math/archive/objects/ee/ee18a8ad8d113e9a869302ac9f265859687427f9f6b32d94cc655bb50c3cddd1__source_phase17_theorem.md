# Phase 17: an all-length, quantitative barrier for sums of independently ordered Green inverses

**Date:** 2026-10-09  
**Research status:** Proven restrictions on a precisely stated computation architecture, with exact modular/symbolic controls. **No new sub-`N log N` FFT and no improved integer-multiplication bound.**  
**Precedence:** The nullity theorem for inverse-banded matrices, Fourier-matrix unitarity, Chebotarev's prime-minor theorem, and Erdős–Szekeres are known mathematics. The combination and experimental tests are a continuation of this project's own Green/graph-reuse program. **No claim of external publication novelty.**

## 0. What problem this solves, and what it doesn't

Phase 16 closed our low-arity unitary network approach via the matrix-entropy theorem. Phase 17 examines a genuinely *nonunitary* alternative: apply several inverses of sparse operators, each in a different coordinate order, and sum their results. This model permits dense, noncommuting terms, arbitrary per-term phases and unbounded coefficients; a term costs `O(w^2 N)` via a bandwidth-`w` solve when standard banded elimination works. The question is whether a small number of terms can exactly or approximately produce the normalized DFT.

The main result is a **uniform all-length rank and spectral-norm obstruction** for this additive architecture. It neither covers arbitrary products of these sums nor proves an unrestricted Fourier lower bound. The last section gives an exact positive control showing that products can evade our additive argument.

## 1. Definition of the architecture

Let `N >= 2` and `q >= 1`. Define

\[
A_N=\sum_{r=1}^{q}L_r P_r^{-1} B_r^{-1}P_r R_r.
\]

- `P_r` is any permutation of the same `N` coordinates, different for every term. **The same P_r relabels both row and column indices of one inverse** (conjugation); independent row and column permutations inside the same term are outside this theorem.
- `B_r` is an **invertible** complex `N x N` matrix with upper and lower half-bandwidth at most `w_r`, in its own path ordering; thus `(B_r)_{ij}=0` if `|i-j|>w_r`.
- `L_r, R_r` are arbitrary diagonal matrices (invertible or not). They include per-term complex phases, rescalings and arbitrary nonunitary gauges.
- No constraint is imposed on the condition number or on whether the summands commute.

Put `W = w_1 + ... + w_q`. For tridiagonal Green inverses, every `w_r=1` and `W=q`.

Let the normalized DFT be

\[
\widehat F_N[j,k]=\frac{e^{-2\pi i jk/N}}{\sqrt N},\qquad 0\le j,k<N.
\]

## 2. Lemma A: off-diagonal ranks survive taking the inverse

Let an invertible square matrix `B` be partitioned across any **principal** coordinate cut `L|R`, with inverse `G=B^{-1}`. Then

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{rank} G_{L,R}=\operatorname{rank} B_{L,R}},\qquad
\boxed{\operatorname{rank} G_{R,L}=\operatorname{rank} B_{R,L}}.
\]

This is the off-diagonal case of the classical **nullity theorem**. For completeness, here is a short self-contained proof of the first equality. Left-annihilators of `G_{L,R}` are row vectors `y` such that `(y,0)G=(z,0)` for some `z`; multiplying by `B` gives `(y,0)=(zB_LL,zB_LR)`. Thus `z B_LR=0`. The map `z -> y=z B_LL` is one-to-one on `ker(B_LR^T)` because `(z,0)B=0` would force `z=0`. Conversely every such `z` gives a left-annihilator `y`. Therefore the left nullities of `G_LR` and `B_LR` coincide, hence so do their ranks. The second identity follows by reversing the blocks.

When `B` has half-bandwidth `w`, `B_{L,R}` and `B_{R,L}` each have rank at most `w`. So both corresponding inverse blocks have rank at most `w`, regardless of how dense `B^{-1}` is. Selecting a subset of the rows and columns cannot increase rank; multiplying by diagonal gauges cannot increase rank.

## 3. Lemma B: two large groups can be separated in **all** q orders

Let `pi_1, ..., pi_q` be `q` arbitrary linear orders on `N` different labels. Then there are disjoint subsets `R,C` of equal cardinality `m` such that

\[
\boxed{m\ge \left\lfloor \frac{N}{2^q}\right\rfloor}
\]

and in **each** order `pi_r` *either every member of R precedes every member of C, or every member of C precedes every member of R*.

**Constructive proof:** In the first order take the first `floor(N/2)` elements for R and the last `floor(N/2)` for C, ignoring at most one middle element. Suppose that after some steps `|R|=|C|=m` and every old order separates the two groups. In the next order, sort the `2m` selected elements, taking the first `m` as Left and the last `m` as Right. Write `a=|R cap Left|`. Because the groups are balanced, `|C cap Right|=a`, while `|R cap Right|=|C cap Left|=m-a`. Therefore we can retain either the pair `(R cap Left, C cap Right)` of size `a`, or `(R cap Right, C cap Left)` of size `m-a`. Choose the larger, of size at least `ceil(m/2)`. Both groups remain subsets of their former selves, so every previously established separation remains valid. The new order separates them by construction. Iterate.

This bisection lemma is **stronger for our purpose** than demanding a single common monotone subsequence. Its bound is actually attained by an adversarial interleaving of the two currently retained groups at each subsequent ordering; `balanced_interleaving_adversarial_orders` checks exact equality `m=N/2^q` for N divisible by `2^q`. It needs no prime-number assumption or heavy Ramsey theorem.

## 4. Lemma C: Fourier submatrices have quantitative rank

For any `R,C` of size `m`, let `X=(Fhat_N)_{R,C}`. Since **all entries** of `Fhat_N` have squared absolute value `1/N`, 

\[
\|X\|_F^2=\frac{m^2}{N}.
\]

Since `Fhat_N` is unitary and taking a submatrix is a coordinate restriction, `||X||_2 <= 1`. If `sigma_1 >= ... >= sigma_m` are its singular values,

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}X\ge m^2/N}.
\]

More quantitatively, for `m>W` and `m^2/N>W`, using that each of the first `W` squared singular values is at most `1`, 

\[
\frac{m^2}{N}=\sum_{j=1}^m \sigma_j^2
\le W+(m-W)\sigma_{W+1}^2.
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{\sigma_{W+1}(X)\ge
\sqrt{\frac{m^2/N-W}{m-W}}}.
\]

By the Eckart–Young best-rank-`W` approximation theorem, the right side lower-bounds the operator-norm error of approximating `X` with **any** rank-`W` matrix. This argument is valid over complex numbers for **every N**, including powers of two. It also holds for every normalized complex Hadamard matrix (unitary matrix with flat entry modulus).

## 5. Main theorem: all-length noncommuting Green-sum obstruction

Apply Lemma B to the `q` orderings in Section 1 and obtain separated `R,C` with size `m >= floor(N/2^q)`. In each term of `A_N`, the `(R,C)` submatrix sits in one off-diagonal cut of `B_r^{-1}` (orientation is allowed to vary with `r`). Lemma A bounds the rank of that submatrix by `w_r`. Hence

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}(A_N)_{R,C}\le W}. \tag{*}
\]

By Lemma C the Fourier block has rank at least `m^2/N`. Therefore:

**Exact obstruction**

\[
\boxed{ A_N=\widehat F_N\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\left\lfloor\frac N{2^q}\right\rfloor^2\le WN.}\tag{1}
\]

In particular, for `2^q` dividing `N`, 

\[
\boxed{N\le 4^q W}.\tag{2}
\]

For `q` tridiagonal resolvents `W=q`. Thus `N <= q 4^q` is necessary. With fixed half-bandwidth `w`, this forces `q=Omega(log N)` Green *terms* in a **sum**. If `N=2^k`, one can rearrange it to

\[
\boxed{q\ge \tfrac12(\log_2N-\log_2(qw))},
\]

hence `q >= (1/2-o(1)) log_2 N` as `N` grows, for fixed `w`, whenever the divisibility condition is met. We do **not** infer an arithmetic-operation lower bound for a shared-state implementation from term count alone.

**Robust approximation obstruction**. If `m0=floor(N/2^q)` satisfies `m0>W` and `m0^2>WN`, then *every* candidate `A_N` in this model satisfies

\[
\boxed{\|A_N-\widehat F_N\|_2\ge
\sqrt{\frac{m_0^2/N-W}{m_0-W}}}.\tag{3}
\]

Indeed the global operator norm dominates the norm of every submatrix error. On the separated `R,C`, rank `(A_N)_{R,C}<=W`, and Eq. (3) follows from Lemma C, using the conservative minimum group size `m0`. The right side is increasing in group size for `m>W` and `W<N` (alternatively, discard extra elements so the groups have exactly size `m0`).

For any fixed `q` and fixed bandwidth, as `N -> infinity`, the error bound approaches `2^{-q/2}`. In particular, for **q=2** it approaches **1/2**, even when coefficients are complex, arbitrarily large, and the Green terms are ill-conditioned and noncommuting.

This norm bound is a direct elementary proof and does **not** require Ailon's unitary-entropy or well-conditioned-in-place-gate assumptions.

## 6. Illustrative powers-of-two thresholds

For tridiagonal terms `W=q`, an exact Fourier representation is ruled out whenever `N>q*4^q` and `N` is divisible by `2^q`:

| Number of Green terms q | `q*4^q` threshold | First excluded power of two |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 4 | 8 |
| 2 | 32 | 64 |
| 3 | 192 | 256 |
| 4 | 1,024 | 2,048 |
| 5 | 5,120 | 8,192 |
| 6 | 24,576 | 32,768 |

This is **not** the minimum number of Green terms needed by an optimal representation; it is a rigorously proved necessary condition for this architecture.

## 7. Two independent specialized checks

### 7.1 Prime-order full-spark improvement for small N

For **prime** `N`, the Fourier matrix has *every* square minor nonzero (Chebotarev's theorem, e.g. Tao 2005). To obtain a separate small-size criterion, list labels in the first path order and repeatedly apply the classical Erdős–Szekeres increasing/decreasing subsequence theorem to positions in each of the remaining `q-1` orders. This yields a chain of length at least `ceil(N^(1/2^(q-1)))`, monotone (in either direction) in every path order. If the chain has at least `2(W+1)` elements, take its first and last `W+1` labels for R,C. Every Green term has crossblock rank at most its bandwidth, while Chebotarev makes the `(W+1) x (W+1)` Fourier minor nonsingular, a contradiction. Thus necessarily `N <= (2W+1)^(2^(q-1))`, and for q tridiagonal terms this reads `N <= (2q+1)^(2^(q-1))`. For `q=2`, prime sizes `N>=29` are excluded. This specializes to smaller N where the purely norm-based all-length theorem is inconclusive. The `green_sum_cert.py` tool outputs exact integer polynomials representing minor determinants, independent of floating-point rounding.

The prime threshold is **subsumed asymptotically** by the all-length balanced-cut result, but it strengthens small-size exclusion. No claim is made that the stated prime constant is optimal.

### 7.2 A Bluestein chirp needs many simple cyclic Green resolvents

For **even** `N` let `xi=exp(-pi i/N)`, `omega=xi^2=exp(-2pi i/N)`. The exact Bluestein identity is

\[
\omega^{jk}=\xi^{j^2}\xi^{-(j-k)^2}\xi^{k^2}.
\]

Set `c_t=xi^(-t^2)`. For even N this sequence is N-periodic. Hence a chirp circulant with entries `C_jk=c_(j-k mod N)` is (up to diagonal chirp factors and normalization) the DFT. Its `m x m` Hankel minor `H_ab=c_(a+b)`, `0<=a,b<m=N/2`, has

\[
H_{ab}=\xi^{-a^2}\,(\omega^{-a})^b\,\xi^{-b^2}.
\]

The middle matrix is a Vandermonde matrix on **distinct Nth roots**, so `rank H=m`. In contrast the kernel of a first-order cyclic-shift Green resolvent `(I-alpha S)^-1` (assuming `alpha^N !=1`) is a scalar multiple of `alpha^t`, whose Hankel matrix has rank 1. A sum of q such shift-resolvent kernels therefore needs `q>=N/2` to reproduce the chirp kernel **exactly**.

This is a special **commuting shift-resolvent** subfamily and should not be confused with a lower bound for arbitrary graph-resolvent products.

## 8. Independent exact controls and an escape route

Code and outputs are adjacent to this theorem:

- `green_sum_cert.py`: exact tridiagonal solves and ranks over finite fields, the balanced separator, symbolic cyclotomic Fourier minors, and a direct nonzero commutator control.
- `chirp_hankel.py`: exact finite-field Bluestein identity and full-rank Hankel certificates at `N=8,16,32,64,128`.
- `approximation_gap.py`: independent numerical SVD check of the proven analytic operator-norm lower bound.
- `product_escape_probe.py`: exact positive control for **a product of two noncommuting sums** of Green resolvents. At `N=32` the first sum has crosscut rank 2, while the two-sum product has rank 9; at `N=64`, the ranks are 2 and 17 respectively. Both products still FAIL Fourier covariance, with full-rank defects 32 and 64. Their application costs four linear-time path solves, but they are *not* FFTs.
- `tests/test_phase17.py`: 22 independent tests covering root orders, Gaussian elimination, exact minor coefficients, prefix/suffix ranks, universal balanced cuts and covariance control.

For a finite-field Fourier minor, a nonzero determinant modulo a prime `ell !=N` with a primitive Nth root is a valid **one-way certificate** of nonzero complex determinant: the evaluation is a ring homomorphism from the cyclotomic integer ring. Conversely, a zero determinant at one modulus would **not** prove a complex minor zero; for prime/power-of-two sizes we use exact integer cyclotomic polynomial reductions to avoid that false inference.

All `n x n` Green matrices used in numerical controls are checked in finite fields. The main theorems are symbolic and do not depend on successful numerical searches.

## 9. Honest remaining frontier — and a concrete Phase 18 gate

The theorem kills **sums** of a sublogarithmic number of independently ordered bounded-bandwidth Green inverses, including noncommuting and nonunitary sums. It does **not** kill:

1. Products of Green sums; their intermediate multiplication can regenerate high crosscut rank in linear application work (exact controls show it does).
2. Shared-state DAGs with intermediate branching, feedback, or cancellations that are **not expressible as one such sum**.
3. Independent row/column permutations *inside one summand*, or arbitrary non-diagonal outer mixers; our proof assumes one common ordering for rows and columns within each inverse.
4. Solvers whose inverses have growing off-diagonal rank (e.g. expander-graph operators), **provided** the actual solve work is charged.
5. Operations with increasing bandwidth, dynamic non-diagonal gauges, or higher-arity kernels, when their arithmetic cost is included.
6. Special arithmetic/bit-operation models distinct from fixed-field scalar arithmetic.

**The next concrete task:** For `M=(G_1+G_2)(G_3+G_4)`, use the exact Weyl covariance equation `M Z = D M` as the **primary gate**. Derive invariant consequences of this equation that survive intermediate high-rank mixing. Test symbolic structural identities, not just closeness to DFT; audit conditioning, inversion, multiplication count and any shared-state reuse. If a construction appears faster, independently verify its full Fourier matrix and its entire operation budget. Do not restart the folded radix-2 or unitary gate optimization paths closed in Phases 15–16.
