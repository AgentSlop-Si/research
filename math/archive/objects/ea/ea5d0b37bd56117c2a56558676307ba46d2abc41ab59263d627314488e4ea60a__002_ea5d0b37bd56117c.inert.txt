TERMINAL FUSION THROUGH THE ENTIRE RESIDUE TREE
An exact decoded-state construction and its charged remaining primitive
October 9, 2026

RESULT IN BRIEF
===============
The terminal decoder really CAN be fused through every ancestor. This is
not obstructed by the earlier half-grid commutator calculation.

Carry a length-L block of actual sequence values at each node, rather than
its numerator spectrum. If Q is that node's denominator and v is its first
L values, regarded as a polynomial, define

    R = floor(Q v / t^L),
    J_Q v = -((Q^{-1} mod t^L) R mod t^L).

Then [v ; J_Q v] is the first 2L values. Its even and odd entries are the
two child states, already in the final output basis. Every leaf decoder
has disappeared by an exact intertwining identity. There is no uncharged
terminal inverse FFT, and no missing half-grid operator in this algorithm.

The remaining per-parent map is J_Q = -T_{1/Q} U_Q, a lower-triangular
Toeplitz map times an upper-triangular Toeplitz map. Its ordinary charged
cost is O(M(L)); with L=Theta(k), the complete algorithm still costs

    C_setup + O((N/L) M(L) + N) = C_setup + O(N log k)

under ordinary FFT multiplication. No sub-N log k total bound is proved.

Fusing an entire h-level subtree is equally exact. Put H=2^h. All its
outputs, after one permutation, are

    v - t^L (R/Q mod t^((H-1)L)).

Thus the exact remaining primitive is one short-by-long convolution with
a support-dependent reciprocal kernel, applied to the actual constrained
family of states. Merely doing a longer FFT gives no gain. Shared blocked
FFT multiplication still costs O(H L log k) per macro-node.

This is standard recurrence extension/transposed polynomial remainder,
not a new recurrence algorithm. The contribution of this audit is the
explicit complete fusion and the precise cost target left after it. In
particular, a separate terminal-decoding charge is not intrinsic to every
representation of the tree. The earlier sum of separate half-grid and
terminal costs describes one implementation, not a universal barrier.


1. MODEL AND NOTATION
====================
Input: distinct z_1,...,z_k with z_s^N=1, and arbitrary amplitudes a_s.
Output: every f_m = sum_s a_s z_s^m for 0 <= m < N.

All scalar field arithmetic is counted, including support-only setup,
initial amplitude-dependent data, transforms, and final output. The proof
works over C or over a field supporting the stated root inputs. FFT cost
statements use the usual suitable-root arithmetic model. More generally,
retain M(L) as the fully charged polynomial-multiplication bound; no claim
about constructing unavailable field extensions at zero cost is needed.
Bit complexity and numerical conditioning are separate questions.

Let k <= L < 2k be a power of two; k=1 is harmless. Put

    D = max(0, ceil(log_2(N/L))),  B = 2^D.

Then N <= B L < 2N when N >= L. Any extra outputs are discarded. Nothing
requires N to be a power of two, or B to divide N.

At level ell and residue 0 <= r < 2^ell, set

    w_{ell,s} = z_s^(2^ell),
    Q_ell(t) = product_s (1 - w_{ell,s} t),
    F_{ell,r}(t) = sum_{j>=0} f_{r+2^ell j} t^j
                = P_{ell,r}(t)/Q_ell(t).

Here deg P < k and Q_ell(0)=1. Repeated w_{ell,s} cause no problem: keeping
the unreduced degree-k denominator preserves every identity below.

For a polynomial or series A, [A]_<n means A mod t^n. The operation
floor(A/t^L) means drop terms of degree below L and shift the rest down;
it is a coefficient operation, not evaluation or numerical division.


2. EXACT ONE-STEP EXTENSION IN THE OUTPUT BASIS
==============================================
Suppose Q(0)=1, deg Q <= k <= L, and

    v = [P/Q]_<L.

Write the ordinary polynomial product as

    Q v = P + t^L R,                 (1)
    R = floor(Q v/t^L),  deg R < k.

Why the low part is exactly P: Qv equals P modulo t^L, and deg P<k<=L.
Consequently

    P/Q = v - t^L R/Q.               (2)

Let h_L = [1/Q]_<L. Reading the next L coefficients in (2) gives

    [P/Q]_<2L = v - t^L [h_L R]_<L. (3)

Define J_{Q,L} by

    J_{Q,L} v = -[h_L R]_<L.

Then the first 2L values, in their ordinary sequential order, are precisely
[v ; J_{Q,L}v]. The state v already contains real requested coefficients;
there is no spectrum-to-coefficient conversion left to defer.

The formula also holds for arbitrary v in the full L-dimensional vector
space if P is defined to be [Qv]_<L. Thus this is an exact linear map, not
an identity justified only by a degree-k or rank-k heuristic.

Toeplitz factorization. Write Q=sum_{i=0}^L q_i t^i, padding q_i=0 for i>k,
and h_L=sum_{i=0}^{L-1} h_i t^i. For 0<=j,l<L,

    (U_{Q,L})_{j,l} = q_{L+j-l} if j<=l, and 0 otherwise,
    (T_{h,L})_{j,l} = h_{j-l}   if j>=l, and 0 otherwise.

Hence

    R = U_{Q,L} v,
    J_{Q,L} = - T_{h,L} U_{Q,L}.    (4)

At L=k, U is upper triangular with diagonal q_k, which is nonzero for
these nonzero roots. At L>k, only its first k rows may be nonzero and it
uses only the last k entries of v. This is useful structure, but is not
itself an algorithmic lower bound or an O(L)-application theorem.

The two triangular Toeplitz applications are two truncated convolution
operations. Their ordinary cost is O(M(L)) altogether. Both fixed kernels
and their Fourier transforms can be precomputed once per level. That
preprocessing does not apply either map to the changing state v.


3. ACTUAL FUSION WITH THE BOSTAN-MORI BRANCH
=========================================
Let A_ell be the numerator-coefficient map sending P to the two children:

    U(t)=P(t)Q_ell(-t),
    P_even(t^2) = (U(t)+U(-t))/2,
    t P_odd(t^2) = (U(t)-U(-t))/2,
    Q_{ell+1}(t^2) = Q_ell(t)Q_ell(-t).

Let D_ell be the L-by-L lower-triangular decoding map

    D_ell P = [P/Q_ell]_<L.

In the previous spectral state, the corresponding terminal map was
D_ell F_L^{-1}. Here the inverse transform is absent because states are
already coefficients of the sequence itself.

Let S take a 2L-vector and output its even and odd subsequences, each of
length L, and put

    C_ell = S [ I_L ; J_{Q_ell,L} ].

Equation (3), followed by parity extraction, proves

    (D_{ell+1} direct_sum D_{ell+1}) A_ell
        = C_ell D_ell.                          (5)

This is the promised exact fusion. It has incorporated both siblings and
their decoded output basis. Applying (5) at every node telescopes all
terminal decoders back to the root:

    D_D^{direct_sum B} A_tree
       = C_{D-1}^{direct_sum 2^(D-1)} ... C_0 D_0,  (6)

with the obvious residue-order permutations between levels.

An actual implementation need not construct these matrices. Start with
v_{0,0}=(f_0,...,f_{L-1}). At each node:

    rpoly = floor(Q_ell v_{ell,r}/t^L)
    tail  = -(h_ell rpoly mod t^L)
    full  = concatenate(v_{ell,r}, tail)
    v_{ell+1,r}        = full[even indices]
    v_{ell+1,r+2^ell}  = full[odd indices].

At depth D, the j-th entry of v_{D,r} is f_{r+B j}. Scatter these entries
into their final order and discard indices >=N. Nothing else is decoded.

This answers the algebraic fusion question affirmatively. It does not
establish that the resulting convolution primitive has sub-FFT cost.


4. FULL CHARGED COST AND INITIALIZATION
======================================
There are B-1 internal nodes. Each costs O(M(L)+L) by (4). The total
amplitude-dependent work after initialization is therefore

    O((B-1)(M(L)+L) + BL)
       = O((N/L)M(L) + N).                     (7)

All setup can be explicitly charged in either of two ways.

Simple sufficient setup, using only elementary formulas:
- Compute z_s^j and f_j for 0<=j<L: O(kL) field operations.
- Square all w_{ell,s} between levels: O(kD).
- Form each Q_ell by successive linear-factor multiplication: O(Dk^2).
- Compute each reciprocal prefix h_ell of length L by the elementary
  recurrence: O(DkL).
- Prepare all convolution kernel transforms and transform constants at
  their required sizes: O(D M(L)) in the ordinary FFT implementation.

For k=Theta(log N) and L=Theta(k), this is polynomial in log N, and hence
o(N log k). It is not omitted from (7).

A faster conventional setup is

    O(kL + M(k) log k + D M(L) + kD),

using a product tree for Q_0, Graeffe products for successive Q_ell,
Newton reciprocal computation, and reusable kernel transforms. Either
version establishes the same asymptotic conclusion in this regime.

With M(L)=O(L log L), (7) is O(N log k). This is an upper bound for this
implementation. It is NOT a lower bound on a joint algorithm exploiting
the states' common amplitudes, and it is NOT an improvement over the
previous established N log k construction.

There is no justified constant-factor speedup claim either: the exact
number and sizes of transforms depend on the short/middle-product
implementation, padding, and which data are carried. The value of this
representation is that all terminal work is explicitly inside one fused
branch primitive.


5. FUSING A WHOLE SUBTREE, WITHOUT HIDDEN TERMINAL WORK
======================================================
Take H=2^h descendants of one node and retain L values at each descendant.
Interleaving those descendants produces the first HL values of the
ancestor's sequence. Equation (2) gives the entire macro-map directly:

    K_{Q,H,L}(v)
      = v - t^L [ R/Q ]_<((H-1)L),
      R = floor(Qv/t^L).                       (8)

Only one R of degree below k is needed. All h levels of intermediate
numerators, half-grid extensions, and individual leaf decoders have been
eliminated from this expression.

If H_Q=[1/Q]_<((H-1)L) has been precomputed, the new output is one product

    -[H_Q R]_<((H-1)L),  deg R<k.              (9)

The kernel is shared across every macro-node with the same starting level.
It may be much longer than k. This is a concrete short-by-long convolution
primitive, with ordinary coefficient output, rather than an unexplained
terminal fixed matrix.

Honest conventional costs:
- One full transform of length Theta(HL) gives O(HL log(HL)), not O(HL).
- Blocking the long kernel into Theta(k)-coefficient pieces, using the
  shared short-input transform and each precomputed kernel transform,
  gives O(HL M(k)/k + M(L)) = O(HL log k) for L=Theta(k).
  Every changing output block still has to be inverse-transformed or
  produced by some other explicitly charged arithmetic circuit.
- Kernel setup is not free. A reciprocal prefix of length Theta(HL) can
  be generated by ordinary recurrence extension in O(HL M(k)/k) after
  polynomial-in-k startup. If HL is polynomial in k, doing this at every
  macro starting level is still polynomial-in-log-N setup. If HL is
  comparable to N, the kernel construction itself can already cost
  Theta(N log k) in this implementation.

Grouping a tree into H-way macro-nodes changes its count to approximately
B/(H-1), while each macro-node creates (H-1)L new values. The total
conventional arithmetic remains O(N M(k)/k), rather than gaining a factor
h or H. This is a charged construction and count, not a proof that a
better macro-map cannot exist.

The actual states must not be treated as independent generic vectors:

    v_{ell,r}[j] = sum_s a_s z_s^r w_{ell,s}^j.

All states at one level come from the same k amplitudes. A future joint
algorithm could exploit this. It must compute (8) or its equivalent on
this family at a proven smaller TOTAL arithmetic cost, including kernels,
initialization, any basis changes, and every output.

At the extreme, fusing the entire root tree simply returns the first BL
coefficients of P_0/Q_0. Calling this one fused operation does not give
an algorithm for performing it faster.


6. AN EXACT REFORMULATION OF WHAT REMAINS
========================================
The fused primitive can be stated without any BM or FFT notation.
Define the characteristic polynomial

    A(x) = x^k Q_0(1/x) = product_s (x-z_s).

Let Rem_{A,T} be the linear map taking the T coefficients of a polynomial
to the k coefficients of its remainder modulo A. Its column m is the
coefficient vector of x^m mod A.

Given v=(f_0,...,f_{k-1}), define a linear functional ell on degree-<k
polynomials by ell(x^j)=v_j. The sequence

    g_m = ell(x^m mod A)

has initial values v and satisfies the recurrence Q_0, so g_m=f_m. Hence

    (f_0,...,f_{T-1}) = Rem_{A,T}^transpose v.  (10)

Let V_{T,k}(z) have entries z_s^m, with rows m and columns s. Then

    V_{T,k}(z) = Rem_{A,T}^transpose V_{k,k}(z). (11)

Distinctness of the original z_s makes V_{k,k} invertible. Thus (11)
identifies the sparse expansion with transposed reduction modulo a
particular degree-k divisor A of x^N-1, up to a k-by-k basis change.
Computing and even densely inverting that small basis can be charged
within polynomial-in-k setup. The input here includes the roots, so this
observation does not assume a free factoring algorithm for a supplied A.

For linear arithmetic circuits, transposition also relates the cost of
(10) to the cost of polynomial remaindering itself, with the usual linear
input/output overhead and unchanged support setup. No claim about
transposing an arbitrary nonlinear amplitude-dependent algorithm is used.

This is a sharper remaining question than "can E be applied cheaply?":

    Can transposed remainder modulo an arbitrary k-root factor of
    x^N-1, including all support preprocessing, be done in
    o(N log k) arithmetic for k=Theta(log N)?

An affirmative construction would automatically handle the fused output
map. This audit does not supply that construction.


7. THE MODAL REPRESENTATION IS A CHECK, NOT A NEW SAVING
======================================================
For completeness, the already-known modal state

    b_{ell,r,s}=a_s z_s^r

branches as

    b_even=b,
    b_odd=diag(w_ell) b,
    w_{ell+1}=w_ell^2.

Its trunk costs O(kB)=O(N). The decoded state is

    v_{ell,r}=V_{L,k}(w_ell)b_{ell,r}.

This shows another algebraic removal of half-grid operations, but it moves
the output cost into short Vandermonde applications. For arbitrary N,
w_D is NOT automatically a convenient L-point FFT grid. The generic
transposed product-tree Vandermonde algorithm is O(M(k) log k) per
k-sized vector, not automatically O(M(k)). Shared point setup does not
remove its amplitude-dependent application cost. Powers-of-two N can
provide extra FFT-grid or collision structure, but do not justify a claim
for arbitrary N.

Therefore the O(N) modal trunk is not presented as a new algorithm or
asymptotic improvement. Formula (8), unlike that modal observation alone,
identifies an entirely coefficient-output subtree map with no separate
Vandermonde decoder remaining.


8. WHAT THE CURRENT FACTORIZATION DOES AND DOES NOT SHOW
=======================================================
1. Complete algebraic cancellation of terminal decoders is possible.
   It is incorrect to treat a separate sigma(L) terminal charge as
   mandatory for every conceivable representation.

2. The replacement J_Q is still a real amplitude-dependent operation.
   Precomputing Q, 1/Q, their spectra, J_Q itself, or a rank factorization
   does not apply J_Q. Its standard Toeplitz application costs O(M(L)).

3. Padding away an alias defect in numerator space does not turn (4) into
   a pointwise operation. In decoded-state space v is generally dense;
   R is a nonzero tail of Qv. The intermediate coefficient selection
   between the two convolutions cannot just be erased from an FFT
   implementation. This observation is not a circuit lower bound.

4. An actual sufficient single-node improvement is a uniform fused map
   cost gamma(L)=o(L log k) for the relevant Q family, with all setup
   o(N log k). Then (7) improves. There is no additional terminal sigma
   term to prove in this particular representation.

5. Alternatively, a jointly batched or macro-node implementation can
   improve the sum of the costs without improving generic gamma. Its
   proof must count the actual family in (8)-(9), not pretend that
   low rank alone supplies a fast circuit.

6. For perspective only, the unrestricted Q family of J maps contains
   arbitrary triangular convolution. Let k=L=2m,

       Q(t)=1+t^m A(t),  deg A<=m,
       v(t)=t^m u(t),    deg u<m.

   The first m entries of J_Q v are -(A u mod t^m): the first m
   reciprocal coefficients are 1,0,...,0 and the same entries of R
   equal A u mod t^m. Thus even this fully fused primitive includes
   familiar convolution in the unrestricted-denominator setting.
   These Q need not have roots among Nth roots of unity, so this is NOT
   a reduction proving hardness for the present restricted family.
   Nor is ordinary convolution known here to require L log L in the
   unrestricted arithmetic model. No such lower bound is claimed.


9. PRIOR ART AND NOVELTY BOUNDARY
================================
The one-step and macro extension formulas were derived here to audit
complete terminal fusion. They match established recurrence extension.
Bostan, Lecerf, and Schost's 2003 paper, "Tellegen's Principle into
Practice," Section 5.2, expresses transposed remainder as an initial
vector minus a shifted truncated reciprocal-times-middle-product term.
It explicitly identifies that construction with a prior recurrence-
extension algorithm. Section 6 gives the transposed Vandermonde/product-
tree treatment and charges its per-vector arithmetic even with shared
point preprocessing. These are primary-source confirmations, not a
claim that this audit originated those algorithms.

https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~eschost/publications/p33-bostan.pdf#page=6
https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~eschost/publications/p33-bostan.pdf#page=7

The BM numerator/denominator parity formulation and its Fourier
implementations are established in Bostan and Mori, "A Simple and Fast
Algorithm for Computing the N-th Term of a Linearly Recurrent Sequence."
The present note uses those identities as its starting point.

https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.08822
https://mathexp.eu/bostan/publications/BoMo21.pdf

No claim of novelty is made for modal advancement, polynomial recurrence
extension, Toeplitz factorization, transposition, or the elementary
identities in this note. No discovery about CrocSwap or issue #130 is
claimed or used.

==================================
The companion script check_fusion.py uses exact arithmetic modulo 97.
It tests distinct supports among Nth roots for
N=1,2,3,4,6,8,12,16,24,32,48,96, including padded L and many collisions
after repeated squaring. It checks against direct geometric sums.

Results in fusion_checks.json:
- 4,890 one-step extension checks
- 4,890 four-leaf macro extension checks
- 4,890 BM/decoded-state intertwining checks
- 542 complete output-tree checks
- 216 transpose-of-remainder identity checks
- 2,086 tested nodes with collisions among squared roots
- 130 unrestricted convolution-embedding checks
All passed.

Run:
    python check_fusion.py

These tests check algebraic implementation, not a faster running time,
complex floating-point stability, or an arithmetic lower bound. The
proofs above establish the general identities; the tests supplement them.

BOTTOM LINE
===========
Fusion succeeds completely at the algebraic level. It removes the
separate half-grid/terminal-decoder formulation and leaves a standard,
fully explicit recurrence-extension primitive. With all current costs
charged, that primitive still yields O(N log k), and no o(N log k)
algorithm has been obtained. The live target is now an exact cheaper
joint implementation of (8)-(10) for arbitrary root-of-unity supports,
not another argument that individual terminal FFTs cannot cancel.
