## Primary literature checked

- Josh Alman and Kevin Rao, *Faster Walsh-Hadamard and Discrete Fourier
  Transforms From Matrix Non-Rigidity* (2023 revision):
  <https://arxiv.org/html/2211.06459v2>.
  Their arithmetic improvement exploits more structured decompositions than
  the single common-core residual tested here. In their specific reduction,
  even a sub-`N log N` Walsh-Hadamard component leaves a
  `(28/9) N log N + o(N log N)` Fourier operation count. This illustrates why
  replacing one component does not by itself remove all logarithmic work.

- Josh Alman, *Faster Walsh-Hadamard Transform and Matrix Multiplication over
  Finite Fields using Lookup Tables* (2022):
  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.04643>.
  The constant-field lookup result has a different computational contract from
  an arbitrary exact complex FFT. The user's checkpoint already documents it;
  this experiment does not claim to rediscover or implement that result.

- OpenAI, *Exact Fourier transforms below n log n*, formalization scope:
  <https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/lean/docs/130.md>.
  The current document distinguishes its subsequential circuit statement from
  a separate uniform exact-complex-arithmetic statement. It explicitly charges
  scalar preparation in the latter and does not turn it into a bit-complexity
  or bounded-conditioning claim. The present restricted audit neither improves
  nor refutes that result.

The proof and support count in `AUDIT.md` are derived explicitly for the stated
matrices. The literature check is not an exhaustive novelty review. Standard
telescoping, rank subadditivity and modular nonzero-minor arguments are used
without claiming them as new inventions.
