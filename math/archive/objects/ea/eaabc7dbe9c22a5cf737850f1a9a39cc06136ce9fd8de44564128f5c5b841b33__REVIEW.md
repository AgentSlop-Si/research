## Coset spectrum, rank budgets and trie

Let N=dm, g=gcd(d,m), A=a+d Z_m. Removing the coset row/column phases from C=U[A,A] gives C_uv=N^{-1/2} exp(−2pi i d uv/m). Its Gram is (1/d) times the equivalence relation u=v modulo m/g, so it consists of m/g all-ones g-blocks divided by d. Since U is unitary, the complementary-cut Gram is I−CC*. Its singular spectrum is therefore 1 with multiplicity m−m/g and sqrt(1−g/d) with multiplicity m/g, including zeros. This handles d=N, g=1, and the rank-deficient g=d case correctly. For nonzero coset shift the raw Gram carries diagonal unit phases; the spectrum is unchanged.

For an invertible weighted-permutation difference K=I−diag(w)P, complementary inverse nullity gives rank K^{-1}[A,A^c]=rank K[A,A^c]. The latter is exactly the active outgoing crossing count, since it is a weighted partial permutation. Zero weights are retained correctly. Diagonal endpoints preserve the bound; addition and multiplication in the same coordinate frame add budgets. Arbitrary unmatched monomial endpoints or uncharged shuffles do not obey the stated unchanged-cut formula. The source explicitly retains the outer-endpoint qualification from the earlier WEDC correction.

Eckart–Young gives the claimed operator error floors from the cut’s next singular value. For N=2^k and d=2^r, r<=k/2, the nonzero cut singular values are all one and the necessary budget is (N/d)(1−1/d). The grouped-cycle parity corollary qh>=N/4 follows immediately. This excludes only a representation family, not every non-affine cycle or every FFT.

An edge u->v contributes to exactly one residue cut at each level r>nu_2(u−v). Summing the required budgets over levels 1..R yields exactly N(R−1+2^{−R}). The source's sum criterion is weaker than all individual cut tests, as it says. For the LSB trie, adding one at the source leaf and subtracting one at its lowest common ancestor with the target accumulates to one precisely along the outgoing-cut path. Thus every residue budget is obtained in O(N+E) word/arithmetic operations, O(N) storage. The trailing-zero table is explicitly constructed. Counters need O(log(N+E)) bits; this is not a unit-bit-complexity claim.

Related subgroup-selected Fourier isometries were already described by [Tao in a 2020 primary MathOverflow answer](https://mathoverflow.net/questions/351118/large-fourier-submatrices-with-small-operator-norm). The exact local derivation here is self-contained; no new harmonic-analysis priority is asserted.

## Aggregate-budget algorithm: independent proof and cost audit

Work in an exact field with a supplied primitive Nth root, characteristic not dividing N. For N=QM, let eta=omega^Q and write y_t[u]=x[t+Qu]. A genuine r-mode fiber is a sum of r distinct nonzero characters. Its Hankel factorization through distinct-node Vandermonde matrices has rank r, so 2r samples determine its minimal recurrence. With H=floor(M/3), the complete M-sample bounded Berlekamp–Massey scan certainly recovers every r<=H fiber. Any candidate is root-scanned against all M known roots, amplitudes are recovered, and every raw sample is checked. No unverified recurrence is accepted.

A quadratic transposed Vandermonde solve can be implemented without a hidden dense inverse: form P(z)=product(z−a_j) in O(r²), compute each quotient P(z)/(z−a_j) in O(r), divide by P′(a_j), and pair these Lagrange coefficients with the first r moments. These are exactly rows of the inverse moment matrix. Root scanning and full verification cost O(Mr), amplitude work O(r²).

The BM degree is monotone. Capping it by min(H,remaining) prevents a bad fiber from causing unbounded work before rejection/fallback. If verified recovery fails while remaining<=H, no signal within the remaining Fourier budget was missed. If remaining>H, a direct M² transform is allowed. Every such fallback has more than H nonzero modes, because a light fiber would have passed; an accepted fallback charges its M² work to its modes. A final rejected fallback also satisfies remaining>H, hence K0>H and M²=O(MK0). A final failed capped scan costs O(MK0) as well. Zero/read work is O(N), and M<3 is constant-size direct work. These points establish the bound for all rejected inputs, not merely promised inputs.

Summing verified light costs gives M*sum r_t+sum r_t² <=MK0+K0². With a negative-exponent DFT convention, character orthogonality gives

(Fx)[k]=M sum_{t,h congruent k mod M} c_{t,h} omega^{−kt}.

Each recovered mode contributes to Q bins, giving O(N+QK0) including all output writes. Root tables cost O(N). The total is O(N+MK0+QK0+K0²). Thus K0=O(Q), Q²=O(N) gives O(N) exact work on the accepted structured family, with all rejection work also bounded. An explicit full input scan can and should precede early rejection to meet the source's full-read contract.

At Q=M, one fiber can have all Q modes and the other Q−1 fibers one distinct mode each: K=2Q−1 while the Q-by-M raw fiber matrix is full rank Q. This strictly escapes every fixed per-fiber cap. Generic inputs still have K≈N and are rejected at a linear-family budget. Exact root testing, field inversions and supplied coefficients are in the ideal field model; bit complexity, numerical recovery and finding a suitable root/field are not proved.
