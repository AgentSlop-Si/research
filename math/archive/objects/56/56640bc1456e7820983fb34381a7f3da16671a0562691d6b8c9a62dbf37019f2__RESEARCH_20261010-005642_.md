# Beyond rank feasibility: exact expansion of the surviving 256-point FFT architecture

**October 10, 2026 — Reproducible continuation of the long-running FFT scan project**

## Question

The *unmodified* four-stage N=256 design from the nonlinear rank checkpoint passed 255 parity masks, many interval partitions and sampled full-network cuts. Is that an accident of incomplete testing, or does it reflect a mathematical reason that the usual partition-rank obstruction cannot see a contradiction? We still want an exact, all-input Fourier algorithm with fully charged arithmetic better than N log N; passing a rank test is not such an algorithm.

## Main result: an all-subsets rank-test-immunity theorem for this layout

Represent the stage ordering as four Hamilton paths transported into the original input index labels. Exact integer matrix congruence certifies the second Laplacian eigenvalue of the resulting 1020-edge multigraph as **strictly greater than 2.02**. This yields a conductance inequality stronger than any possible rank of a target matrix block, for **every input partition**. Consequently, the *specific moving-partition boundary-rank tests* used in past checkpoints are exhausted: they cannot exclude this architecture, no matter how clever the input subset selection. This includes, but is not limited to, all parity, interval, periodic universal, and nonlinear input masks within that particular test formulation.

This says **nothing** about coefficient realizability, and nothing conclusive about general full-network min-cuts.

- Proof implies every balanced graph partition crosses **at least 130 edges**. Fourier rank of any 128×128 submatrix can be at most 128.

## Contrast with the old nonlinear edit

The prior checkpoint excluded a *modified* version of this layout by a 128 > 124 rank witness, after inserting five Toffoli gates. It did **not** exclude the original. The spectral estimates provide a geometric explanation of why these layouts behave differently:

| Layout | Numerical second eigenvalue | Exact theorem? |
|---|---:|---|
| Original dense GF(2)-linear four-path mixer | 2.0286733431 | Yes, rigorous > 2.02 integer certificate |
| Five-Toffoli edited mixer | 1.5089523802 | No new spectral certificate; prior exact 128 > 124 witness still stands |

The latter observed eigenvalue is compatible with the known low-capacity partition; the calculation is explanatory, not a replacement for that prior proof.

## Comparison with outside work

The classical butterfly factorization explicitly captures an FFT with O(N log N) arithmetic, and general butterfly methods are expressive; **this project is not rediscovering that as a new result**. For background, see Dao et al. 2019 https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.05895 and Osgood–Siripuram–Wu 2012 https://arxiv.org/abs/1204.0992. OpenAI's September 2026 preprint independently claims a much smaller-than-practical exact complex-register power saving, O(N(log N)^(1-10^-13)), at every length: https://github.com/openai/math/tree/main/preprints/An-explicit-power-saving-for-the-exact-discrete-Fourier-transform-September-25-2026 . Our target is an independently constructive and meaningfully stronger method, not a reproduction of those architectures.

## What this does not solve

We have **no exact S1,S2,S3,S4** implementing F256 for the preserved affine permutations, much less a construction for growing N. We have not counted a working transform's scalar operations because no such transform has been found.

The next valid gate is **coefficient compatibility**, with all degenerate generator branches represented. For a first exact-algebra attempt:

1. Introduce all lower, upper, and diagonal stage generators, with fixed shuffles from `AFFINE_LAYOUT.json`.
2. Preserve zero coefficients and zero corner generators—do not divide by unproved nonzero values.
3. Impose polynomial equations from selected Fourier entries; use stage-gauge reduction and the full-network graph's nonlinear constraints, not merely cut capacities.
4. Search for a certificate of infeasibility (or an exact construction) over the cyclotomic field, rather than mistaking a numerical optimizer's residual for proof.
5. If coefficients exist, build an exact executable transform for arbitrary fresh inputs and audit both arithmetic and scalar preparation. Do not infer asymptotic savings from one length.

## Additional result: a true Fourier-specific two-outer-stage gate

After the spectral result proved our old moving-partition tests cannot settle the N256 survivor, we returned to Fourier coefficient compatibility. See `COEFFICIENT_GATE.md` and the executable `fourier_compatibility_gate.py`.

Eliminate the first and last invertible quasiseparable stages algebraically:

\[
H=P_3^{-1}S_4^{-1}F_{256}S_1^{-1}P_1^{-1}=S_3P_2S_2.
\]

For explicit 128-element row and column subsets induced by input parity mask 64, the two middle stages imply **rank(H[R,C])≤16**, so every 17×17 selected minor must vanish. This yields exact polynomial equations in *only the two outer inverse stages*, without dividing by corner generators. These are genuine Fourier-specific necessary equations—not solved feasibility conditions.

We verified one fixed minor at C4=C1=I is nonzero modulo 12289 (8799) and 65537 (20067), and the whole Fourier selected block has rank 128 in both fields. These nonzero modular determinants certify that the equation is nontrivial over the complex cyclotomic field. Synthetic valid full four-stage products reconstruct their middle stages exactly over both fields, including nontrivial unitriangular outer factors. Thus the 17-minor gate is now an executable target for *real coefficient* research.
