# Weyl–Green orbit fusion and exact Krylov propagation gates — v20

**Checkpoint:** 10 October 2026  
**Primary target:** exact, full-output DFT of an arbitrary fresh input in fully charged sequential `o(N log N)` arithmetic, ideally `O(N)`.  
**Status:** one exact positive joint-evaluation identity and two scoped impossibility theorems. **No exact faster FFT is constructed.** The v15 randomized approximate all-length result and all v16–v19 exact gates remain unchanged.

## 1. Starting point

v19 left two exact escape routes:

1. jointly evaluate many noncommuting Green-like operators rather than paying one `O(N)` scan per term;
2. use a constant-degree, high-treewidth sparse denominator with an exact `O(N)` inverse application that is not ordinary separator elimination.

This checkpoint attacks both.

The positive construction below uses a finite Weyl orbit. It is more expressive than v19's scalar root-of-unity shifts of one resolvent: its generators are projectively noncommuting. However, all generators still use one Hamiltonian support order dressed by different phase gauges; this is **not** yet the genuinely different-permutation-order bank left open by v18.

## 2. Weyl–Green orbit

Let `N` be an odd prime and work over a field of characteristic not dividing `N` containing a primitive `N`th root `omega`. Let

- `X^h e_j = e_(j+h)` for a unit `h mod N`;
- `Z e_j = omega^j e_j`;
- `W_r = X^h Z^r`, `r in Z_N`.

Then

\[
W_rW_q=\omega^{h(r-q)}W_qW_r,
\]

so distinct generators are genuinely noncommuting up to a nontrivial central phase.

For a scalar `alpha` with `alpha^N != 1`, define

\[
G_r=(I-\alpha W_r)^{-1}.
\]

Every `G_r` is a dense, invertible Green operator and applies in `O(N)` arithmetic by one first-order cyclic recurrence.

Because `N` is odd,

\[
W_r^N=I.
\]

Hence

\[
G_r=\frac1{1-\alpha^N}\sum_{m=0}^{N-1}\alpha^mW_r^m.
\]

The exact power action is

\[
W_r^m e_j
=\omega^{r(mj+h\binom m2)}e_{j+mh}.
\tag{2.1}
\]

---

## 3. Positive result: an `N`-operator character packet in `O(N)`

For `s in Z_N`, take the operator-index Fourier character

\[
H_s
=\frac1N\sum_{r=0}^{N-1}\omega^{-rs}G_r.
\]

### Theorem 3.1 — sparse character-packet identity

For every input basis vector `e_j`,

\[
\boxed{
H_s e_j
=\frac1{1-\alpha^N}
\sum_{\substack{0\le m<N\\
mj+h\binom m2=s}}
\alpha^m e_{j+mh}.
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

### Proof

Insert (2.1) into the finite geometric expansion of `G_r`. The average over `r` is

\[
\frac1N\sum_{r=0}^{N-1}
\omega^{r(mj+h\binom m2-s)},
\]

which is one exactly when the displayed congruence holds and zero otherwise. QED.

### Linear-work evaluator

- If `m=0`, the congruence holds for all `j` only when `s=0`; this contributes the diagonal.
- For each nonzero `m`, the congruence is linear in `j` and has the unique solution

\[
j=m^{-1}\left(s-h\binom m2\right)\pmod N.
\]

Thus:

- `H_0` has exactly `2N-1` nonzero entries;
- every `H_s`, `s!=0`, has exactly `N-1` nonzero entries.

All modular inverses `m^{-1} mod N` can be prepared in `O(N)` word operations by the standard prime-modulus recurrence, and the powers `alpha^m` are generated incrementally. Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
H_sx\text{ is evaluated in }O(N)\text{ field arithmetic and }O(N)\text{ storage.}
}
\]

This fuses **all `N` dense Green solves** into one sparse pass for a selected operator-index character.

The Python finite-field prototype shows large speedups over literally evaluating all `N` Green rows and averaging them, but those timings compare two research implementations and are not FFT-library benchmarks.

## 4. Arbitrary orbit weights: exact entry formula

Let `c_0,...,c_(N-1)` be arbitrary and put

\[
T_c=\sum_{r=0}^{N-1}c_rG_r,
\qquad
\widehat c(q)=\sum_{r=0}^{N-1}c_r\omega^{rq}.
\]

For row `i`, column `j`, let

\[
m=h^{-1}(i-j)\pmod N,
\]

represented by `0,...,N-1`, and define the quadratic coordinate

\[
u_h(t)=\frac{t(t-h)}{2h}\pmod N.
\]

Since

\[
u_h(i)-u_h(j)=mj+h\binom m2,
\]

we obtain the exact formula

\[
\boxed{
T_c[i,j]
=\frac{\alpha^m}{1-\alpha^N}
\widehat c\bigl(u_h(i)-u_h(j)\bigr).
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

This completely describes the `N`-dimensional linear span of the Weyl–Green orbit.

## 5. Main obstruction: every weighted packet has a zero fiber minor

The quadratic coordinate has the involutive symmetry

\[
u_h(h-t)=u_h(t).
\]

Choose rows

\[
\{i,h-i\}
\]

and columns

\[
\{j,h-j\},
\]

with

\[
i\ne j,
\qquad
i+j\ne h,
\]

and neither pair collapsed to its fixed point. For `N>=5`, the explicit choice `i=0`, `j=2h` works.

All four entries in this `2 x 2` block use the same value of `widehat c`, because the quadratic coordinate is unchanged by either reflection.

The four cyclic step lengths occur as

\[
m,-m,s,-s
\]

with both `m,s` nonzero. Their canonical representatives satisfy

\[
[m]_N+[-m]_N=N=[s]_N+[-s]_N.
\]

Therefore the Green factors also cancel pairwise, and

\[
\boxed{
\det T_c[\{i,h-i\},\{j,h-j\}]=0.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

Arbitrary nonzero left/right diagonal gauges multiply this determinant by a nonzero scalar and cannot remove the zero.

### Theorem 5.1 — affine Fourier exclusion

Let

\[
M[i,j]
=\lambda_i\rho_j
\omega^{(ai+b)(cj+d)},
\]

where `a,c` are nonzero modulo prime `N`, and every `lambda_i,rho_j` is nonzero. This covers independent affine endpoint permutations and arbitrary endpoint diagonal gauges of the Fourier matrix.

For the same reflected row/column pairs, its `2 x 2` determinant is a nonzero scalar times

\[
1-\omega^{ac(i-(h-i))((h-j)-j)}.
\]

At `i=0`, `j=2h`, the exponent is a nonzero multiple of `3ac h^2` for every prime `N>=5`. Hence the determinant is nonzero.

Consequently:

\[
\boxed{
M\ne L\left(\sum_r c_rG_r\right)R
}
\]

for every choice of packet weights and invertible diagonal `L,R`.

### Interpretation

We found an exact linear-work fusion of `N` projectively noncommuting Green operators for each character aggregate. But the entire weighted orbit span has a rigid quadratic-fiber compatibility relation that an affine Fourier matrix violates.

This closes the most symmetric Weyl-orbit fusion as the missing exact FFT stage.

It does **not** cover:

- arbitrary non-affine endpoint permutations;
- independently varying left/right gauges on every term before summation;
- packets using several different shift steps `h` simultaneously;
- genuinely different permutation support orders.

## 6. High-treewidth sparse inverses: exact Krylov propagation gate

v19 showed that a stable sparse denominator whose inverse is Fourier-like must have linear treewidth. A constant-degree expander can satisfy that requirement, so a second hope was an exact `O(N)` Krylov-style solve.

Let `A` be invertible. Form the directed support graph with an arc

\[
j\to i
\]

whenever `A[i,j]` is nonzero.

For a right-hand side `e_j`, every vector in

\[
\mathcal K_{d+1}(A,e_j)
=\operatorname{span}\{e_j,Ae_j,\ldots,A^de_j\}
\]

is supported only on vertices reachable from `j` in at most `d` directed steps.

### Theorem 6.1 — distance lower bound for exact Krylov solves

If `(A^{-1})[i,j] != 0` and the directed distance from `j` to `i` is `D`, then any exact solver whose answer for `e_j` lies in `K_(d+1)(A,e_j)` requires

\[
\boxed{d\ge D.}
\]

In particular, if `A^{-1}` is dense, the worst-case Krylov depth is at least the directed diameter of the support graph.

This applies even when the scalar coefficients are selected adaptively from the current right-hand side: support cannot propagate beyond one graph edge per multiplication by `A`.

### Degree/size corollary

If every vertex has at most `Delta` outgoing support positions, then after `d` steps at most

\[
1+\Delta+\cdots+\Delta^d
\]

vertices can be reached. A dense inverse therefore requires

\[
\boxed{
N\le\frac{\Delta^{d+1}-1}{\Delta-1},
}
\]

or

\[
\boxed{
d\ge\log_\Delta((\Delta-1)N+1)-1.}
\]

For constant `Delta`, this is `Omega(log N)` sparse matrix-vector passes. If each pass touches `Theta(N)` nonzeros, a literal exact Krylov schedule costs

\[
\boxed{\Omega(N\log N).}
\]

### Polynomial inverse form

If a common-coefficient solver gives

\[
A^{-1}=p_d(A),
\]

then the same support argument forces `deg p_d` at least the graph diameter. The exact diagnostics in this package compute the least such degree for small finite-field examples.

### Scope

This is a Krylov/polynomial propagation theorem, not a universal sparse-solve lower bound. It does not rule out:

- nested dissection with special cancellation;
- group-algebra diagonalization;
- a non-Krylov direct recurrence carrying nonlocal state;
- input-independent factorization using additional structured operators;
- a solver that has already precomputed a dense transform, if that cost is honestly charged.

Together with v19, an exact sparse-denominator candidate must now be both high-treewidth **and** non-Krylov in its source of linear-time solvability.

## 7. Verification

The final cold suite has eight automated groups.

### Weyl packet checks

- dense inverse versus `O(N)` Green recurrence;
- every character `s` for lengths `5,7,11,13,17` and steps `h=1,2`;
- exact support counts `2N-1` for `s=0`, `N-1` otherwise;
- arbitrary weighted packet formula versus literal dense summation;
- twelve exact quadratic-fiber minors, all zero;
- twelve affine Fourier comparison minors, all nonzero;
- projective noncommutation witnesses.

An independently written verifier, which does not import `weyl_packet.py`, reproduces 72 packet identities and all support counts.

### Krylov checks

- cyclic Green denominators at lengths `5,7,9,11,13` have dense inverses and least inverse-polynomial degree `N-1`;
- four one- and two-shift sparse denominators have dense inverses;
- every measured inverse-polynomial degree is at least the directed graph diameter and the branching lower bound.

For one selected character packet, the exact Python finite-field implementation compared with literal evaluation of all `N` Green solves:

| 31 | about 47x |

| 127 | about 277x |
| 251 | about 517x |

These are algorithm-mechanism diagnostics, not comparisons against an FFT library and not an FFT speedup.

## 8. Prior-art and claim boundary

Finite shift/modulation operators and their projective Weyl–Heisenberg commutation relations are classical. Krylov subspace methods and sparse-matrix graph interpretations are also classical. The roots-of-unity character filter used in Theorem 3.1 is elementary.

The limited audit did not locate this exact combination of:

- a resolvent character transform over the full Weyl slope orbit;
- its `N-1`/`2N-1` sparse formula;
- the quadratic-fiber zero-minor obstruction;
- and the paired exact Krylov support gate.

That is **not** a literature-wide novelty proof. No priority claim is made.

## 9. Honest conclusion and next gate

We still do **not** have an exact `o(N log N)` FFT.

What v20 establishes is:

\[
\boxed{
N\text{ noncommuting phase-gauged Green resolvents can have an }O(N)
\text{ character aggregate.}
}
\]

But:

\[
\boxed{
\text{their complete weighted orbit span cannot equal an affine Fourier matrix.}
}
\]

And for a constant-degree high-treewidth denominator:

\[
\boxed{
\text{an exact Krylov solve still needs }\Omega(\log N)\text{ sparse passes.}
}
\]

The next exact experiment must therefore do one of the following:

1. fuse **several distinct Weyl shift steps** `h` while escaping all simultaneous fiber-minor identities;
2. jointly evaluate genuinely different permutation support orders, not only phase gauges of one cycle;
3. construct a high-treewidth sparse inverse with an explicit exact non-Krylov `O(N)` solver;
4. leave the Green-resolvent framework entirely.
