# Independent Phase21 audit: Weyl chirps, Green cuts, and stronger bounds

9 October 2026. This audit concerns the new `fft_green_phase21_weyl_resolvent` checkpoint. The previous Phase20 archive is unchanged according to the batch's inert provenance check. No uploaded program, binary, bytecode, or nested verification script was executed. The accompanying checks were independently authored and import no uploaded code.

## Executive result

The main complex proofs are correct in their stated even-length domains:

- the exact Weyl chirp identity;
- the `q >= N/2` common-generator pole budget, including arbitrary invertible outer diagonals;
- the `Q >= N/2` total-term barrier for same-forward-order weighted cyclic Green products/sums, including arbitrary internal diagonal masks and zero weights;
- the stated spectral approximation floor;
- the full-rank commutator of a linear-work Green inverse.

The exact minimal-pole existence certificates were independently reconstructed for every supplied dyadic size `4,8,...,8192`. Eighteen additional even, non-dyadic sizes were certified as a separate finite extension. These checks prove characteristic-zero existence at those specific sizes through nonzero modular resultants; they do not establish uniform all-even-length nondegeneracy.

Two useful rigorous extensions are proved below:

1. The common-order spectral-error floor tends to **one**, rather than `1/sqrt(2)`, when `Q=o(N)`. In fact it tends to one uniformly whenever `Q <= (1/4-epsilon)N` for a fixed positive epsilon.
2. The exact cut rank of an arbitrary-order weighted cyclic Green inverse equals the number of its nonzero directed cycle edges entering the row side of the cut. Thus reordered cycles have a quantitative crossing budget; reversing the common cyclic direction alone still has budget one.

A further small refinement completely classifies the outer-diagonal freedom in the chirp polynomial: the geometric multiplier forced by interior cross-ratios must satisfy `rho^N=1` after the wraparound minors are included.

There is no unrestricted Fourier operation-count lower bound or new sub-`N log N` exact evaluator here. In particular, a same-order representation with `N/2` or more Green terms remains a possible object for a shared evaluator. The term-count obstruction does not exclude that route.

## 1. Source identities and proof subtleties

Use `omega=exp(-2 pi i/N)`, `F[j,k]=omega^(jk)/sqrt(N)`, `Ze_k=e_(k+1)`, `D=diag(omega^j)`, `T=D^-1 Z`, `U=ZD`, and `Q0=diag(omega^(j^2))`.

### 1.1 Dense flat Green inverse

For even N, the product of the N weights of T or U is `omega^(N(N-1)/2)=-1`. Consequently `T^N=U^N=-I` and

`(I-alpha T)^-1 = (sum_(m=0)^(N-1) alpha^m T^m)/(1+alpha^N)`.

The denominator condition is essential. For nonzero alpha all N cyclic diagonals are nonzero. For unit-modulus alpha the entries all have magnitude `1/|1+alpha^N|`. At alpha=1, the smallest and largest distances from 1 to the N roots of -1 are `2 sin(pi/(2N))` and `2 cos(pi/(2N))`, respectively. This proves the reported condition number `cot(pi/(2N))`.

A distinct pair of columns of this Green matrix has exactly two entrywise ratios: the two sets of rows correspond to which cyclic path wraps. The two values coincide only at the excluded singular value `alpha^N=-1`. Row scaling cancels in column ratios, column scaling multiplies all ratios by a common nonzero scalar, and permutations preserve their cardinality. Fourier has a pair of consecutive-frequency columns with N distinct ratios. This is a valid obstruction even to arbitrary outer monomial equivalence of a *single* Green inverse.

### 1.2 The chirp identity

For `m=(j-k mod N)`, the only power contributing at `(j,k)` is

`T^m[j,k]=omega^(-mk-m(m+1)/2)`.

With `c_m=omega^(-m(m-1)/2)`, multiplication by `Q0[j,j]` gives exponent

`j^2-m^2-mk = j^2-m(m+k) = jk (mod N)`.

The wrap is harmless because `m+k=j (mod N)`. Hence

`F=(Q0/sqrt(N)) sum_m c_m T^m`.

The direct polynomial implementation has quadratic arithmetic work. The identity does not imply that its resolvent representation has a cheap preprocessing step or shared evaluator.

### 1.3 Arbitrary outer diagonals and the Hankel lower bound

Writing a resolvent sum as `C(T)=sum_m d_m T^m`, each coefficient is a sum of q exponential moments:

`d_m=sum_l b_l alpha_l^m`, where `b_l=a_l/(1+alpha_l^N)`.

Because Fourier has no zero entries and the outer diagonals are invertible, every d_m is nonzero. The source's selected cross-ratios correctly cancel both the Weyl phase and the outside diagonals. They force

`d_m^2=omega d_(m-1)d_(m+1)` and thus `d_m=gamma rho^m c_m`.

The half-size Hankel block is diagonal-equivalent to `(omega^(-rs))`, a Vandermonde on distinct nodes. Its rank is N/2. Each exponential moment contributes a rank-one Hankel matrix, so `q>=N/2`. Coincident poles, zero scalar coefficients, and alpha=0 cannot invalidate the upper rank bound.

**Wraparound refinement.** Interior cross-ratios alone permit arbitrary nonzero rho, but full diagonal equivalence does not. The entrywise quotient of `C(T)` by the chirp matrix is

`E[j,k]=gamma rho^((j-k) mod N)`.

It must have rank one. Its `(0,1)` by `(0,1)` minor gives `1=rho^N`. Conversely, when `rho^N=1`, the quotient separates as `gamma rho^j rho^(-k)`. Thus the displayed geometric freedom is precisely an Nth-root character. This strengthens the necessary-coefficient classification without changing the N/2 lower bound. The source did not assert the converse, so its original proof remains valid.

## 2. Independent reconstruction of the minimal-pole certificates

Put `h=N/2`, `q0=omega^-1`, and

`R_h(z)=product_(k=0)^(h-1)(z-q0^k)=sum_j r_j z^j`.

The independently authored script constructs these coefficients with a q-binomial recurrence, rather than the uploaded code's repeated polynomial multiplication:

`r_0=(-1)^h q0^(h(h-1)/2)`,

`r_(j+1)=-r_j q0^(-(h-j-1)) (1-q0^(h-j))/(1-q0^(j+1))`.

All denominators here are nonzero because `j+1<=h<N` and omega has exact order N. It then constructs `A_j=(c_h/c_j) r_j`. Monicity and the nonzero constant term are checked. At sizes through128, an independently written root-product construction agrees coefficient for coefficient. At every checked size, Horner evaluation checks every prescribed root of R_h. The moment recurrence is additionally checked directly through256.

The script computes both modular resultants by exact Euclidean polynomial arithmetic:

- `Res(A_h,A_h')`;
- `Res(A_h,z^N+1)`.

Both are nonzero at each supplied size `N=4,8,16,32,64,128,256,512,1024,2048,4096,8192`, over `p=998244353`. For example, at8192 the independent values are `338814782` and `850988529`, respectively, with the primitive root recorded in the certificate file.

The program independently checks the primality of p by trial division and verifies the exact order of each selected root. Modular polynomial operations use signed 64-bit NumPy storage only for an individual product/subtraction of residues smaller than p; these intermediates have magnitude below `10^18`, so no integer overflow or floating convolution is involved. Matrix operations use Python integers. No root approximations enter these certificates.

### Why this proves a complex existence statement

The A_h coefficients lie in `Z[omega]`: c_h/c_j is a cyclotomic unit, and the r_j are integral. Sending omega to an exact primitive Nth root modulo a prime with `N | p-1` defines a ring homomorphism. Resultants are polynomial expressions in the coefficients, so a zero cyclotomic resultant would reduce to zero. A nonzero modular resultant therefore proves that the complex A_h is squarefree and avoids every forbidden root of `z^N+1`.

Its h distinct roots define an invertible initial-moment Vandermonde system. Matching the first h moments and using the common monic order-h recurrence matches all N moments. The resulting residues yield the claimed h-resolvent representation. This conclusion is stronger than a finite-field search that merely fails to find a solution: here a nonzero reduction certifies a specific algebraic nonvanishing assertion.

### Additional finite sizes

Over the independently checked prime `p=20161`, the same two resultants are nonzero for

`6,10,12,14,18,20,24,28,30,36,40,42,48,56,60,84,90,120`.

These are additional exact finite-size complex-existence results. No inference to all even N is made. The uniform h-pole question remains open in this audit. The accompanying Prony extension gives a separately proved all-even-length upper bound.

### What the representation does not supply

It does not produce an efficient/stable root-and-residue setup, an inexpensive simultaneous resolver, or a finite-precision error certificate. Applying h independent linear-work solves costs order N squared. Sharing the solves could alter that cost, but no such faster algorithm is supplied or excluded by the term-count argument.

## 3. Same-order Green products, including singular masks

For a weighted cyclic shift `(S_w x)[j]=w_j x[j-1]`, the inverse exists exactly when `1-product_j w_j` is nonzero. At the first-half rows R and second-half columns C,

`G_w[j,k]=(product_(r=0)^j w_r)(product_(r=k+1)^(N-1)w_r)/(1-product_r w_r)`.

This is a genuine outer product even when some weights vanish. No division by individual weights is allowed or needed.

For matrices on this complementary partition,

`(AB)[R,C]=A[R,R]B[R,C]+A[R,C]B[C,C]`.

Thus cross rank is subadditive under multiplication. It is also subadditive under addition. Diagonal factors have zero cross rank, regardless of invertibility. A product of sums with total term count Q therefore has cross rank at most Q. Fourier's half block is a nonsingular Vandermonde, so exactness needs `Q>=N/2`.

This proof does not assume the Green matrices commute, are normal, are well-conditioned, or have nonzero individual edge weights. The source's treatment of singular diagonal masks is correct.

## 4. Stronger spectral approximation floor

Let `C0=F[R,C]`, `m=N/2`, `A=C0 C0*`, and

`v_N=tr(A-A^2)`.

The eigenvalues `lambda_1>=...>=lambda_m` of A lie in [0,1], and `tr(A)=N/4`. For any integer `0<=Q<N/4`,

`sum_(j>Q) lambda_j >= N/4-Q`.

Since `lambda_j<=lambda_(Q+1)` on this tail,

`v_N >= sum_(j>Q) lambda_j(1-lambda_j)`

`    >= (1-lambda_(Q+1))(N/4-Q)`.

Therefore every matrix M with `rank(M[R,C])<=Q` obeys

**`||M-F||_2 >= sqrt(max(0,1-v_N/(N/4-Q)))`.**

This follows from the best rank-Q spectral approximation bound for the half block. The source's valid floor can be retained simultaneously by taking the maximum of the two lower bounds.

### Exact leakage sum and a harmonic upper bound

Let `P=F[:,C] F[:,C]*`, an orthogonal projection. Its restriction to R is A. The projection identity gives

`v_N=sum_(r in R,s outside R)|P[r,s]|^2`.

At a nonzero cyclic separation d, the geometric sum is zero for even d and has magnitude `1/(N |sin(pi d/N)|)` for odd d. Counting pairs across the half cut gives the exact formula

`v_N=(2/N^2) sum_(1<=d<m, d odd) d csc^2(pi d/N)`

`    + 1_(m odd) m/N^2`.

Using `sin(pi d/N)>=2d/N` for `1<=d<=m`,

`v_N <= B_N := (1/2) sum_(1<=d<m, d odd) 1/d + 1_(m odd) m/N^2`.

In particular `B_N=O(log N)`. The same norm bound with B_N replacing v_N is a completely explicit, conservative harmonic bound.

Consequences:

- If `Q=o(N)`, the spectral error is at least `1-O(log N/N)` and tends to one.
- More generally this holds for `Q <= (1/4-epsilon)N`, fixed epsilon>0.
- Any fixed error target `delta<1` requires `Q >= N/4-v_N/(1-delta^2)` (automatically true if Q is already at least N/4). Thus any uniformly nontrivial spectral accuracy needs essentially N/4 terms in this restricted architecture.

Numerical illustration, separate from the proof: at N=512 and Q=32, `v_N` is approximately0.746124475. The source floor is approximately0.654654; the stronger floor is approximately0.996106. The script checks the exact trace formula against independently formed dense matrices and checks both singular-value inequalities at15 even lengths through512. Floating-point checks support the implementation only; the theorem follows from the identities and inequalities above.

## 5. Quantitative order-crossing extension

The restriction to one forward order can be replaced by an explicit cut-crossing budget.

### Complementary-block inverse identity

For any invertible matrix B partitioned into complementary coordinate sets R,C,

**`rank(B^-1[R,C])=rank(B[R,C])`.**

A proof avoiding invertibility assumptions on the diagonal blocks is useful here. A vector x supported on C lies in the kernel of `B^-1[R,C]` exactly when `y=B^-1 x` is supported on C. Such vectors y are precisely `y_C in ker B[R,C]`, and `x_C=B[C,C]y_C`. This map from that kernel is injective: if its image were zero, both blocks of By would be zero, contradicting invertibility of B. It is also surjective onto the stated inverse-block kernel. The two kernels have equal dimension, hence the two off-diagonal blocks have equal rank.

### Application to arbitrary cycle orders

If S is a weighted permutation representing an arbitrarily ordered directed cycle, `B=I-S` has an off-diagonal block that is a weighted partial permutation. Its rank is exactly the number k of **nonzero-weight** edges with source in C and destination in R. Therefore

**`rank((I-S)^-1[R,C])=k`.**

With all weights nonzero, k is the number of runs of R encountered around the directed cycle. The same proof works for an invertible denominator made from multiple disjoint weighted cycles.

A product of Green sums and diagonal masks now has cross rank at most the sum of these crossing counts over its Green terms. An explicitly inserted permutation P adds its own cross rank, namely the number of coordinates it routes from C to R. Consequently exact Fourier requires the total charged crossing budget to be at least N/2. The stronger approximation theorem in Section4 applies with this total budget in place of Q.

This extension does not require the inverse or its diagonal blocks to be well-conditioned. It correctly handles zero edge weights.

### Why reversal is not enough

A common forward cycle enters R once; a common reversed cycle also enters R once. Both have rank-one half cuts, even if the forward and backward terms are mixed. The source properly excludes reversed/nonidentical orders from its formal grammar, but a reversed direction alone is not an actual escape from this cut-rank obstruction. A cycle alternating coordinates from R and C can instead have N/2 entering edges and full half-block rank even for one linear-work solve. Large crossing budget therefore does not establish Fourier coherence or hard computation.

## 6. Full-rank commutator: accepted, with the intended limitation

Direct multiplication yields `TU=omega^-2 Z^2`, `UT=Z^2`, and thus

`[T,U]=(omega^-2-1)Z^2`.

For N>2 this is invertible. The inverse-commutator identity gives

`[T,(I-beta U)^-1]=beta (I-beta U)^-1 [T,U] (I-beta U)^-1`.

For nonzero beta and an existing inverse, its rank is N. Applying this inverse still uses one cyclic first-order recurrence. Hence high commutator rank is not an operation-count lower bound.

Both original generators use the same coordinate cycle, so their small-total-term circuits remain covered by Section3. The independently authored matrix checks reproduce the full commutator rank and exact identity at18 even lengths, including non-dyadic sizes.

## 7. Scope repairs for the next checkpoint

The phrase that the surviving route “must use independent coordinate orders” needs qualification:

- A small-Q architecture needs a sufficiently large total cut-crossing budget to escape this particular obstruction. Distinct order names or reverse direction alone do not suffice.
- A common-order representation with Q>=N/2 is **not excluded** from admitting a clever shared evaluator. The source explicitly distinguishes term count from runtime, and that distinction must also govern the proposed next-step conclusions.
- Neither noncommutativity, full-rank displacement, many crossings, nor flat magnitude certifies the two Fourier Weyl identities.
- There is no conclusion against arbitrary shared scalar graphs, arbitrary independent coordinate orders, or unrestricted Fourier algorithms.

The new finite Prony existence results are useful positive controls and a sharp representation endpoint. They do not settle coefficient preparation, stability, output accuracy, or fresh-input arithmetic work.
