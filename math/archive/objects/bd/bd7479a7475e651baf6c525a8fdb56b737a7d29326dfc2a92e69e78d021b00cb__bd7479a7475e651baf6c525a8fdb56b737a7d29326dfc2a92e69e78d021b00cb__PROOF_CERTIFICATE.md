# Exact mathematical certificates: Fourier sparse-defect barrier and a scan escape

**Date:** October 10, 2026. **Status:** Theorems proved by elementary exact algebra; runnable modular checks are independent sanity tests. Neither an unrestricted FFT lower bound nor a new `o(N log N)` algorithm is claimed.

## Definitions

Work over the complex numbers except where an exact finite-field check is specified. Let `F_N[k,j] = zeta_N^(kj)` be the unnormalized DFT matrix, with `zeta_N` a primitive `N`th root of unity. An `N x N` matrix is **row-q-sparse** if every row has at most `q` nonzeros. A **monomial connector** is the product of a permutation matrix and a nonsingular diagonal matrix. Bandwidth `b` means `T[i,j]=0` whenever `|i-j|>b`.

## Theorem 1. Fourier row-sparse rank obstruction

For any row-q-sparse matrix `B`, even after arbitrary independent permutations and nonzero diagonal scalings of the DFT's rows and columns,

\[
\operatorname{rank}(F_N-B)\ \ge\ \left\lfloor\frac{N}{q+1}\right\rfloor.
\]

Here the displayed `F_N` can also denote a Fourier matrix with those endpoint monomial connectors; a nonzero diagonal factor does not change the ranks of its submatrices.

**Proof.** Put `m=floor(N/(q+1))`. Choose `m` output coordinates corresponding (before the endpoint row permutation) to any `m` consecutive frequency indices `k0,k0+1,...,k0+m-1`. The union of the supports of the corresponding rows of `B` occupies at most `mq` columns. Since `(q+1)m<=N`, there are at least `m` unused columns; choose any `m` of them. On the resulting `m x m` submatrix `B[R,C]=0`. Writing `x_j=zeta_N^(original_column_index_j)`, the Fourier minor is (up to invertible row/column rescalings)

\[
 \det F_N[R,C]=\left(\prod_{j=1}^{m} x_j^{k_0}\right)
  \prod_{1\le u<v\le m}(x_v-x_u)\ne0,
\]

because the input coordinates correspond to **distinct** roots. Therefore `(F_N-B)[R,C]` is invertible, so the rank of the full difference is at least `m`. QED.

**Finite-field validity:** The same argument holds whenever the field contains `N` distinct `N`th roots. The verifier uses moduli 12,289 and 65,537 at powers of two through 128. This does not rely on floating-point ranks.

## Theorem 2. Multi-stage sparse-plus-low-rank defect barrier

Suppose an exact Fourier (or inverse Fourier) factorization has `t` invertible stages, arbitrary monomial connectors, and

\[
 G_j^{-1}=B_j+E_j,\quad
 B_j\ \text{row-}s_j\text{-sparse},\quad \operatorname{rank} E_j\le r_j.
\]

Then the mandatory arithmetic-independent condition is

\[
\boxed{\sum_{j=1}^t r_j\ \ge\
\left\lfloor\frac{N}{1+\prod_{j=1}^t s_j}\right\rfloor.}
\]

For `s_j=s>=2` and `r_j<=r` constant, it implies `t>=log_s N-O(log log N)`. For `s=1`, it implies `t*r>=floor(N/2)`. If all `r_j=0`, the stronger equivalent support conclusion is `s^t >= N`.

**Proof.** Invert the assumed factorization. Permutations and nonsingular diagonals do not increase maximum row support or rank, so they may be absorbed into its inverse factors. Let `A_j=B_j+E_j`, including connectors as needed. Define the baseline product `B=B_t...B_1` and the full inverse transform `F'=A_t...A_1`. Every row of `B` has at most `q=product_j(s_j)` nonzeros, because row support sizes multiply under matrix products. Telescoping yields

\[
 F'-B=\sum_{j=1}^{t} A_t\cdots A_{j+1}
  (A_j-B_j) B_{j-1}\cdots B_1,
\]

with the straightforward convention for empty products. Each summand has rank at most `r_j`, so `rank(F'-B)<=sum r_j`. On the other hand, Theorem 1 applied to the (permuted, scaled) Fourier inverse gives `rank(F'-B)>=floor(N/(q+1))`. Combine. QED.

**Asymptotic derivation.** From `tr>=floor(N/(s^t+1))`, one obtains `s^t+1 > N/(tr+1)`. If `t<=log_s N`, then

\[
 s^t > \frac{N}{r\log_s N+1}-1,
\]

forcing `t>=log_s N-O(log log N)` for fixed `s>=2,r`. If `t>log_s N`, that lower bound is automatic. No stability, magnitude, unitarity, condition number, or nonzero-corner assumption is used.

**Implementation scope:** A fixed-rank correction to an invertible tridiagonal `T` has an `O(Nr+r^3)`-arithmetic Woodbury application after charging coefficient preparation. Constant `r` gives an `O(N)` stage; the lower bound rules out a *constant number* of such stages for unbounded Fourier lengths. It does not prove a general circuit lower bound for unrestricted structured stages or all implementations.

## Theorem 3. Explicit separated-scan family evades sparse-inverse-plus-small-rank reduction

For `N>=2`, let

\[
 S_{ij}=\begin{cases}
 1&i>j,\\3&i=j,\\2&i<j.
\end{cases}
\]

This is a fully dense two-sided rank-one **separated-generator scan stage**. A vector application uses a global sum and a running prefix sum, so `O(N)` arithmetic. Moreover its inverse can itself be applied in `O(N)` arithmetic.

**Inverse formula.** Let `L` be the strictly lower matrix of ones, `J=11^T`, `T_0=I-L`, and `D=2^(N+1)-1`. Then `S=T_0+2J`. The inverse of `T_0` has entries `(T_0^-1)[i,j]=1` if `i=j`, `2^(i-j-1)` if `i>j`, and `0` otherwise. Also `(T_0^-1)1=(1,2,4,...,2^(N-1))^T` and `1^T T_0^-1 1 = 2^N-1`. Sherman-Morrison gives

\[
 S^{-1}=T_0^{-1}-\frac{2}{D}(T_0^{-1}1)(1^TT_0^{-1}),
\]

and therefore

\[
 (S^{-1})_{ij}=\begin{cases}
 -2^{i-j-1}/D&i>j,\\
 (2^N-1)/D&i=j,\\
 -2^{N+i-j}/D&i<j.
\end{cases}
\]

All entries are nonzero over `C`; `det(S)=D` by the matrix determinant lemma.

**Bandwidth-separation theorem.** For **every** matrix `T` of bandwidth `b`, with `0<=b<=N-2`,

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}(S^{-1}-T)\ \ge\
\left\lfloor\frac{N+b}{2b+2}\right\rfloor.}
\]

**Proof.** Set `h=b+1`, `L0=2h`, `m=floor((N+b)/(2b+2))` and choose rows `R_i=L0*i` and columns `C_j=L0*j+h` for `0<=i,j<m`. These lie in `[0,N)` and satisfy `|R_i-C_j|>=h>b`. Thus `T[R,C]=0`. By the inverse formula and diagonal row/column scalings of determinants (whose products cancel), `S^-1[R,C]` has determinant equal to that of the `m x m` matrix with `alpha=-2^(-h-1)/D` below the diagonal and `beta=-2^(N-h)/D` on/above it. Elementary adjacent-row subtraction gives

\[
\det (S^{-1}[R,C]) =\beta(\beta-\alpha)^{m-1}.
\]

Since `beta-alpha=-2^(-h-1)` and `beta` is nonzero, this determinant is nonzero over the complex numbers. The rank of `(S^-1-T)[R,C]` equals `m`, proving the assertion. QED.

**Cheap inverse executor.** Let `y=T_0^-1 x`, given by the prefix recurrence `y_i=x_i+sum_(j<i) y_j`. Let `c=2*(sum_i y_i)/D`; output `S^-1 x = y-c*(1,2,4,...,2^(N-1))`. This costs `O(N)` input-dependent operations, including the correction, with all coefficients prepared in `O(N)` work if preparation is charged.

**Interpretation.** Theorem 2 blocks low-rank corrections to sparse inverses. This concrete invertible `O(N)`-work scan stage does not admit any such correction with rank `o(N/b)` when `b=O(1)`; hence the broader separated-generator approach studied in our previous checkpoint genuinely lies outside the new obstruction. The stage alone is **not** a Fourier algorithm.

**Prior-art boundary:** Vandermonde minors, Sherman-Morrison, Woodbury, telescoping rank, and the matrix rigidity viewpoint are established tools. Dvir and Liu (CCC 2019, DOI `10.4230/LIPIcs.CCC.2019.17`) prove non-rigidity results for complex Fourier matrices in broader parameter regimes. We claim no global novelty or strong Fourier matrix rigidity theorem; the statements here are exact **architecture-specific consequences** worked out for this research chain. Our previous `Green` checkpoint already had the `r=0` sparse-inverse support obstruction. The added rank-defect tradeoff and explicit separated-scan counter-class are the current continuation.
