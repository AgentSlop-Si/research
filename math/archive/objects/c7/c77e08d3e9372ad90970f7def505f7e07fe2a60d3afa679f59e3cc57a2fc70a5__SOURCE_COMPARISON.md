# Primary-source comparison and inspection ledger

Read date: 2026-10-10. “No implication established” describes the inspected evidence, not an assertion that the entire literature lacks the result. No paper is classified as a duplicate from title or abstract alone. All substantive mathematical comparisons below use primary papers or author-hosted originals.

## Closest exact antecedents

1. **Bruce E. Sagan, “Congruence properties of q-analogs,” Advances in Mathematics 95 (1992), 127–143.** Theorem 2.2, pp. 131–132, states q-Lucas modulo the d-th cyclotomic polynomial. Summing that coefficient identity proves the displayed degenerate Rogers–Szegő factorization. Sagan also identifies earlier proofs; cite it as an accessible treatment, not as sole originator. Relevant theorem and surrounding explanation inspected. [Author PDF](https://users.math.msu.edu/users/bsagan/Papers/Old/cpq-pub.pdf)

2. **M. K. Atakishiyeva, N. M. Atakishiyev, T. H. Koornwinder, “On a q-extension of Mehta's eigenvectors of the finite Fourier transform for q a root of unity,” Journal of Physics A 42 (2009), 454004; arXiv:0811.4100v2.** Sections 2–3, especially (2.2), (2.7), (2.8), and (3.8), inspected. Equation (2.8) gives H_(mM+n)(x|q)=(2T_M(x))^m H_n(x|q); (3.8) supplies the classical scaled Hermite endpoint limit. These are direct antecedents to the degeneration, but not the first nonzero fixed-root splitting coefficient. Its root-of-unity orthogonality is expressly allowed complex weights. [Primary PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0811.4100)

3. **M. E. H. Ismail and N. S. Witte, “Discriminants and Functional Equations for Polynomials Orthogonal on the Unit Circle,” arXiv:math/0012259.** Relevant definitions and §5, particularly (5.12)–(5.18), inspected. The paper explicitly distinguishes the ordinary resultant with f' from a generalized resultant with T f. The Rogers–Szegő product takes T=D_q. Consequently it is a close discriminant antecedent but not the same nonvanishing invariant. No ordinary fixed-root coefficient or dp squarefreeness consequence was established from the inspected material. [Primary PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0012259)

4. **V. Spiridonov and A. Zhedanov, “Zeros and orthogonality of the Askey–Wilson polynomials for q a root of unity,” Duke Mathematical Journal 89 (1997), 283–305; arXiv:q-alg/9605034.** Introduction and relevant terminal-zero/orthogonality portions of §§2 and 5 inspected. The text explicitly warns that complex recurrence coefficients do not guarantee simple zeros. Its terminal-degree formulas therefore do not automatically settle the target half-degree problem or separated local clusters. This is strong context for why a distinct simplicity proof is needed, not proof of priority. [Primary PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/q-alg/9605034)

5. **D. S. Lubinsky, author-hosted “q-Series as Counterexamples” proceedings paper.** Section 2, Lemma 2.1 and Theorem 2.2 inspected. The Padé denominator formula is an exact antecedent to the project's annihilator under scaling/reversal. The zero-location theorem quoted there excludes roots of unity. The prior project's detailed substitution is retained as an attributed application; the present pass does not reopen all realization theory. [Author PDF](https://lubinsky.math.gatech.edu/Research%20papers/DortmundProcCuriousqSeries.pdf)

## Search and access limitations

Search families included Rogers–Szegő spelling variants with discriminant, squarefree, multiple zeros, cyclotomic, root of unity, Hermite, and local limits, plus named-paper/title/DOI checks. Results were used to locate originals; unauthored aggregators and generated summaries were not mathematical evidence. The bounded search is neither a systematic MathSciNet/Zentralblatt review nor an exhaustive backward/forward citation crawl. No absence-of-hit statement is used to infer originality.

PDF screenshots were unavailable through the web tool on the attempted sources; extracted source text was usable for the specific comparisons above. This matters especially for Cigler's typography, so its inspection depth is explicitly limited. No protected source was circumvented, no credentials were entered, and no author was contacted.
