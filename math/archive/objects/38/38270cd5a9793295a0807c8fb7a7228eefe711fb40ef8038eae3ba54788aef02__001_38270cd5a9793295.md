# Audit of the new exact checkpoint additions

Audit date: 2026-10-09. Materials: `fft_macro_wordram_checkpoint_20261009`.

## Bottom line

**The central new exact claims are sound within their stated scopes.** The checkpoint adds:

1. A detected weighted-affine-cycle input family whose complete exact Fourier transform costs O(N(s+1)) field operations, including fresh-input discovery/verification and all outputs.
2. A genuine boundary-sensitive strengthening of the one-way-scan/broadcast obstruction:
   N <= 2^(t-1) + B(2^t+1), for t >= 1.
3. A usable tridiagonal-sandwich representation and linear evaluator for a specified nondegenerate one-channel bidirectional class.
4. Scoped necessary conditions based on tridiagonal component size and aggregate permutation displacement.

**None supplies an arbitrary-input exact o(N log N) FFT.** The affine input family is restricted; the fallback remains O(N log N) for a fixed defect budget. The other results constrain particular representations rather than giving a Fourier factorization or a general arithmetic/RAM lower bound.

Two corrections/qualifications should accompany any summary:

- `fft_bidirectional_graph_research/README.md:143–145` says the special family uses “two fewer comparisons.” It actually uses **one fewer cross-product comparison, saving two multiplications**. The 14N+1 count is correct.
- O(N) auxiliary “words” and output movement for arbitrary exact complex/cyclotomic data must mean **field registers plus bounded-word index/control work**. Such field elements do not generally fit in bounded machine words. This is an exact-field operation result, not an exact bit-complexity or general word-RAM theorem. The README otherwise explicitly acknowledges coefficient growth, excludes bit/wall-clock claims, and states the finite-prime length cap.

## 1. Weighted-affine-cycle Fourier evaluator

Primary locations: `fft_bidirectional_graph_research/README.md:17–162`; `affine_cycle.py:29–121,124–157`.

### Intertwining and invertibility

For the positive-exponent Fourier convention,

M x[j] = q^(bj) x[aj+c],
Mhat y[k] = q^(-c a^(-1)(k+b)) y[a^(-1)(k+b)],

all indices modulo N. Substituting t=aj+c into the defining Fourier sum gives F M = Mhat F with exactly the displayed sign and indexing.

For dyadic N, a=1 mod 4 and odd b,c, both affine index maps are full cycles. The supplied valuation argument is correct: v2(1+a+...+a^(2^r-1))=r and v2(a^(2^r)-1)>=r+2. Together with odd translation, the orbit doubles at every dyadic modulus. The source performs separate full-cycle validation with boolean arrays.

In target cycle order, the product of edge coefficients is

C = lambda^N q^(-c N(N-1)/2) = -lambda^N.

Thus I-lambda Mhat is invertible precisely when 1+lambda^N != 0. The backward accumulation in the submitted source computes the expanded cyclic numerator, then divides by 1-C and traverses backward again to recover every output. It never divides by individual edge coefficients, so lambda=0 is handled correctly. Singular compatible nonzero inputs deliberately decline rather than guessing a kernel component; the zero vector has an independently valid direct return.

### Discovery and accepted-input scope

Among m nonzero denominators (Mx)[j], at most s ratios x[j]/(Mx)[j] disagree with the true lambda. The condition m>2s therefore gives a strict majority. Cross products compare the ratios exactly without N separate inversions. The candidate is subsequently verified over all source coordinates, so a failed promise cannot produce an unsupported output. Low support can cause a valid structured input to be declined when lambda is not supplied; this restriction is expressly stated.

For a verified residual u with s nonzero entries, Fu is evaluated explicitly as s geometric modes using 2Ns field calls. Solving the conjugate cycle then gives F x. This is a complete exact output algorithm, not output sampling or reuse of a previous transform.

For a=5,b=c=1,lambda=2 over C and a nonzero one-entry residual, the source system is nonsingular at every dyadic N>=4, and x is fully nonzero. This is a uniform dense structured family, with defect positions/amplitudes and lambda varying; it does not give an arbitrary N-dimensional input family or a bounded-size decomposition of arbitrary inputs.

### Charged scalar operations

The source loops support the stated charges:

- Root table and primitive-root validation: N multiplications
- Weighted source values: N multiplications
- Discovery: at most N-1 cross-product comparisons, hence at most 2N-2 multiplications, plus one inverse and one multiplication, at most 2N calls
- Complete residual: 2N calls
- Defect transform: 2Ns calls
- Target edge coefficients: N calls
- Nonsingular cycle solve: N product multiplications; one subtraction; two N-step multiply/add traversals; one inverse and one final multiplication, total 5N+3

Total: at most (12+2s)N+3 with discovery, or (10+2s)N+3 with supplied lambda.

For the fully dense one-defect-at-zero family, the first bad ratio is canceled by the next good ratio, and the third position restarts the vote. There are N-2 comparisons, not N-1. Hence discovery saves two multiplications and the total is **14N+1**. The README's “two fewer comparisons” phrase should be replaced by “one fewer comparison / two fewer multiplications.” The submitted independent review already explains this correctly.

The specific radix comparator pays N root multiplications and three calls per butterfly over (N/2)log2 N butterflies, yielding N+(3/2)N log2 N. Independent output checks and static counts agree with:

| N | Affine count | Specified radix count |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 1,024 | 14,337 | 16,384 |
| 4,096 | 57,345 | 77,824 |
| 8,192 | 114,689 | 167,936 |
| 32,768 | 458,753 | 770,048 |

These counts do not compare against every optimized radix algorithm. Finding the primitive root q is outside both bills, by the common-input contract. For the modular examples, p=65,537 supports dyadic N only through 65,536. For all-length complex/cyclotomic claims, field operations and element storage are not constant-cost bounded-word operations.

The failed-attempt overhead is O(N) only when the defect budget is fixed. A growing budget can incur O(N(s+1)), including before a singular-cycle decline. The final README and wrapper documentation correctly include this fixed-budget condition.

## 2. Boundary-sensitive scan/broadcast obstruction

Primary location: `fft_macro_review_research/README.md:8–91`.

The new inequality is correct. Let u be the smallest row support of S, s the largest, and r=rank(F-S). If r<N, select r+1 cyclic-consecutive Fourier rows including a minimum-support row of S. Their support union contains at most u+rs columns. If that number were <=N-r-1, at least r+1 columns would be zero in the selected S rows. The corresponding minor of F-S is then a cyclic-consecutive Fourier Vandermonde minor of order r+1 and is nonsingular. Contradiction. Consequently,

N <= u+r(s+1).

This proof works over any field with a primitive Nth root and the stated characteristic condition; it does not rely on a complex norm estimate or dyadic N.

Invert a product of t invertible scalar one-way scans, arbitrary monomials, and invertible rank-budget-B updates. Replace each inverse update by identity. Telescoping gives rank(F^(-1)-S)<=B because an invertible update's inverse differs from identity by no greater rank. The remaining inverse scans are bidiagonal. Every row has support <=2^t. A boundary singleton row of the leftmost bidiagonal survives the preceding monomials and expands through only the remaining t-1 bidiagonals, yielding at least one row with support <=2^(t-1). Therefore

N <= 2^(t-1)+B(2^t+1), t>=1.

For t=0 the remaining matrix is monomial and N<=1+2B, giving B>=floor(N/2).

At N=64 the resulting minimum necessary budgets for t=0,...,7 are 32,21,13,7,4,2,1,0. These improve the prior budget at t=2,4,5,6. The B=0 consequence t>=1+ceil(log2 N) is already known within the project and should not be counted as a new contribution.

Scope remains important: the theorem concerns products of square invertible scalar one-way scans and low-rank updates. It does not automatically apply to sums of bidirectional scan outputs, extra-state representations, or unrestricted arithmetic circuits. The budget inequalities are necessary conditions, not matching constructions.

## 3. Tridiagonal sandwich and counts

Primary locations: `fft_bidirectional_extension_research/README.md:7–66`; `sandwich.py`.

For A with lower entries p_i q_j, upper entries u_i v_j, and arbitrary diagonal d_i, assume p_i and v_i are nonzero. Set alpha_i=p_i/p_(i-1), beta_i=v_i/v_(i-1), and let L and R be unit lower/upper bidiagonal with respective negative coefficients. Row cancellation followed by column cancellation yields tridiagonal T=L A R. The three displayed diagonals match direct expansion, including boundaries. Therefore A=L^(-1) T R^(-1).

Static source accounting confirms preparation 17N-19, application 9N-8, cold total 26N-27 field gates for N>=2. In preparation, the contributions are 4(N-1)+3(N-1)+3(N-1)+2(N-1)+3+5(N-2). In application, the two triangular sweeps and off-diagonal tridiagonal contributions give 8(N-1), with N diagonal multiplications.

All coefficient inversions are charged. Dense matrices occur only in verification helpers, not the preparation/application fast path. This evaluates a supplied stage, not discovery of a Fourier factorization; no such sublogarithmic-stage factorization is supplied.

The converse cut-rank statement also holds when bidiagonal coefficients vanish: across each cut, the upper-right and lower-left blocks factor into outer products because the tridiagonal boundary link and triangular inverse cross block share a boundary vector. Unit triangular block inverses exist without assumptions on T, so no hidden nonsingularity of principal blocks is required.

The specific p,q,u,v preparation is a nondegenerate subclass. Do not broaden it into an unqualified statement that arbitrary rank-one quasiseparable data already come with literal independent scalar-scan generators or that a generator-finding cost has been paid.

## 4. Component-size and movement bounds

Primary location: `fft_bidirectional_extension_research/README.md:68–135`.

### Component support

For invertible A=L^(-1) T R^(-1), suppose every contiguous block-diagonal component of T has size <=b, where a separating cut has both tridiagonal links zero. A^(-1)=R T^(-1) L. Each row of T^(-1) lies in a single component. R combines at most two neighboring rows and therefore at most two contiguous components, covering <=2b coordinates. Multiplication on the right by lower-bidiagonal L can add only the immediately preceding coordinate. Thus max row support of A^(-1) <=2b+1.

This constant is genuinely sharp. Our independent exact examples reproduce sharpness for b=1,...,8. For products, row-support bounds multiply and monomials do not increase them. Since every Fourier inverse entry is nonzero, product_j(2b_j+1)>=N. Fixed b therefore forces logarithmic stage count, and Theta(N) per complete-length stage gives Omega(N log N) work in that implementation model.

Irreducible T has b=N and escapes this obstruction. The proof does not cover noncontiguous arbitrary sparse blocks or prove a cost lower bound for implementations that avoid traversing degenerate portions of a stage.

### Inverse cut rank and density

The equality between corresponding off-diagonal cut ranks of an invertible matrix and its inverse is correct even when diagonal principal blocks are singular. The supplied kernel-bijection argument establishes it directly. Consequently a dense inverse does not contradict order-one quasiseparability. Shuffling can destroy a common-order small-cut-rank invariant; the submitted examples are plausible mechanisms, not Fourier factorizations. This familiar inversion fact is not claimed as a new theorem.

### Permutation movement

At a fixed cut, (AB)_12=A_11 B_12+A_12 B_22 implies cut-rank subadditivity. A permutation has upper cut rank equal to its one-direction crossing count c_s, and sum_s c_s=(1/2)sum_i |pi(i)-i|.

For a complex Fourier submatrix with s rows and N-s columns, the squared Frobenius norm is s(N-s), while the squared operator norm is <=N. Hence rank>=s(N-s)/N, also after arbitrary independent endpoint reorderings. Summing the cut inequalities and using sum_{s=1}^{N-1}s(N-s)/N=(N^2-1)/6 yields exactly

(1/2) sum_j sum_i |pi_j(i)-i| >= (N^2-1)/6 -(N-1)sum_j r_j.

This is a structural displacement condition, not a random-access running-time bound. A permutation can have quadratic total displacement and still be materialized in O(N) RAM moves. The norm-based Fourier step is over C; it has not thereby been proved in arbitrary finite characteristic.

## 5. Relationship to already established checkpoint facts

Do not reclassify the following as new:

- F8 endpoint quasiseparable optimum 3.
- The project's existing explicit F8 realization by 3 forward + 3 backward literal scans.
- The F32 rank gate for all 11x11 Fourier submatrices and consequent exclusion of all 120 unsigned coordinate-bit middle permutations.
- The pure-scan lower bound t>=1+ceil(log2 N).

The macro review's H=[[1,0,0],[1,1,0],[0,1,1]] example correctly shows that cut rank alone does not automatically synthesize a literal scalar scan. Its equations force a_1 b_0 and a_2 b_1 nonzero, and hence a_2 a_1 b_0 nonzero, contradicting H_20=0. That generic caveat does **not** negate or reopen an independently constructed explicit F8 3+3 realization. The supplied review appears to discuss a predecessor that did not include that construction; integrate the new theorem without downgrading the established F8 result.

## Decision

Accept the exact additions as scoped progress and preserve their explicit limitations. The strongest constructive addition is the cold affine-cycle low-defect evaluator. The strongest new obstruction is the boundary-sensitive scan/broadcast inequality. The sandwich/component/movement results sharpen what a viable nonunitary, globally shuffled bidirectional factorization would need, but do not supply one. No new general exact FFT breakthrough is established.
