# FFT research: three-tap annihilators and three-stage permutation gates

**Date:** October 10, 2026  
**Goal:** a truly executable exact Fourier transform on arbitrary fresh vectors in `o(N log N)` arithmetic, ideally `O(N)`, including preparation, addressing, and every scalar operation.  
**Current status:** two exact restrictions on scan factorizations, one explicit small-support mismatch verifier, and new permutation-only certificates for **three** independently generated stages. **No new faster FFT.**

## Why this checkpoint matters

The previous research excluded homogeneous two- and three-stage scans and certain asymmetrically weighted two-stage scans. It did not address all independently variable generators. This round develops a proof method that requires only *quasiseparable cut ranks*, so it includes the full separated-generator model and more general rank-one quasiseparable matrices. Unlike searching for one polynomial normalization, it handles singular/zero-generator cases automatically.

We deliberately did not move to the CrocSwap or integer-mult-kappa paths. All conclusions are about fresh-input exact Fourier factorizations with charged arithmetic.

## Main findings

**1. Nonzero three-tap Fourier determinant.** Any three linearly independent covectors supported on five consecutive Fourier output positions produce a three-column Fourier determinant `D(z)=z^3 Q(z)` with `deg Q<=6` and `Q != 0`. This is proved by Cauchy–Binet and a leading-coefficient Vandermonde argument. It gives a six-exception bound for *every* choice of covectors, not just generic coefficients.

**2. Constructive two-stage rejection.** For `N>=24` and natural Fourier endpoints, every candidate `F_N=S2 P S1` with rank-one quasiseparable stages has an explicit three-consecutive-coordinate input which the candidate computes incorrectly. At most 15 sliding positions touch the chosen permutation footprint, and at most six Fourier determinant positions are singular; at least `N-23` witness positions remain. The dimension-independent construction is implemented and checked exactly. This is not a better numerical threshold than our earlier natural-endpoint rank obstruction; it is a new reusable *algebraic witness* technique.

**3. Three-stage rank obstruction.** For `F_N=S3 P2 S2 P1 S1`, all rank-one quasiseparable stages, input/output windows of length `W`, and middle mapped sets `R,C`, it is necessary that the minimum order-separation defect satisfy `k(R,C)>=W-5`. This rejects entire pairs of internal permutations independently of the stage coefficients. The general rank-`r_i` theorem reads `rank(T[I,J])<=2r1+2r3+r2+k`. Under arbitrary Fourier endpoint reordering, the actual submatrix rank must be used, avoiding the counterexample found in earlier work.

**4. Random-shuffle expectation.** For independent random internal permutations, the expected number of *strictly separated* length-`W` window pairs is `(N-W+1)^2 * 2*C(N,2W)/C(N,W)^2`. In contrast, repeating bit reversal survived **every** tested length-`W` gate for `N=16,32,64`. This does not establish the existence of a Fourier factorization with repeated bit reversal; it merely identifies layouts that survive this new necessary condition.

## Exact witness and costs

At `N=64`, the independent dense-matrix verifier reconstructed random three-stage products over `F_12289`. For identity/identity, bitrev/identity, and a fixed random/random layout, a `W=6` certificate exhibited compressed ranks `4` (Fourier) versus `1` (stage product). Its rank bounds alone guarantee `>=2` versus `<=1` for *every* choice of stage parameters for those same window pairs. At other layouts the chosen sample may show a higher middle rank, in agreement with the cut bound.

A rank-one separated-generator scan can be evaluated using two cumulative sums in `O(N)` operations. In the provided implementation, after reusing `e_i*x_i`, one call uses `5N` field multiplications and `5N-1` field additions/subtractions (ordinary scalar-field model; modular reduction and reading/permutation costs must be accounted for separately in a full implementation). Three calls thus cost at least `15N` multiplications plus `15N-3` additions/subtractions, **before** coefficient generation and permuting data. The tested stage products do *not* compute the DFT.

## Constraints and caveats

- The earlier **two-stage** rank theorem is numerically stronger at natural endpoints; we do not rebrand the new `N>=24` result as a complexity breakthrough.
- This round rules out **some** three-stage layouts, not arbitrary internal permutations. The most important survivor remains repeated bit reversal with fully independent generator coefficients.
- For non-affine endpoint rearrangements, the Fourier submatrix may be rank-deficient. The corrected theorem uses its actual rank, never assumes a Vandermonde full-rank block blindly.
- Existing structured-matrix theory and prior Fourier circuit factorization results may overlap with the techniques. The scope of originality has not been established through publication review.
- The October 2026 OpenAI mathematics repository describes an unrestricted exact-arithmetic `o(N log N)` DFT in a very permissive complex arithmetic model. It does not supply an `O(N)` scan implementation or practical stable FFT; our objective is a separate constructive architecture.

## Next falsifiable research gate

1. On the surviving bitrev/bitrev architecture, derive **nonlinear compatibility constraints** that couple the three stages, not merely their cut capacities. Avoid normalizations that lose zero-generator branches.
2. Extend the local-annihilator argument to **four or more stages** by computing the *joint* network min-cut of the unannihilated middle stages, then add Fourier-specific Casoratian constraints. A count of stages without full scalar work is not an improvement.
3. Search for an **exact scalable factorization** with provably `o(log N)` fully linear-work stages; separately audit coefficient construction, every scalar operation, permutation addressing, memory and worst-case fresh inputs.
