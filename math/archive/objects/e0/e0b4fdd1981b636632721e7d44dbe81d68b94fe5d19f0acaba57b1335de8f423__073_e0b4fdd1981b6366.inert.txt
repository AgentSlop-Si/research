# Exact orbit compression for the q-factorial convolution graph

This is a positive, conditional graph rewrite valid for **arbitrary fresh input**.
A complete orbit of b sequential full-length convolution operators collapses to
b shorter residue-chain convolutions. It does not evaluate an arbitrary Fourier
transform faster: the Fourier factorization does not contain the needed operator
pattern. The negative control records the failure of making that substitution.

## Definition and finite identity

Let N=2^L, let q have exact order N in a field of odd characteristic (or
characteristic zero), and work modulo z^N. Define

    f_j = product_(r=1..j)(1-q^r),  f_0=1
    H_q(z) = sum_(j=0..N-1) z^j/f_j.

No displayed denominator vanishes. For a power-of-two b dividing N, put
m=N/b and xi=q^m. Then

    product_(a=0..b-1) H_q(xi^a z) = H_(q^b)(z^b) modulo z^N.       (1)

The right-side kernel has length m. When m=1 it is exactly 1. This is a finite
q-exponential norm identity; we do not claim that identity as globally new.

Here is an elementary proof that avoids infinite products at roots of unity.
Coefficientwise, H_q(qz)=(1-z)H_q(z) modulo z^N. The polynomial
P(z)=H_q(z)H_q(-z) is even, and

    P(qz)=(1-z^2)P(z) modulo z^N.

Writing P(z)=G(z^2) gives G(q^2 u)=(1-u)G(u) modulo u^(N/2).
Since G(0)=1, its coefficients are uniquely determined by

    (1-q^(2j)) G_j = G_(j-1),   1 <= j < N/2.

Therefore G=H_(q^2). Repeating this dyadic argument proves (1) for every allowed
b, including the full-orbit identity product H_q(q^a z)=1 modulo z^N. The proof
applies over the exact complex cyclotomic field and the finite fields in the audit.

## Concrete compiler rule and its cost

For coefficient vector x, let T(q,a) be multiplication modulo z^N by
H_q(xi^a z). Its convolution kernel is t_j xi^(aj), where t_j=1/f_j. These
operators commute because all are polynomials in the same nilpotent shift.

If one graph path consists of the complete set of b such operators applied in
sequence, with no intervening operator, replace that path by:

1. Gather the b input residue chains x[r], x[r+b], ... of length m.
2. Apply the *same* length-m kernel H_(q^b) to each chain.
3. Scatter every result into its original index.

Only one smaller kernel needs preparation. No answer from a past input is needed.
This works for every vector x. It does not identify b transforms of b independent
vectors with one product, nor does it discard any output coordinate.

If a length-k fixed-kernel application costs C(k), online work changes from
b C(N) to b C(N/b)+O(N). Recognizing the prescribed symbolic pattern takes O(b)
word operations; arbitrary graph equivalence search is not assumed free. The
implementation charges log2(b) scalar squarings to obtain q^b and all O(N) gather,
scatter, and output traffic. Indices are O(log N)-bit word indices in this count.

Root-dependent preparation of the smaller kernel costs
6m-4+log2(m) multiplications, m-1 subtractions, m-1 negations, and one constant
inversion for 1/2, using the previous audited preparation routine. An implementation
given the constant 1/2 can omit its inversion. Recognition should occur before
constructing unused full-size kernels; if those were already constructed, their
cost must also be counted. No bit-complexity or floating-point stability result
is asserted.

The preparation formula applies to m>1. For m=1 the collapsed operator is the
identity: the implementation prepares no kernel and performs no convolution,
while charging root squarings and the complete output copy.

The reference evaluator intentionally uses direct convolution. The collapsed
implementation then uses b*m*(m+1)/2 multiplications and b*m*(m-1)/2 additions,
plus preparation; these counts are checked exactly. They describe the verifier,
not a faster FFT. With an FFT-based C(k), the conditional rewrite saves work in
the matched graph, but that graph is a special operator class.

The direct-convolution formula likewise applies to m>1; the m=1 identity path
performs zero field operations on the input.

For b=2, with J=diag((-1)^j), an equivalent useful pattern is

    T_q J T_q = J block_by_parity(T_(q^2), T_(q^2)).

This follows by conjugating a Toeplitz convolution with J to negate its kernel
argument. It compresses two matched full-length kernels into two half-length
kernels and parity/sign traffic.

## What the norm does not solve

For a single transform, writing H_q(z)=A(u)+z B(u), u=z^2, gives

    even output = A E + u B O
    odd output  = B E + A O
    A^2-u B^2 = H_(q^2)(u) modulo u^(N/2).

The last identity specifies the determinant of the 2-by-2 block action. It does
not supply A and B as cheap smaller q-kernel operators. A direct parity split
still requires general polynomial products; using Karatsuba to combine them does
not establish a closed recurrence in the family H_(q^2).

Rationalization is also circular:

    H_q(z) = H_(q^2)(z^2) / H_q(-z)
    1/H_q(-z) = H_(q^(-1))(-q^(-1) z) modulo z^N.

The reciprocal is another **full-length, order-N** kernel, not a smaller call.
Thus replacing a single T_q application by this identity retains the unresolved
size-N application and adds two size-N/2 calls.

The previously verified Fourier factorization is

    F_N = S T_q diag(d) T_q^transpose S
        = S T_q diag(d) R T_q R S,

where S=diag(f), d_j=(-1)^j q^(j(j-1)/2)/f_j, and R reverses coordinates.
Its middle operator is diag(d)R. It is not the alternating-sign diagonal J.
Consequently the paired norm rule cannot simply replace the middle two kernels.
`false_fourier_collapse` makes exactly that illegal substitution, and the audit
exhibits a basis vector on which it gives wrong Fourier outputs.

## Verification and prior work

Run `python run_audit.py`. Results are in `results/orbit_audit.json`.
This audit checks the new orbit rule against the explicit sequential graph for
b=2,4,8, full orbits, complete basis sets over two finite fields, small rational
cyclotomic cases, mixed inputs, and charged direct operation counts. It does not
rerun the old factorization suite and label it new progress.

The q-factorial kernel and its Fourier factorization are known prior work:
Alexey Kuznetsov, *Using q-calculus to study LDL^T factorization of a certain
Vandermonde matrix*, https://arxiv.org/abs/1507.02959 . The present checkpoint
isolates the exact graph pattern and its costs; it makes no global novelty claim.
`fixed_kernel.py` and `prior_exact_arithmetic.py` are copied unchanged from the
previous fixed-kernel checkpoint so that this experiment is reproducible.

The open research step is to construct a Fourier graph with sufficiently many
complete orbit paths, or another legal rewrite removing its intervening middle
operators, while charging construction and full output recovery. No such
arbitrary-input Fourier construction is claimed here.
