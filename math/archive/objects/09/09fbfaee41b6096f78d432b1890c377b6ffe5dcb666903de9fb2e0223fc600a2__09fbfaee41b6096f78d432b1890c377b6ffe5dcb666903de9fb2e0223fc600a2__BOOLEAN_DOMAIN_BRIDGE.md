# The Boolean measurement boundary: quadratic rigidity, sharp randomized failure, and cubic escape

Independent synthesis and exact audit, 9 October 2026.

## Result in brief

The continuous-input measurement obstruction does not extend to the Boolean cube by dimension counting. Nevertheless, a precise Boolean replacement holds: a polynomial decoder whose composite output has total degree at most two cannot turn a rank-deficient linear sketch into an exact full Fourier transform on every Boolean input. For randomized, input-independent sketches of this type, some Boolean input has success probability at most **3/4**. The constant is sharp. Degree three already permits exact reconstruction from a rank-`N−1` sketch, although this supplies no faster FFT.

This result synthesizes the supplied quadratic midpoint identity with the retained-measurement model. It is not a general nonlinear FFT lower bound, and no publication-priority claim is made.

## 1. One clean theorem

Let `K` be `R` or `C`, let `A: K^N → K^m` be linear, and let `P: K^m → K^r` be a polynomial map. Allow a fixed affine offset in the measurements: `y=Ax+a`. Suppose the formal composite polynomial

    Q(x) = P(Ax+a)

has total degree at most two. If

    Q(x) = Bx+b   for every x ∈ {0,1}^N,

then

    B = DP(Ac+a) A,       c = (1/2,…,1/2).                (1)

Consequently `row(B) ⊆ row(A)` and `rank(B) ≤ rank(A)`. In particular, if the target is the complete invertible Fourier transform, exact Boolean recovery requires `rank(A)=N`.

The degree condition concerns the polynomial actually computed after cancellations. It holds in particular when the decoder `P` itself has degree at most two; it does not follow merely from a small number of nonlinear gates.

### Proof

Uniqueness of the multilinear representative on the Boolean cube implies that every degree-at-most-two polynomial map with the displayed Boolean values has the form

    Q(x) = Bx+b + Σ_i v_i (x_i²−x_i)                   (2)

for fixed output vectors `v_i`. This can also be checked directly at `0`, the standard basis vectors, and their pairwise sums: the constant and all cross-term coefficients are determined there.

At `c=(1/2,…,1/2)`, the derivative of each `x_i²−x_i` is zero. Therefore `DQ(c)=B`. The polynomial chain rule gives `DQ(c)=DP(Ac+a)A`, proving (1). □

This proof subsumes the supplied Fourier-specific quadratic rigidity theorem **for polynomials in the retained complex bins without conjugation or component extraction**. For a source subset of Fourier rows and an omitted target row, basis independence makes `row(B) ⊆ row(A)` impossible. No special property of a Fourier basis is needed for the general statement.

This qualification is essential: Boolean inputs are real, so `X_k=conjugate(X_(−k))`. If a missing bin’s conjugate partner remains, conjugating that partner already completes the missing bin. This operation is not a holomorphic polynomial in the retained scalar and is not forbidden by this theorem. Section 6 states the realified and conjugate-augmented versions.

### Midpoint evaluator corollary

Equation (2) also gives, for every `h ∈ K^N`,

    Bh = [Q(c+h)−Q(c−h)]/2.                           (3)

Thus a uniform division-free arithmetic circuit of size `s` computing a degree-at-most-two Boolean-correct output yields a general-input evaluator for the same linear map using two circuit evaluations and `O(N+r)` additional operations. This is a constant-factor reduction, not a new circuit lower bound. It does not prove that an `O(N)` circuit is impossible. A circuit with divisions would additionally need to be defined at every required off-cube evaluation; correctness only on the cube does not ensure that.

## 2. A sharp randomized Boolean gate

Assume `N≥2`, the target `B: K^N→K^r` is injective, and a random seed `ω` is independent of the input. Include any decoder randomness in that seed. For almost every seed, assume:

- The retained measurements are `A_ω x+a_ω`, with `rank(A_ω)<N`.
- The decoder is a polynomial `P_ω` in those measurements only.
- `Q_ω(x)=P_ω(A_ωx+a_ω)` has total degree at most two.

Then

    2^(−N) Σ_(x∈{0,1}^N) Pr_ω[Q_ω(x)=Bx+b] ≤ 3/4.    (4)

In particular, at least one fixed Boolean input has success probability at most `3/4`. This rules out a uniform success guarantee strictly above `3/4` for this architecture, even though its retained scalar measurements can be injective on the finite input set.

### Proof of the failure fraction

For each admissible seed, the preceding theorem says that `Q_ω−(Bx+b)` cannot vanish on the whole cube. At least one component therefore has a nonzero multilinear Boolean representative of degree at most two.

A nonzero multilinear polynomial of degree `d` is nonzero at at least a `2^(−d)` fraction of the cube. To see this, select a nonzero monomial of maximum degree `t≤d`. For every assignment of coordinates outside that monomial, the restricted polynomial on its `t` coordinates still has that nonzero top coefficient, so it is nonzero at at least one of the `2^t` assignments. Thus there are at least `2^(N−t)` nonzero evaluations.

Each seed consequently fails on at least one quarter of the cube. Averaging over the finite cube and the seed proves (4). There is no exchange of “one seed works for all inputs” with “each input works with high probability.” □

### Sharpness over the real or complex numbers

Retain `N−1` measurements:

    t=x_1+2x_2,  x_3,…,x_N.

The four possible values of `t` are `0,1,2,3`. Choose one omitted value uniformly at random. For each of the first two bits, use its quadratic Lagrange interpolant on the other three values of `t`. Retain the remaining coordinates exactly and apply the target linear map `B`.

Each seed is correct at three of the four bit pairs and fails at the omitted pair. Every Boolean input therefore succeeds with probability exactly `3/4`. This construction is checked with exact rational arithmetic in the accompanying audit. It establishes the sharpness of (4) for arbitrary linear sketches, not necessarily for a sketch restricted to selected Fourier rows.

## 3. Degree three really is a boundary

For the same packed measurement, both bits have exact cubic interpolants on all four values:

    x_1 = (2/3)t³ − 3t² + (10/3)t,
    x_2 = −(1/3)t³ + (3/2)t² − (7/6)t.

Thus a polynomial decoder of degree three can reconstruct every Boolean input from these `N−1` measurements. Applying `B` afterward yields a degree-three polynomial decoder for any full linear target, including the Fourier transform.

This does not give a faster FFT: after recovering the bits, the transform still has to be evaluated. It demonstrates exactly why the quadratic rank theorem and the `3/4` ceiling cannot be promoted to arbitrary-degree polynomial decoders.

The supplied 8-point Fourier-specific cubic identity is a second, distinct control. With `X_7` omitted but `X_0,…,X_6` retained, define

    Q = X_1X_6 + X_2X_5 + X_3X_4,
    R = X_1²X_5 + X_1X_3² + X_2²X_3.

Then on every Boolean input,

    4X_7 = −(X_0−4)Q − R − 2X_1X_2X_4.               (5)

The independent audit verified all 256 inputs exactly in `Z[z]/(z^4+1)`. This is a genuine cubic holomorphic identity; ordinary complex conjugation would recover `X_7` from `X_1` more cheaply for real inputs. Neither interpretation reduces the cost of an all-output FFT.

## 4. The finite cubic non-completion certificates are valid

The supplied certificate interface is:

- `N ∈ {8,16,32}`.
- Known modes are all modes except `1` and `N−1`.
- Target is `X_1`.
- Decoder is a complex polynomial of total degree at most three in the known modes.

A cyclic-shift character average projects any successful polynomial onto monomials whose frequency-index sums are `1 mod N`, without increasing degree or changing correctness on the cube. This justifies considering only those monomials. The constant term is absent because the target character is nontrivial.

Our checker independently enumerated all candidate monomials, reconstructed their values on the supplied witness supports, checked the supports and primitive roots, and evaluated the augmented square determinant using a fraction-free recurrence in `F_998244353`. It did not import or execute any uploaded code.

| N | Candidate monomials | Witness rows | Recomputed determinant mod 998244353 |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 8 | 8 | 9 | 244742411 |
| 16 | 40 | 41 | 897006988 |
| 32 | 168 | 169 | 712284710 |

All three residues match the supplied data and are nonzero. The prime was checked by trial division; each modular root has exact order `N`.

The matrix entries before specialization lie in `Z[ζ_N]`. Mapping `ζ_N` to the primitive modular root is a ring homomorphism. A determinant equal to zero over the cyclotomic field would remain zero after this specialization. Thus the nonzero residues certify full augmented column rank in characteristic zero, including over `C`, not merely over the finite field. No degree-at-most-three completion polynomial exists in these three finite cases.

Here the retained set is closed under frequency negation, because both `1` and `N−1` are omitted together. Therefore conjugates, real parts, and imaginary parts of every retained bin are already complex-linear combinations of retained bins. Allowing those operations does not evade these particular degree-at-most-three polynomial certificates.

These are finite interface-specific certificates. They neither establish an all-`N` cubic theorem nor forbid degree four and above, rational/branching decoders, or access to original samples. Modular rank deficiency would not by itself have established characteristic-zero existence; the actual certificates use nonzero determinants for nonexistence.

## 5. Information ambiguity and information injectivity must be kept separate

### Even modes alone genuinely lose Boolean information

For `N=2m` and any `u∈{0,1}^m`, put `x=(u,1−u)`. Every such input has the same even-frequency spectrum:

    X_0=m,  X_2=X_4=…=X_(N−2)=0.

The odd spectrum is a length-`m` Fourier transform of the independently chosen sign vector `2u−1`, after fixed modulation. It therefore takes `2^m` distinct values. No decoder of any kind can recover all odd modes solely from these even modes on every Boolean input.

The exact independent audit checked all 16 choices at `N=8` and all 256 choices at `N=16`, including distinctness of the odd spectra. The algebraic construction proves the statement for every even `N`.

### Other small Fourier subsets can be injective on the Boolean cube

The related supplied orbit report proves that, for `N=2^L`, a Fourier subset is injective on all Boolean inputs exactly when it includes a representative from each conductor orbit `1,2,4,…,N`. The minimal such subset has `L+1` bins. Its proof is consistent with the present theorem:

- Sufficiency: a rational coefficient difference polynomial vanishing at one representative of each conductor is divisible by all corresponding cyclotomic polynomials. Their product is `z^N−1`; degree below `N` forces the difference to vanish.
- Necessity: if an orbit of conductor `m≥2` is absent, repeating `(u,1−u)` with period `m` gives collisions supported spectrally only on that orbit. If DC is absent, zero and all-one inputs collide.

Consequently the full transform is some function of these `L+1` exact scalars on the finite domain; a polynomial interpolant also exists on any finite set of distinct measurement vectors. Its degree, evaluation work, precision, and output expansion are not free. The quadratic theorem says that a degree-at-most-two decoder cannot exploit this information sufficiency to supply an omitted independent Fourier row.

This orbit theorem was source-reviewed and its short proof independently checked here. The orbit archive's interpolation/rational-recovery computations were not rerun as part of this audit.

## 6. Correct integration with the continuous measurement gate

The prior `FUSION_THEOREMS.md`, Section B, proves a stronger success obstruction on a different domain. For arbitrary complex continuous inputs `x∈C^N`, an input-independent complex-linear sketch of complex rank below `N` leaves a nondegenerate continuous conditional distribution in its kernel. Any measurable decoder has exact-recovery probability zero for Lebesgue-almost-every input. No polynomial or degree restriction is needed.

There is no analogous conclusion from ambient rank alone on the Boolean cube. Even one real scalar `Σ_j 2^j x_j` is injective there. Instead, Sections 1–2 give a different, explicitly polynomial obstruction.

### Important real-versus-complex rank qualification

When the continuous input domain is `R^N` but the retained measurements are complex, the rank in the information-loss argument must be measured over `R`. One complex scalar `x_1+i x_2` already determines two real coordinates. Therefore:

- On `C^N`, `RK` retained complex-linear scalars have rank at most `RK`; exact continuous recovery requires `RK≥N`.
- On `R^N`, the same count gives at most `2RK` real dimensions. Exact continuous recovery requires the actual real rank to be at least `N`, giving only the crude count `2RK≥N` unless more structure is proved.

Accordingly, the earlier sentence referring generally to “continuous real/complex inputs” must carry this field qualification. The original complex-domain theorem and its complex-input cost gate remain intact.

The Boolean theorem here uses holomorphic polynomial dependence on the retained complex scalars. A conjugation or real-part primitive is not a complex polynomial in those scalars. If conjugation is permitted on a real-input domain, augment the retained linear forms by their conjugates before applying the row-span theorem. This correctly permits ordinary conjugate symmetry. For a general polynomial decoder in real and imaginary measurement components, instead apply the theorem over `R` to the realified measurement matrix `A_R` and target matrix `B_R`, formed by stacking real and imaginary parts. The correct conclusion is `row_R(B_R) ⊆ row_R(A_R)`; full Boolean input recovery requires real rank `N`. The randomized `3/4` gate likewise applies when this actual real rank is deficient. The control `x_1+i x_2` plus real/imaginary extraction has full real rank two and violates none of these statements.

### What this says about the v6 evaluator

The v6 median decoder uses comparisons and branching. It is not covered by the polynomial degree-at-most-two theorem merely because its separate regions are linear. Its continuous complex-input impossibility result still follows from the measurable retained-information argument when `RK<N`.

Neither theorem automatically closes an exact Boolean v6 route. Such a route needs its own evidence about information injectivity, exact decoding, and fully charged complexity. Conversely, merely observing finite-domain injectivity or an interpolating polynomial does not establish an efficient decoder.

## 7. Research consequence and stopping boundary

These branches combine into an honest hierarchy:

1. Continuous inputs: a genuinely rank-deficient retained sketch loses information, for arbitrary measurable decoders, using the correct underlying field.
2. Boolean inputs with polynomial composite degree at most two: exact full recovery still requires full sketch rank; input-independent randomization cannot raise the worst-case success above `3/4`.
3. Boolean inputs with degree three: rank compression becomes possible in general; specific missing-conjugate-pair Fourier completion is nevertheless excluded at `N=8,16,32` by exact certificates.
4. Boolean inputs with arbitrary decoding: injectivity depends on the actual measurements. Even-only spectra are ambiguous; one-per-conductor Fourier spectra are injective but have no supplied fast recovery algorithm.

A viable faster exact FFT still needs a uniform, fully charged multi-output algorithm. No result here yields `O(N)` fresh exact Fourier evaluation, excludes all cubic or higher-degree methods, or proves an unrestricted `Ω(N log N)` lower bound.
