# Phase 23 — short-probe and joint-pencil certified reciprocal recovery

**Date:** 2026-10-10. **Status:** all-input exactness proved by a full cyclic certificate/fallback; work improvement on a *structured* family; finite-field implementations regression-tested. **No unconditional sub-\(N\log N\) FFT theorem.** The algorithm builds on Phase 21/22, Prony interpolation and Dickson-polynomial trace sieving; independent priority is not established.

## Precise algebraic setting

Work over \(\mathbb F_p\) for an odd prime with \(N\mid p-1\), a *supplied* primitive \(N\)th root \(\omega\), and the unnormalized \(N\)-point Fourier transform \((F_Nr)_k=\sum_j r_j\omega^{jk}\). Inherited Green matrices \(L\) and \(P\) are invertible and obey \(LF_N=F_NP\). Given \(x\), compute \(r=Px\); the goal is to find \(F_Nr\), then solve \(Ly=F_Nr\).

The candidate structured family has \(A(z)=\sum_{j\in J}a_jz^j\) with \(|J|\le s\), \(1\le\delta<N\) and \(\alpha\ne0\), satisfying

\[
 A(S)r=(I-\alpha S^\delta)e_0,\qquad
 A(\omega^k)\ne0 \quad(0\le k<N).
\]

This includes the two-unknown-factor/two-defect graph-union examples when \(s=4\). Every accepted candidate is *fully checked* on all \(N\) input positions; none is assumed true from a short Fourier prefix.

## Theorem 1: 2s+2 samples suffice for both Hankel pencils

For \(R_k=(F_Nr)_k\ne0\), set \(T_k=R_k^{-1}\). A correct model has

\[
  Y_k=(1-\alpha w^k)T_k=A(\omega^k), \quad w=\omega^\delta.
\]

As a sum of at most \(s\) distinct geometric modes, \(Y\) has Hankel rank at most \(s\). Define two adjacent minors of order \(s+1\):

\[
 P_b(\alpha,w)=\det[\,(1-\alpha w^{b+i+j})T_{b+i+j}\,]_{i,j=0}^{s},\quad b\in\{0,1\}.
\]

The greatest sampled index is \(1+2s\); therefore the *union* of all indices used by both determinants is precisely \(\{0,\ldots,2s+1\}\), containing **2s+2**, rather than 4s, samples. For \(s=4\) this is ten rather than sixteen. Thus the same eliminant \(\operatorname{Res}_\alpha(P_0,P_1)\) can be constructed from ten exact Fourier probes, with no missing determinant entries.

This is an exact observation about the input dependency of the existing Hankel elimination algorithm. It **does not** claim that ten samples uniquely specify an arbitrary Fourier transform or even an arbitrary candidate sparse rational representation.

## Theorem 2: shared streaming frequency-prefix circuit

For \(m=2s+2\) initialize \(R_0,\ldots,R_{m-1}=0\). For each input coordinate \(j\), read the already initialized root \(v=\omega^j\), set \(u=r_j\), and sequentially perform

```
for k = 0,...,m-1:
    R[k] += u
    if k+1 < m: u *= v
```

Induction gives \(u=r_j(\omega^j)^k\) upon entering iteration \(k\). Hence all \(m\) coefficients are exact. The field arithmetic count is **exactly** \(N(m-1)\) multiplications and \(Nm\) additions, for a total of \((2m-1)N\). This excludes the separately priced input/Green preprocessing but uses no input-dependent precomputation or free Fourier transforms. The Phase 22 per-frequency geometric scans charged \(2Nm\) multiplications and \(Nm\) additions. Accounting for its original \(4s\) frequencies, the total old probe cost was \(12sN\), versus new \((4s+3)N\) operations. At \(s=4\), that is \(48N\) versus \(19N\) field operations for the probes.

## Theorem 3: joint pencil compiler

For each interpolation node \(w\), compute \(w^k\) just once for \(k=0,\ldots,2s+1\). For each sampled \(\alpha\), compute \(Y_k=(1-\alpha w^k)T_k\) once over the same \(2s+2\)-element window, then form its two overlapping Hankel matrices from \(Y_0,\ldots,Y_{2s}\) and \(Y_1,\ldots,Y_{2s+1}\). Their determinants and subsequent exact interpolations equal the two independently computed pencils *term by term*. Applying the original Sylvester determinant therefore yields **exactly the same resultant** as Phase 22. Direct independent comparisons of the old and joint evaluators over 75 finite-field instances at \(s=2,3,4\) passed.

This is a constant-factor compiler improvement, not a new degree or asymptotic bound.

## Theorem 4: all-input soundness with fewer probes

Build the Phase 21/22 compressed self-reciprocal resultant and Dickson trace sieve from the short prefix, under its inherited polynomial-factor theorem. Recover all candidate nonidentity subgroup shift roots. For each candidate \(w=\omega^\delta\) whose two pencil polynomials have **linear gcd** identifying \(\alpha\), recover a sparse \(A\) from the available \(2s\) corrected Prony samples. Reject any candidate if its \(A(\omega^k)\) vanishes. Otherwise compute and *explicitly verify*

\[
  A(S)r=(I-\alpha S^\delta)e_0
\]

at every one of the \(N\) coordinates. In Fourier space, this gives the exact identity

\[
  A(\omega^k)(F_Nr)_k=1-\alpha\omega^{k\delta}.
\]

Since \(A(\omega^k)\ne0\), the forcing vector is uniquely determined. The project returns \(L^{-1}F_Nr\), hence exactly \(F_Nx\). If **no candidate is fully certified**, it invokes the inherited exact Bluestein evaluator and then the same Green solve. Thus **every input** produces the exact transform in the supported field model, even if the Hankel compression theorem or candidate-finding branch fails to identify a structured representation.

Unlike the Phase 21 implementation, a failed full certificate **does not terminate candidate search**. The old early-exit argument used the longer 4s prefix for uniqueness. We explicitly remove that assumption with only 2s+2 probes.

For any nonzero reduced eliminant \(U\), the Phase 21 algebra bounds its degree by \(2b\), \(b=\sum_{j=1}^s j^2\); hence at most \(2b\) admissible distinct \(N\)th-root candidates need validation. With fixed \(s\), even a complete \(O(sN)\) certificate at every candidate remains \(O_s(N)\). Cases with identically zero resultant, vanishing probes, nonlinear parameter gcd, singular spectral denominator or unavailable field setup simply fall back. Root extraction uses the previously charged trace-gcd/half-group scan and BSGS routines, never an uncharged oracle.

**Cost:** accepted promised instances take \(O_s(N+b^2\log N+\sqrt N+\operatorname{polylog}p)\) field arithmetic under the inherited assumptions on root tables and modular square-root setup; higher-degree trace gcd may use \(O_s(N)\) scanning. For fixed \(s\), this is \(O_s(N)\) in the previously specified finite-field arithmetic model with a fixed or appropriately bounded field setup. Arbitrary inputs take at most \(O(N\log N)+O_s(N)\) with the baseline FFT fallback. Bit complexity, floating-point stability, and field/primitive-root construction have **not** been improved or proved by this project step.

## Counterexample: short prefixes never certify arbitrary input

For \(N>m\), choose any \(k_0\ge m\) and set \(\eta_j=\omega^{-k_0j}\). Then

\[
 (F_N\eta)_k=\begin{cases}N&k=k_0\\0&k\ne k_0.\end{cases}
\]

because \(\omega\) has exact order \(N\) and \(p\nmid N\). Thus \(r\) and \(r+\eta\) have **identical first m Fourier coefficients**, but their full transforms differ. An explicit \(N=128\), \(m=10\), \(k_0=19\) adversarial regression has two plausible shift candidates and two failed full cyclic checks, followed by an exact FFT fallback. Any shortcut that accepts solely on the ten probes is demonstrably unsound.

## Verification scope and limits

- 58 regression tests (51 inherited + 7 new); additional independent direct DFT audit checks **35,206** output coordinates over two distinct prime fields and 11 lengths. Includes generic data, structured families and prefix-matched adversaries.
- The joint pencil equals the independent Phase 22 resultant evaluation on 75 direct randomized instances for \(s=2,3,4\).
- Benchmarked full arithmetic ledger shows **32.26%** reduction versus Phase 22 on the structured length \(N=32768\) example. On generic input the new branch still has positive failed-discovery overhead relative to doing the FFT immediately.
- No proof of historical novelty; no full machine-checked formal proof of inherited Phase 21 reciprocal-resultant factorization. We reuse it as a discovery mechanism and protect output correctness with a full check and fallback. No general exact \(o(N\log N)\) Fourier algorithm is claimed.
