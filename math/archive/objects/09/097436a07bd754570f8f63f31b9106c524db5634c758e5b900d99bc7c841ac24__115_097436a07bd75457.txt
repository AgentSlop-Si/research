Conditional linear cost for logarithmic support
October 9 2026

This is an explicit reduction, not an independently verified thin-matrix algorithm or a novelty claim.

Hypothesis H
There is a uniform exact algorithm over the intended field, including C, that multiplies any n-by-p and p-by-n matrices for p<=floor(log_2 n) in O(n^2) TOTAL scalar arithmetic operations. Its construction, all additions, scalar multiplications, and decoding must be included. An essential-multiplication or tensor-rank count alone does not imply H. Any constants and field assumptions must be uniform in n,p.

Consequence of H
For arbitrary distinct Nth roots z_s, arbitrary amplitudes a_s, and k=O(log N), all N values
  f_m=sum_s a_s z_s^m
can be produced in O(N) exact field arithmetic, including support/root preparation under the usual supplied-root model.

Proof
Let n=ceil(sqrt(N)). Pad the requested output interval to n^2, producing at most O(N) entries. Build
  U[r,s]=z_s^r,                 0<=r<n;
  W[s,b]=a_s(z_s^n)^b,          0<=b<n.
Then (UW)[r,b]=f_(r+nb).

The root powers z_s=zeta_N^(j_s) can be formed in O(k log N) operations if only a primitive root and indices are supplied. Both power tables cost O(nk), using consecutive powers. These costs are o(N) when k=O(log N). Separate the inner dimension into R=ceil(k/floor(log_2 n)) groups, padding the final group with zeros. For k<=C log N with fixed C, R=O(1). Hypothesis H computes each n-by-n partial product in O(n^2), and summing R outputs costs O(R n^2). Thus the total is O(N). Rearrangement and discarding padding cost O(N).

For more general k, the same reduction gives
  O(k log N + sqrt(N) k + N[1+k/log N])
provided H holds with the stated uniformity. This expression does not claim linear time when k/log N grows.

Evidence and unresolved issue
Timothy Chan's author paper, Speeding up the Four Russians Algorithm by About One More Logarithmic Factor, final remarks on PDF page 5, explicitly states the n-by-log n case in O(n^2) operations and cites Brockett and Dobkin 1976:
https://tmc.web.engr.illinois.edu/bmm9_14.pdf

The original Brockett-Dobkin abstract instead foregrounds n^2+o(n^2) multiplications, allowing multiplication/division by 2:
https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/0205041

The two descriptions make the full construction and addition counts worth checking. This note does not resolve that discrepancy by assuming either wording is wrong. See THIN_MATRIX.txt and the independent source audit for the final verification status of this pass.

Scope
Even an affirmative H would give a literature-based sparse-expansion result. It would not by itself establish a novel algorithm, a general dense exact FFT improvement, practical floating-point speed, or a bit-complexity bound. It might be useful as a component of an approximate or structured-input method, whose other costs and error guarantees would require their own analysis.
