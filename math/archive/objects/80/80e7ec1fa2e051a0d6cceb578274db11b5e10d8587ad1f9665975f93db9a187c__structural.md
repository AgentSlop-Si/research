## 1. Historical item 2: exact identification and accepted scope

**Identification:** Hero0 Phase12, “Fourier–Green obstruction by permutation order and Schur-positive minors”; C05 in the 02:08 final focused publication assessment. The recovered historical chain explicitly identifies its second publication direction as “Fourier–Green restricted representation impossibility (Hero #0 Phase 12).” [S1, S2, S3]

**Accepted statement:** Over C, a Fourier matrix cannot be a one-layer sum of r legal weighted-permutation resolvents with diagonal endpoint masks, plus rank-t, row-degree-s sparse and arbitrary diagonal corrections, if

    N > 2(m−1)(B−m)(B−1),
    m=r+t+1, L=m(s+2), B=(L−1)^(2^(r−1))+1.

The resolvents may have independent cycle orders, multiple cycle components, zero edge weights, and singular diagonal endpoint masks. At r=2, t=s=0 this excludes all N≥2301, with an arbitrary added diagonal still allowed. Fixed t,s imply r=Ω(log log N). The audit also accepts the stated prime-divisor extension and N=4096 cyclotomic control. This is an all-size mathematical proof in its model, not only a finite experiment. [S2:3–35; S3:108–126]

**Boundary:** Independent unmatched row/column endpoint permutations are not covered in this original statement; products of sum-layers escape the proof. It counts representation terms, not the arithmetic required by an arbitrary or shared evaluator. Phase11's sparse-inverse/low-rank gate did not already prove it. [S2:7–9, 27–47]

**Publication assessment:** “Substantial specialized application of classical combinatorics and Fourier minors,” with a plausible compound theorem but unresolved historical priority. Its rank 4 in the 02:08 assessment was a dated judgment for that selected set. “Strong matrix paper” is best understood as a developmental assessment, not confirmed novelty or acceptance. [S3:108–126]

## 2. Its relationship to the newer separator-inverse A

The project line is:

    C05 one-layer Green obstruction
      → simultaneous independently ordered separation-rank obstruction
      → bounded-separator inverse-sum obstruction A
      + tree witness establishing additional structural coverage.

This diagram expresses mathematical continuity, not a claim that every statement is a formal special case of the next.

**Intermediate accepted theorem:** If H_j have separation ranks r_j under d distinct independently chosen row/column order pairs, a common m-square rectangle exists with m=floor(N/2^d) and sum rank at most R=sum r_j. For a complex normalized Fourier target, m²≤NR; at prime length, m≤R. With Q rank-one branches this already implies Q≥(1/2)log₂N−O(log log N) for arbitrary N, and a stronger near-log₂ bound for primes. It already allows independent endpoint permutations and gives an operator-error gap. Bare disjoint cycle blocks also fit by concatenating their orders. [S4, S5]

**Newer A, accepted:** For a complex flat unitary U and

    T=Σ_(i=1)^r L_i Q_i^(-1) R_i,

where Q_i are invertible and L_i,R_i have permutation-contained supports with independent routes and possibly zero weights, a half-balanced separator budget q on every required marked restriction gives

    U=T ⇒ N≤4qr36^r;
    U=D+T ⇒ N≤16qr36^r.

Treewidth w of the symmetric bipartite lift suffices with q=w+1. Every invertible column-two Q has a pseudoforest lift, so q=3 gives N≤12r36^r and N≤48r36^r. Thus fixed q forces r=Ω(log N) for exact representation. The separate approximation result excludes vanishing error with fixed r,q, not logarithmic growth for a fixed positive tolerance. [S6, S7:13–43]

**Actual increment:** Arbitrary attached-tree column-two supports, and more general bounded-separator lifts, under independently routed sums. Logarithmic Hamiltonian bounds, independent routing, bare-cycle multiplicity, and the approximation mechanism were already in S4. Marketing those as A's new discoveries would erase the inherited theorem. The separator formulation uses classical nullity, weighted separators, and graph-induced inverse-rank structures; its independently routed flat-unitary application is the remaining publication candidate. [S6, “Important antecedent correction”; S7:33–43]

**Not a wholesale deletion of C05:** C05 explicitly includes bounded-rank and row-sparse correction classes and has its own finite thresholds/minor arguments. A's displayed accepted theorem specifies an arbitrary diagonal correction. The overlapping additive Green core is strengthened, but the inspected documents do not license silently dropping or claiming formal subsumption of every C05 clause.

## 3. The tree witness: what it settles and what it does not

For the complete rooted binary tree of height h, N=2^(h+1)−1, let P[u,v]=1 when u is v's parent. For Q=I−P and B=Q^(-1), the accepted all-field result is

    floor((h+1)/4) ≤ sr(B)=sr(Q) ≤ h,

where sr minimizes over independent row and column orders and controls both separated cut orientations. Q has at most two nonzeros per column and three per row; its bipartite lift is a subcubic tree. A therefore has constant q=2 on this family although its optimized ordered separation rank is Θ(log N). [S8 §§1–8]

The separately accepted scaled family Q_(h,α)=I−αP, 0<α<1/√2, has identical exact ranks and

    κ₂(Q_(h,α))=κ₂(Q_(h,α)^(-1))≤(1+α√2)/(1−α√2).

At α=1/2 the bound is 3+2√2<6 uniformly in h. [S9]

**Settled:** A's internal non-containment gate: column-two inverse families are not contained in any fixed ordered-separation-rank budget, even when uniformly conditioned. **Not settled:** reverse containment or a literal proper-superset relation between the complete classes; numerical-rank bounds at a fixed tolerance; optimal HSS/SSS storage; unrestricted arithmetic lower bounds; or worldwide novelty. [S8 §§1,10; S9 “Scope”]

The 05:58 publication update explicitly treats this witness as an attributed supporting proposition. Its proof is close to classical forest pathwidth/linear rank-width plus an elementary independent-axis bridge; a specializing binary-tree family and tree-versus-sequential motivation already occur in the final 2025 TQS paper. It strengthens A's paper story and closes the internal objection, but is not a separate breakthrough or a resolution of TQS's storage question. The update ranks B ahead of A only within that pair, while making A the cleaner near-term short-draft candidate. [S10:7–63]

## 4. Historical item 3: Weyl–Green branching is distinct

Hero17 Phase13 has an explicit internally accepted audit of all six scoped proof statements. [S11, S12]

For odd N≥3 with primitive ω and fixed canonical S,T:

- Fourier's Weyl coefficient matrix has rank N.
- Each canonical ordered product G_S(α)G_T(β), even with Weyl-monomial endpoints, has coefficient rank at most one. Therefore a sum of B such paths representing F needs **B≥N**.
- This excludes reverse order, mixed orders, different Weyl generator pairs per branch, arbitrary diagonal endpoint gauges, and deeper paths. Matrix orientation is literal: G_S G_T applies G_T first to a column input.
- In a rotated generating pair U=S^uT^v, V=S^rT^s, D=us−vr a unit, coefficient rank is **N/gcd(N,δ)**, δ=(D−ur−vs)/2. In particular, basis changes can collapse the rank to one.
- At odd prime p, for every **single fixed generating pair shared by all branches**, with no extra Weyl endpoints, the ordered two-resolvent sum requires **B≥(p+1)/2**. The δ≠0 case gives B≥p; the δ=0 case uses a chirp Hankel minor. Additional endpoints would break this Hankel rank-one argument.

These are stronger numerical branch counts than A's logarithmic bound in much narrower grammars. They are not contradictory and do not supply a scalar arithmetic lower bound. Reverse-order and three-stage Green products have full Weyl coefficient rank while applying in O(N) work, explicitly demonstrating the rank-to-cost failure. [S11:17–59]

**Relationship to the list:** This is neither C05's sum-of-single-inverses theorem nor C04's three-stage discrepancy theorem. It is not one of the thirteen stable C01–C13 cards in the 02:08 assessment and was not evaluated in the later A/B comparison. Its omission cannot be read as rejection, demotion, or novelty resolution.

**Publication status:** A correct internally audited restricted representation theorem, potentially useful as a focused theorem/section. The source and audit explicitly leave historical priority unestablished. “Potential original restricted-complexity result” remains a possibility, but “restricted representation/branch-count theorem” is more accurate than an unqualified runtime lower bound. The inspected record does not justify a new standalone global ranking for it.

- **C01, exact additive Green length three:** For N=5q², q positive odd, gcd(q,5)=1, q≡1 mod 241, the specified five-coordinate principal block C_q of B_N=(F_N^(-1)+A_N)^(-1) has minimum exactly three cyclic-resolvent terms over a genuine quadratic extension. The full growing B_N inherits only a ≥3 lower bound; the accepted upper theorem is fixed dimension five. Two terms are nontrivial solves and the third is diagonal. This was priority 1 in that broader assessment, plausibly original but still needing a focused additive-resolvent comparison. [S3:5–7, 50–70]
- **C02, sharp dyadic endpoint minimax:** For N=2^m≥8 in characteristic zero, min_(ρ,σ) q(P_ρF_NP_σ^T)=N/4+1, attained by double bit reversal. This concerns Fourier's own optimized cut rank, not the binary-tree inverse witness. Priority 2 in that assessment; final 2008 Delvaux–Van Barel full-text/citation comparison remains open. [S3:72–88]
- **C04, three-stage discrepancy obstruction:** For F_N=S₃P₂S₂P₁S₁, q(S_i)≤r_i, arbitrary internal permutations, and at least one affine-coprime endpoint ordering, 2r₁+r₂+r₃≥c log N−2, with transposed counterpart. Both arbitrary non-affine endpoints and arbitrary depth are outside scope. Distinct product theorem; priority 3 in the dated assessment and a substantial application candidate. [S3:90–106]
