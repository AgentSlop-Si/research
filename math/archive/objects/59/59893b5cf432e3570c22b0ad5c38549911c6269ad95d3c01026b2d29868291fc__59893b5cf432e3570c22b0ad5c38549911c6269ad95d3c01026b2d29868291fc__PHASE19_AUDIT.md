# Hero #1 Phase19: independent source and mathematical review

Date: 2026-10-09 UTC. No uploaded code was executed, imported, or modified.

## Verdict

The principal extension beyond Phase18 is sound: an unknown s-sparse circular inverse can be recovered and fully certified, and then used to evaluate the complete Fourier vector in O(Ns+s³) exact field arithmetic. In particular, this removes supplied-factor information for an invertible product of a fixed number of unknown graph binomials driven by one unknown impulse. The separate one-defect graph detector and its separated-wrap bounded-defect extension are also sound within the assumptions below.

Accept this as a structured-input algorithm and a certified selector with an ordinary FFT fallback. Do not promote it to a generic-input sub-N log N FFT, a floating-point stability theorem, a bit-complexity bound, or a historical-priority claim.

The review requires four qualifications beyond the source's headline wording:

1. One-defect discovery finds a valid delay representation, not necessarily the original hidden delay. The orientations delta and −delta are observationally equivalent after changing multiplier and impulse location.
2. Graph-operation proxies omit subtraction charges in projective equality tests and full candidate scans. Restoring them preserves O(N) for fixed bounds but changes the printed finite arithmetic totals.
3. The all-input O(N log N) bound is for fixed graph-defect and inverse-tap caps, and fields supporting the implementation's fallback. Variable s retains the s³ term; the graph cap has an exponential dependence.
4. The inherited Green solver admissibility, convolution-root supply, root-planning, and rational sign-counter qualifications from Phase18 remain. Their implementation files are unchanged.

## 2. Unknown one-defect shift: valid discovery and the orientation gauge

Use the positive-sign DFT and `(Sr)[j]=r[j−1]`. Let r be nowhere zero, N≥3, and suppose `(I−alpha S^delta)r=c e_j0`, with c≠0, alpha≠0, and gcd(delta,N)=1. Moving along the delta-cycle gives

    r[j0+t delta] = b alpha^t,  0≤t<N,
    c = b(1−alpha^N).

Thus alpha^N≠1. If u is the inverse of delta modulo N, adjacent ordinary ratios take the two distinct values alpha^u and alpha^(u−N), with occurrence counts N−u and u. Counting projective classes costs O(N) field operations without field inversions. Inverting either class count modulo N supplies at most two delay candidates. At a valid delay, at most one of three distinct sampled edges is defective, so the other two determine a valid ratio. A complete scan then certifies at most one defect.

The spectral denominator `1−alpha omega^(k delta)` cannot vanish in the one-defect case. The impulse forcing is one geometric stream, so full Fourier recovery is linear. A coprime zero-defect eigenvector is an ordinary Fourier character; delay 1 detects it, and its one erased Fourier coordinate is repaired directly in O(N). Noncoprime zero-defect cycles are outside this completeness statement.

The source theorem's phrase “the delay delta” must be read as “a valid coprime delay.” In fact,

    (I−alpha^(−1) S^(−delta))r
      = −alpha^(−1) S^(−delta) (I−alpha S^delta)r.

The reverse orientation therefore has one defect too. Our exact example at N=7 over GF(29) is r=(5,15,11,10,1,22,20). Delay 3 with multiplier 2 gives `3 e0`; delay 4 with multiplier 15 gives `13 e4`. No method receiving only r can distinguish which description originally generated it. The Fourier output is unaffected by this ambiguity, and the source candidate-verification logic accepts valid descriptions rather than relying on uniqueness.

## 3. Bounded multiple defects: conditional completeness

For a nowhere-zero r, a coprime delta, and K≥1 defect edges, choose the cycle origin at a defect. The cycle splits into at most K geometric runs. An ordinary adjacent ratio equals a run-coefficient quotient times alpha^u or alpha^(u−N), so there are at most 2K² ratio classes.

Under the explicit wrap-separation hypothesis, no numerical class mixes wrapping and nonwrapping edges. Therefore u is a sum of complete class multiplicities. Enumerating these subset sums includes u, and a sample of 2K+1 distinct edges contains a strict majority of correct ratios. The full defect scan is the final acceptance certificate. For the implemented sampler require N≥2K+1, in addition to nowhere-zero input; smaller lengths safely reject the route.

The coarse detector cost is O(N(K²+K·2^(2K²))) field operations, plus integer candidate generation and modular Euclid. It is linear only for fixed K. For a coprime binomial, at most one frequency needs resonance repair. The default K=2 route consequently has linear discovery and decoding work on its promised family. Nothing here provides an efficient uniform discovery bound when K grows.

Our checker verifies 30 separated-wrap positive examples for K=2 and 3 at five lengths. It also independently reproduces the source's N=128, delta=63, alpha=omega^9, two-run counterexample: exactly two graph defects, but colliding wrap classes, and no accepted bounded-ratio certificate. Full Fourier correctness after rejection depends on the fallback, not on extending the separated-wrap theorem.

## 4. Fully blind sparse circular inverse

Suppose an unknown polynomial C with t distinct nonzero taps, 1≤t≤s≤N/2, satisfies `C(S)r=e0`. Applying Fourier diagonalization gives

    C(omega^k) R_k = 1,
    1/R_k = sum_l c_l (omega^a_l)^k.

All R_k are nonzero. Compute the first 2s Fourier samples by explicitly charged dense dot products, costing O(Ns). Their reciprocals are t distinct nonzero exponentials. The t-by-t Hankel matrix factors as a Vandermonde matrix, a nonzero diagonal of amplitudes, and its transpose; hence it is nonsingular even in the exact finite-field setting. A length-2s prefix therefore determines the minimal order-t recurrence. Standard BM supplies it in O(s²) field operations.

The recurrence's characteristic roots are exactly the subgroup nodes omega^a_l. Evaluating the degree-t polynomial at all N supplied root powers costs O(Ns) and identifies their exponent labels directly. No discrete-log oracle or general polynomial factorization is hidden in this step. A t-by-t Vandermonde solve costs O(s³). The final full cyclic convolution certificate costs O(Ns).

Soundness does not depend on a short prefix uniquely representing arbitrary input. On any input, an accepted C has been checked against every equation of `C(S)r=e0`. Fourier diagonalization then forces every denominator to be nonzero and the output to be `1/C(omega^k)`. Evaluating C by sparse geometric streams and batch inversion costs O(Ns). A zero sample, unsuitable recurrence, missing subgroup roots, degenerate interpolation, or failed full certificate causes rejection.

Thus the accepted arithmetic bound is O(Ns+s³). The source's proof paragraph describing a general “O(sN) discovery toll” omits the variable-s interpolation term; the ledger correctly retains it. For fixed s this distinction vanishes. For s=O(log log N), the promised-family bound is O(N log log N) asymptotically. That is still a small structured family, not a bound on arbitrary fresh vectors.

Our independent recurrence reconstruction deliberately uses an order-by-order Hankel solve rather than reproducing the uploaded BM code. This supplies an independent exact mathematical control, not an optimized implementation timing claim or execution-level certification of the uploaded program.

## 5. What is genuinely new relative to Phase18

If an invertible unknown union Q of m graph binomials satisfies `Q(S)r=c e_j`, then

    C(S) = c^(−1) S^(−j) Q(S)

is a circular inverse with at most 2^m taps after collisions are combined modulo N. The sparse-inverse route does not need any factor, impulse location, amplitude, or factorization supplied. It recovers the combined inverse rather than a unique underlying factor list. Coprimality of the individual graph delays is not needed by this inverse theorem; invertibility is.

Phase18's corresponding evaluator required supplied factors. Removing that structural information for the one-impulse case is a real algorithmic extension within the project. Our controls recover 36 unknown-union examples with one, two, and three factors at four lengths, plus three noncoprime-union examples. The three-factor examples include six-tap collisions from an eight-monomial expansion.

For general K>1 forcing, however, `R_k=H(omega^k)/Q(omega^k)` and the reciprocals are Q/H. They need not be sparse exponential sums. No general blind recovery of both sparse numerator and sparse denominator has been proved here. The source correctly preserves this as an open gate.

For a fixed support J of size t, invertible C is parametrized by t coefficients, and `C -> C(S)^(−1)e0` is rational. Finite union over supports at fixed N has complex algebraic dimension at most s. An invertible Green change of coordinates preserves that dimension. For s<N the family has measure zero in complex N-space. This rules out calling this family generic; it proves no lower bound against unrelated FFT algorithms.

## 6. Implementation domain, setup, counters, and precision

### Exactness and fallback domain

The abstract sparse-inverse theorem requires a field whose characteristic does not divide N and a supplied primitive Nth root. The uploaded all-input implementation requires more: its Bluestein fallback needs a primitive 2Nth root compatible with its generator and enough power-of-two roots for convolution of length `nextpow2(2N−1)`. An Nth root alone does not guarantee that this particular fallback is available.

The unchanged Green setup builds both P and L using `CyclicSolver`, which inverts every diagonal entry before checking cyclic closure. This remains stricter than nonsingularity of the operator. At GF(17), N=16, omega=3, the L and P diagonals vanish at indices 3 and 11 respectively, although both operator determinants are 16 modulo 17. The abstract transform identity survives; this source constructor rejects those cases. Our independent Green controls use dense pivoted solves and must not be interpreted as patches to the uploaded implementation.

A supplied primitive root yields its N powers in O(N). Finding a field and primitive generator, verifying its admissibility, and deriving roots by exponentiation are distinct setup tasks. Growing-modulus root initialization must charge O(log p) exponentiation work, and the inherited rational short-NTT branch additionally needs the relevant short roots supplied or an O(log p · log(K+1)) field-multiplication setup allowance for separate stage-root powers. Restoring its omitted rational sign/subtraction operations adds O(N+K log K) field additions without changing the accepted asymptotic bound. Fixed-field experiments are finite experiments, not an asymptotic sequence of arbitrary lengths with constant bit costs.

For fixed caps the rejected routes add O(N) work before a valid ordinary O(N log N) fallback. For variable s retain O(Ns+s³+N log N), and for variable graph cap retain its exponential candidate bound. No source-dependent preprocessing of a future fresh vector is free. Test-data construction from sparse forcing is allowed for experiments, but is not a preprocessing privilege for arbitrary input.

### Newly identified graph-counter omissions

`blind_discovery.py` computes `(a*d−c*b) mod p` in `_equal_projective`, but charges only two products and a comparison. Its full candidate loops likewise compute one subtraction per edge but charge only the products. `Work.arithmetic()` excludes comparisons, so the missing field additions are not recovered there.

For each saved graph benchmark row, at least

    projective_comparisons + candidate_edge_checks

subtractions are missing from its reported scalar total. `STATIC_ACCOUNTING_CORRECTIONS.json` records the exact additive corrections derivable from those saved counters, without executing the benchmark. At N=4096, the standalone one-defect row changes from 103,242 to 113,891 after these 10,649 subtractions alone; the semi-blind row changes from 148,322 to 158,971. These are accounting corrections to saved proxies, not independently measured instruction counts. The hybrid JSON lacks these detailed counters, so we do not invent corrected hybrid totals.

Successful nowhere-zero prechecks and some guard accesses are also omitted from `vector_reads`; some `gcd_calls` count only successful predicates. The inherited rational-series sign/subtraction omissions persist. These issues change literal finite counters, not the accepted fixed-parameter asymptotic bounds. The benchmark fractions are neither wall-clock measurements nor exact whole-program operation counts. Shared setup and the selected Bluestein baseline must remain explicit when using them.

### Complex and bit costs

For the 2-by-2 Vandermonde with nodes 1 and exp(2 pi i/N), the Gram eigenvalues are `2±2 cos(pi/N)`, giving condition number `cot(pi/(2N))`, asymptotic to 2N/pi. This is an exact numerical-conditioning warning, independently checked here. More generally small Fourier samples, close nodes, near-resonant denominators, and exact support tests are problematic under rounding. Finite-field soundness does not prove stable complex recovery, finite precision requirements, or a favorable bit bound.

These checks support the algebraic arguments and the restrictions. They do not certify every uploaded branch by execution, rerun the source's 337,307 reported comparisons, validate its exact source benchmark totals, or establish asymptotic improvement on generic inputs.

## 8. Prior-art boundary

The standard ingredients are not new historical claims. The [Plonka–Tasche 2014 paper](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/gamm.201410011) discusses Prony recovery of structured exponential sums and sparse vectors; [Marvasti et al.'s 2009 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/0902.1853) explicitly relates Prony, annihilating filters, coding, and sparse spectral representations. We verified these primary bibliographic/abstract sources. This review makes no claim to have exhaustively searched the literature or established priority for the specific integration.

The accepted project-local result is a certified combination of known algebraic tools, removing supplied-filter information in a sharply defined one-impulse family. A broader exact FFT breakthrough remains unproved.
