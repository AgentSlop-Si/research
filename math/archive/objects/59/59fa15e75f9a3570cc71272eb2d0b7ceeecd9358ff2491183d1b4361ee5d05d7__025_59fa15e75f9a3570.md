# Bidirectional scan extension: the surviving graph mechanism

Research checkpoint, 9 October 2026. **The exact arbitrary-input DFT has not been reduced below N log N.** This component narrows the nonunitary bidirectional route using exact identities and scoped necessary conditions. It does not repeat the previously established bound for products of ordinary one-way scans.

The useful outcome is a concrete target: in this representation, a sublogarithmic-stage Fourier factorization must use tridiagonal couplings whose connected blocks grow with N, and permutations with large aggregate movement across the chosen linear ordering. Dense inverses alone are insufficient evidence of either a speedup or an obstruction.

## 1. Linear-cost tridiagonal sandwich

Consider a one-channel bidirectional scan sum written as

\[
A_{ij}=\begin{cases}p_iq_j&i>j,\\d_i&i=j,\\u_iv_j&i<j.\end{cases}
\]

This form evaluates by a weighted strict prefix, a weighted strict suffix, and a diagonal. It is a nondegenerate subclass of order-one quasiseparable matrices. A weighted scan with nonzero recurrence multipliers can be converted to it by accumulating those multipliers. Zero transitions may require separate blocks; the conversion below does not silently divide by zero.

Assume all p_i and v_i are nonzero. Set, for i>=1,

\[
\alpha_i=p_i/p_{i-1},\qquad\beta_i=v_i/v_{i-1},
\]

and let L have unit diagonal and subdiagonal -alpha_i, while R has unit diagonal and superdiagonal -beta_(i+1). Then

\[
\boxed{T=LAR\text{ is tridiagonal},\qquad A=L^{-1}TR^{-1}.}
\]

**Proof.** Left multiplication by L subtracts alpha_i times row i-1 from row i. All entries strictly below the first subdiagonal vanish because p_i-alpha_i p_(i-1)=0. Above the diagonal, each surviving row still has entries proportional to v_j. Right multiplication by R subtracts beta_j times column j-1 from column j, eliminating entries strictly above the first superdiagonal and preserving the lower bandwidth. No principal-minor or numerical-stability assumption is used.

For explicit preparation, put alpha_0=0 and write

\[
\begin{aligned}
m_i&=d_i-\alpha_i u_{i-1}v_i,\\
\ell_i&=p_iq_{i-1}-\alpha_i d_{i-1},\\
T_{ii}&=m_i-\beta_i\ell_i,\\
T_{i,i-1}&=\ell_i,\\
T_{i,i+1}&=(u_i-\alpha_i u_{i-1})v_{i+1}-\beta_{i+1}m_i,
\end{aligned}
\]

with missing boundary terms omitted. These formulas are implemented in `sandwich.py` without constructing dense matrices.

The evaluator first solves the unit upper-bidiagonal system Rz=x by a backward scan, multiplies z by T, and solves Ly=Tz by a forward scan. The precise counts in this implementation, for N>=2, are:

| Work | Field gates |
|---|---:|
| Prepare alpha, beta, and the three diagonals from supplied generators | 17N-19 |
| Apply to one arbitrary fresh input, including all N outputs | 9N-8 |
| Cold preparation plus one application | 26N-27 |

Each scalar add, subtract, multiply, or inverse counts separately. Inversions are of supplied coefficients and are charged. Array preparation, copying, and output writes require O(N) word operations and O(N) storage. These counts are for the specified evaluator, not a claim that it is cheaper than the direct prefix/suffix implementation. Finding generators for a Fourier factorization is not included: no such sublogarithmic-stage construction has been found.

Conversely, for any unit lower/upper bidiagonal L,R and tridiagonal T, the product L^-1 T R^-1 has upper and lower off-diagonal cut rank at most one. This remains true if some bidiagonal coefficients vanish: the triangular recurrences imply the rank-one cut factorizations without division. Thus the sandwich itself is a useful exact parameterization to search.

For an explicit proof, partition at a cut s. Let e be the last coordinate vector of the left block and f the first coordinate vector of the right block. Write the diagonal blocks as L11,L22,R11,R22,T11,T22, and put T12=t e f^T and T21=h f e^T. The bidiagonal cross blocks are L21=-alpha_s f e^T and R12=-beta_s e f^T. Block triangular inversion and multiplication give

\[
\begin{aligned}
A_{12}&=L_{11}^{-1}\bigl[\beta_sT_{11}R_{11}^{-1}e+t e\bigr]f^TR_{22}^{-1},\\
A_{21}&=L_{22}^{-1}f\bigl[\alpha_s e^TL_{11}^{-1}T_{11}+h e^T\bigr]R_{11}^{-1}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Each expression is an outer product. The diagonal blocks of L and R are unit triangular, so their inverses always exist, including when alpha_s, beta_s, t, or h vanishes. No inverse of T or a principal block of T is needed for this converse.

## 2. A new scoped obstruction: bounded tridiagonal components

Suppose an invertible stage has the sandwich form A=L^-1 T R^-1 and every contiguous block-diagonal component of T has size at most b. A cut separates components precisely when both tridiagonal entries across the cut vanish. Invertibility of T implies invertibility of each component.

Its inverse is

\[
A^{-1}=RT^{-1}L.
\]

Each row of T^-1 is confined to one component. Left multiplication by upper-bidiagonal R combines at most two adjacent rows. If these rows lie in the same component, their union covers at most b coordinates. If they lie in adjacent components, their union covers at most 2b consecutive coordinates. Right multiplication by lower-bidiagonal L extends such an interval by at most one position on its left. Consequently

\[
\boxed{\max_i\|A^{-1}_{i,:}\|_0\le 2b+1.}
\]

For a product of t such invertible stages, with arbitrary internal permutations and invertible diagonal factors, row-support multiplication gives

\[
\boxed{\prod_{j=1}^t(2b_j+1)\ge N}
\]

whenever the result is a Fourier matrix, whose inverse has no zero entry. In particular, fixed b requires

\[
t\ge\left\lceil\frac{\log N}{\log(2b+1)}\right\rceil.
\]

If full-length stages each charge Theta(N), bounded-component versions cannot achieve o(N log N). **Scope:** generic irreducible T has b=N and escapes this bound. This is not a theorem excluding all bidirectional scan sums. Nor does it prove an arithmetic lower bound for implementations that skip most positions of a degenerate stage. It isolates long connected tridiagonal couplings as a necessary mechanism for the proposed low-depth route.

## 3. Dense inverse is compatible with very low cut rank

For every invertible matrix A, the upper-right block across any fixed cut has exactly the same rank as the corresponding block of A^-1; the lower-left assertion is analogous. This is a familiar consequence of the nullity theorem, not a new structural theorem claimed here.

An elementary proof does not require invertible diagonal blocks. Partition A=[[A11,A12],[A21,A22]] and A^-1 similarly as B. If A12 z=0, then A(0,z)=(0,A22 z), so B12 A22 z=0. The map z -> A22 z is injective on ker A12 because A is invertible; the inverse construction using B22 supplies a bijection with ker B12. Both blocks have the same number of columns, so their ranks agree.

The audit constructs invertible order-one stages at N=4,8,16,32 whose inverses have all N² entries nonzero and still have rank-one off-diagonal blocks at every cut. The old row-sparsity theorem cannot reject those stages. On the other hand, density of the inverse does not mean that one such stage can represent an arbitrary dense Fourier matrix.

Arbitrary shuffles genuinely break a shared-order rank invariant. Exact examples of A P B P^-1, with A and B individually order-one, have balanced-cut ranks (4,4) at N=8, (8,8) at N=16, and (15,15) at N=32. The terminal inverse permutation is included so the increase is not just an unremoved permutation matrix at the output. These are mechanisms, not Fourier factorizations.

## 4. A permutation movement tradeoff

Let rho_s(M) denote the rank of M[0:s,s:N]. Block multiplication gives

\[
\rho_s(AB)\le\rho_s(A)+\rho_s(B).
\]

For a permutation matrix P, rho_s(P) equals c_s(P), the number of coordinates crossing the cut from one side to the other. If P maps row i to column pi(i), then

\[
\sum_{s=1}^{N-1}c_s(P)=\tfrac12\sum_i|\pi(i)-i|.
\]

For a complex Fourier matrix, every s-by-(N-s) submatrix, even after arbitrary independent endpoint reorderings, has rank at least s(N-s)/N. Indeed its squared Frobenius norm is s(N-s), while its squared operator norm is at most N. This norm proof is over C; we do not assert it unchanged in arbitrary finite characteristic.

If a product of stages with quasiseparable orders r_j and internal permutations P_j equals an endpoint-reordered Fourier matrix, summing the cut-rank inequalities gives

\[
\boxed{
\frac12\sum_j\sum_i|\pi_j(i)-i|
\ge\frac{N^2-1}{6}-(N-1)\sum_jr_j.
}
\]

The bound may be negative and then says nothing. For order-one stages and sublogarithmic depth, it requires Omega(N²) aggregate coordinate travel in the fixed linear ordering. Without any internal permutations, it already forces a linear number of fixed-order stages. This explains why a viable route must use extensive global rearrangements.

**This is not a RAM running-time lower bound.** A permutation with quadratic total travel still costs O(N) on a random-access machine. The result becomes a communication cost only in a model that actually charges travel distance. It is a useful structural certificate, not a substitute for a missing exact Fourier factorization.

## 5. Reproduction and limits

Run `python3 run_audit.py` in this directory. The audit uses two finite fields, exact dyadic cyclotomic fields, full basis vectors, and independent dense matrix identities. It verifies:

- 48 tridiagonal reconstructions, all 228 basis columns, and 144 fresh inputs;
- exact setup and application gate counts on every corresponding case;
- 406 inverse cut-rank equalities, including matrices with singular principal blocks;
- 48 bounded-block inverse-support cases and 96 shuffled products;
- 155 permutation rank/travel identities;
- 176 small Fourier submatrix rank inequalities over exact cyclotomic fields;
- four invalid coefficient inputs and dense-inverse/shuffle escape controls.

The dense matrices are confined to verification. They are not hidden within the O(N) evaluator. Tests certify the stated finite cases; algebraic proofs supply the general identities and the restricted bounds. A fixed finite-field modulus is not an all-length asymptotic NTT construction. No floating-point stability result, bit-complexity theorem, or new priority claim is made.
