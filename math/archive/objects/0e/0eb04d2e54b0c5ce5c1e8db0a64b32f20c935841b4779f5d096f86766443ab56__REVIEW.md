## Proof independent of source programs

### Cross-partition rank

For any coordinate subset A, let d(A) count changes in its indicator along the natural line. Decompose the columns of the strictly lower block S[A^c,A] by maximal runs of A. A run followed by A^c contributes a submatrix crossing the cut after that run, hence rank at most one; a final run contributes nothing below it. Therefore the lower contribution has rank at most the number of A-to-A^c boundaries. The upper contribution analogously costs the number of A^c-to-A boundaries. Their sum is d(A).

This argument uses the quasiseparable cut-rank definition directly, so it covers general rank-one propagation generators, including zero propagation factors, not only a globally separated outer product. The diagonal does not contribute to the cross-partition block.

### Transport and factor replacement

For a row-sampling permutation p, an input-supported set A becomes p^−1(A). Thus the source sets A0,A1,A2,A3 are the correct interfaces. Each permutation is block-preserving between its two explicitly transported partitions. The off-diagonal block of a two-factor product is a sum of two terms, so ranks add. Replacing the stages in J by diagonals makes their crossing contribution zero; the replacement product has selected-block rank at most the sum of d(Aj) over unreplaced stages.

A sequential telescoping replacement changes the full product by a sum of terms, one containing Sj−Dj for each replaced stage. Other factors cannot increase that term's rank. Hence rank(F−M_J)≤sum_{j in J}hj. Restricting to the selected rows and columns gives

rank F[R,C]≤c_J+sum_{j in J}hj.

No stage, generator, diagonal baseline or intermediate product was divided by or assumed invertible. This is why singular/zero branches remain covered. The explicitly transported permutations avoid the previously identified uncharged-shuffle error.

### Fourier rank and exact bounds

The reviewer independently reconstructs all affine permutations from the eight supplied binary columns, transports every parity set, and recomputes all boundary counts. All fifteen saved row/column sets, transported masks and capacities agree exactly.

For each witness the 128-by-128 Fourier determinant is independently computed modulo 12289 at root 8340 and modulo 65537 at root 282. Both roots have exact order 256 and satisfy X^128+1=0. All thirty residues match the saved nonzero values. A zero cyclotomic determinant over C would reduce to zero at either valid prime; thus the complex rank is 128. These are exact algebraic certificates, not numerical rank thresholds.

The resulting bounds are:

- singles: 38, 44, 69, 46;

The disjoint constraints h1+h2≥119, h3≥69, h4≥46 give 234. The displayed feasible relaxation vector verifies that these fifteen inequalities alone cannot imply a larger total.

## Degeneracy, inverse stages and cost distinctions

An additional fresh modular checker generates general order-one lower/upper propagation stages with zero generator values and zero transfer coefficients. It verifies 96 local partition bounds and 360 four-stage replacement/telescoping controls at N4,8,16, with arbitrary permutations and singular diagonal baselines. This strengthens the controls beyond the strictly separated-generator subclass but is supplementary to the proof.

If both S and diagonal D are invertible, S^−1−D^−1=−S^−1(S−D)D^−1 proves equality of the ordinary ranks. The inverse-stage extension must keep both invertibility conditions; the primary theorem itself does not need them. Rank(L+U)≤rank L+rank U is also valid, but the number of quasiseparable generator families is not the ordinary rank of their strict triangular matrices. A single order-one scan can have ordinary diagonal-defect rank N−1 while applying in O(N). Consequently 234 is a representation-specific rank condition, not an arithmetic lower bound.
