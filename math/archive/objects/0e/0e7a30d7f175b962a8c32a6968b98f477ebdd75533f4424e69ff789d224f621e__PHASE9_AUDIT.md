# Hero17 Phase9: independent root-exchange audit

## Decision

**Accept the exact root-exchange formulas, inverse CRT bridge, and commutator-rank theorem, with the stated local scope.** For N=2m and t roots exchanged between the parity cosets, the two child remainders can be produced and inverted in linear field arithmetic for fixed t, including a separately charged linear-size plan. Input coefficients are arbitrary; the root partition is restricted.

This is a substantive refinement of Phase8's matrix-density discussion: dense transfer matrices can have cheap shared low-rank corrections. The complete source evaluator still invokes two ordinary child FFTs and remains O(N log N) for fixed t. Neither literal density nor the new invariant proves an unrestricted FFT lower bound. No historical priority is established.

## Field, sign and interface guards

Assume N=2m≥2, a field K with a supplied primitive Nth root ω, and characteristic not dividing N. The source uses negative-sign evaluation nodes z_j=ω^(−j). The even coset E consists of roots e with e^m=1; the odd coset O has o^m=−1. Distinctness and characteristic different from 2 are indispensable.

Choose t distinct removed even roots e_i and t distinct added odd roots o_i. Let A=(E minus the e_i) union the o_i; B is the complementary half. Every balanced partition is represented this way, but small-t complexity applies only near parity partitions. Exchanging A and B replaces t by m−t, so an adaptive orientation can use min(t,m−t).

For f(z)=L(z)+z^m H(z), with degree L,H<m, let R_A H be the coefficient vector of z^m H modulo P_A(z)=product over a in A of (z−a); define R_B similarly. The local interface outputs a=L+R_A H and b=L+R_B H. These are coefficient remainders, not the complete Fourier outputs.

The executable works in a prime field and assumes the supplied root is primitive. It does not prove primality or exact root order at each API call. The fixture search is explicitly test setup, not a free uniform root-generation algorithm. The mathematical bridge applies to any even N satisfying the field assumptions; the source's full hybrid requires a power-of-two N≥4.

## Exact identities and proof review

### Low-rank corrections and involutions

Let U_A have columns the degree-<m cardinal polynomials ℓ_(A,o_i), and U_B the cardinal polynomials ℓ_(B,e_j). Let V_O,V_E evaluate a coefficient vector at the exchanged odd/even nodes. Interpolation at the retained roots and newcomers gives

    R_A = I − 2 U_A V_O,
    R_B = −I + 2 U_B V_E.

Consequently

    a = L+H − 2 sum H(o_i) ℓ_(A,o_i),
    b = L−H + 2 sum H(e_j) ℓ_(B,e_j).

The Vandermonde evaluation basis diagonalizes each remainder transfer with entries ±1. Therefore R_A²=R_B²=I and rank(R_A−I)=rank(R_B+I)=t. Since the combined 2t evaluation nodes are distinct, their rectangular Vandermonde has rank min(m,2t). The two cardinal maps are individually injective and occupy separate row blocks, proving

    rank([R_A−I ; R_B+I]) = min(m,2t).

This is an exact minimum rank-one-channel count within this particular stacked additive-correction representation. It is not a scalar-operation lower bound on other circuits.

For t=1, the cardinal normalization simplifies to

    a = L+H + (o−e) H(o) (z^m−1)/(z−e),
    b = L−H + (e−o) H(e) (z^m+1)/(z−o).

The quotients have geometric coefficients and admit O(m) generation by recurrence.

### Shared-basis-invariant rank

Phase8's full CRT bridge [[I,R_A],[I,R_B]] is invertible, so R_A−R_B has rank m. For 2t≤m, the images of U_A and U_B intersect trivially: a polynomial in their intersection vanishes at all 2(m−t) retained roots, at least m distinct roots, while its degree is less than m. Thus [U_A U_B] has column rank 2t. The stacked evaluation map has row rank 2t, so R_A+R_B has rank 2t. Swapping A,B proves the remaining case. Finally,

    R_A(R_A+R_B) = I+R_A R_B,
    (R_A+R_B)(R_A−R_B) = −(R_A R_B−R_B R_A).

The multipliers are invertible, giving

    rank(R_A+R_B)
      = rank(I+R_A R_B)
      = rank(R_A R_B−R_B R_A)
      = 2 min(t,m−t).

In particular only t=0 or t=m, the two parity partitions, give commuting transfers. The theorem survives a common invertible basis conjugation. It does not survive arbitrary unrelated basis changes at the separate interfaces, and it does not charge the cost of finding or applying any such changes.

### Correct inverse, including t=0 and t=m

Set U=[U_A U_B], V=[V_O;V_E] and y=(a−b)/2=(I−UV)H. Cardinal evaluations on their own newcomer sets give

    I−VU = [[0,−C], [−D,0]],
    C_ij = ℓ_(B,e_j)(o_i),
    D_ji = ℓ_(A,o_i)(e_j).

Here

    C_ij = P_B(o_i) / ((o_i−e_j) P_B'(e_j)),
    D_ji = P_A(e_j) / ((e_j−o_i) P_A'(o_i)).

All node differences, derivative factors and cross-polynomial values are nonzero. C,D are invertible diagonally scaled Cauchy matrices. Solving the small block system gives

    z_O = −D^(−1) V_E y,
    z_E = −C^(−1) V_O y,
    H = y + U_A z_O + U_B z_E,
    L = a−H + 2 U_A z_O.

This is a valid Woodbury reduction; the order of C,D and the minus signs matter. Empty matrices at t=0 recover the usual half-sum/half-difference. The direct t=m formulation is valid but expensive; adaptive swapping recovers the zero-exchange orientation.

## Costs: what is actually paid

With root ω and exchange labels supplied, the main plan constructs the entire root table, modified coset polynomials, cardinal coefficients, cross-node matrices and two small inverses. It uses O(N+mt+t³) additions/multiplications, O(t²) inversions and O(N+mt+t²) storage. The forward interface costs O(m(t+1)); its inverse costs O(m(t+1)+t²). Each writes N output coefficients. No previous input spectrum is needed.

From the static loops, the exact forward ledger count is

    N + 4Nt + 3t

additions/multiplications. The inverse count is

    3m + 10mt + 4t² + 3t.

The plan uses exactly 2t²+4t+2=2(t+1)² inversions. For t=1, its scalar count is 13N+13, forward count 5N+3, inverse count 13N/2+7, and plan plus forward 18N+16. These independently derived formulas match every saved one-swap row, including N=65,536: 851,981 plan, 327,683 forward, 425,991 inverse and eight plan inversions.

For a full dyadic transform, the source evaluates both ordinary parity cosets with two length-m FFTs and directly repairs the 2t newcomer positions. Thus total work including preparation is O(N log N+tN+t³). For t=1, the source's full addition/multiplication count is

    23N+14 + (3N/2)(log2 N−1),

versus N−1+(3N/2)log2 N for its ordinary radix-2 reference. At N=65,536 these are 2,981,902 and 1,638,399. We independently derive these counts; we do not rerun the uploaded 65,536-point audit or certify wall-clock speed. The saved N=16 same-partition 801-versus-883 predecessor comparison remains an implementation-specific source result, with the optional predecessor dependency noted above. Ordinary radix-2 there is 111.

Several accounting qualifications matter:

- Inversions are separately counted field primitives. Prime/root discovery, integer modular arithmetic, label operations and memory traffic are additional costs. A bounded number of O(log p) inversion steps does not make all finite-field arithmetic bit-constant.
- `plan_accesses` and `online_accesses` exist but are not incremented. The ledger is not a complete memory-access ledger.
- Online counters accumulate across calls on a bridge, despite a comment implying independent call counters. The source audit correctly uses differences where required; consumers must reset or subtract snapshots.
- The unmetered `single_swap_closed_form` reference uses a fresh modular exponentiation for each geometric coefficient. That literal helper is O(m log m) modular multiplications under usual powering, whereas the theorem and charged `ExchangeBridge` admit the asserted linear fixed-t cost.
- The algorithms are exact-field statements. No stable floating-point implementation or precision/error bound is established; Vandermonde/cardinal/Cauchy representations can have conditioning costs.

For fixed t, the partition count is binom(m,t)² among binom(2m,m) balanced partitions. This fraction decays exponentially up to polynomial factors. For unrestricted balanced partitions t is often proportional to m, losing the fixed-t linear plan advantage.

## Independent controls and reproducibility

The new checker forms each R_A,R_B by monic polynomial remainder division, independently of the low-rank formulas it tests. It uses separately authored Gaussian elimination, direct polynomial products/evaluations, explicit cardinal construction and exact finite-field arithmetic.

Passed controls:

- All 1,274 balanced partitions for N=2,4,6,8,10,12 across suitable prime fields: both involutions, both individual ranks, stacked rank, full difference rank, sum rank, product-plus-identity rank and commutator rank.
- 88 full arbitrary-coefficient cases through N=64, including nondyadic N=30 and t=0,1,m/2,m−1,m: direct remainder equality, the scaled-Cauchy formulas, forward and inverse recovery, all child-root evaluations and complete corrected child evaluation outputs.
- The same full cases verify shared-basis conjugation invariance. Wrong inverse signs are independently detected on nondegenerate controls; the exact count appears in the JSON.
- The explicit N=16, GF(17), one-exchange transfer has all 64 entries nonzero in both R_A and R_B despite rank-one corrections.
- Repeated Cauchy row nodes produce rank one instead of two; an imprimitive fourth root produces a rank-two rather than rank-four Fourier evaluation matrix. These controls enforce the domain assumptions.
- The N=16 combinatorial histogram is [1,64,784,3136,4900,3136,784,64,1], totaling 12,870. This is a combinatorial count, not a claim that our checker individually tested all N=16 partitions.
- Independent closed-form scalar counts match all five saved large one-swap rows and the full N=65,536 count comparison.

## Literature and final boundary

The Cauchy inverse and Lagrange relationship is classical. [Thomas Alberts' direct mathematical notes](https://www.math.utah.edu/~alberts/notes/cauchy-determinant-formula/c_det_formula/) derive the inverse and explain its validity over general fields, citing Samuel Schechter's 1959 paper, *On the Inversion of Certain Matrices*. The paper DOI was identified, but its publisher endpoint did not render in this check; we do not claim to have independently inspected that paper here. The local rank formulas above have self-contained proofs. No novelty or priority conclusion follows from this bounded literature check.

Accepted: exact low-rank correction, reflection/commutator invariants, the Cauchy inverse, and fixed-exchange local complexity with preparation paid. Qualified: supplied field/root and restricted partitions, cumulative operation counters, unmetered reference-helper powering, and the optional predecessor comparison. Not established: a faster complete generic-input FFT, an unrestricted circuit lower bound, or a floating-point stability guarantee.
