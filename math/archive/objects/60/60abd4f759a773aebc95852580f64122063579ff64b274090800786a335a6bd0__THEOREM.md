# Phase 31 — optimal cubic Fourier projective windows for five Green operators

**Research date:** 10 October 2026  
**Status:** Exact family-specific algebraic exclusion at N=4 and N=8; sampled exclusion at N=16; certified linear-work *candidate* executor. **No general-input sub-`N log N` Fourier algorithm.**

## 1. Precise factorization model

Let `N` be a power of two, let `F_N=(ζ_N^{jk})` be the unnormalized DFT matrix, and let fixed invertible outer Green sums `A_N,C_N` be those constructed by the seeded, preserved Phase 29 routine. Define

\[
T_N=A_N^{-1}F_NC_N^{-1}.
\]

All entries of `T_N` used here are nonzero in the chosen exact field, and invertibility/nonvanishing lift to characteristic zero by the same good reduction. Let `G_0,...,G_4` be five independently ordered, diagonally gauged, cyclic Hamiltonian Green inverses, with rational cyclic couplings inherited from Phase 29/30 and the new fifth term using `1/13`. Each `G_k x` has an exact `O(N)` field-operation executor after preprocessing.

The middle pencil is

\[
B(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_4)
=G_0+\lambda_1G_1+\cdots+\lambda_4G_4.
\]

We ask whether for any complex couplings, any permutations `P_L,P_R`, and any invertible complex diagonal masks `D_L,D_R`, we can have

\[
F_N=A_ND_LP_L B P_RD_R C_N.
\]

For the obstruction, homogenize **all five** generators with a nonzero projective vector `z=(z_0,...,z_4)`, considering `B(z)=Σ z_k G_k`. Ruling out all projective solutions also rules out the prescribed affine normalization `z_0=1`.

## 2. Rectangular projective geometry — the first improvement

The nonzero rank-one `r×c` matrices form the Segre variety `P^{r-1} × P^{c-1}` in `P^{rc-1}`, with dimension `r+c-2` and codimension `(r-1)(c-1)`.

A projective `(q-1)`-plane spanned by `q` independent generators must intersect the full rank-one variety over an algebraically closed field **when**

\[
q \ge (r-1)(c-1)+1.
\]

If `q≤(r-1)(c-1)`, a generic such projective plane *can* avoid the rank-one variety. This is a criterion for possible algebraic certificates, **not** a lower bound on all algorithms or a proof that every particular generator family avoids it.

For `q=5`, a `3×3` window has codimension 4 and cannot establish projective emptiness; a `3×4` window has codimension 6 and can. A `2×6` window also has codimension 5, but at N=8 would require `P(8,2)P(8,6)=1,128,960` ordered tests, compared with `P(8,3)P(8,4)=564,480` for `3×4`.

The same `3×4` window can handle `q=6` in principle. For `q=7`, `3×5` has codimension 8 and is one economical candidate.

## 3. Seven exact minors and an optimal cubic certificate

Select three ordered source rows `r_0,r_1,r_2`, four ordered source columns `c_0,c_1,c_2,c_3`, and target rows `0,1,2`, columns `0,1,2,3`. Let

\[
E_{ij}(z)=\frac{B(z)_{r_i,c_j}}{T_{ij}}.
\]

If `T=D_LP_L B(z)P_R D_R`, then `E_{ij}=u_i v_j` with all `u_i,v_j` nonzero. Thus all its 2-by-2 minors vanish. The projective test needs only the following **seven** minors, described by `(row-pair;column-pair)`:

```
(0,1 ; 0,1)   (0,1 ; 0,2)   (0,1 ; 0,3)
(0,2 ; 1,2)   (0,2 ; 2,3)
(1,2 ; 0,1)   (1,2 ; 0,3)
```

After clearing nonzero target denominators, each minor is a homogeneous quadratic `Q_l(z)` in five variables. Explicitly, for rows `(i,i')`, columns `(j,j')`, the quadratic is

\[
Q(z)=T_{i,j'}T_{i',j}\,B(z)_{r_i,c_j}B(z)_{r_{i'},c_{j'}}
-T_{i,j}T_{i',j'}\,B(z)_{r_i,c_{j'}}B(z)_{r_{i'},c_j}.
\]

Form 35 homogeneous cubics `z_k Q_l(z)` for `k=0,..,4`, `l=1,..,7`. The vector space of degree-three homogeneous polynomials in five variables has dimension

\[
\binom{5+3-1}{3}=35.
\]

Let `M_(r,c)` be their 35-by-35 coefficient matrix in a fixed monomial basis. If its determinant is nonzero, its rows span **every cubic**, including `z_0^3,...,z_4^3`. Therefore any common zero of all seven quadrics has all five coordinates zero; there is **no nonzero complex projective coupling** at this selected row/column mapping.

Every full permutation restricts to one of the tested ordered mappings, so nonzero determinants for **all** mappings exclude **all** internal permutations and diagonal masks for the fixed outer mixers.

### Minimality at degree three

With only `h` quadrics, multiplying each by five linear variables supplies at most `5h` cubics. Spanning all 35 cubic dimensions requires `h≥7`. **This certificate achieves that lower bound.** This is optimal in the number of quadratic relations *for a degree-three multiplication certificate*, not an information-theoretic lower bound on all possible algebraic proofs.

### Characteristic-zero lifting

The entries are rational functions of rational Green couplings and powers of `ζ_N`. The audit uses prime `p=998244353`, with `N | (p-1)` and a primitive N-th root of unity. The chosen denominators are nonzero under reduction. A nonzero determinant in this finite-field specialization implies that the original cyclotomic-rational determinant is nonzero over `Q(ζ_N)`, hence over `C`. This implication is one-way; a rank-deficient modular matrix is **not** a proof that a complex factorization exists.

## 5. Computation model and no hidden FFT claim

The new `FiveGreenExecutor` performs five independent Hamiltonian cyclic linear recurrences and diagonal passes, consuming `O(N)` finite-field operations per vector for **the seeded candidate middle pencil**. Precomputed cycles and root powers can be prepared in `O(N)` field operations (with modular power inversions and parameter generation counted as setup); the vendored numerical-reference builder may form dense matrices *offline* for the feasibility certificates. No arithmetic is hidden in lookup tables.

**The O(N) middle pencil is provably NOT the DFT factorization for the exhausted fixed families.** The Macaulay certificate itself is an expensive offline impossibility test; it does not reduce Fourier execution cost. The fixed finite-field arithmetic model is not a bound on arbitrary-precision integer multiplication, nor does it establish any new `o(N log N)` general complex DFT algorithm.

## 6. Constructive next experiment

The projective seven-minor determinants define algebraic conditions on the outer Green couplings, inner Green edge weights, and their diagonal gauges. A successful future *exceptional* design would need to make these determinants vanish **consistently across all overlapping windows**, solve the remaining global row/column scale consistency, and then satisfy **both exact Fourier Weyl identities**. A rank-deficient window alone can arise from zero rows or inadmissible projective parameters and must never be treated as a valid FFT.

Seek a **uniform in N** law for genuinely noncommuting, independently ordered Green networks. In addition to exact covariance, count coefficient synthesis, preprocessing, full field/bit complexity, numerical conditioning and data movement.
