# Independent audit: Phase 5 accuracy and bounded-coefficient scope

Audit date: 9 October 2026 UTC. The submission is dated 10 October 2026. Uploaded source was read only as data; no uploaded program was imported or executed. This report changes no master or Library material.

## Verdict

The Gaussian small-ball argument in §5 is mathematically valid for the stated measurement-only, input-independent-sketch model. It establishes a failure-probability obstruction for arbitrary nonlinear/random decoders, rather than merely an expected-error obstruction. A transcript-adaptive linear-measurement extension is possible with explicit restrictions.

The bounded-coefficient statement in §6 is classical and correct when its circuit starts with **Q independent raw scalar inputs**, or when the indicated K-point transforms receive those independent coordinates as their circuit inputs. Two missing scope qualifications matter: invertibility does not erase the constraints of N-to-Q zero padding, and an arbitrary invertible change of the supplied input basis does not preserve the determinant lower bound. The padded problem nevertheless admits a repaired Ω(N log K) bound for explicitly materializing the same unnormalized intermediates, proved below. None of these statements is an unrestricted Fourier arithmetic lower bound.

## 1. Gaussian proof and quantifiers

Use the circular complex Gaussian convention E|x_i|²=1. Conditional on a fixed measurement map A and its observation Ax,

    x = A†Ax + z,   z ~ CN(0, Π_ker A),   d = dim ker A.

The residual is independent of Ax. Input-independent random measurement states are handled by first conditioning on their entire realized state. Independent decoder randomness can also be conditioned on. Thus an arbitrary estimator merely chooses a center for the remaining Gaussian; linearity of the decoder is unnecessary.

For any unitary target U, the conditional covariance UΠ_ker A U* has trace d. Some output coordinate has variance ν≥d/N. A complex Gaussian of variance ν has density at most 1/(πν), so every radius-r disk has probability at most r²/ν, regardless of its center. On ||x||₂≤2√N, the proposed all-bin tolerance is at most r=2C N^(1/2−α). Hence

    P(success) ≤ min{1, 4 C² N^(2−2α)/d + exp[−(3−ln 4)N]}.

The tail follows from ||x||₂² being a sum of N independent unit-mean exponentials. Crucially, one uses the inclusion success ∩ {||x||≤2√N} ⊆ {coordinate error≤r}; one must not condition the residual Gaussian on the norm event. The submitted text has a valid interpretation of this step.

For centered consecutive support, at most two occupied boundary blocks are partial. The displayed conservative bound B≥floor(N/M)−2 is safe. With at most s observations per full block, rank deficiency is at least B(M−s), irrespective of nonzero Gaussian preweights, chirps, or linear dependence among observations. For M=Θ(log N), s≤M−1, this is Ω(N/log N).

The conclusion requires fixed C and fixed α>1/2 as N increases. More generally, this argument forces success to zero whenever C_N²N^(2−2α_N)/d_N→0. A guarantee “for every fixed input, over fresh algorithm randomness” would average to the same high success probability under an independent Gaussian input, contradicting the displayed estimate. Consequently, at each large N there is a deterministic input with small algorithmic success probability. The theorem does not require one seed working simultaneously on all inputs, and does not assert one fixed finite-dimensional input witnesses failure for all N.

The α=1/2 boundary is inconclusive here. Neither the proof nor its stronger form below refutes the audited Phase 4 α<1/2 approximation theorem. That earlier theorem also has an ideal supplied-coefficient/RAM and genuine-randomness contract; the present information argument does not resolve its numerical or bit-complexity questions.

## 2. Warranted measurement extensions

**Adaptive linear queries.** The argument extends beyond input-independent A if each next exact complex-linear query is a measurable function only of prior query answers and independent random coins. Sequential Gaussian conditioning leaves, for almost every transcript, an affine Gaussian with covariance equal to the orthogonal projector onto the common kernel of the realized queries. A varying query direction does not itself reveal extra input information, because its choice is already determined by the transcript. The same holds for transcript-determined stopping.

This needs a transcriptwise rank-deficit bound, for example at most s queries supported on each full block of a fixed partition. Direct access to additional raw-input information, data-dependent query coefficients selected using such information, arbitrary nonlinear measurements, or extra stored input-derived state is outside the extension. An unrestricted adaptive FFT can simply read the entire input. Choosing a new partition invalidates the particular block-count proof unless a separate global query/rank bound is supplied.

**Stronger failure probability.** Let V identify the d-dimensional unknown Fourier subspace isometrically with C^d. All-bin success plus ||x||≤2√N implies

    ||V*(U x − Xhat)||₂ ≤ 2C N^(1−α) = R.

Conditional on the observations, this is an isotropic d-dimensional complex Gaussian lying in a ball centered by the decoder. Maximum density π^(−d) times ball volume π^d R^(2d)/d! yields

    P(success) ≤ min{1, (4C² N^(2−2α))^d/d!
                 + exp[−(3−ln 4)N]}.

For d≥d₀, select any transcript-measurable d₀-dimensional subspace of the residual instead. With d₀=Ω(N/log N), fixed C and fixed α>1/2, the inequality d₀!≥(d₀/e)^d₀ gives **P(success)≤exp(−Ω(N))**. This is a valid strengthening under exactly the same measurement restriction; it is still not an arithmetic-complexity lower bound. The separate probability reviewer was sent this derivation for final cross-check.

## 3. Exact bounded-coefficient gate convention

A precise sufficient model starts from the independent raw coordinate forms and permits a new scalar linear form αu+βv per gate, with |α|,|β|≤1. Coefficients are constants independent of the input; arbitrary nonlinear operations and input-dependent branching are not included. Unlimited retention and fan-out do not invalidate the proof.

Let Δ be the largest absolute determinant formed from any n available linear forms on n independent starting variables. Initially Δ=1. Multilinearity and the triangle inequality show that a gate can increase Δ by at most a factor 2. Thus explicitly producing a square target matrix H needs at least log₂|det H| gates. This derivation fixes the convention without relying on an ambiguous “operation” count.

For H=I_M⊗F_K on Q=MK independent variables,

    |det H| = K^(Q/2),
    gates ≥ (Q/2) log₂ K.

This is the submitted equation (15). It applies to any bounded-coefficient linear circuit that explicitly computes these intermediates; it does not require computing the M transforms separately. Merely calling the channels independent is not a general arithmetic direct-sum theorem.

The original publisher abstract confirms Morgenstern's n/2 log₂n statement at coefficient bound one: [Morgenstern, JACM 20(2), 305–306 (1973)](https://doi.org/10.1145/321752.321761). The publisher full PDF returned HTTP 403, so the abstract was verified and the gate argument above was independently reconstructed. The scale restriction is also explicit in the primary research paper [Ailon (2014)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1403.1307). No claim from the package's modern OpenAI #130 comparison is needed or verified here.

## 4. Two input-domain qualifications and a padded repair

**Previously supplied unnormalized summaries.** Let D=Cx be the complete phase-summary map. Its determinant magnitude is M^(Q/2). If a new circuit starts with the Q values D already available for free and must produce the same intermediate vector Hx, its target matrix is HC^(−1), with

    |det(HC^(−1))| = (K/M)^(Q/2).

Thus the determinant calculation at that cut gives only max{0,(Q/2)log₂(K/M)}, not the submitted (Q/2)log₂K. Invertibility implies independent coordinate values, but does not preserve determinant magnitude. If creating D is charged as part of a whole bounded-coefficient circuit beginning at raw x, the original target determinant bound remains valid for that whole circuit. Do not apply it anew as a remaining-stage cost without specifying the supplied basis.

To distinguish the execution graphs precisely, let B_x apply the M unnormalized K-point transforms to the recovered x columns, so Z_x=B_x x is the intermediate in equation (4). The three maps are:

- Raw x→D=Cx: determinant magnitude M^(Q/2).
- Recovered/raw x→Z_x=B_x x: determinant magnitude K^(Q/2).
- Supplied D→Z_x=B_x C^(−1)D: determinant magnitude (K/M)^(Q/2).

A different stage that transforms the phase summaries themselves has Z_D=B_D D, where B_D applies one K-point DFT for each phase r. The supplied D→Z_D map has determinant magnitude K^(Q/2), while raw x→Z_D=B_D Cx has determinant magnitude (KM)^(Q/2). These are different targets. No determinant-ratio reduction applies to the D→Z_D stage merely because its inputs are called summaries; the ratio above concerns the recovered-x intermediates Z_x. These equalities assume the unit-modulus phases specified in §1 and no additional nonunit preweight.

**Zero padding.** Phase 4 embeds N arbitrary coordinates into Q≈4N, so the padded Q-vector ranges over only an N-dimensional subspace. Invertible phase transformations preserve that dimension. Equation (15), interpreted literally as a Q-independent-input determinant argument, is therefore not established for these restricted padded inputs.

A repair preserves the asymptotic obstruction for the specified unweighted intermediates. Write E for the **isometric** zero-padding/centering injection and H=(I_M⊗F_K)PE for the Q×N intermediate map, where P is the required permutation. Unit-modulus input gauges can be included in E. Then H*H=K I_N. Nonunit Gaussian preweights are not included in this identity. Cauchy–Binet gives

    Σ_{J⊆{1,…,Q}, |J|=N} |det H_J|² = K^N.

Some N-row minor therefore has magnitude at least K^(N/2)/sqrt(binomial(Q,N)). A circuit starting at the raw N inputs and explicitly computing all Q intermediate values consequently needs

    gates ≥ max{0, (N/2)log₂K − (1/2)log₂ binomial(Q,N)}
          ≥ max{0, (N/2)log₂K − Q/2}.

For Q=Θ(N) and K→∞ this is Ω(N log K), and for K=Θ(N/log N) it is Ω(N log N). This is a defensible unweighted padded-instance replacement for (15), with different constants and the same strict architecture/model qualifications. If the requested intermediate instead includes a fixed nonunit input weight G=diag(g_j), then (HG)*(HG)=K G*G and the Cauchy–Binet determinant is K^N product_j |g_j|². The lower bound correspondingly adds Σ_j log₂|g_j| and must be assessed for those actual weights. It must not silently inherit the isometric formula.

## 5. Limits and relationship to previous audits

The determinant potential is uninformative on the corresponding normalized unitary intermediate map, whose full determinant has magnitude one. This does not prove normalized transforms easy; it means this proof does not address them. Free gain factors, arbitrary coefficients, or avoidance of the named unnormalized intermediates likewise prevent the stated inference. Coefficient-generation costs, supplied exact transcendental constants, finite precision and bit costs require separate models.

The earlier correlated-scan audit proves an exact restricted commutator-rank obstruction on a correlated forcing family. It does not turn high rank into a general running-time lower bound, and its model is distinct from the present Gaussian information loss. The earlier all-length Gaussian audit supplies the positive α<1/2 result with its own quantifiers; no conflict arises.

The exact complete-bank Cooley–Tukey collapse predates this package in additions/1717/astra_exact_bridge. The new implementation may be useful regression evidence, but the identity should not be presented as a new Fourier decomposition. Neither scan elimination, the local full-rank transfer gate, the Gaussian obstruction, nor the determinant gate excludes a different global circuit.
