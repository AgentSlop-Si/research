# Exact FFT operator compression: a falsifiable research audit

**Date:** 2026-10-09  
**Original target:** A uniform, single-threaded, *fresh arbitrary-input*, *full-output*, **exact** complex DFT in `O(N)` properly charged arithmetic/word operations.  
**Status:** The candidate representation is exact and compact, but does **not** yield an `O(N)` evaluator. Two rigorous model-specific barriers and an exact modular verifier explain why. This is not a new lower bound; all substantive mathematics is established (Vandermonde structure, Morgenstern, Ailon). No assertion of a universal FFT barrier or a practical breakthrough is warranted.

## 1. Why we tested this and what is different from earlier phases

The canonical `FFT_RESEARCH_CANONICAL_CHECKPOINT_2026-10-09.md` excluded rediscovering collision memoization, binary leaf tables, selective outputs, prepared input updates, WHT full tables, short recurrence filters, 2-round cancellation searches, and published exact-DFT/CrocSwap finite-network retuning. The preceding `fft_multilevel_quantized_tabulation/NEXT_EXACT_CHALLENGE.md` asked for **an exact compact mathematical representation spanning arbitrarily many FFT layers** with honestly charged recovery of all outputs. We therefore focused on a *different* object: the complete Fourier **linear operator** and its compressibility, not the values of individual inputs.

We tested an exact rank-one displacement (positive compactness), Fourier submatrix ranks (negative standard off-diagonal compression), and normalized-transform entropy (negative small-unitary-gate evaluation). Independently, a simple volume/determinant argument rules out hiding all NlogN butterflies as O(N) constant-volume two-register operations on the unnormalized transform. None of this relies on caching, finite precision, multithreading, or smaller output scope.

## 2. Positive result: full Fourier matrix has displacement rank ONE

Let `N>=2`, `omega` an exact primitive Nth root of unity, and

`F[j,k] = omega**(j*k)`, for `0<=j,k<N`.

Define `Z` as the nilpotent downshift matrix, `Z[j,j-1]=1` for `j>=1`, and

`D_inv = diag(1,omega**(-1),...,omega**(-(N-1)))`.

Then, entrywise,

`(Z F)[j,k] = F[j-1,k] = omega**((j-1)*k)` for `j>=1`,

`(F D_inv)[j,k] = omega**(j*k)*omega**(-k) = omega**((j-1)*k)` for `j>=1`.

For `j=0`, the former is zero and the latter is `omega**(-k)`. Hence

`Z F - F D_inv = -e_0 v^T`, where `v[k]=omega**(-k)`.

**Exactly one nonzero outer product describes the operator's displacement.** `Z` and `D_inv` have only O(N) nonzero scalar data. In fact, `N` and `omega` are already a constant-description mathematical specification of the full N-by-N matrix. This is **classical displacement structure of Vandermonde matrices**, not a newly discovered FFT factorization.

### Why compression does not prove fast evaluation

The associated generating function of the FFT outputs is a rational function with only N terms:

`G_x(z) = sum_(k=0)^(N-1) x[k]/(1-omega**k*z)`.

The coefficient of `z**j` is precisely `(F x)[j]` for every `j>=0`, and the first N coefficients are the requested transform. `G_x` has an O(N)-size description, yet direct simultaneous coefficient extraction by updating all pole residues performs `N(N-1)` field multiplications and `N(N-1)` additions. An FFT computes the same coefficients in O(NlogN) field arithmetic, but this compact rational encoding **by itself supplies no better evaluator**. The verifier checks both algorithms exactly modulo the prime 998244353. This is a demonstration, **not** a lower bound on *all* algorithms for rational-function coefficient extraction.

## 3. Why ordinary hierarchical exact low-rank blocks fail

A natural attempt is to compress each large off-diagonal submatrix of F into `U V^T` with constant rank `r`, giving nearly linear application in a hierarchical matrix-vector multiply.

There is a sharp obstruction for **contiguous row blocks** in the ordinary index order. Consider `r` consecutive rows `a,a+1,...,a+r-1` and `c` arbitrary, distinct columns `k_1,...,k_c`. Put `t_j=omega**k_j`, all distinct. The submatrix is

`F[a+i,k_j] = t_j**a * t_j**i`.

For `c>=r`, pick any r distinct columns. Its determinant equals

`(prod_j t_j**a) * (prod_(i<j) (t_j-t_i))`,

which is nonzero over C or a field with a primitive root of order N. Therefore the original r-by-c block has **rank r**. For `c<r`, choose any c consecutive rows to obtain rank c. In general,

`rank(F[consecutive rows, distinct columns]) = min(r,c)`.

In particular, both N/2-by-N/2 off-diagonal quadrants of F are **full-rank N/2** in natural contiguous order, and their exact rank does not decrease as N grows. This rules out bounded-exact-rank approximation of these blocks, **not** every permutation, algebraic displacement representation, low-rank approximation, or butterfly architecture.

## 4. Volume lower bound (unnormalized FFT, restricted gate model)

The unnormalized Fourier matrix has `F F* = N I`; therefore

`abs(det(F)) = N**(N/2) = 2**((N/2) log2(N))`.

Start with N registers containing the N inputs. Allow permutations and any **in-place two-register invertible linear gate** whose local 2-by-2 determinant has modulus at most 2; count every such gate. Gates act by left-multiplication, so after G gates,

`abs(det(total linear transformation)) <= 2**G`.

To realize F, then, `G >= (N/2) log2(N)`.

An unnormalized radix-2 FFT uses exactly `(N/2)log2(N)` butterflies of the form `[[1,w],[1,-w]]`; their determinant has modulus 2, so this elementary in-place lower bound is **tight**. Input/output reordering contributes only determinant modulus one.

This is a deliberately simple **restricted special case of the classical Morgenstern (1973) bounded-coefficient lower-bound phenomenon**, not a new all-circuits proof. Morgenstern's actual result covers a wider bounded-coefficient linear-circuit model than these in-place gates; we must not misstate our narrower proof as the published general theorem.

Critically, a *normalized* DFT `F/sqrt(N)` has determinant modulus one; **this volume argument is vacuous there**. Nor does the argument apply if a gate may multiply an entry by an arbitrarily large scalar at unit cost, since its determinant can be arbitrarily large. In particular it cannot refute OpenAI #130's exact-unbounded-coefficient model.

## 5. Entropy lower bound (normalized FFT, unitary two-register gates)

Normalize `U=F/sqrt(N)` so U is unitary. Any unitary state matrix `M` has a nonnegative entropy potential

`H(M) = - sum_(i,j) abs(M[i,j])**2 * log2(abs(M[i,j])**2)`, using `0 log 0 = 0`.

For the identity, `H(I)=0`. For U all N^2 entries have squared modulus 1/N, so `H(U)=N log2(N)`.

Suppose an *arbitrary* unitary 2-by-2 gate changes two rows a and b of M. For each column j, let `s_j=|M[a,j]|^2+|M[b,j]|^2`. Unitarity conserves s_j. Conditional on this mass, the two-point Shannon entropy lies between zero and one bit, so the increase in the corresponding pair's contribution is at most s_j. Summing over columns,

`Delta H <= sum_j s_j = ||row a||_2^2 + ||row b||_2^2 = 2`.

Hence **G >= N log2(N)/2 unitary 2-register gates** for any in-place unitary algorithm for the normalized Fourier matrix. This is exactly attained by the standard normalized radix-2 FFT: stage t makes each row have 2^t disjoint active input coordinates, each of squared modulus 2^-t; it increases total entropy by N bits using N/2 butterflies. Our verifier tracks these supports **exactly using Python integer bitsets**, without a floating-point entropy approximation.

The same argument extends to arbitrary unitary b-register macros: one gate increases entropy by at most `b log2(b)` bits. Consequently G>=`N log2(N)/(b log2(b))` such macros. **But this is only a fair computational improvement if the macro itself can be implemented at its advertised cost.** Charging its decomposition into bounded two-register unitary gates brings back the original work.

This is a known model-specific entropy bound related to Nir Ailon's 2013 work, not a new FFT lower bound. It does **not** apply to nonunitary or ill-conditioned intermediate transformations or to arbitrary extra operations. Ailon's later work establishes other more general well-conditioned-model barriers, under explicit assumptions.

## 6. Direct falsification of free stage fusion

Let `N=2^L`. Merge h consecutive radix-2 stages into each b=2^h-component macro (for h dividing L). Then

- calls = `(L/h)*(N/b)` macros;
- each macro has determinant modulus `b**(b/2)` (unnormalized) and needs at least `b*h/2` primitive det<=2 gates;
- total charged primitive gates = `(L/h)*(N/b)*(b*h/2) = N*L/2`.

At N=4096 (L=12), fusing all stages into **one macro call** appears to cost O(1), but that macro must conceal at least 24,576 bounded-volume butterfly-equivalent gates. Counting it as one ordinary gate changes the model, not the mathematics. In normalized/unitary language the same obstruction follows from entropy.

This is a model-specific reason the preceding bundled-stage lookup experiment cannot be promoted to exact O(N) by relabeling a growing FFT block as a constant-cost operation. It leaves open algorithms that legitimately escape the gate model.

## 8. Acceptance rules: what remains a legitimate research target?

**Disproved for the stated architecture, not universally:**

- merely storing F in displacement-rank-one form does not itself provide an O(N) exact dense evaluator;
- bounded-exact-rank compression of large **natural contiguous-row** Fourier blocks;
- O(N) total **in-place two-register bounded-volume linear gates** for unnormalized F;
- O(N) total **in-place two-register unitary gates** for normalized F;
- free-large-macro reinterpretations of already-accounted butterfly work.

**Still open in this research project:** a fresh arbitrary-input, all-output exact DFT using a precise and sufficiently powerful uniform model with an actual O(N) cost. Candidate must show a *different mechanism*, not just a new encoding: e.g. a non-unitary/global algebraic structure, charged evaluation and recovery, controlled preparation, and a proof overcoming all relevant potential-function restrictions. Note that well-conditioned nonunitary models also have known superlinear restrictions, and exact unbounded coefficients and numerical stability are different questions.

An honest next hypothesis should specify (i) permitted nonunitary/global operations, (ii) why its work per operator is smaller without hiding a Fourier transform, (iii) the complete input-to-N-outputs algorithm, and (iv) a uniform recurrence/operation bound. A linear-size description or improved finite benchmark alone is insufficient.
