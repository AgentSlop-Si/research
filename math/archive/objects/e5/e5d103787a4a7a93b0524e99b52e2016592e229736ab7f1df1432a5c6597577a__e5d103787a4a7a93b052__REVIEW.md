# Independent review: twisted displacement obstruction

Date: 9 October 2026.

## Verdict

**Approved for the explicitly stated restricted factor grammar.** The phase factor, affine-conjugation rank budget, polynomial rank estimate, and resulting exact logarithmic stage obstruction are valid. No conditioning or factor unitarity hypothesis is needed. This is not an unrestricted Fourier arithmetic lower bound, and no publication-priority claim has been reviewed.

The author's clarified grammar is

\[
M=S_t\cdots S_1,\qquad
S_j=\sum_{\ell=1}^{q_j}c_{j\ell}D^{a_{j\ell}}P_{j\ell}B_{j\ell}^{-1}P_{j\ell}^{-1}D^{b_{j\ell}},
\]

where every invertible denominator is a constant-coefficient path tridiagonal matrix \(B=\alpha I+\beta L+\gamma U\), every \(P\) is an affine permutation \(e_k\mapsto e_{v+uk}\) with \(\gcd(u,N)=1\), and the displayed diagonal powers have integer exponents. Independent affine **conjugations** are allowed for all summands. This grammar does not include arbitrary path permutations, arbitrary diagonal masks, independent left/right affine permutations, or otherwise uncharged affine-multiplier wiring.

## 1. Conventions and exact phase

Take \(Ze_k=e_{k+1}\), \(De_k=\omega^k e_k\), and \(F_{jk}=\omega^{jk}\), with indices modulo \(N\) and a primitive \(N\)-th root \(\omega\). Fourier normalization by a nonzero scalar makes no difference. Define

\[
\mathcal T(A)=Z^{-1}AZ,\qquad W=Z^{-1}D.
\]

Then \(FZ=DF\), \(DZ^{-1}=\omega^{-1}Z^{-1}D\), and

\[
W^k=\omega^{-k(k-1)/2}Z^{-k}D^k,
\qquad
\mathcal T^k(F)=\omega^{k(k-1)/2}W^kF.
\]

The sign in the proposed draft is correct. The exponent \(k(k-1)/2\) is an integer, not a division operation in the coefficient field.

## 2. Path and affine-path budgets

A path denominator can be written

\[
B=\alpha I+\beta Z+\gamma Z^{-1}
-\beta e_0e_{N-1}^{\mathsf T}-\gamma e_{N-1}e_0^{\mathsf T}.
\]

Direct multiplication gives \(\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal T(B)-B)\le2\). For invertible \(B\),

\[
\mathcal T(B^{-1})-B^{-1}
=-\mathcal T(B)^{-1}(\mathcal T(B)-B)B^{-1},
\]

so inversion preserves that displacement rank exactly.

For an affine \(P\), \(PZP^{-1}=Z^u\). Therefore \(PBP^{-1}=C-R\), where \(C=\alpha I+\beta Z^u+\gamma Z^{-u}\) commutes with \(Z\), and \(R\) has rank at most two. It follows that

\[
\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal T(PBP^{-1})-PBP^{-1})
\le\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal T(R))+\operatorname{rank}(R)\le4.
\]

Inversion gives the same bound for \(PB^{-1}P^{-1}\). Thus use \(\rho=2\) in the un-reordered common path frame, or \(\rho=4\) for arbitrary independent affine-conjugated paths. More generally one may charge the actual rank of each factor.

The qualification is material: at \(N=9\), the bare affine multiplier \(P e_j=e_{2j}\) has \(\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal T(P)-P)=8\). It cannot be inserted for free using the rank-four claim. The factorization grammar must be stated, rather than shortened to “arbitrary affine factors.”

## 3. Twisted products and branching

For \(\Delta_\lambda(A)=\mathcal T(A)-\lambda A\),

\[
\Delta_{\lambda\mu}(AB)
=\mathcal T(A)\Delta_\mu(B)+\mu\Delta_\lambda(A)B.
\]

Hence the ranks add under products. Also \(\mathcal T(D^a)=\omega^aD^a\), so

\[
\operatorname{rank}\Delta_{\omega^{a+b}}(cD^aHD^b)
\le\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal T(H)-H).
\]

Expand the product of sums into \(b=\prod_jq_j\) branches \(A_\nu\). Every branch has a phase \(\lambda_\nu=\omega^{\sum_j(a_j+b_j)}\) and

\[
r_\nu:=\operatorname{rank}\Delta_{\lambda_\nu}(A_\nu)\le\rho t.
\]

Zero or redundant branches can be deleted, improving the bound. No assertion that the individual stage sums have low ordinary commutator rank is needed or generally valid.

## 4. Polynomial upper bound

Let \(\Lambda\) be the set of distinct surviving branch phases, \(d=|\Lambda|\le b\), and

\[
p(x)=\prod_{\lambda\in\Lambda}(x-\lambda).
\]

For a branch \(A_\nu\), write \(p(x)=(x-\lambda_\nu)q_\nu(x)\). The quotient has degree \(d-1\), and thus at most \(d\) monomials. Since every \(\mathcal T^k\) preserves matrix rank,

\[
\operatorname{rank}p(\mathcal T)A_\nu
=\operatorname{rank}q_\nu(\mathcal T)\Delta_{\lambda_\nu}(A_\nu)
\le d r_\nu.
\]

Summing gives

\[
\operatorname{rank}p(\mathcal T)M\le d\sum_\nu r_\nu\le d b\rho t.
\]

Repeated phase values require only one root of \(p\), not root multiplicity. Coincident or canceled coefficients of a quotient only improve its monomial-count bound.

## 5. Fourier lower bound and field issues

Writing \(p(x)=\sum_{k=0}^d p_kx^k\), define

\[
h(x)=\sum_{k=0}^d p_k\omega^{k(k-1)/2}x^k.
\]

The phase coefficients are nonzero, so \(\deg h=d\). The identity in section 1 gives

\[
p(\mathcal T)F=h(W)F.
\]

The proposed simple-spectrum proof works: \(W\) is a weighted cyclic shift and

\[
W^N=\omega^{-N(N-1)/2}I=(-1)^{N-1}I.
\]

With a primitive \(N\)-th root in a field, the characteristic does not divide \(N\), and the polynomial on the right has distinct roots in a splitting field. Rank is unchanged by field extension. Thus a nonzero degree-\(d\) polynomial kills at most \(d\) eigenvectors.

A cleaner proof needs no spectral extension: \(e_0,W e_0,\ldots,W^{N-1}e_0\) is a basis. Consequently \(W\) is cyclic with minimal polynomial \(m(x)=x^N-\omega^{-N(N-1)/2}\). In the cyclic module \(K[x]/(m)\), the nullity of multiplication by \(h\) is \(\deg\gcd(h,m)\le d\). Therefore

\[
\operatorname{rank}p(\mathcal T)F\ge N-d.
\]

This cyclic proof is valid over the base field and also explains why ordinary primitive-root Fourier assumptions are sufficient at composite lengths. Over a nonfield ring, the argument cannot simply be reused with an unspecified notion of rank. Over characteristic dividing \(N\), a primitive \(N\)-th root cannot exist in a field, and the usual invertible Fourier premise fails. Normalized Fourier also requires its scalar normalizer to exist and be nonzero; unnormalized Fourier avoids that extra convention.

## 6. Consequence and sharper one-phase case

If the restricted architecture equals Fourier exactly, then

\[
N\le d\left(1+\sum_\nu r_\nu\right)
\le d(1+\rho tb)
\le b+\rho tb^2.
\]

For \(q_j\le q\), \(b\le q^t\). Fixed \(q\ge2\) and fixed \(\rho\) therefore imply \(t=\Omega(\log N)\). For \(q=1\), the conclusion is stronger: \(t\ge(N-1)/\rho\).

When all branches have the same phase and \(N\) is even, \(\lambda^N=1\) but \(W^N=-I\), so \(W-\lambda I\) is invertible. The exact necessary bound strengthens to \(N\le\sum_\nu r_\nu\le\rho tb\). For odd \(N\), the corresponding Fourier displacement has rank exactly \(N-1\).

These are stage/branch restrictions. An \(\Omega(N\log N)\) scalar-work claim needs an independently justified per-stage cost under the full shared-state computational model. The result supplies neither an approximation floor nor a bound for arbitrary nonstationary, arbitrary-diagonal, or arbitrary-permutation Green circuits.

## 7. Independent finite checks

The newly written `independent_checks.py` imports no uploaded research implementation and executes none. It uses exact arithmetic in GF(2521) at

`N = 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 12, 14, 18, 20`.

All checks passed:

- Fourier projective phase identity through powers beyond one full cycle.
- The weighted-shift scalar-cycle identity.
- Every subset of phase roots at each \(N\le8\), and representative increasing subsets at larger lengths, satisfies the polynomial Fourier rank lower bound.
- Every affine conjugation at every tested length satisfies the inverse displacement budget; rank four is attained for suitable lengths and slopes.
- Fresh three-stage, eight-branch masked affine-Green products obey the individual twisted budgets and distribute exactly to their branch expansion.
- A bare affine-multiplier permutation provides the scope counterexample above.

The large branch upper bounds can be vacuous at these small lengths; the proof, rather than those finite checks, establishes the general upper bound. Results are recorded in `results.json`.

## 8. Ancillary inverse-row sharpness check

The independent Phase20 review's stronger inverse-row floor \(\|M-F\|_2\ge\sqrt{1-s/N}\) is sharp when the only restriction is one inverse row with support at most \(s\), for \(1\le s\le N\).

For normalized unitary Fourier, fix row index \(i\), a coordinate set \(S\) of size \(s\), and let \(u\) be row \(i\) of \(F^*\) restricted to \(S\). Set \(\beta=\sqrt{s/N}\), \(b=u/\beta^2\), \(r=e_i^{\mathsf T}-bF\), and

\[
M=F+\frac{b^*r}{\|b\|_2^2}.
\]

Then \(bM=e_i^{\mathsf T}\), \(\|M-F\|_2=\sqrt{1-\beta^2}\), and the determinant lemma gives \(\det M/\det F=\beta^2>0\). Thus \(M\) is invertible and its \(i\)-th inverse row is exactly \(b\), supported on \(S\). This proves the stated one-row sharpness. It does not establish sharpness when every inverse row or an entire Green factorization must satisfy additional restrictions.
