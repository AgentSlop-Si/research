# Independent review: N=13 four-by-four two-Green coefficient gate

Date: 2026-10-10 UTC. Verdict: the division-free gate and its polynomial extension to singular endpoint masks are valid. The exact N=13 determinant certificate is independently reproduced and proves the stated complex fixed-order exclusion. This is not a general paired-Green impossibility theorem.

## 1. Correct inverse identity and support bookkeeping

For individually invertible branch maps T0,T1 and their inverses Q0,Q1, the identity B=T0+T1 gives

Q0 B Q1=Q0+Q1.

The orientation is essential and correct. Target B need not itself be invertible for this identity.

For known monomial endpoint permutations, each inverse is supported on two known permutation patterns. Describe row i of Q0 by coefficients (a_i,b_i) at positions r0(i),r1(i); describe column j of Q1 by (d_j,e_j) at c0(j),c1(j). Structural supports of Q0+Q1 must retain all potential positions, including edges whose current weights vanish.

For j outside the allowed right-hand support of row i, one gets the scalar equation

(a_i,b_i) M_ij (d_j,e_j)^T=0,

where M_ij is the corresponding two-by-two rectangle of B. For two distinct rows i,k and a column off both right-hand supports, both resulting row vectors annihilate the same nonzero vector (d_j,e_j). It is nonzero because column j of the invertible Q1 is nonzero. Hence the determinant of those two row vectors vanishes.

If M_ij=[[A,C],[B,D]] and M_kj=[[E,G],[F,H]], the coefficients of (a_i a_k,b_i a_k,a_i b_k,b_i b_k) are exactly

AG-CE, BG-DE, AH-CF, BH-DF.

No division by an edge weight, B entry, or two-by-two minor occurs. This retains zero-edge and singular-intermediate-rectangle branches.

## 2. Why the four-by-four determinant must vanish

Stack four eligible-column equations into Rz=0, where z=(a_i a_k,b_i a_k,a_i b_k,b_i b_k)^T. Every row of Q0 is nonzero, so both coefficient vectors are nonzero and their tensor z is nonzero. Therefore det R=0 is necessary.

Using all eligible columns gives a matrix with four columns and rank at most three. If it has rank three, its unique nonzero kernel direction must additionally obey z0 z3=z1 z2, the two-by-two rank-one condition. Rank three alone does not make that condition automatic. The reviewer included an algebraic countercontrol with rank-three kernel (1,0,0,1), which is not decomposable.

These remain local necessary conditions. They do not enforce all shared denominator coefficients, supported entries of the inverse identity, or the original Green guards. A vanishing determinant is not a construction.

## 3. Singular endpoints are genuinely included

For fixed valid Green denominators, replace every zero endpoint diagonal scale by a formal epsilon, leaving nonzero scales and endpoint permutations fixed. Over E(epsilon), every endpoint is invertible, so the preceding gate applies. Each resulting branch T_s(epsilon) remains regular at epsilon=0 because its Green denominator has not changed. Thus B(epsilon) is regular there.

The determinant is a polynomial in B's entries alone. Its identity over E(epsilon) can be evaluated at zero, proving the same determinant condition for the original singular endpoint masks. This works over finite fields; it does not rely on infinitely many numerical choices of epsilon.

Zero edge weights already belong to the original argument and require no perturbation. A singular Green denominator itself remains invalid: a zero endpoint does not define an inverse that does not exist. These are the correct boundaries of the continuation argument.

## 6. Why the complex exclusion follows exactly

At a primitive complex thirteenth root zeta, the target entries lie in Q(zeta), with the explicit denominator 1+c. Each gate coefficient is quadratic in target entries, and the four-by-four determinant can be placed over denominator (1+c)^8.

The map zeta ->56909 is a valid reduction of the cyclotomic integer ring because the residue has exact order 13 and hence satisfies Phi_13. The denominator reduces to 41758, so localization is valid. A zero characteristic-zero determinant would reduce to zero. Its nonzero residue 60131 therefore proves nonzero determinant over Q(zeta), and hence over C.

Other primitive complex thirteenth roots are Galois conjugates, and their denominators are also nonzero by the already proved complex chirp guard. The exclusion therefore holds for each primitive complex root, not just one formal label. The unknown weights and masks may be arbitrary complex numbers; they are not being restricted to the finite test field.
