# Phase 35 — Fourier seed balancing and an exact no-go for weak Green mixers

**Date:** 2026-10-10  
**Status:** Characteristic-zero algebraic theorems, explicitly reconstructed integer-polynomial certificates, exact finite-field implementations. **Not a sub-`N log N` Fourier algorithm.**

We continue from Phase 34's rank-distant, noncommuting product of Green *sums*, without returning to low-rank-near cycles, ordinary FFT caching, or unitary butterfly fusion. The new invariant is about the **first Fourier row, its first column, and the all-ones vector**. Unlike sampled Weyl defects, it tests *all* possible single intermediate diagonal masks and permutations simultaneously.

Throughout this document `F` is the **unnormalized** length-`N` Fourier matrix, `F[j,k] = omega^(jk)`. Write `u=(1,...,1)^T`, `e0=(1,0,...,0)^T`. The only Fourier facts needed for our main theorem are

`e0^T F = u^T`, `F e0 = u`, and `F u = N e0`.

The balancing invariant itself is unchanged if `F` is scaled to a unitary DFT.

## Theorem 35A — seed-balancing, necessary **and** sufficient

Let `A` be any invertible `N x N` complex matrix. The following are equivalent:

1. There exist an invertible diagonal matrix `D` and an invertible **bi-regular** matrix `B` (i.e. `B u=b u` and `u^T B=b u^T` for the same nonzero `b`) such that `F=A D B`.
2. All entries `A[0,i]` are nonzero and

   `A ( 1 / A[0,*] ) = N e0`, where reciprocation is **entrywise**.
3. All entries `A[0,i]` are nonzero and

   `A[0,i] * (A^-1)[i,0] = 1/N` for **every** `i`.
4. The rank-one idempotent `A^-1 e0 e0^T A` has constant diagonal `1/N`.

**Proof (necessity).** Let `F=A D B` with diagonal entries `d_i`. Since `e0^T F=u^T` and `u^T B^-1 = (1/b)u^T`, we have

`A[0,i] d_i = 1/b` for all `i`. In particular all `A[0,i]` are nonzero and `d_i=1/(b*A[0,i])`. Now use `F u=N e0` and `B u=b u`:

`N e0 = A D B u = b A D u = A(1/A[0,*])`.

Inverting `A` gives the pointwise identity in (3), and its left side is exactly the `i`-th diagonal of the rank-one idempotent in (4).

**Proof (sufficiency).** If (2) holds, set `D=diag(1/A[0,*])` and **construct**

`B = D^-1 A^-1 F`.

Then `A D B=F`. Also,

`B u = D^-1 A^-1 (N e0) = D^-1 (1/A[0,*])=u`, and

`u^T B = A[0,*] A^-1 F=e0^T F=u^T`.

Thus `B` is invertible and bi-regular with `b=1`. □

The sufficiency construction may produce a **dense expensive** `B`; it does not promise a fast algorithm.

**Unitary special case.** When `A` is unitary, `(A^-1)[i,0]=conj(A[0,i])`; hence the condition is exactly `|A[0,i]|=1/sqrt(N)` for every `i`. A unitary left stage requires a **flat entire first row**, even if its other rows mix globally. This statement concerns existence of an invertible regular `B` without promising that `B` is unitary or fast.

### Intermediate arbitrary permutation

The necessity remains valid for `F=A D P B` with any permutation matrix `P`, since `P u=u` and `u^T P=u^T`. The sufficiency statement needs only `P=I`.

### All-size one-Green consequence

For `A=(I-alpha W)^-1`, where `W` is any Hamiltonian permutation, `alpha!=0` and `alpha^N!=1`, its first row has no zeros, but `A^-1 e0=(I-alpha W)e0` is supported on at most two entries. This contradicts (3) for **every `N>2`**, regardless of the invertible bi-regular right factor, middle monomial gate, or complex coupling. For `alpha=0`, `A=I` has zeros in its first row and is excluded separately.

## Theorem 35B — a universal one-mask barrier near the identity

Suppose `A=c(I+E)` is invertible, `c!=0`, and `||E||_2 <= delta < 1`. Put `R=(I+E)^-1`. Then

`A[0,0] * (A^-1)[0,0] = (1+E[0,0]) R[0,0]`

and the row-zero entry of `(I+E)R=I` gives

`(1+E[0,0])R[0,0]-1 = -sum_{j!=0} E[0,j] R[j,0]`.

Since `R e0-e0 = -R E e0`, its nonzero-index part has Euclidean norm at most `delta/(1-delta)`. Therefore Cauchy–Schwarz proves

**`|A[0,0]*(A^-1)[0,0] - 1| <= delta^2/(1-delta)`**.  (35.1)

If `delta < 1/2`, the right side is strictly less than `1/2`. Thus the modulus of the product exceeds `1/2`, contradicting the required value `1/N <= 1/2` at every `N>=2`.

**Consequence:** no `F=A D P B` is possible with a bi-regular `B` and an arbitrary middle monomial gate if `A` is a scalar multiple of a matrix within spectral distance `<1/2` of identity.

### Every positive weighted sum of weak cyclic Green solves is blocked

Let

`A = sum_s c_s (I-alpha_s W_s)^-1`, with `c_s>0`, `W_s` arbitrary permutation matrices and `|alpha_s| <= rho <1/3`.

The operator Neumann series gives

`||(I-alpha_s W_s)^-1-I||_2 <= |alpha_s|/(1-|alpha_s|)`.

After dividing by `c=sum c_s`, the perturbation `E=A/c-I` satisfies

`||E||_2 <= rho/(1-rho) <1/2`.

Therefore Theorem 35B applies **for any number of summands, any permutations, even with arbitrarily complex phases in the couplings**, as long as the real coefficients `c_s` are positive and the couplings obey that bound. The right factor `B` need not itself have a Green implementation; only invertible bi-regularity is required.

For the previous Phase 27 expander-Green parameters `alpha=1/4`, `beta=1/5` with equal weights, the sharper individual estimate is

`delta <= ( (1/3)+(1/4) )/2 = 7/24`, giving

`|A[0,0]*(A^-1)[0,0]-1| <= 49/408` and

**`|A[0,0]*(A^-1)[0,0]| >= 359/408 > 1/2`**.

This uniformly eliminates the tempting *single-mask* repair of the earlier rank-distant, well-conditioned mixer without inspecting its cycle permutations.

## Theorem 35C — full first-row/column calibration between regular stages

Suppose `A` and `B` are invertible **bi-regular** matrices with common row/column sums `a` and `b`, respectively. Assume all entries of the first row of `A` and first column of `B` are nonzero. Let `r_i=A[0,i]`, `s_i=B[i,0]`.

There exist a permutation `P` and invertible diagonal `D` for which `M=A D P B` matches **both** `F e0=u` and `e0^T F=u^T` exactly **if and only if** the multisets

`{r_i/a : 0<=i<N}` and `{s_i/b : 0<=i<N}`

are identical. When this is true, pair the equal entries with `P` and set

**`D[i,i]=1/(b*r_i)`**.

*Proof.* The first-row equation forces that choice of `D` as in 35A. Since `A^-1 u = u/a`, the first-column equation is equivalent to `D P s = u/a`, i.e. `(P s)_i/b=r_i/a` for every `i`, exactly the stated multiset condition. □

This is a constructive procedure. Cyclic Green sums provide `r=A^T e0` and `s=B e0` in `O(qN)` field operations. Matching their signatures is `O(N log N)` by sorting or expected `O(N)` hashing. The squared-sum moment of each normalized signature is a cheaper, `O(N)` **necessary** test that often excludes candidate pairs immediately.

**Crucial limitation:** this calibration matches only the first row and column, not the entire Fourier matrix.

### Constructed first-cross Green circuit

Take *any* bi-regular Green sum `A` with nonzero first row and sum `a`. Set `B=A^T` and

`M=A diag(1/(a*A[0,*])) A^T`.

Then `M e0 = u` and `e0^T M=u^T` **exactly**. Applying the circuit takes `O(qN)` work; computing its input-independent mask also takes `O(qN)` field work using one transpose application.

The diagonal gauges in this control are **arbitrary invertible scalings, not necessarily unit-modulus phase gates**. This is a phase-aware **partial Fourier construction** rather than a new FFT. Our exact tests through `N=4096` show that every coordinate of its next Fourier column, except the forced row-zero coordinate, is wrong for the seeded non-affine Green stages.

### Positive control (actually Fourier)

At `N=4`, let `A=J-2I`, where `J` is the all-ones matrix. Then `A^2=4I`, each row/column sum of `A` is `2`, and `A[0,i]*(A^-1)[i,0]=1/4`. Set `D=diag(1/A[0,*])` and `B=D^-1 A^-1 F_4`. Then `B` has row and column sums `1`, and `A D B=F_4` exactly. The standalone implementation checks this over two independent finite fields. `B` is not claimed to be a Green sum or inexpensive.

## Theorem 35D — exact, all-complex-coupling exclusion for explicit rank-distant Green pairs

We now give a stronger certificate with **no small-coupling assumption** for two fixed non-affine Hamiltonian cycle pairs.

Let `W0,W1` be the explicit cycles stored in `polynomial_audit.json` (seeds generated by `make_cycles(N,2,35123)`). Use a **common arbitrary complex** coupling `z` and define

`A(z)=(I-z W0)^-1+(I-z W1)^-1`.

For admissible `z^N !=1`, put

`C(z) = sum_{k=0}^{N-1} z^k (W0^k+W1^k)`, so `A(z)=C(z)/(1-z^N)`.

For `i=0,1`, define the **integer polynomials**

`H_i(z)=N*C[0,i](z)*cofactor(C(z),0,i)-det(C(z))`.

For invertible `A(z)`, Theorem 35A forces `H_0(z)=H_1(z)=0`. We reconstructed their **actual characteristic-zero integer coefficients** and obtained:

| N | Rank(W0-W1) | deg H0 | deg H1 | deg det(C) | deg gcd(H0,H1) | gcd divides det(C) over Z |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 8 | 6 | 54 | 54 | 54 | 46 | **Yes** |
| 16 | 14 | 240 | 240 | 240 | 209 | **Yes** |

**Proof of exact certificate.** Every polynomial entry of `C` has degree at most `N-1` and coefficient `l1`-norm at most `2`. By the Leibniz determinant expansion, the coefficient `l1`-norm of `det(C)` is bounded by `N! 2^N`, as is `N*C[0,i]*cofactor(C,0,i)`. Therefore the absolute value of **every coefficient of each `H_i`** is at most `2*N!*2^N`. Their degree is at most `N(N-1)`.

Use the prime `p=2^127-1`, greater than *twice* this coefficient bound at `N=8,16`. We evaluate `H0,H1,det(C)` at the `N(N-1)+1` distinct integers `0,1,...,N(N-1)` in the field `F_p`, interpolate the coefficients, and lift each modular coefficient to its **unique signed integer value within the proved bound**. This recovers the exact polynomials **over Z**, not merely modular approximations. Separate integer Bareiss determinants at `z=2,3` and evaluations over both `F_998244353` and `F_2013265921` agree.

The integer-polynomial gcds and divisibility checks are then performed using `sympy.Poly(...,domain=ZZ)`. At each size, `gcd(H0,H1)` **divides `det(C)` over Z**. Hence any complex common zero of `H0,H1` is a zero of `det(C)`. But `det(C)=0` means `A(z)` is singular when `z^N!=1`; otherwise the original Green inverses are undefined. Both possibilities exclude equality to the invertible Fourier matrix. □

**Scope:** These are all-coupling exclusions for the precisely specified, rank-distant pairs at `N=8,16`, with any invertible bi-regular right factor and any one middle monomial gate. It is not an all-cycle or all-length theorem; we did not claim a new worst-case FFT upper or lower bound.

## Complexity, correctness gates and remaining challenge

1. A calibrated circuit with two Green resolvents on each side uses four independent `O(N)` cyclic solve passes, with one `O(N)` diagonal multiplication and an `O(N)` permutation. This gives **first-row/first-column correctness only**.
2. The seed-balancing residual `A(1/A[0,*])-N e0` can be checked with **one transpose application plus one forward application** of the candidate left stage, without matrix inversion or dense matrices.
3. Weak-coupling and the explicit polynomial certificates are true all-parameter statements **within their assumptions**. They do not exclude additional internal noncommuting phase masks or nonlinear/shared-state networks.
4. The next remaining construction must have **nonflat seed projector obstruction removed**, then satisfy an entire Weyl covariance equation, not merely the easy Fourier boundaries. Any new executor must separately count phase generation, preprocessing, arithmetic, coordinate movement, and precision.
