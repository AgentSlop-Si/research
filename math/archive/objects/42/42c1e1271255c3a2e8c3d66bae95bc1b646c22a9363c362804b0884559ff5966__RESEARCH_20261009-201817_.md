## Primary theorem: Fourier local annihilators in a broader scan class

The first stage has offdiagonal entries `u_i r_j` below and `v_i r_j` above, and arbitrary diagonal. The second has offdiagonal entries `ell_i b_j` below and `ell_i e_j` above, and arbitrary diagonal. Here each `r_j` and `ell_i` is nonzero; the other generator arrays are completely arbitrary. Internal permutation P and endpoint diagonal scalings are also arbitrary; independent **affine** (coprime-stride) endpoint permutations are likewise covered by absorbing their row/column phases into diagonal scalings.

**Theorem:** `D_out F_N D_in != S2 P S1` for every `N >= 10`.

The method is to choose two local adjacent-coordinate input differences that S1 maps into overlapping two-coordinate supports. A weighted output first-difference turns S2 into a two-coordinate-local operator. The candidate product must therefore annihilate both selected columns on at least `N-7` of its `N-1` row-pairs.

But the Fourier pair produces a determinant polynomial of degree exactly two, with nonzero constant coefficient `(omega-1) alpha_0 alpha_1`. It cannot vanish at more than two distinct powers `omega^i`. This contradiction is exact over complex numbers, needs no numerical stability assumption, and does not divide by unknown stage determinants. The proof is in `PROOF_CERTIFICATE.md`.

This excludes a broader subclass than homogeneous constant-coefficient scans, but **does not cover the unrestricted separated-generator architecture**, where lower/upper row and column generators can all vary independently. It is also not a stronger asymptotic claim than the earlier natural-endpoint two-stage rank barrier; its contribution is an explicit local, Fourier-polynomial compatibility certificate.

## Second theorem: exact exclusion of two homogeneous stages

For `H(a,b,d)` with constant strict-lower, diagonal, strict-upper coefficients, an explicit cyclic bidiagonal pair A,B satisfies `BH=HB=A`. The forward and backward versions cover a=0 or b=0 without inverting any unknown coefficients.

If `F_N=H2 P H1`, the transformed candidate `B2 F_N B1=A2 P A1` must have a column supported on at most four rows. But its selected Fourier column is nonzero on at least `N-1` rows. Therefore no such factorization exists for N>=6, for any internal permutation P. Scalar degeneracies are separately excluded by distinct-value counts.

## Third theorem: exact exclusion of three homogeneous stages

For `F_N=H3 P2 H2 P1 H1`, the same cyclic-bidiagonal relation reduces the input to a two-sparse vector. The middle homogeneous scan maps it into at most **three ordinary scalar levels plus two isolated exceptional entries**, regardless of P1. After P2, the last bidiagonal operator can make at most **13 distinct interior values**. The matching transformed Fourier column has `N-1` distinct interior values. Thus **N>=15** is impossible, even with arbitrary P1 and P2. This includes N=16,32,64,... . Scalar end-stage cases are covered separately in the proof.

These are exact limitations of a narrow but globally dense O(N)-work scan family; they do not imply any bound for fully variable separated generators.

## Structural surprise: the earlier dense escaping scan is a gauged circulant

`S[i,j]=1` below, `3` on the diagonal, `2` above is diagonally similar to a circulant. With `q^N=1/2`, the similarity matrix is `diag(1,q,...,q^(N-1))`, and its eigenvectors are `(q*omega^k)^i`. The eigenvalue as a function of `z=q*omega^k` is `(z-2)/(z-1)`. The explicit O(N)-work apply/inverse scan therefore has an unexpected Fourier spectral basis. **It does not mean that basis can be applied in O(N).**

## Research interpretation and next gate

The spectral identity clarifies why a dense Green-style scan can appear more globally expressive than its exact algorithmic content. The two-stage degree-two polynomial gate is a useful tool for pruning searches whose generators share an envelope. The three-stage alphabet argument survives arbitrary internal permutations but only while the stages are homogeneous.

**Next difficult case:** independently variable lower and upper row/column generators interleaved with arbitrary permutations, with all exceptional zero branches retained. For a general rank-one separated scan, a three-coordinate input vector can cancel both offdiagonal tails, producing a three-sparse intermediate vector; analogously a three-row output combination cancels both tails. Combining these three-tap annihilators with Fourier-minor compatibility is a concrete next mathematical experiment. The earlier whole-network and nonlinear-gauge results warn that cut capacities alone may be insufficient.

**No FFT breakthrough has been claimed.** Only a true all-length exact factorization, tested on fresh vectors with full arithmetic cost, would answer the original goal.
