# HERO #0 — Phase 4: coprime Green padding, sharp covariance, and the exact batching gate

**Date:** 10 October 2026  
**Status:** Derived a **conditional theoretical improvement** of the Phase 3 randomized approximate DFT, independently reproduced it via finite exhaustive covariance and exactness tests, and isolated a rigorous rank obstruction to making the current constant-row mechanism exact. **No exact `O(N)` or exact `O(N log log N)` FFT; no claimed novelty or numerical-library speed record.**

## 1. Precise objective

For any fresh arbitrary `x∈C^N`, including prime `N`, approximate every coefficient of the unitary transform `U_Nx` with failure probability `o(1)` in sequential asymptotic work `O(N log log N)`, in the *same idealized arithmetic/coefficients model* as Phase 3. The original unrestricted exact FFT target remains separate.

The starting Phase 3 routine: embed centered length-`N` input into working length `Q=KM≈4N`; form Gaussian preweights, generate a random rank-one-per-block chirp projector `P_{a,beta}`, decode `U_QPz` via one length-`K` FFT and one `O(Q)` chirp/Green scan, then use short Gaussian Fourier-grid interpolation. It already handles all `N`; the loose variance bound was

`(Q/N) R² M(M-1)/phi(Q) ||x||₂²`, with local preweight ratio `R=1+o(1)`.

## 2. New algorithmic number theory: choose all short displacements invertible

Let `C=4` and choose a prime `M` with

`ceil(log₂(CN)) ≤ M ≤ 2 ceil(log₂(CN))+2`, e.g. the first prime at least that logarithm (Bertrand suffices asymptotically). Set `K₀=ceil(CN/M)`.

Search `K≥K₀` until no prime `p<M` divides `K`, i.e.

`gcd(K, product_{prime p<M} p)=1`.

Set `Q=M*K`. Because `M` is prime and neither `M` nor `K` shares any prime factor with `1≤d<M`, we have

> **Coprime short displacement:** `gcd(Q,d)=1` for every `1≤d<M`.

The search needs *neither* primality of `K` *nor* unproved short-prime-gap conjectures. It has a uniform subpolynomial upper bound:

Let `B=π(M−1)` and `P=∏_{p<M}p`. For any interval of `T` consecutive integers, inclusion–exclusion gives

`#{K coprime to P in interval} = T φ(P)/P + E, |E| ≤ 2^B`.

Each factor `(1−1/p)≥1/2`, hence `φ(P)/P ≥2^{-B}`. Therefore `T=2^{2B}+1` always contains an admissible `K`. The standard prime-counting upper bound `B=O(M/log M)` and `M=Theta(log N)` imply

`K−K₀ ≤ exp(O(log N/log log N)) = N^{o(1)}`.

Thus the padding slack satisfies

`r=Q−CN = O(M (K−K₀+1)) = N^{o(1)}`,

and constructing `M` and testing successive candidates uses `N^{o(1)}` word-arithmetic tests, smaller than the full input-scan cost. The actual prototype searches are far shorter (0–5 extra `K` candidates in eleven tests through `N=1,000,003`); the asymptotic proof does **not** assume typical gaps.

## 3. Independent exact Green covariance improvement

Fix an arbitrary real frequency `ξ` and an arbitrary input vector supported on the `N` centered input positions. Let `P=P_{a,beta}`, where `a` is uniform in the units mod `Q`, and `beta` is uniform mod `Q`. Let `g_j=e^{πσ²t_j²}/σ` be the Gaussian input preweight.

After Fourier dewindowing, the noise at frequency `ξ` (scaled to the unitary length-`N` convention) is

`Δ_ξ=(1/√N) Σ_j x_j Σ_{d≠0, j+d in same M-block} (g_j/g_{j+d}) exp(−2πi ξ t_{j+d}) S_j conjugate(S_{j+d})`.

Here `d` runs through the signed nonzero in-block displacements, not all shifts. For this chirp,

`S_j conjugate(S_{j+d}) = c_{a,d} exp(−2πi a d j/Q) exp(−2πi beta d/Q)`

where `|c_{a,d}|=1` and it does **not** depend on `j`.

**Step 1: beta kills mixed displacements.** The distinct signed shifts satisfy `|d−d'|<2M<Q`. Thus uniform beta averages `exp(2πi beta(d−d')/Q)` to zero for `d≠d'`. Therefore variance is the sum of separate displacement variances.

**Step 2: all displacements are invertible.** For a fixed `d`, multiplication `a↦a d (mod Q)` permutes the units of `Z/QZ`. If `v_d(j)` denotes the input `x_j` multiplied by the dewindow factor and the support mask, Parseval gives

`(1/(N φ(Q))) Σ_{a∈units} |Σ_j v_d(j) exp(−2πi a d j/Q)|²`
` ≤ (1/(N φ(Q))) Σ_{s=0}^{Q−1} |Σ_j v_d(j) exp(−2πi s j/Q)|²`
` = (Q/(N φ(Q))) Σ_j |v_d(j)|²`.

**Step 3: use the blocks rather than counting all possible signed shifts.** The local Gaussian ratio obeys `|g_j/g_{j+d}|≤R`, where

`R = exp(πσ²(2τ M/Q+(M/Q)²))` and `τ≤1/8`.

Each occupied input position belongs to *exactly one* length-`M` block. It has **at most `M−1`** other output positions in that block. Consequently

`Σ_{d≠0}Σ_j |v_d(j)|² ≤ R²(M−1)||x||₂²`.

Combining yields the **new variance inequality**:

\[
\boxed{\mathbb E|\Delta_\xi|^2\le \frac{Q}{N}\frac{R^2(M-1)}{\varphi(Q)}\|x\|_2^2.}\tag{P4.1}
\]

The main noise has mean zero. This bound includes arbitrary adversarial complex input and every fractional target frequency, not only the integer grid. Relative to the previous local bound using exactly the same `Q,M,R`, this is an **exact factor `M` in the proof's upper bound**, not a measured M-fold runtime speedup.

This result uses the exact structural projector with a uniformly sampled invertible `a`; it does not assert that *arbitrary* low-rank sketches share this variance.

## 4. Gaussian interpolation and a general all-length approximate guarantee

Reuse Phase 3 Gaussian padding and finite-frequency FIR theorem. At a target `k`, write `kQ/N=4k+f_k+θ_k`, where `f_k=floor(k r/N)` and `0≤θ_k<1`. Bucket `θ_k` into `H=ceil(N^α)` bins. The rounded grid frequency uses a Gaussian FIR of halfwidth `L=Theta(log N)`.

Choose `0<α<1/2` fixed, let `κ=2α/π`, set `σ²=L=max(12,ceil(κ ln N))`, and use `Q≥4N` and the prime-rough padding above. The deterministic remainder from replacing ideal Gaussian dewindowing with the finite FIR is bounded (as in Phase 3) by

`D_G ≤ √(Q/N) M e^{πσ²τ²} [A(σ,τ+M/Q)+ T(L,σ)/σ]`,

with `A=2e^{−πσ²(1−2τ−2M/Q)}/(1−e^{−πσ²(1−2τ−2M/Q)})` and `T=2e^{−πL²/σ²}/(1−e^{−2πL/σ²})`.

As `τ≤1/8`, `M/Q=o(1)`, and `Q/N→4`, the two exponential terms have decay powers at least `(47/32)α−o(1)` and `(63/32)α−o(1)`, strictly faster than the phase-bucket error `π τ/H=O(N^{-α})`. The random component has variance by (P4.1)

`B²=O(M loglog Q /N)=N^{-1+o(1)}`,

using the classical totient lower bound `φ(Q)/Q=Ω(1/loglog(Q+3))`.

For `u=N^{-α}`, Chebyshev yields `p=Pr[ |one-row noise|>u ||x||₂ ]≤N^{-(1−2α)+o(1)}`. Take any fixed odd `s=2h−1` with `h>1/(1−2α)`. Independent coordinatewise real/imag medians and a union bound across all `N` outputs give

\[
\boxed{\Pr[\|\hat X-U_Nx\|_\infty>C_\alpha N^{-\alpha}\|x\|_2]\le N^{1-h(1-2\alpha)+o(1)}.}\tag{P4.2}
\]

**Example:** `α=1/3`, `h=4`, `s=7`: error `O(N^{-1/3}||x||₂)` with failure `N^{-1/3+o(1)}`. With `s=9`, `h=5`, the bound strengthens to `N^{-2/3+o(1)}`. More rows do not alter the asymptotic operations for any fixed α.

### Work accounting, including the nontrivial new slack

- Input weighting / scans / Green decoding: `O(Q+(Q/M)log(Q/M))=O(N)` per row.
- The integer-stride structure is still `j_k=4k+floor(kr/N)`, but now `r=N^{o(1)}` rather than `O(log N)`.
- `floor(kr/N)` changes at most `r` times, and each monotone fractional sweep meets at most `H` phase buckets. Number of constant-kernel segments is `O((r+1)(H+1))=N^{α+o(1)}`.
- Length-`O(L)` overlap-save small FFTs, reusing each bucket's kernel, cost `O(N log L + r H L log L)=O(N loglog N)+N^{α+o(1)}=O(N loglog N)`.
- The number-theoretic search needs `N^{o(1)}` integer operations; `H L log L=o(N)` for kernel preparation.
- `O(N)` storage plus `o(N)` cached short kernels and constant many output buffers.

Thus the **conditional Phase 4 theorem candidate**, in the same idealized arithmetic/coefficient-access model as Phase 3, still has `O_α(N loglog N)` operations and `O_α(N)` storage for *all* `N`, including primes, with the sharper (P4.2) guarantee. This is **not** a bit-complexity theorem, fixed binary64 guarantee, exact algorithm, published novelty claim, or fastest measured FFT implementation.

## 5. Exact Green resolution of identity: the other side of the research

Let `K=Q/M`, fix any unit chirp slope `a` and any `beta₀`, and choose

`beta_r = beta₀ + r*K (mod Q),  r=0,...,M−1`.

The phase functions satisfy

`S_{a,beta_r}[b,t] = S_{a,beta₀}[b,t] exp(2πi r t/M)`.

For the rank-one projector `P_r[b] = conjugate(S_r[b]) · S_r[b]^T` within every block,

\[
\boxed{\frac1M\sum_{r=0}^{M-1}P_{a,\beta_r}=I_Q,\qquad
 U_Qz=\frac1M\sum_{r=0}^{M-1}U_QP_{a,\beta_r}z.}\tag{P4.3}
\]

Proof: the `t,u` entry of the averaged block matrix contains `(1/M)Σ_r exp(2πi r(u−t)/M)=δ_{tu}`. This is an exact identity for **every** length `Q=KM`, not a numerical approximation.

### First partial batching actually implemented

All `M` block summaries can be computed by *one M-point inverse FFT per block*, rather than by `M` separately paid compressions:

`D_r[b] = Σ_{t=0}^{M−1} S_{a,beta₀}[b,t] z[bM+t] exp(2πi r t/M)`.

Thus building every `D_r[b]` costs `O(Q log M)` instead of `O(Q M)`. `exact_batch.py` verifies the batched summaries and the exact returned DFT. **The remaining M length-K Fourier transforms cost `O(Q log K)`, and M separate Green scans cost `O(QM)`**. So the implemented exact full transform is still `O(Q log Q)` overall.

The concrete *next exact research gate* is a genuine shared evaluator of these batched `K`-FFTs and `M` Green scans, with rigorously charged cost **strictly below `QM` for the combined output**. This is a different and much harder task than simply observing identity (P4.3).

## 6. Why a fixed number of rows cannot be exact for every input

On any fully occupied source block of `M` input coordinates, one sampled Green row records one scalar `d_b=Σ_t S[b,t]z_{bM+t}`. For `s<M` fixed chirp states, the whole computation depends on at most `s` scalar measurements of the `M` unknown values in that block. The measurement matrix has a nonzero kernel of dimension at least `M−s`. Its nonzero kernel vectors yield *zero data for all s projected Green outputs but a nonzero true Fourier transform*. No nonlinear postprocessing of just those measurements can recover every input exactly.

For seeds chosen independently of the input, every fixed-seed decoder can be exact on at most a Lebesgue-null subset of all complex input vectors when `s<M`, under ordinary measurability assumptions. This is because exact reconstruction selects at most one point on each positive-dimensional fiber of a linear measurement map (Fubini). Integrating over seeds shows there is no positive exact-success probability for almost every arbitrary continuous input within this restricted measurement architecture.

This is **not** an FFT arithmetic-complexity lower bound and **does not refute** the per-fixed-input probabilistic approximation theorem (P4.2). It explains why one must invent a new multi-row sharing mechanism for the *exact* goal, or acquire additional independent information.

### Scope, prior art and independent review

Gaussian NUFFT interpolation is classical, including Dutt–Rokhlin (1993), `https://doi.org/10.1137/0914081`, and Greengard–Lee (2004), `https://doi.org/10.1137/S003614450343200X`. The Fourier full-spectrum transform remains a different task from sparse Fourier recovery and from exact multiplication. We have not established that the full Green/random-scan composition, the coprime-padding lemma in this application, or the sharp variance result are new in the scholarly literature. Independent mathematical review, arithmetic-model audit, and precision bounds are needed before external breakthrough claims.

## 8. Recommended next research experiment

Analyze the **joint M-channel Green scans** under the exact complete frame. Reparameterize the `beta` phases by residue modulo `M`, express all scans as a polyphase/cyclic operator, and attempt a shared *aggregate-only* application costing `O(Q log M)` rather than `O(QM)`. Avoid merely computing M separate trajectories or exchanging loops. A correct exact all-input bound must charge the length-K Fourier components, every coefficient, the joint scan, memory access, and complete output. Do not borrow an existing faster transform as an oracle. A genuine resolution would address the user's exact `O(N loglog N)` target; currently this remains **open**.
