# Phase25 independent audit: affine orders, Green sums, and the hidden cut

9 October 2026. This is a bounded independent mathematical and source audit, not peer review or a claim of priority. The original upload is preserved unchanged. No uploaded program or test was imported or executed. Executable files in this supplement were independently authored.

## Main result

The affine-run Fourier identity, all-stride rank obstruction, commutator rank, four-track sparsity, and rank-five hidden-cut obstruction are correct with the parameter/model qualifications below. Theorem 4 needs a substantive correction: nonsingularity of its two individual resolvents does **not** imply nonsingularity of their sum. Moreover, even when the sum is invertible, the supplied unpivoted band-LU/Woodbury construction can fail on a zero pivot or a singular band base.

This supplement repairs the executor, rather than merely restricting it to favorable pivot patterns. An exact pivoted constant-frontier factorization operates on the **whole** four-track matrix, detects singularity, and solves every nonsingular instance in O(N) field operations and O(N) field/index words. It does not require the band-only base to be invertible. The linear bound is an arithmetic bound, with field inversions counted as field operations; it is not a bit-complexity or floating-point stability theorem.

A second bounded improvement is a uniform complex condition bound valid for every |alpha|, |beta| < 1. At alpha=1/4 and beta=1/5 it gives **kappa_2(S) <= 155/98**, improving the source's valid 31/17 bound.

There remains no arbitrary-input sub-N log N FFT or integer-multiplication result. The hidden-cut theorem rules out the stated single Green-sum stage even after its allowed gauges and outer reorderings.

## 2. Affine-run Fourier theorem and relation to Phase24

For a supplied run L[t_s+v]=b_s+a_s v (mod N), and input x[L[t]]=z^t, its unnormalized Fourier contribution at k is

    z^(t_s) omega^(k b_s) sum_(v=0)^(l_s-1) (z omega^(k a_s))^v.

Thus the submitted formula follows directly, including G_l(1)=l. At z=0, x[L[0]]=1 and all later coordinates vanish; the convention z^0=1 handles this exactly. There is no need to infer a node from arbitrary input data. Empty runs can be discarded; if supplied, they contribute zero, including at q=1.

For each run, prepare z^(t_s), z^(l_s), omega^a_s, omega^b_s, and omega^(a_s l_s). Across k, the needed phases advance by multiplication. Every numerator and denominator therefore costs O(1) per output, rather than a fresh exponentiation. Batch-invert only the nonzero denominators using prefix products, one inversion, and a reverse pass. q=1 outputs use the finite sum value. Processing runs sequentially uses O(N+r) workspace and O(rN+r log N) arithmetic, with at most r field inversions. A seen-array validator takes O(N+r) index work for valid runs; run starts, lengths, spatial positions, and coverage must all be checked. Field/root/normalization preparation is separate.

The independent controls evaluate the **unnormalized** formula against direct DFTs and an independently written radix-two NTT; multiplication by one common supplied normalization scalar preserves equality. They include every Nth-root node for the small lengths, z=0, nonroots, all admissible two-run slopes, ordinary/reversed runs, and singleton-run descriptions.

The earlier independent Phase24 addition proved all-z O(N(s+1)) evaluation for supplied permutations with s changed successor edges relative to a cyclic shift, using sparse twisted differences and one exceptional-bin calculation. Phase25's affine-run description is a different, useful structural parameter. For these two-run orders, r=2 while their rank distance from every nontrivial cyclic stride is m-1; their changed-edge count relative to each such stride is at least that large. A constant-run executor can consequently be linear where the earlier defect-based upper bound is quadratic. This is a genuine extension of structured-input coverage, not an arbitrary-vector speedup and not a contradiction of the Phase24 result. No claim is made that these are optimal bounds or that a small-r representation can always be found.

## 3. All-stride and commutator ranks

For permutation matrices P and Q, left multiplication by Q^-1 gives

    rank(P-Q) = rank(Q^-1 P-I) = N - number_of_cycles(Q^-1 P).

The kernel consists of vectors constant on each permutation cycle, over any field. No division by a cycle length is needed, so characteristic two is covered.

In W_a, the m-1 internal even edges have displacement +a, the m-1 internal odd edges displacement -a, and the two bridge displacements are a-1 and -(a-1). These four displacements are distinct for dyadic N>=8 and odd u. A stride b other than +/-a agrees on at most one edge. The remaining nonfixed permutation cycles have length at least two, giving rank at least ceil((N-1)/2)=m.

The parity-preserving permutation

    phi(j)=u j+(u-1)(j mod 2) mod N

sends L_2 to L_a, conjugates W_2 to W_a, and conjugates Z^(+/-2) to Z^(+/-a). For a=2, Z^-2 W_2 has m-1 fixed points and nontrivial cycles of lengths N/4+1 and N/4. More explicitly, the nontrivial cycles are (1, N-2, N-3, N-7, ..., 5) and (3, N-1, N-5, ..., 7), with the evident termination for N=8. Its rank is m-1; reflection gives the other sign. This proves the source's exact minimum.

For V=(ZW)^-1(WZ), the submitted generic maps j -> j-2a on evens and j -> j+2a on odds, together with its three exceptional maps, are correct. Since a=2 mod 4, the generic steps separately form cycles on the four residue classes mod 4. The exceptions replace one outgoing edge in each of classes 0,2,1, connecting those classes in order 0 -> 2 -> 1 -> 0. They form one cycle of length 3N/4. Class 3 remains a cycle of length N/4. Hence rank(WZ-ZW)=N-2.

Writing A=I-alpha W, B=I-beta Z, the inverse commutator identity is

    [A^-1,B^-1] = A^-1 B^-1 [A,B] B^-1 A^-1.

All outer factors are invertible for admissible parameters, and [A,B]=alpha beta[W,Z]. Thus the Green commutator has rank N-2 precisely under the stated nonzero-parameter condition. Its large rank provides no Fourier covariance or general transform construction.

## 4. Theorem 4: two distinct missing conditions

The algebraic identity

    S=A^-1+B^-1=A^-1(A+B)B^-1

is correct. Put C=A+B. Then **S is invertible if and only if C is invertible**, and only in that case

    S^-1=B C^-1 A.

### 4.1 Admissible resolvents can have a singular sum

W and Z both fix the all-ones vector. Its C eigenvalue is 2-alpha-beta; its S eigenvalue is

    1/(1-alpha)+1/(1-beta)
      = (2-alpha-beta)/((1-alpha)(1-beta)).

At alpha=3/2 and beta=1/2 over the rationals or complex numbers, both cyclic resolvents are nonsingular for every positive N, but S annihilates the all-ones vector. In particular this is a counterexample at the source's twisted N=16. Individual admissibility cannot replace the condition det(C) != 0. The source's small-coupling complex example is unaffected.

### 4.2 An invertible band base can still have a zero unpivoted pivot

The four-track reordering has exactly the four listed out-of-band graph edges for N>=16. To check this algebraically, the new index of physical coordinate j is

    f(j)=2(j mod m)-(j mod 2)+2 indicator(j>=m).

For W, the generic even-index displacements f(Wj)-f(j) are +6 in the first half and +2 in the second; the generic odd displacements are -2 and -6. The two cycle-bridge columns m-2 and m+1 instead give +3 and -3. The only other exceptions are j=1, giving N-4, and j=N-2, giving -(N-4). For Z the ordinary displacements are +1 on even columns and +3 on odd columns, except j=m-1 gives 5-N and j=N-1 gives 1-N. Since N>=16, exactly these last four W/Z entries lie outside half-bandwidth six. Their endpoints are W: 1 -> m-1 and N-2 -> m; Z: m-1 -> m and N-1 -> 0.

With T denoting the width-six matrix after removing those entries, the source factors T without pivoting and applies a four-entry Woodbury correction.

At N=16 over F_998244353, alpha=beta=2:

- 1-2^16 = 998178818, so both cyclic resolvents exist;
- det(C) = 980549633, so the Green sum is invertible;
- det(T) = 995491841, so the band base also is invertible;
- ordinary no-row-exchange elimination first reaches a zero pivot at index 5 (the sixth pivot).

The submitted `FixedBandLU.factor` raises `ArithmeticError('zero band pivot')` on this mathematical instance. These values were computed by independent dense elimination, not by running the submitted code. Pivoting only the 4x4 correction cannot repair a preceding band-factorization failure.

### 4.3 Even the band base itself need not be invertible

At N=16 over F_19, alpha=2 and beta=8 are individually admissible. Here det(C)=13 while rank(T)=15. The Green sum exists and is invertible, but this particular Woodbury base does not. Row-pivoting T alone cannot fix a genuinely singular base. This is a distinct obstruction from the previous zero-pivot example.

### 4.4 Corrected scope

For the original implementation, require (i) admissible individual resolvents, (ii) invertible C, and (iii) nonzero prescribed leading band pivots. Equivalently, the band factorization and correction must both succeed; the correction is invertible when T and C are invertible. Source tests at selected parameters do not prove these conditions universally.

For the mathematical linear-inverse claim, condition (iii) is unnecessary after the independent whole-matrix repair below. The corrected theorem is: **for every admissible parameter pair with det(C) != 0, the twisted S and S^-1 have exact O(N)-field-operation applications and O(N) setup/storage; singular C can be detected within the same bound.**

## 5. Independent whole-matrix constant-frontier repair

`frontier_solver.py` contains a deterministic sparse row-pivoted factorization. It is not a dense fallback, a random parameter perturbation, or a claim that a singular S has an inverse.

Consider an n by n matrix with symmetric half-bandwidth w except for E individual entries, with w and E fixed. Mark every row containing an out-of-band entry as exceptional; their number e is at most E. Activate those rows initially. Before eliminating column j, also activate each ordinary row whose original row index is at most j+w. Among active unused rows choose any row with a nonzero entry in column j, use it as the pivot, and eliminate that column from all other active rows.

**Why no needed pivot is missed.** A not-yet-activated ordinary row i has i>j+w, so its original support starts strictly after column j. It has no entry in any previously eliminated column either, and earlier eliminations would leave it unchanged. Therefore every possible nonzero in the current column is in the active frontier. If no active pivot exists, the full square matrix is singular. If the matrix is nonsingular, a pivot exists at every column.

**Why the frontier stays bounded.** At column j, at most j+w+1 ordinary-index rows plus e exceptional rows have entered, while exactly j rows have already been used as pivots. Hence there are at most w+1+e active rows. Counting an exceptional row both ways only weakens the bound.

**Why fill stays bounded.** Let Tcols be the union of original column supports of all exceptional rows. Its size is at most e(2w+1)+E for a band-plus-E-entry matrix. At elimination column j, each active row's surviving support is contained in

    {j,j+1,...,j+2w} union Tcols.

This follows by induction. Newly activated ordinary rows have original index at most j+w and hence no original column beyond j+2w. Exceptional row support is already in Tcols. A pivot's upper support obeys the same bound, so its elimination introduces nothing else. Each row operation and pivot search therefore costs only a constant number of field operations for fixed w,E.

For this denominator, w=6 and E<=4. Each original row has at most three nonzeros (diagonal, W predecessor, Z predecessor); thus |Tcols|<=12. Conservative bounds are at most 11 active rows and at most 25 entries in a surviving row. Recorded fill and elimination multipliers consequently occupy O(N) words. Factorization performs O(N) arithmetic and at most N inversions; forward elimination on a right-hand side followed by back substitution costs O(N). Nonsingularity detection does not need any inversions of zero.

The stored pivot record uses original row identities, pivot inverses, upper entries, and the row-elimination coefficients. Applying those same row operations to an arbitrary right-hand side and back-substituting solves C. Combining this with sparse A and B applications implements S^-1=B C^-1 A, with coordinate reordering in between. `TwistedInverse.create` separately rejects singular individual resolvents and then factors the full C. `apply` works on arbitrary right-hand sides for the represented Green sum.

The implementation assumes a known prime modulus. The algebraic method works over any field with exact zero tests and inversions, but the provided Python modular executor is not an implementation of arbitrary extension fields or certified complex arithmetic. No numerical stability is claimed for arbitrary row choices in floating point.

## 6. Stronger complex condition bound

Let U be unitary, |gamma|=r<1, and H=(I-gamma U)^-1. By unitary diagonalization, every eigenvalue has the form h=1/(1-z), |z|=r. Then

    |h| <= 1/(1-r),
    Re(h) >= 1/(1+r).

For the second inequality, write z=r exp(i theta) and c=cos(theta):

    Re(h)=(1-r c)/(1-2r c+r^2).

Subtracting 1/(1+r) gives

    r(1-r)(1+c) / ((1+r)(1-2r c+r^2)) >= 0.

Thus the Hermitian part of H is bounded below by (1+r)^-1 I. With r=|alpha| and s=|beta|, the two unitary permutations need not commute: their Hermitian parts add. For every unit vector x,

    ||S x|| >= |x* S x| >= Re(x* S x)
             >= 1/(1+r)+1/(1+s).

Also ||S|| <= 1/(1-r)+1/(1-s). Consequently S is nonsingular throughout the open bidisk, and

    kappa_2(S)
      <= [1/(1-r)+1/(1-s)] / [1/(1+r)+1/(1+s)]
       = (2-r-s)(1+r)(1+s) / ((2+r+s)(1-r)(1-s)).

For r=1/4, s=1/5 this is 155/98, approximately 1.58163. The source's 31/17 bound is correct but weaker and uses an unnecessary delta<2 restriction for its invertibility argument. Independently, C is strictly diagonally dominant whenever r+s<2, and deleting the four off-diagonal entries preserves that property for T. Every leading principal submatrix of T is then strictly diagonally dominant, so its prescribed leading pivots exist in exact arithmetic; det(C) and det(T) are nonzero, hence the Woodbury correction is invertible. This establishes the source algorithm's exact small-coupling scope. Matrix conditioning is not, by itself, a backward-stability proof for the implementation.

## 7. Hidden balanced cut and approximation floor

For a proper coordinate subset J of a single directed Hamiltonian cycle, W[J,J] is nilpotent: its induced graph has no directed cycle. Hence A[J,J]=I-alpha W[J,J] is invertible for every alpha. If A itself is invertible, block inversion gives

    (A^-1)[I,J] = - Schur(A)^-1 A[I,J] A[J,J]^-1.

Both side factors are invertible, proving equality of these off-diagonal block ranks. A nonzero scalar multiple of a permutation's crossing-edge block has rank equal to its edge count, because the corresponding rows and columns are distinct.

For the alternating-quarter cut in the source, the three listed W edges and two listed Z edges are exact at every dyadic N>=16. For completeness, write N=4q. In J's first quarter [q,2q-1], the only W crossing is the odd column q+1 -> 3q-1: the putative even wrap at 2q-2 is the cycle bridge to 4q-1 and stays in J. In J's second quarter [3q,4q-1], the only crossings are the odd column 3q+1 -> q-1 and the even column 4q-2 -> 2q. The natural-cycle crossings are 2q-1 -> 2q and 4q-1 -> 0. This proves the all-size counts directly. Therefore each nonzero-parameter resolvent has cross-rank 3 or 2, and their sum has rank at most five. Multiplying either term on either side by diagonal matrices preserves the bound; invertibility of those gauges is not needed for an upper bound. The sum need not be invertible for this obstruction. Arbitrary outer independent row/column permutations and invertible diagonal scalings move this balanced block and preserve its rank. Its relocated row and column sets need not be complements, which is harmless because the Fourier argument allows arbitrary balanced sets R,C.

For the unitary normalized Fourier matrix, every m by m submatrix has squared Frobenius norm N/4, while every singular value is at most one. Its rank is consequently at least N/4. If its singular values are sigma_1 >= ... >= sigma_m, then

    sum_(j=6)^m sigma_j^2 >= N/4-5,
    (m-5) sigma_6^2 >= N/4-5.

By the optimal rank-five approximation bound, every rank-at-most-five block differs from it in spectral norm by at least sigma_6. Coordinate restriction cannot increase operator norm. Hence, for N>20,

    ||M-Fhat||_2 >= sqrt((N/4-5)/(N/2-5)).

This tends to 1/sqrt(2) and applies to the stated diagonal-gauged sum and every outer monomial equivalent. The finite source observation of full **natural** cross-cut rank does not conflict with a different rank-five balanced cut. The theorem says nothing prohibitive about products with internal phases, more stages, or unrestricted shared-state computations; no universal FFT lower bound follows.

## 9. Arithmetic and research boundaries

The source's fixed prime F_998244353 supports dyadic roots only up to N=2^23; the standalone inverse does not itself require a Fourier root but still works in a specified field. An unbounded Fourier family must specify root/field construction and charge it. With B-bit field elements, arithmetic and inversions have B-dependent bit costs. O(N) field operations do not mean O(N) bit operations; explicit Fourier output in growing prime fields already requires Omega(N log N) output bits. Exact complex identities likewise do not supply a finite-precision algorithm or algebraic-number bit bound.

The useful additions to preserve are the affine-run parameterization, the all-slope rank theorem, the near-maximal commutator construction, the four-track solver structure **with corrected nonsingularity/pivot scope**, and the invariant hidden-cut obstruction. The independent frontier repair and stronger condition estimate can be reused with their stated assumptions. None supplies arbitrary-input Fourier covariance, a sub-N log N exact all-output FFT, or a new integer-multiplication bound. The next research gate should test genuine Fourier structure and fully charged arbitrary-input work rather than treating high natural-cut rank, commutator rank, conditioning, or inexpensive inverses as substitutes.
