# Phase 20 — Exact Weyl coherence, projective Fourier fingerprints, and Green-circuit barriers

**Research date:** 9 October 2026  
**Status:** Proven project-specific algebraic statements; executable finite-field and numerical experiments.  
**Main negative result:** **No sub-`N log N` Fourier transform or integer-multiplication bound is established.**

## 0. Scope and research lineage

This continues the exact Phase 19 `M T=V M`, `M V=T M` project, not the CrocSwap/Swapnil-jain integer-multiplication development. Phase 19 showed the symmetrized intertwiners admit a two-dimensional solution family and developed dense finite-field joint-monomial gates. Phase 20 makes correctness testing *black-box linear-work* and supplies a stronger structural target for Green solvers.

Let `N>=4` be a power of 2, `omega=e^{-2 pi i/N}`, and write

`F[j,k] = omega^(j*k)/sqrt(N)` (normalized unitary DFT),

`Z e_k = e_(k+1 mod N)` (forward cyclic coordinate shift),

`D e_k = omega^k e_k` (Fourier modulation diagonal).

These obey `FZ=DF`, `FD=Z^-1 F`, `F^2=J`, where `J e_k=e_-k`. Unless stated otherwise, norms and ranks are over complex numbers; the finite-field verifier is explicitly distinguished.

## 1. Full Weyl duality characterizes the exact DFT

**Theorem 1.** An `N x N` matrix `M` satisfies

`MZ=DM` and `MD=Z^-1 M`

if and only if `M=cF` for a scalar `c`. If also `M[0,0]=1/sqrt(N)`, then `M=F`.

**Proof.** Write `b_j=M[j,0]`. From `MZ=DM`, each next column is `D` times the preceding column, so `M[j,k]=b_j omega^(jk)`. Substituting into `MD=Z^-1 M`, the left side is `b_j omega^((j+1)k)` and the right side is `b_(j+1) omega^((j+1)k)`. Thus `b_(j+1)=b_j` for every `j`, giving `M[j,k]=b omega^(jk)=b sqrt(N) F[j,k]`. The `(0,0)` probe fixes the scalar. No inverse of `M` was assumed.

Unlike the prior *symmetrized* equations, the full Weyl pair has no reflection `FJ` ambiguity. The underlying Schur/Weyl observation is classical, not claimed as new mathematics.

## 2. A sharp quantitative Weyl-residual gap

Define

`R(M)^2 = ||MZ-DM||_F^2 + ||MD-Z^-1 M||_F^2`.

**Theorem 2.** For every complex `M`,

`min_c ||M-cF||_F <= R(M)/(2 sin(pi/N))`.

The factor `2 sin(pi/N)` is **best possible**.

**Proof.** Let `K=F* M`, so the two residuals, conjugated by `F*`, are `[K,Z]` and `[K,D]`. The normalized Weyl matrices `W_(a,b)=D^a Z^b/sqrt(N)` for `a,b in {0,...,N-1}` form an orthonormal Frobenius basis. Write `K=sum c_(a,b) W_(a,b)`. The Weyl relation `DZ=omega ZD` gives the *exact* diagonal quadratic identity

`R(M)^2 = sum_(a,b) (|1-omega^a|^2+|1-omega^b|^2) |c_(a,b)|^2`.

The only zero eigenvalue is at `(a,b)=(0,0)`, corresponding to scalar multiples of `I`, hence scalar multiples of `F` in the original coordinates. Every other coefficient has weight at least `4 sin^2(pi/N)`. The inequality follows by Parseval. Equality is attained by `M=F D` (or `F Z`): its non-scalar Weyl content is in the minimal-weight mode.

**Numerical check:** exact equality up to roundoff was observed for `N=8,16,32,64,128`, including an explicit example where the residual shrinks as `N` grows despite substantial distance from `cF`. The gap is `Theta(1/N)`; raw small residuals at growing `N` are therefore *not* sufficient to claim Fourier accuracy.

## 3. Exact four-call probabilistic black-box verifier

Fix a prime field `F_p` with a primitive `N`th root `omega` and `sqrt(N)`. Our implementation uses `p=998244353`, root generator `3`, and powers of two dividing `p-1`. Let a candidate oracle implement a **fixed linear map** `M: F_p^N -> F_p^N` at cost `C(N)`.

- First compute `u=M e_0` and test `u[0]=1/sqrt(N)` (one oracle call).
- Draw a uniform random field vector `r` and compute `y=M r`, `a=M(Zr)`, and `b=M(Dr)` (three oracle calls).
- Require `a=D y` and `b=Z^-1 y` exactly.

If a nonzero residual matrix exists, the probability that an independent uniform `r` lies in its kernel is at most `1/p`, so `h` independent trials have false-acceptance probability at most `p^-h` **in the finite field**. The test has one-sided error: true Fourier oracles never fail. With a fixed software pseudorandom seed, the outcome is reproducible and exact, but the formal probabilistic guarantee requires genuinely uniform independent vectors; `seed=None` uses OS-backed `SystemRandom`.

**Cost:** `1+3h` oracle applications and `O(hN)` field operations/memory movement, with no dense matrix inversion. If `C(N)=O(qtN)` for `t` stages each containing `q` pre-factored tridiagonal solves, testing is `O(hqtN)` field operations, **but this does not compute the FFT**. Preprocessing, modular inverses, memory accesses, and the cost of implementing the candidate circuit are not magically eliminated.

A rejection is an exact statement about the candidate's chosen finite-field realization. A pass on finitely many random vectors does **not** prove an exact identity over complex numbers; a field reduction of an algebraic complex circuit is sound only when the coefficient specialization and denominators are valid.

## 4. Fourier projective curvature: an outer-gauge invariant

For four nonzero entries of `M`, with distinct `j,j'` and `k,k'`, define the cross-ratio

`chi_(j,j';k,k') = M[j,k] M[j',k'] / (M[j,k'] M[j',k])`.

For `F`, this equals `omega^((j-j')(k-k'))`. Multiplying entire rows/columns by arbitrary nonzero complex values cancels in the ratio; permuting rows and columns only relabels the selected quadruple.

**Theorem 3 (necessary test).** If `M=L P_row F P_col R` with invertible diagonal `L,R` and arbitrary coordinate permutations, then every entry of `M` is nonzero and *every* cross-ratio satisfies `chi^N=1`.

Thus one non-root cross-ratio is an **exact certificate excluding all outer diagonal scalings and row/column permutations**, even when the Green core is nonunitary. The `cross_ratio_witness` procedure uses just **two basis-vector oracle calls** to obtain two columns, then evaluates the selected four entries. A passing quadruple is *not* a proof of equivalence.

**Prior art:** Four-entry cross-ratio invariants are related to the classical Haagerup invariant used to classify complex Hadamard matrices. See Östergård & Valtonen, *Equivalence of complex Hadamard matrices*, Journal of Algebraic Combinatorics (2026), https://doi.org/10.1007/s10801-026-01568-x. We claim an inexpensive **adaptation to black-box Green-circuit falsification**, not invention of the invariant.

### Stronger two-column fingerprint

For any two different columns `k,l`, fix one row `j0` and set

`r_j = M[j,l] M[j0,k] / (M[j,k] M[j0,l])`.

For a Fourier matrix after outer monomial gauges there are row and column permutations `rho,sigma` such that

`r_j = omega^((rho(j)-rho(j0))*(sigma(l)-sigma(k)))`.

Let `d=gcd(sigma(l)-sigma(k),N)` (`1<=d<N`). The `N` values `r_j` must then enumerate **every root in the subgroup of order `N/d` exactly `d` times**. In particular, for power-of-two `N`, `d` is a power of two. The histogram test costs two oracle calls and `O(N)` field work and does not need to know the outer permutations. It is necessary, not sufficient.

### Complete explicit-matrix equivalence criterion

Let `M` have no zero entries and define anchored ratios

`R_(j,k) = M[j,k] M[0,0]/(M[j,0] M[0,k])`.

**Theorem 4.** `M` is equivalent to `F` by invertible diagonal matrices and row/column permutations iff there exist two permutations of the *residue classes modulo `N`*, `alpha_j` and `beta_k`, such that

`R_(j,k) = omega^(alpha_j beta_k)` for every `j,k`.

The result follows directly by expressing row/column Fourier indices relative to the anchors. The converse reconstructs `L_j=M[j,0]*sqrt(N)` and `R_k=M[0,k]/M[0,0]`.

For powers of two, a primitive anchored root must occur; using its position as a pivot, scale the exponent labels so that one `alpha` value becomes `1`, recover the other `alpha,beta` values from one row/column, check both are residue permutations, and check the full outer-product exponent grid. The `full_monomial_gate` program implements the complete test in `O(N^2)` field operations for *explicitly available* matrices, not for large black-box circuits.

**Controls:** all entries of deliberately permuted/scaled Fourier matrices were reconstructed for `N=8,16,32,64`. A doubled-frequency Fourier kernel and an arbitrary root-valued counterfeit demonstrate why the simple condition `chi^N=1` alone is insufficient.

## 5. A sharp sparse-column Weyl-defect bound

**Theorem 5.** Let `B` be any complex `N x N` matrix with at most `s<N` nonzero entries in each column. Then

`||ZB-BD||_F >= 2 sin(pi/(2(s+1))) ||B||_F`.

This constant is sharp for any `1<=s<N`.

**Proof.** For the `k`th column `b`, the corresponding defect is `(Z-omega^k I)b`. Rephase `b_j=omega^(-kj)c_j`. Its squared norm is the sum of squared cyclic differences `sum_j |c_(j-1)-c_j|^2`. Because `c` has at most `s<N` nonzero coordinates, deleting its zero positions breaks the cycle into paths, each of length at most `s` with zero Dirichlet boundary. The smallest Dirichlet Laplacian eigenvalue on a path of length `s` is `4 sin^2(pi/(2(s+1)))`. Sum over columns. Equality comes from one contiguous length-`s` support with sine eigenvector and the appropriate phase reweighting.

If `B` is invertible and `M=B^-1`, then

`B (MZ-DM) B = ZB-BD`.

Therefore the single-Green first Weyl defect cannot vanish as long as its denominator has sparse columns. This conclusion holds **independently of conditioning**. A usable absolute Frobenius lower bound also follows by dividing the right-hand theorem by `||B||_2^2`; it may shrink under large gain, so it is *not* a universal uniform-error bound without normalization.

## 6. New conditioning-independent approximation bound for pure products of Green solves

Suppose `B=M^-1` has at most `s` nonzeros in each row. Set

`delta = sqrt(max(0,1-s/N))`.

**Theorem 6.** With normalized unitary `F`, every such invertible `M` obeys

`||M-F||_2 >= delta/(1+delta)`.

**Proof.** Every row of the unitary `F^-1` has `N` entries of modulus `1/sqrt(N)`, so any `s`-row-sparse matrix `B` has `||B-F^-1||_F^2 >= N-s`, hence `||B-F^-1||_2 >= delta`. Let `epsilon=||M-F||_2`. If `epsilon>=1` we are done. Otherwise `||B||_2<=1/(1-epsilon)`, since `M` is within `epsilon` of a unitary. The identity

`B-F^-1 = B(F-M)F^-1`

gives `delta<=epsilon/(1-epsilon)`, which rearranges to the theorem.

If `M` is a product of `t` Green inverses of matrices having at most `s` entries per row, with arbitrary intermediate row/column monomial coordinate reordering, then `M^-1` has at most `s^t` nonzeros per row. Thus

`||M-F||_2 >= sqrt(max(0,1-s^t/N)) / (1+sqrt(max(0,1-s^t/N)))`.

For fixed `s` and `t=o(log_s N)` this bound approaches `1/2`, with **no condition-number requirement**. This extends Phase 15's *exact* inverse-support obstruction to approximation. It does **not** apply to sums of inverses, whose total inverse may be dense.

## 7. Another no-go: shift-stationary Green sums, even at resonance

Suppose every Green denominator is a constant-coefficient tridiagonal *path* matrix

`B=aI+bL+cU` (`L` lower and `U` upper noncyclic shift), expressed in the same cyclic coordinate frame. It need not be Hermitian, normal, uniformly conditioned, or commuting with other denominators.

**Lemma.** For `N>=4`, `[B,Z]=BZ-ZB` has rank at most `2`. An explicit decomposition is

`[B,Z]= e_0 (c e_0^T-b e_(N-2)^T) + (b e_1-c e_(N-1)) e_(N-1)^T`.

Since `[B^-1,Z]=-B^-1[B,Z]B^-1`, the inverse also has commutator rank at most `2`. A sum of `q` such inverses has rank at most `2q`, and a product of `t` such sums satisfies

`rank([M,Z])<=2qt`,

by `[AB,Z]=A[B,Z]+[A,Z]B` and rank subadditivity.

**Theorem 7.** For even `N`, `rank([F,Z])=N`, because `[F,Z]=(D-Z)F` and `det(D-Z)=prod_j omega^j -1=-2 !=0`. Thus an exact shift-stationary Green circuit must have `2qt>=N`.

**Approximation:** `||[F,Z]||_F^2=2N`, `||[F,Z]||_2<=2`. If a candidate has commutator rank at most `r`, the best-rank approximation theorem and `||[(F-M),Z]||_F<=2sqrt(N)||F-M||_2` yield

`||M-F||_2 >= sqrt(max(0,1/2-r/N))`.

Consequently, `2qt=o(N)` shift-stationary Green products have spectral error at least `1/sqrt(2)-o(1)`, even at near resonance. Exact modular rank certificates at `N=16,32,64` and numerical independent controls agree.

**Crucial limitation:** arbitrary intermediate diagonal phase masks or independently permuted Green path orders can have full-rank commutators with `Z`. They are excluded from this theorem. This is *not* a lower bound on our surviving permuted Green-sum architecture.

## 8. Real-core obstruction and sharp one-mask phase classification

**Real core (arbitrary outer monomial gauges).** For any `N>=3`, no real `N x N` matrix, even after arbitrary complex invertible outer diagonal scalings and row/column permutations, can equal `F_N`. If the real matrix has a zero entry, the Fourier matrix's nonzero entries immediately exclude it. Otherwise all of its cross-ratios are real, whereas the Fourier cross-ratio multiset contains the non-real primitive root `omega`. This directly rules out all-real near-resonant Green product cores as *exact* Fourier realizations up to outer gauges, regardless of path permutations, commutativity, or conditioning. Internal non-real mixing is necessary.

**Theorem 8 (single complex mask between real mixers).** Suppose exactly

`F_N=A Q B`

with `A,B` arbitrary **real invertible** matrices, and `Q` diagonal, complex, invertible. There are no other non-real outer or intermediate gauges. Let `c=number of coordinates fixed by reflection J`; `c=2` for even `N`, `c=1` for odd `N`. Then every diagonal entry of `Q` is **either purely real or purely imaginary, never mixed**, with exactly `(N+c)/2` real entries and `(N-c)/2` imaginary entries. If `|Q_kk|=1`, every entry is in `{1,-1,i,-i}`.

**Proof.** Put `F=C+iS`, where `C=Re F` and `S=Im F` are real symmetric. Unitarity gives `C^2+S^2=I` and `CS=SC`, while `F^2=J` gives `C^2-S^2=J` and `CS+SC=0`. Hence `CS=0`, `C^2=(I+J)/2` and `S^2=(I-J)/2`. Therefore

`rank(C)=(N+c)/2`, `rank(S)=(N-c)/2`.

Because `A,B` are real invertible,

`C=A Re(Q) B`, `S=A Im(Q) B`.

Their ranks count respectively how many entries of `Q` have nonzero real and imaginary parts. Since their sum is exactly `N`, and no entry of invertible `Q` is zero, no entry can contribute to both counts. The stated counts and axis restriction follow. This bound is **sharp**: the real orthogonal Fourier eigenbasis from Phase 18 realizes `F=O diag(±1,±i) O^T`, with exactly the required multiplicities.

**Numerical independent controls:** `N=4,8,16,32,64,128`, with reconstruction errors below `3e-14`. Dense `O` is not a claimed fast transform. Adding arbitrary complex *outer* gauges changes the assumptions and need not preserve this axis classification.

## 9. What was actually tested

- A genuine `O(qtN)` field-operation implementation of a product of two sums of independently permuted tridiagonal inverses (`t=2`, `q=2`), with input/output cyclotomic phase gauges for each term.
- A separate `t=2`, `q=2` **near-resonant** family with each real denominator proportional to `I-[(N-1-r)/(2N)] T_path`, `r` small and fixed, then independently permuted and cyclotomic-phase-gauged. Over the complex reals, its smallest singular value decreases with `N`; over the field, the experiment checks exact algebra and does not assign a condition number.
- Both tested families failed both Weyl identities and the two-column projective necessary condition at checked sizes. The near-resonant case was checked through `N=65536` using linear-work oracle queries, not a dense matrix.
- The exact FFT and arbitrary real/imaginary outer-scaled/permuted Fourier controls were accepted. One-equation-only and wrong-scalar controls were distinguished correctly.
- `74` new regression tests passed; this is independent of the `128` inherited tests from Phases 12–19, which are rerunnable from the nested checkpoint.

**Not proved:** a uniform obstruction against all possible independently permuted, parameter-tuned noncommuting Green sums with multiple complex masks; an exact `o(N log N)` FFT; a bit-complexity speedup for arbitrary integer multiplication.

## 10. Follow-on gate: Phase 21

Use the exact projective constraints as *design equations*, not merely rejection heuristics. For a parametrized Green-sum product `M(theta)`, two-column ratios must lie in one `N`-root subgroup with uniform multiplicity. After fixing candidate row/column residue permutations, enforce

`M[j,k] M[j0,l] = omega^(alpha_j delta) M[j,l] M[j0,k]`

symbolically for all `j` (choose distinct `k,l` and `j0`, and a unit `delta` where feasible), before testing full Weyl duality. Search small `N` with **genuinely internal complex phases**, noncommuting independently ordered denominators, and independent exact field certificates. If a candidate passes, recover the complete monomial Fourier structure, prove the identity for arbitrary `N`, and **count all forward solve, scalar preparation, precision and memory operations**. Never infer asymptotic savings from the number of phase masks, Green terms, or cached graph nodes alone.

## 11. Public context and novelty caution

The Haagerup cross-ratio invariant is established prior art, and the discrete Weyl relations, Dirichlet path eigenvalue, rank inequality and Fourier parity eigenspaces are classical. These results are a *verified, project-specific combination* and experimental toolchain, not a claim of first publication for these ingredients.

OpenAI's publicly documented exact-arithmetic complex-register model already states an all-length algorithm with `O(N (log N)^(1-10^-13))` work and unlimited exact complex scalar precision; it does not establish a finite-precision numerical speedup. See https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/lean/docs/130.md . Our Phase 20 results do not improve on that algorithm's general upper bound.
