# Hero17 Phase10: independent spectral-projector audit

## Decision

**Accept the projector algebra, periodic-comb formula, sparse exceptional corrections, exact correction rank, and fixed-complexity linear filtering theorem.** The input vector is arbitrary. The output is the complete filtered time-domain vector P_K x, not the full Fourier spectrum F_N x. The batch construction validly shares repeated bases, selected Fourier measurements and correction vectors while still materializing every requested output.

The numerical field-operation comparison is reproducible analytically from the static loops, including root validation and inverse exponentiation. The source's stronger wording about counting every integer/index or memory operation should be qualified: its integer counters and write counters are summaries, not complete instruction/store ledgers. This does not undermine the fixed-d,q arithmetic theorem.

The distinction from Phase9 is correct. Its m×m quotient-remainder reflections can have a nonzero commutator; the N×N Fourier spectral projectors all commute. Phase9's previously accepted theorem was already restricted to its quotient interfaces and common basis conjugation. Phase10 prevents applying that theorem to different operators; it does not refute the scoped rank result.

## Algebra and exact output contract

Assume N is invertible in the field and ω has exact order N. Use the negative-sign unnormalized transform

    X_k = sum_j ω^(−kj) x_j,
    P_K = F_N^(−1) diag(1_K) F_N.

Diagonal mask multiplication immediately gives

    P_A P_B = P_B P_A = P_(A intersection B),
    P_(A union B) = P_A+P_B−P_A P_B,
    P_A²=P_A,  P_(complement A)=I−P_A.

For d dividing N, let m=N/d, S be a set of residues modulo d and B={k : k mod d belongs to S}. Character orthogonality shows

    (P_B x)_j = sum_(ell=0 to d−1) c_ell x_(j+ell*m mod N),
    c_ell = (1/d) sum_(a in S) ω^(−a*ell*m).

Indeed the sum over frequencies a+dr vanishes unless j−input_index is a multiple of m. The remaining phase has the stated negative sign. A singleton correction is

    (P_{k} x)_j = (1/N) ω^(kj) sum_s ω^(−ks) x_s.

If E+ is outside B and E− is inside B, both without duplicates, then

    P_K = P_B + sum_(k in E+) P_k − sum_(k in E−) P_k,
    K=(B minus E−) union E+.

This gives O((d+q)N) field arithmetic for q=|E+|+|E−|, plus the supplied-field plan. It applies to every fresh x. A dense matrix representation does not prevent this cheap formula; it is a cyclic comb plus q rank-one Fourier modes.

## Exact rank and restricted lower bounds

Because F_N is invertible,

    rank(P_A−P_B)=rank(diag(1_A−1_B))=|A symmetric_difference B|.

Thus q exceptional frequencies have exact additive correction rank q. Any factorization P_K−P_B=UV needs inner dimension at least q. This rank statement does not prove Ω(qN) scalar arithmetic for an unrestricted circuit.

For comparison, a one-root exchange has quotient-transfer commutator rank two when 0<t=1<m, while its complementary true projectors satisfy P_A P_B=P_B P_A=0. These are different dimensions and interface meanings. Neither a coordinate-invariant quotient commutator nor the adjective “quantum” changes the distinction.

The binary-signature barrier is also valid with its explicit restriction. B binary masks give at most 2^B distinct membership signatures. If those signatures alone must identify N frequency labels, B≥ceil(log₂N). Separately materializing each full N-vector then requires at least N ceil(log₂N) output entries. It does not apply to implicit shared representations, different primitives, or unrestricted arithmetic combinations and is not a general FFT lower bound.

## Preparation, uniformity and exact scalar counts

The finite-field executor assumes a supplied odd prime p with N dividing p−1 and, for its word-RAM statement, p polynomially bounded in N. It checks root order by testing ω^N and ω^(N/q) for each distinct prime divisor q of N. Trial factorization is charged at a coarse integer-operation level; primality and root discovery are supplied obligations. `validate_root=False` shifts the order promise back to the caller.

The plan generates N−1 root powers, computes d weights, and computes 1/d and 1/N by charged binary exponentiation to p−2. No hidden constant-time modular inverse is used. Root validation costs O(log²N) field products, and trial division O(sqrt N) integer iterations; both fit within the linear plan envelope. The log p term matters before imposing p=N^O(1).

For the exact source loops define

    B(e)=bit_length(e)−1+popcount(e), for e>0,
    V(N)=B(N)+sum_(q distinct prime divisor of N) B(N/q).

B(e) counts the field products used by its binary-power routine. If r is the number of nonzero comb weights, the single-filter counts are

    plan = V(N)+(N−1)+2B(p−2)+d|S|+d,
    apply = 2rN + q(6N+1) + |E−|.

These include separately generated phase powers and all field additions in the exceptional-mode loops, including the removable final phase updates. For the swapped parity mask, r=2,q=2,|E−|=1 and apply=16N+3. The total is 17N+6+2B(p−2)+V(N).

The source's generic two-FFT same-output comparator has, at dyadic N,

    2V(N)+3N−2+3N log₂N+B(N−1)+B(p−2)

field additions/multiplications. It includes two root validations/tables, forward and inverse butterflies, inverse-root powering and final normalization.

Our independently evaluated formulas reproduce all five saved rows. At N=65,536, p=65,537, the projector total is 1,114,213, compared with 3,342,462 for the generic FFT-mask-inverse-FFT pipeline. This is a comparison for the same filtering task. The comparator is not an optimized periodic/coset filter, and the numbers are not wall-clock or historical-novelty evidence.

## Batch sharing and its limits

For B requested output masks, let s be the number of distinct residue sets, q the number of distinct exceptional frequencies, h the total number of signed events across distinct patterns and v the number of patterns. The source shares each base, selected coefficient/response and pattern once, then copies or adds complete vectors for each consumer. Its field work is bounded by

    O(N(1+s*d+q+h+B)+B*d²+log p),

with O(N(s+q+v+B)+B*d) retained/output words. The B*N output writes remain even when masks repeat. The returned lists are separate arrays rather than free aliases.

For the recorded N=4,096 eight-output workload, d=4, two bases, two exceptional frequencies and one two-event pattern, the source loops yield 4,192 plan operations and 131,074 application operations: 135,266 total. Eight standalone filters total 623,304. We independently derive both figures. All 32,768 requested output coordinates are materialized. The source's 78.30% reduction is valid as this arithmetic-ledger ratio, not a universal filtering optimum or a speedup for a fresh full DFT.

Integer bookkeeping is less complete than the prose implies. Hash/set construction, repeated exception sorting, branch tests, many loop/index operations and allocation/initialization stores are not individually counted. In particular the general variable-q batch word cost also needs mask parsing and sorting costs; the displayed field bound is not an exact word-operation total. For fixed d,q and bounded canonical mask descriptions these costs do not destroy the claimed O(N) single-filter result or O(BN) output-scale contribution. `output_writes` records completed vector entries, not all intermediate memory traffic. Online ledgers also accumulate if the same object is applied repeatedly; differences or resets are needed to isolate one fresh application.

Exact complex arithmetic satisfies the algebraic identities, but this source does not prove stable floating-point evaluation or a precision bound. Modular arithmetic on log p-bit values is not bit-constant. These restrictions are separate from exact field correctness.
