# Phase 22 theorem — exact trace-cyclotomic elimination of Hankel-resultant shift roots

**Status (2026-10-10):** Proved algebraically, independently tested over finite fields. A project-specific improvement in certified sparse-rational discovery; **not an exact all-input sub-`N log N` DFT**. Familiar underlying objects are the Dickson/Chebyshev polynomials, cyclic polynomial gcds, Tonelli–Shanks and baby-step/giant-step. Novelty of the assembly as a research contribution is **not** established.

## Assumptions and inherited interface

Work over an odd prime field `F_p` with `N | (p-1)`, a supplied primitive `N`th root `omega`, and the same unit-cost field-operation model and exact Green identity as in Phase 21. Input `x` is arbitrary. The Phase 21 routine obtains `4s` direct Fourier probes of its dense Green residual, computes a universal self-reciprocal Hankel resultant, and extracts the *trace polynomial*

`U(w) = w^b V(w+w^(-1))`, with `b=s(s+1)(2s+1)/6`.

This identity, and the exact cyclic certificate that ultimately validates candidates, are preserved without change. For `s=4`, `deg V<=30`; for `s=8`, `deg V<=204`.

## Theorem 1: exact subgroup-root characterization

Let `D_n(t)` denote the Dickson polynomial (`D_0=2`, `D_1=t`, `D_{n+1}=t D_n - D_{n-1}`), characterized by

`D_n(w+w^(-1)) = w^n + w^(-n)`.

If `p` does not divide `N` (automatic from `N | p-1`), then in `F_p[t]`

`D_N(t)-2 = (t-2) [t+2 if N is even] prod_{ {w,w^-1} subset mu_N\{1,-1} } (t-w-w^-1)^2`.

The factors range over unordered inverse pairs. All root traces lie in the base field because `mu_N` lies in `F_p`. The identity is monic and has total degree N.

Let `V_*(t)` be V with all factors `(t-2)` removed; `t=2` corresponds only to `w=1` in odd characteristic. Assume `V` is nonzero. Then

> **There exists an admissible shift `1<=delta<N` with `U(omega^delta)=0` if and only if `gcd(V_*, D_N-2)` has positive degree.**

Proof: for a nonzero `w`, `D_N(w+w^-1)-2=(w^N-1)^2/w^N`. Its roots are exactly traces of Nth roots; after `t-2` removal all correspond to nonidentity roots. `U(w)=w^b V(t)` vanishes precisely when V(t) vanishes. The explicit factorization above proves both directions and that there are no extension-field false positives under our `N | p-1` assumption. The case `w=-1` (even N) remains allowed.

## Theorem 2: no degree-N polynomial is needed

One can compute `D_N(t) mod V_*` by binary doubling, reducing after every operation:

`D_(2m)=D_m^2-2`,
`D_(2m+1)=D_m D_(m+1)-t`,
`D_(2m+2)=D_(m+1)^2-2`.

The running pair `(D_m,D_(m+1))` is sufficient for arbitrary N. For `N=2^k`, starting from `D_1=t` takes exactly k modular squarings. Euclid's algorithm then computes `gcd(V_*,(D_N mod V_*)-2)`.

With `b=deg V` and dense schoolbook quotient-ring arithmetic, this costs `O(b^2 log N)` field operations plus polynomial gcd costs `O(b^2)` in the conservative ordinary-arithmetic bound. The actual executor counts field multiplications, additions and inversions, including modular reduction and Euclid steps. The initial `4s` input-dependent DFT probes are **still O(sN)** and are fully charged.

If `gcd=1`, returning directly to the exact FFT fallback cannot reject a genuine nondegenerate Phase 21 sparse-rational representation. This is a deterministic, always-sound rejection gate; unlike a random probe, it supplies an exact algebraic *nonexistence certificate* for every possible hidden shift in that model.

## Theorem 3: complete candidate enumeration without the old resultant-DFT scan

Let `G=gcd(V_*,D_N-2)`.

- If `deg G=0`, there are no admissible nonidentity shifts.
- If `deg G=1`, its unique root t produces `w` from `w^2-tw+1=0`. An exact field square-root algorithm gives at most two roots. Filter by `w^N=1`, recover their discrete-log indices relative to the supplied omega using a fully charged baby-step/giant-step lookup, and exclude delta=0. This enumerates every possible shift. For given field nonresidue/root setup, the index recovery uses `O(sqrt N)` group multiplications and storage (or a deterministic sorted-table variant with `O(sqrt N log N)` comparisons).
- If `deg G>=2`, the implementation makes **at most floor(N/2)** trace evaluations of `G(omega^delta+omega^(-delta))`, adding each inverse-index partner. Each evaluation costs `O(deg G)` field arithmetic. This is a complete deterministic fallback avoiding any N-length DFT of the resultant. General finite-field factorization is **not** treated as a free oracle.

The full cyclic convolution identity and all spectral-denominator nonzero checks from Phase 21 remain mandatory. A candidate rejected by those checks is followed by the exact Bluestein fallback. When the Phase 21 input is structured, s is fixed, and the trace gcd is linear, the complete charged branch is

`O_s(N + b^2 log N + sqrt N + log p)`

field operations, under a supplied quadratic nonresidue/standard finite-field setup. This simplifies to `O_s(N)` for fixed s, in the original arithmetic model. Even the general higher-degree trace-gcd scan is `O_s(N)`.

## Completeness and soundness distinctions

**Soundness for any input:** The trace test and candidate enumeration are exact. The output is accepted only after a complete cyclic certificate; otherwise an exact Bluestein DFT is executed. No output is guessed from a few Fourier samples.

**Completeness for a structured promise:** Same nondegeneracy gates as in Phase 21: nonzero first 4s Fourier probes, nonzero Hankel resultant, identifiable linear gamma gcd for an admissible shift, invertible recovered denominator at all N roots, and enough field nodes for short interpolation. Outside them the evaluator may fall back even for structured signals.

**Worst-case cost:** `O(N log N)` arithmetic with the general FFT fallback. Sparse rational families with fixed supports have dimension at most `s+1` after normalization, much smaller than N. This phase does not change that dimension obstruction, and does not claim a generic exact `o(N log N)` FFT.

**Bit time and stability:** Field inversions, modular roots and arithmetic are not constant time for arbitrary-size integer moduli. Finding a suitable prime, root of unity and quadratic nonresidue must be charged in any end-to-end bit-time theorem. No numerically stable floating-point implementation is established.

## Independent tests

- A separate direct oracle tests random polynomial V on all nonidentity Nth roots for odd and even lengths, using two prime fields. The sieve's `possible` flag agrees exactly, as do both complete candidate-recovery paths.
- An exhaustive GF(17) length-4 audit covers all 4,912 nonzero polynomials of degree at most two. It compares the symbolic trace gate against the explicit root traces `{0,-2}`.
- An independent direct `O(N^2)` DFT audit checks 15,492 output coordinates over primes 2013265921 and 998244353; compressed and fallback outputs match every direct coefficient.
- All 46 inherited tests plus 5 new tests pass, including full-vector adversarial perturbation checks.
