# FFT research: two-sided spectral elimination and an eight-parameter candidate chart

**Date:** October 9, 2026  
**Status:** Exact algebraic reductions + executable finite-field checks. **No new FFT algorithm or lower bound.**

## Research question

The historical goal remains a genuinely cheaper, all-output DFT for fresh arbitrary data, with all scalar arithmetic, indexing, and coefficient preparation charged. This checkpoint instead tackles the necessary algebraic feasibility of representing `F_n` as **two overlapping, globally separated rank-one semiseparable stages** with an intermediate permutation. This is a highly restricted architecture, not the general space of FFT circuits.

Original fixed n=8 ordering: `[7,0,6,3,4,1,2,5]`. The new all-n theorem applies to any permutation with first three entries `(n-1,0,n-2)` and the remaining indices arbitrary; other permutations are not covered.

## Main results

1. **Exact double-support polynomial reduction (n=8):** The previous `25 equations / 21 variables / max degree 6` becomes **20 equations / 16 variables / max degree 9**. Its exported exact coefficients in `Z[z]/(z^4+1)` comprise 8,437 nonzero terms and 2,858 distinct monomials. All ten removed stage identities vanish formally as polynomials, while the 20 remaining are verified against direct modular FFT products across both 12,289 and 65,537 and two eighth-root embeddings each.
2. **Three-frequency recurrence:** On an invertible regular chart, the lower-column `j=2` constraints force `h_0..h_(n-3)` into `span(1,z^(-k),z^(-2k))` and hence impose `n-5` exact linear recurrence conditions. The proof is independent of modular experiments. This is a restriction on **candidate factor parameters**, not on FFT input data.
3. **Eight-variable all-n rational chart:** Combining lower columns `j=1,2` and upper rows `i=1,2` reconstructs the candidate first stage from *eight scalar parameters independent of n* on a stricter open chart. Exactly `4n-14` of the B-stage membership identities are then identically satisfied. The unsolved membership system has `(n-4)(n-5)` rational equations in 8 variables (12 in 8 at n=8). We have **not** solved it.
4. **Exceptional patch control:** An exact invertible n=8 example over each of two finite fields satisfies ten of the structural conditions and has `S_1=0`, so a proof for the eight-variable chart cannot be silently extended to the excluded branches. The exception-complete quadratic system from the previous checkpoint is retained.

A singular target example is not mistaken for a solution; the determinant and endpoint chart conditions are audited explicitly.

## Scope of the gain

The polynomial systems are *algebraic feasibility problems*. Reducing their variables is useful for computer algebra, but it does **not** reduce the number of arithmetic operations to apply a valid FFT to an input vector. In particular, the eight parameters specify a candidate first-stage matrix constrained by eighteen (at n=8) identities; **they do not encode the entire Fourier transform**. The remaining equations and the factorization/nonzero conditions have not been solved.

Our n=8 model is the **global separated-generator chart**, stricter than the full class of rank-one quasiseparable operators when corners vanish. Our proof includes only the stated endpoint and denominator assumptions. Do not generalize the result to arbitrary internal permutations, unrestricted global stages, other FFT architectures, or global FFT complexity lower bounds.

## Research follow-up

1. Solve or eliminate the **12 rational equations in eight variables at n=8**, using exact arithmetic over `Q(zeta8)` with saturation by all rational denominators and `det(C)`, B pivot, and inverse-stage-corner factors. Finite-field feasibility tests are excellent diagnostics but do not by themselves prove characteristic-zero impossibility.
2. Enumerate exceptional branches `S_k=0`, `Delta_k=0`, 2×2 determinant=0`, chart corners/diagonal=0`, or use the original exception-complete 176-quadric system; never conclude universal impossibility from the generic chart alone.
3. Seek a Fourier-specific impossibility derived from the three-frequency recurrence **without solving every finite-size polynomial system**. The all-n recurrence and eight-parameter theorem offer a potential algebraic route; no such proof exists in this archive.
4. If an exact factorization is found, verify **every Fourier entry** and compare the all-length arithmetic cost (including permutations, updates, and coefficient preparation) against optimized FFT implementations. Small-n factorizations are not enough.
