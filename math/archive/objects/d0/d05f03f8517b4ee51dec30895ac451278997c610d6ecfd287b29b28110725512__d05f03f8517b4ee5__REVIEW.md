# Independent review of the sparse-surrogate addition

Reviewed on 2026-10-09. Original source was not modified:
`research_inputs/sparse_surrogate/fft130_adaptive_merge/`.

Scope: `SPARSE_SURROGATE_RESEARCH.md`, `sparse_surrogate.py`,
`sparse_surrogate_fast.py`, `complex_surrogate_search.py`, their focused tests
and captured results. The sparse backend identity was read only as a dependency;
the older #130/adaptive project was not re-reviewed.

## Verdict

The new all-bin approximation theorem is correct in its ideal randomized
arithmetic model. It applies to each fresh arbitrary input, rather than only
a finite test family. Combining it with the stated sparse-input transform
does give O(N log log N) work for fixed additive accuracy and constant or
polynomially small failure probability. The source explicitly distinguishes
this from the unresolved exact FFT goal.

The supplied implementation is numerical: complex128 values, floating phases,
floating sampling probabilities, and native FFTs. It is not an exact-complex
algorithm or a certified finite-precision implementation of the theorem.
There is no exact root-recognition or lookup mechanism in these additions.

## The theorem and full cost

For one sample, each Fourier contribution has modulus
a=||x||_1/sqrt(N). Hoeffding for its real and imaginary components, followed by
a union bound, gives

    P(max_f |(U_N y-U_N x)_f| >= epsilon a)
        <= 4N exp(-k epsilon^2/4).

The constant 4 in the required sample count is correct. Replacing the target
by epsilon ||x||_2 introduces tau^2=||x||_1^2/(N||x||_2^2), as stated.
The adaptive scale is weaker since ||x||_1/sqrt(N)<=||x||_2.

If s is the sampled support and b is the least power of two at least s,
the factorization N=bm prepares at most sm<=N phase contributions and at most
N active twiddles. It then performs m length-b FFTs. Initialization, scattering,
output materialization, permutation/copying and normalization each cost O(N).
Several O(N) buffers are live in the vectorized implementation. These costs
are compatible with O(N+N log(s+2)), but must not be replaced by the inner
butterfly count alone.

The source sampler uses a cumulative scan, k binary searches, and sorting to
coalesce samples: O(N+k log N+k log k), not literally the stated alternate
O(N+k log k) sorted-uniform merge implementation. This distinction does not
change the fixed-accuracy result, since k=O(log N). The complete implemented
asymptotic cost is O(N+k log N+k log k+N log(s+2)).

In an ideal exact-root model, one supplied primitive Nth root can prepare an
N-entry power table in O(N) operations; those costs and storage can be charged.
The current backend instead evaluates floating exponentials. Sampling also
uses magnitudes, real comparisons and randomness. Consequently the approximate
model must be stated independently of a straight-line exact-complex FFT model.
Finite-precision sampling error, root error and FFT roundoff are not included
in the Hoeffding proof.

## Why sparse correction does not make this exact

Let x have N nonzero entries and y have at most k nonzero entries. Outside
supp(y), the exact residual r=x-y equals x, so

    |supp(r)| >= N-k.

For k<N/2, this exceeds N/2. This project's sparse-correction evaluator therefore
chooses b=N and m=1, and calls a complete N-point FFT. Computing U_N y and then
correcting U_N r does not produce an exact improvement through that evaluator.
This is a statement about this proposed correction, not a lower bound on all
possible algorithms for the residual. A different independently proved fast
representation of r could change the conclusion.

For arbitrary complex inputs there is no fixed output lattice spacing that
turns a nonzero additive error guarantee into exact recovery by rounding.
Neither more finite tests nor a finite support/weight catalogue supplies such
a guarantee. Finding, storing and applying a data-dependent relation would
also have to be charged.

The additive scale matters. In fact the exact sampling calculation gives

    E ||U_N(y-x)||_2^2 = (||x||_1^2-||x||_2^2)/k.

This follows from independence and unitarity: a single sample vector has squared
norm ||x||_1^2, mean x, and the variance of its k-sample average is divided by k.
For flat x, the relative mean squared l2 error is (N-1)/k. Thus the valid
all-bin additive guarantee does not imply small relative l2 error or small
relative error on each bin. This identity does not contradict the theorem.

## Concrete implementation findings and local fix

1. **Reproduced finite-input range bug.** With N=64 and x_j=1e-200, the norm
   calculation underflows to zero. `approximate_dft_all` returns the zero vector;
   its DC error divided by the true ||x||_2 is 1, violating epsilon=.25.
   With x_j=1e200, intermediate squares overflow and sample-count conversion
   raises `ValueError: cannot convert float NaN to integer`. Both true output
   DC values, 8e-200 and 8e200, are representable.
2. `scaled_adapter.py` fixes those cases without changing the source. It divides
   real and imaginary components by their maximum absolute component, calls
   the prototype, and rescales the result. The plan explicitly identifies norms
   measured on the scaled input. Six range/scale cases pass. This is a targeted
   numerical fix, not a proof of a floating-error bound for all representable
   inputs. Legitimately overflowing outputs and extreme relative dynamic range
   remain outside such a claim.
3. Epsilon/delta are not consistently validated when explicit samples or a zero
   input bypass validation. The adapter validates them on every call and requires
   an explicit sample count to be a positive integer. Explicit sample overrides
   below the theorem budget still do not inherit the theorem's confidence.
4. When exact-sparse bypass is disabled, the dense-fallback test occurs after
   allocating and drawing k samples. A huge budget can therefore fail before
   reaching the intended fallback. Move that budget check before sampling in a
   future source revision. The adapter does not change this separate behavior.
5. The fast backend assumes validated support. It does not reject out-of-range
   indices or support size greater than N; the sampler satisfies that contract.
   As a standalone API it should validate indices and support before computing b.
6. `complex_surrogate_search.py` launches the complete search at import time and
   writes to a hard-coded `...` destination. For reuse, put execution
   under a main guard and accept an output path. This is a portability issue,
   separate from its optimization model.

"Exact-sparse bypass" means no Monte Carlo sparsification. The sparse DFT
identity is exact algebraically, but the delivered float output still has
roundoff; its own tests correctly use numerical tolerances.

## Captured experiments and finite-family boundaries

- Seven focused source tests were rerun and passed. The document's broader
  41-test statement was not independently reproduced in this focused review.
- The saved 1,020 Monte Carlo trials use five generated input vectors, with
  repeated fresh samples for each. Their reported zero failures agree with the
  captured file; this is empirical evidence, not the probability proof.
- All 12 saved vectorized timing cases are slower than the full native FFT,
  by about 2.71x to 11.54x. They test flat input only. The inner butterfly ratios
  (0.45 to 0.714) are neither total-operation ratios nor measured speedups.
  Eight independent small valid-support backend checks also matched NumPy;
  that review found the indexing and full-output scaling consistent.
- The LP uses floating Fourier entries and a rectangular objective t bounding
  real and imaginary sidelobes. The resulting magnitude is at most sqrt(2)t;
  minimizing t is not exactly minimizing the maximum complex magnitude. It
  reports actual numerical magnitudes after solving, which is appropriate.
- Its fixed-support LP optima and random support search cover only finitely many
  candidates. Missing a fixed-k family proves no lower bound. Even the optimum
  for the true circular objective on one support is not computed by this LP.
  Its O(Nk) coefficient matrix construction, numerical solver, trial count and
  stored weights are preparation costs, not free lookup. This experiment
  concerns flat-input probability-mass surrogates, not all arbitrary inputs.
- The source's claimed connection with optimal Abelian Cayley sparsifiers is
  not independently verified here. No general or complex-weight lower bound is
  inferred from that citation or from the LP results.
