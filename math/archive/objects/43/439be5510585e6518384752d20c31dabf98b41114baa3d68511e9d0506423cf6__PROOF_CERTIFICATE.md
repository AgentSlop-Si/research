# Hero #17 — Phase 17 exact proof certificate

**Date:** 10 October 2026  
**Status:** Seven scoped algebraic results, independently tested over finite fields. **No universal linear-time FFT or unrestricted FFT lower bound. Historical novelty not established.**

## 0. Setting and model

Let `K` be a field of characteristic not dividing `N`, containing a primitive `N`-th root `ω`, and let

\[
F_{k,j}=\omega^{-kj},\qquad 0\le k,j<N.
\]

A *monomial Fourier gauge* means arbitrary permutations and nonzero coordinate scalings on **both** endpoints: `W=L F R`, where `L,R` are invertible monomial matrices. Rows of `W` can therefore be listed in *Fourier-frequency order* even when their physical coordinates are arbitrarily permuted. Every collection of `q` physical columns still corresponds to `q` distinct Fourier nodes, up to nonzero scalings.

We investigate the narrowly specified *feedback quotient*

\[
\boxed{W=(P+E_P)(Q+E_Q)^{-1}}, \tag{0.1}
\]

where the corrected denominator is invertible, `P` has no more than `t` allowed nonzero entries in each column, `Q` has no more than `s` allowed nonzero entries in each column, and the corrections have ranks `r_P,r_Q`. Sparse patterns may be completely arbitrary and include long-distance edges. The support sets can depend on `N` and can differ in every column. Exact matrix rank and field operations are the complexity units unless noted.

### Lemma 1 — consecutive Fourier rows give an injective Vandermonde restriction

Let `I` be `L` successive Fourier-frequency rows of `W` (allowing wrap around `N`) and `J` any subset of at most `L` physical input coordinates. Then `W[I,J]` has full column rank `|J|`.

**Proof.** After undoing row/column permutations and scalings, the restricted matrix has entries `ω^{-(k0+i)j}` for `0<=i<L` and distinct exponents `j` from `J`. Taking its first `|J|` rows produces a Vandermonde matrix at distinct nodes `ω^{-j}`. Its determinant is nonzero. A cyclic wrap contributes no change since `ω^N=1`. QED.

### Theorem 2 — graph-neighborhood / feedback cut certificate

Suppose there exists a row set `I` as in Lemma 1 and a set `B` of `b` physical columns such that:

1. `P[I,B]=0`;
2. the union `J=⋃_{j∈B} supp(Q[:,j])` has `|J| <= |I|`.

Then any exact representation (0.1) necessarily obeys

\[
\boxed{r_P+r_Q\ge b}.\tag{2.1}
\]

**Proof.** Assume instead `r_P+r_Q<b`. Restrict both correction matrices to columns in `B`. Rank-nullity ensures a nonzero vector `c` supported inside `B` with `E_P c=0=E_Q c`, because the intersection of the two kernels has dimension at least `b-r_P-r_Q>0`. Multiplying (0.1) by the corrected denominator gives

\[
W (Q+E_Q)c=(P+E_P)c\quad\Longrightarrow\quad WQc=Pc.
\]

The right side is zero on rows `I`. The left input vector `Qc` is supported on `J`. Lemma 1 says `W[I,J]` is injective, hence `Qc=0`. But then `(Q+E_Q)c=Qc=0`, contradicting invertibility and `c!=0`. QED.

The test is **combinatorial** once `I,B,J` are known: it depends only on the sparse incidence graph. It is sound over composite lengths and arbitrary endpoint permutations. It does **not** infer arithmetic operation cost from correction rank.

### Theorem 3 — a uniform sparse-degree lower bound

Under (0.1), write `s,t >= 1`. Then

\[
\boxed{r_P+r_Q\ge\left\lfloor\frac{N}{1+st}\right\rfloor}.\tag{3.1}
\]

**Proof.** Let `b=floor(N/(1+st))`. If `b=0` the claim is vacuous. Put `L=sb` and consider all `N` cyclic intervals of length `L` in the Fourier-frequency row ordering (including an arbitrary physical row permutation). Fix a column of `P`: it has at most `t` nonzero positions. Each such position belongs to exactly `L` of the intervals, so at most `tL` intervals meet its support. Thus the average number of columns whose `P` support is disjoint from an interval is at least `N-tL`. Since

\[
N-tL=N-stb\ge b,
\]

some interval `I` has at least `b` disjoint-support columns. Pick any `b` of them for `B`. Their `Q` neighbor union has cardinality at most `sb=L=|I|`. Apply Theorem 2. QED.

**Strong scope detail:** both Fourier endpoints may be independently permuted and diagonally rescaled. No natural/affine endpoint order restriction is needed for Theorem 3, unlike some older Phase 22 gates.

**Application:** `Q=I-αS-βT` has at most two nonzero entries per column (the `I` and `T` components share one diagonal). With `P=I`, any such quotient with low-rank corrections needs `r_P+r_Q >= floor(N/3)`. For fixed degrees `s,t`, the budget grows linearly in `N`. The inequality is necessary, not claimed sharp.

### Corollary 4 — stronger bound for local cyclic bands

Suppose `P` is cyclicly banded with radius `a`, and `Q` with radius `b`, in the natural Fourier coordinate ordering. Then

\[
\boxed{r_P+r_Q\ge\max\bigl(0,\lfloor N/2\rfloor-a-b\bigr)}.\tag{4.1}
\]

**Proof.** It is enough to treat `a+b<floor(N/2)`. Choose

\[
m=\left\lceil\frac{N-2a+2b}{2}\right\rceil,
\quad I=\{0,\ldots,m-1\},\quad
B=\{m+a,\ldots,N-a-1\}.
\]

There are `|B|=floor(N/2)-a-b` columns in `B`. Every entry of `P[I,B]` vanishes by cyclic distance `>a`. The union of their allowed `Q` row supports is contained in the cyclic interval from `m+a-b` through `N-a-1+b`, of size `|B|+2b<=m=|I|`. Theorem 2 applies. QED.

This refines the no-feedback Phase 16 bandwidth gate to **arbitrary low-rank feedback on both sides**. It requires aligned local bands; it is not invariant under arbitrary physical permutations as a *band statement*. Theorem 3 still covers such permutations.

## 5. Fourier intertwining and covariance-rank obstruction

Let `S e_j=e_(j+1 mod N)`, `T e_j=ω^j e_j`. Then

\[
F S = T^{-1}F,\qquad F e_0=\mathbf1.
\]

For any operator `A`, define

\[
\mathcal D(A)=AS-T^{-1}A.
\]

For any rank-`r` correction `R`,

\[
\operatorname{rank}\mathcal D(R)
 \le \operatorname{rank}(RS)+\operatorname{rank}(T^{-1}R)\le2r.
\]

Thus **if** `A+R=F`, necessarily

\[
\boxed{r\ge\left\lceil\operatorname{rank}\mathcal D(A)/2\right\rceil}.\tag{5.1}
\]

This applies to *any* baseline `A`, even one with a dense inverse. It is a linear-algebraic distance-from-intertwining certificate, not a lower bound on circuit work.

### Theorem 6 — near-full covariance defect for a magnetic Green solve

Let `Q=I-αS-βT` be invertible, `α != 0`, and `N>=3`. Then

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}\mathcal D(Q^{-1})\ge N-2}.\tag{6.1}
\]

**Proof.** Multiplying the defect on the left and right by invertible `Q` preserves rank:

\[
Q\mathcal D(Q^{-1})Q=SQ-QT^{-1}=:B.
\]

Direct expansion gives, for every `i` modulo `N`,

\[
(By)_i=(\beta-\omega^{-i})y_i+
(1+\alpha\omega^{-(i-1)}-\beta\omega^{i-1})y_{i-1}-\alpha y_{i-2}.
\]

Since `α !=0`, equations for `i=N-1,N-2,...,2` determine `y_{N-3},...,y_0` uniquely from the two terminal parameters `y_{N-1},y_{N-2}`. Thus `dim ker B <=2`, and `rank B>=N-2`. QED.

A rank-`r` global-feedback perturbation `Q'=Q+UV^T` (both denominators invertible) satisfies

\[
Q'^{-1}-Q^{-1}=-Q^{-1}UV^T Q'^{-1},
\]

whose rank is at most `r`. Consequently, if `Q'^{-1}=F` (or if any additive rank-`r` correction of `Q^{-1}` equals `F`), then

\[
\boxed{r\ge\left\lceil\frac{N-2}{2}\right\rceil}.\tag{6.2}
\]

No endpoint diagonal or permutation gauges are claimed in (6.2); those would change the covariance defect unless the intertwining is transformed accordingly.

### Theorem 7 — generically full defect for even `N`

Over an infinite field such as `C`, for even `N`, a generic choice of `α,β` with `Q` invertible satisfies

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}\mathcal D(Q^{-1})=N}.\tag{7.1}
\]

**Proof.** `det(SQ-QT^-1)` and `det Q` are polynomials in `α,β`. At `α=β=0`, the second is 1. The first reduces to `det(S-T^-1)`. A vector in its kernel obeys `y_{i-1}=ω^{-i}y_i`; cycling around implies `(1-∏_{i=0}^{N-1}ω^{-i})y_0=0`. For even `N`, this product is `-1`, so `S-T^-1` is invertible (characteristic is not 2 because it contains a primitive even-order root). Hence the determinant polynomial is not identically zero. Outside the union of these two proper algebraic zero loci, both are invertible and the defect has rank `N`. This open set intersects `αβ !=0`. QED.

Accordingly, generic even-length Green inverse plus an additive rank-`r` update needs `r>=N/2` if it is to equal `F`.

## 8. Constructive *non-Fourier* feedback executor

For any `x`, solving `(I-αS-βT)y=x` is the cyclic recurrence

\[
(1-\beta\omega^j)y_j-\alpha y_{j-1}=x_j.
\]

In the implementation, all `d_j=1-βω^j` must be nonzero, and `1-α^N-β^N !=0`; the plan rejects inputs violating those preconditions. Batch inversion of all `d_j` uses one field inversion, forward/backward product scans, and linear arithmetic. Writing `y_j=A_j z+B_j` for `z=y_(N-1)` leads to two vector passes and an explicit scalar closure equation. The `A_j` are precomputed once, and arbitrary RHS vectors cost `O(N)` operations.

For `Q'=Q+uv^T`, Sherman–Morrison gives

\[
Q'^{-1}x=Q^{-1}x-
\frac{Q^{-1}u\,v^TQ^{-1}x}{1+v^TQ^{-1}u}.
\]

After one extra prepared solve `Q^-1u` and one scalar inversion, each application is still `O(N)`. This handles a genuinely dense, global feedback edge and noncommuting `S,T` without hiding an FFT. It is a different operator, not an exception to Theorem 6.

The **charged scalar field-op ledger** (add/sub, multiply, invert each counted as one) for the saved implementation is:

- magnetic Green plan, including all `N` phase powers and batch inversions: `8N+2` operations;
- complete Green-plus-rank-one-feedback preparation: `15N+5` operations;
- one new-vector feedback execution: `9N+2` operations;
- preparation plus one execution: `24N+7` operations.

These counts assume a supplied valid primitive root `ω` and exact prime-field arithmetic. Finding a primitive root, Python integer/array indexing, and audit-only direct exponentiations are **not included** in that field-op ledger; this is not an all-length bit-complexity statement. All algebraic operations performed by the actual plan and online solver are included. The code maintains `O(N)` storage.

## 9. Fast rejection, not an exact proof

For an arbitrary black-box linear operator `M`, a random probe `v∈K^N` checks `M(Sv)=T^-1(Mv)` with two operator calls. If the nonzero covariance-defect matrix has rank at least one and `K=F_p`, each independent uniform random probe passes accidentally with probability at most `1/p`. After `q` probes the false-accept probability is at most `p^-q`. A separate test checks `M e_0=ones`. This is a standard Freivalds-type **probabilistic rejection screen**, not a formal certificate of all-basis equality.

## 10. Conclusions and novelty boundary

- **Proved:** an arbitrary-sparse-graph / low-rank feedback tradeoff, valid for composite lengths and arbitrarily permuted/scaled Fourier endpoints.
- **Proved:** a stronger local-band feedback cut.
- **Proved:** a near-full Fourier covariance defect for mixed magnetic Green inverses and a generic full-rank even-length case.
- **Implemented:** a completely charged (conditional on supplied root) linear-work rank-one-feedback Green evaluator; independently validated against dense Gaussian elimination and whole-vector residuals.
- **Not proved:** a universal sub-`N log N` or `O(N)` exact FFT, any unrestricted FFT time lower bound, numerical floating-point stability, or publication-level historical novelty.

**Known foundations:** finite Fourier Vandermonde restrictions; Tao's Fourier uncertainty principle (2005, https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0308286); matrix-rigidity questions (Codenotti et al., *Matrix rigidity*, Linear Algebra Appl. 304, 2000); Woodbury/Sherman–Morrison identity; finite Weyl operators; Morgenstern/Ailon circuit-model lower bounds. The combined statements require an independent specialist literature comparison before any novelty or priority claim.
