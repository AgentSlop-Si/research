## How to sort and reconnect results

Use RESEARCH_INDEX.csv and SOURCE_INDEX.csv in this checkpoint addition. They separate source identity, construction, independent verification, mathematical scope, and open dependency. Follow the report paths for full proofs and independently authored controls. Each source version is preserved by content hash; same-name files are not automatically duplicates.

For a proposed combination, check all five conditions before claiming a speedup:

1. The factors act on the same coordinates with every permutation explicitly paid.
2. Input alphabets, field roots, precision, and denominator guards agree.
3. Coefficients can be generated uniformly within the claimed work budget.
4. Shared interfaces and polynomial states remain compact through every level.
5. All preparation, recognition, inverse solves, routing, and output writes are charged.

The highest-priority open connection is the marked-tree reduced inverse. The exact finite Fourier seed is a separate construction route; an all-size lift still needs new coefficients and a uniform generator. Neither path inherits a sub-N-log-N bound merely from the number of collected documents.

## Independently accepted infinite-family extension

The two-Green chirp obstruction now extends beyond one size. For N=5q² with positive odd q coprime to 5 and q congruent to 1 modulo 241, the same forced chirp inverse cannot be represented by two Hamiltonian-cycle Green summands with arbitrary diagonal endpoints and legal denominators. The sequence q=2410k+1 is explicit. The proof restricts a hypothetical representation to five coordinates, computes the exact restricted Gauss-sum family, and uses the independently certified closed projective N5 obstruction. It does not reduce a primitive Nth root incorrectly to order five.

The reviewer also accepts a non-effective finite-exception statement on the positive Gauss-sign curve; its exceptional set and cutoff have not been computed. This remains a precise representation obstruction. It does not establish a general Fourier lower bound, a faster algorithm, or historical novelty.
