# Independent proof audit: logarithmic obstruction for routed pseudoforest inverses

Audit date: 2026-10-10 UTC. Verdict: **ACCEPT**, with the proposed constants unchanged.

For every complex flat unitary U of order N>=1 and integer r>=1,

    U = sum_{i=1}^r L_i Q_i^{-1} R_i
    ==> N <= 12 r 36^r,

provided each Q_i is invertible and has at most two nonzeros in each column,
and each endpoint L_i,R_i has support contained in a permutation matrix.
All endpoint permutations are independent. All endpoint weights may vanish.
An additional arbitrary diagonal D is allowed with the necessary bound

    U = D + sum_{i=1}^r L_i Q_i^{-1} R_i
    ==> N <= 48 r 36^r.

Consequently r=Omega(log N) along any such exact representation family. These
are deliberately nonsharp necessary conditions, not constructions or general
arithmetic-runtime lower bounds. The main statement assumes r>=1. Without
D, r=0 cannot represent U. With D, N=1 and r=0 is the trivial exceptional
case; no positive-order theorem with right side zero should be asserted there.

## 1. Support graph and the correct bipartite lift

An invertible Q has no zero column. Make its row-support multigraph with one
vertex per row and one labeled edge per column: a two-entry column joins its
two supported rows, and a singleton column is a loop. If a connected component
contains v vertices and e column labels, its e columns are independent and
supported on v rows, so e<=v. Summing gives both totals N, hence e=v in every
component. Every component is unicyclic. This includes a loop cycle and a
parallel-edge cycle. Unbounded degrees and arbitrarily many components are
allowed; no row-sparsity assumption was made.

Let H be the 2N-by-2N matrix

    H = [ 0  Q ; Q^T  0 ],    H^{-1} = [ 0  Q^{-T} ; Q^{-1}  0 ].

Ordinary transpose suffices over C; H need not be Hermitian. Its undirected
support graph has upper vertices for Q's rows and lower vertices for Q's
columns. Each two-entry column subdivides one ordinary multigraph edge. A
singleton column becomes one leaf edge, so a component whose unique cycle
was a loop becomes a tree. Other components remain unicyclic. Thus this
bipartite graph is a pseudoforest and has treewidth at most two.

A direct decomposition uses edge bags for each hanging tree and a path of
three-vertex bags {v_1,v_j,v_{j+1}} for the cycle. Attach hanging-tree bags at
bags containing their attachment vertex. Components may be connected by
arbitrary decomposition-tree edges between their bags, without introducing
any new common vertices. Every bag has at most three vertices.

## 2. Weighted half-balanced separator, including disconnected graphs

More generally let a graph have a tree decomposition with bags of size at
most q, where q>=1. Give vertices arbitrary nonnegative weights with total W.
Assign each vertex's weight to any one bag containing it. The weighted bag tree
has a centroid t: deleting t leaves each tree component with weight at most
W/2. This follows, for example, by repeatedly moving into a component of
weight greater than W/2; a traversed edge cannot immediately be traversed
back, and the finite tree prevents indefinite progress.

Delete X, the vertices of bag t. A graph vertex outside X occurs only in bags
on a single side of t, by the connectedness axiom for bags containing that
vertex. Adjacent vertices outside X lie on the same side, because some bag
contains both. Therefore every graph component after deleting X lies within
one bag-tree component, and its weight is at most W/2. In particular |X|<=q.
This proof also works with zero-weight vertices and an empty central bag.

We use q=3 for the pseudoforest lift. For a current target rectangle with m
rows and m columns, write a routed branch entry as

    B[a,b] = ell_a rho_b (Q^{-1})[p(a),s(b)],

where p,s are permutations. Any partial permutation support can be extended
to such permutations and unused positions assigned weight zero. Mark lower
vertices N+p(a) for retained target rows and upper vertices s(b) for retained
target columns. Each color has m distinct vertices, and colors cannot overlap
because they lie in different bipartite halves. Mark even zero-weight
endpoint positions: ignoring a scalar zero can only increase an upper rank
bound. Assign unit graph weight to these 2m marked vertices. The separator
leaves components with at most m marked vertices in total.

## 3. Inverse-separator rank, without invertible principal-block assumptions

For an invertible d-by-d matrix M and arbitrary index sets I,J,

    rank M^{-1}[I,J]
      = rank M[J^c,I^c] + |I| + |J| - d.                 (1)

Here the complements are swapped. To prove it directly, let y be supported
on J and put z=M^{-1}y. The equation M^{-1}[I,J] y_J=0 says precisely that
z is supported on I^c and Mz vanishes on J^c. This bijects the two kernels,
whose ambient domain dimensions are |J| and d-|I|. Rank-nullity proves (1).

Partition all vertices outside X into A and B by taking unions of graph
components. No support edge joins A and B. With I=A,J=B, the complementary
matrix is

    M[B^c,A^c] = M[A union X, B union X].

Its A-by-B block is zero. Its rank is at most 2|X|, since entries outside
that zero block are supported on |X| extra rows or |X| extra columns. Also
|A|+|B|=d-|X|. Equation (1) therefore gives

    rank M^{-1}[A,B] <= |X| <= q.                      (2)

The same holds with A,B exchanged. No Schur complement, invertible M[A,A],
or invertible M[B,B] is needed. Restricting to marked lower rows and marked
upper columns yields the corresponding Q^{-1} rectangle. Multiplying by
endpoint diagonal scalings, including zero scalings, preserves the rank
upper bound.

## 4. Two-color selection: an explicit proof of the constant loss

Suppose m>=4q and put t=m-q. At most q vertices of either color were deleted,
so trim surviving marks of each color to exactly t. Every graph component
has retained total weight at most m<=4t/3, while all components have total
weight 2t.

Partition components into sides A,B, each of retained weight at least t/3:
if one component has weight at least t/3, use that component alone for A.
Its weight is at most 4t/3, so B has weight at least 2t/3. If all components
have weight below t/3, accumulate components until A first reaches t/3;
then its weight is below 2t/3, and B has weight above 4t/3. Zero-weight
components can be placed arbitrarily, so A,B still cover every vertex
outside X.

Write the red/blue counts on A as a,b; those on B are t-a,t-b. At least one
of the cross-color pairs

    (a,t-b),   (t-a,b)

has both entries at least t/6. Indeed if neither did, combining their two
failure disjunctions yields either a<t/6 and b<t/6 (so A has total below
t/3), or a>5t/6 and b>5t/6 (so B has total below t/3), or one of the two
impossible possibilities requiring a to be both <t/6 and >5t/6, or requiring
the same of b. All four possibilities contradict the hypotheses.

Choose floor(t/6) target rows and the same number of target columns from
the successful orientation. These have branch rank at most q by (2).
The marks are vertex labels of target indices, so they define actual subsets
of the existing target rows/columns. Independent routing does not require
any shared vertex names or order between different branches.

For q=3 this is exactly the proposal's m>=12 and

    m_next >= floor((m-3)/6).

## 5. Iteration and all integer thresholds

Apply the preceding operation successively to all r branches. Previously
obtained rank bounds persist under further row and column restrictions.
With m_0 the starting common size, define the guaranteed integer sizes by

    m_{j+1} = floor((m_j-q)/6).

Whenever the input to each step is at least 4q, the construction is valid.
Because floor(x)>=x-1,

    m_j >= m_0/6^j - d_q(1-6^{-j}),
    d_q = (q+6)/5 <= 7q/5.                            (3)

After r steps every branch has rank at most q on the final m_r square
rectangle, and their sum has rank at most qr.

### No diagonal

Suppose N>4qr36^r and start with m_0=N. Put A=N/6^r; then A>24q, since
r>=1. For all j<=r, the lower estimate in (3) is greater than

    A-d_q > 24q-7q/5 > 4q.

This validates the induction at every step; it does not assume the
recurrence is applicable before checking its threshold. Also d_q<A/2,
so m_r>=N/(2*6^r).

### Added arbitrary diagonal

Suppose N>16qr36^r. Begin with disjoint target row and column sets of size
m_0=floor(N/2). The diagonal restriction is zero. Put A=N/6^r, now A>96q.
Using floor(N/2)>=N/2-1/2, for every j<=r,

    m_j >= N/(2*6^j) - 1/(2*6^j) - d_q
         >= A/2 - 1/2 - d_q > 4q.

Again all inputs are valid. Because 1/(2*6^r)+d_q<=1/2+7q/5< A/4,

    m_r >= N/(4*6^r).                                (4)

Rows and columns remain disjoint under further restriction. This verifies
odd N, all floors, r=1, and q=1 without any large-N convention.

## 6. Flat-unitary rank lower bound and the contradiction

For any target row and column sets of equal size m, V=U[rows,columns]
satisfies

    ||V||_F^2=m^2/N,    ||V||_2<=1,
    rank(V)>=m^2/N.

The second assertion is coordinate compression of a unitary; the third is
the sum-of-squared-singular-values bound. It needs neither prime Fourier
order nor full spark. For exact branch equality, rank(V)<=qr. Therefore
m_r^2<=Nqr.

Without D, m_r>=N/(2*6^r) contradicts N>4qr36^r. With D, (4) contradicts
N>16qr36^r. Setting q=3 gives the stated constants 12 and 48.

Taking logs of N<=12r36^r gives log N<=log 12+log r+r log 36. For r>=1,
log r<=r, which directly implies a positive constant multiple of log N
as a lower bound on r for sufficiently large N. The same holds with 48.

## 7. General separator / treewidth corollary

The proof used column sparsity two only to produce uniformly bounded
weighted separators in each lifted support graph. If every branch's
symmetric bipartite support graph has a half-balanced separator of at most
q vertices for every marking used above, the same proof gives

    N <= 4 q r 36^r,

or N<=16 q r 36^r with an added arbitrary diagonal. It suffices that every
lift has treewidth at most w, with q=w+1. No common decomposition between
branches is required. Arbitrary higher-column-sparsity matrices are covered
only when this separator/treewidth hypothesis is actually verified; a
column-sparsity bound larger than two does not itself provide that property.

## 8. Separately proved accuracy corollary

Let T be any such branch sum, without assuming equality with U. On the
constructed m-by-m rectangle its rank is at most qr. Best rank-qr
approximation and the fact that each singular value of V is at most one give

    ||U-T||_F^2 >= max(0,m^2/N-qr).                    (5)

For example, under N>4qr36^r and without an added diagonal,

    ||U-T||_F / ||U||_F
      >= sqrt(max(0,1/(4*36^r)-qr/N)).                (6)

The denominator ||U||_F is sqrt(N). An operator-norm version is

    ||U-T||_2^2 >= max(0,m/N-qr/m),                    (7)

because the restricted error has at most m singular values. Equations
(5)-(7) also hold in the diagonal-correction model using the disjoint
rectangle and its smaller m. In particular fixed r and q prevent vanishing
normalized Frobenius or operator error as N tends to infinity. This does
not prove r=Omega(log N) for a fixed nonzero tolerated error. These are
explicit additional analytic corollaries, not claims inferred merely from
finite-field tests or from the exact-equality theorem's statement.

## 9. Scope and inherited precedent

The inherited separation-rank theorem already proves the logarithmic
conclusion for Hamiltonian Green branches with independent endpoint orders,
and also contains the operator-norm accuracy mechanism. Its all-length
norm proof applies verbatim to any complex flat unitary. The new separator
argument must not be presented as discovering either of those conclusions
in the Hamiltonian setting.

Moreover, a direct sum of separation-rank-one cycle blocks remains
separation-rank-one when the block orders are concatenated. In a strictly
separated rectangle only the block containing the separating cut can
contribute a nonzero cross-block; all other component interactions vanish.
Thus merely allowing arbitrarily many bare cycle components is an immediate
corollary of the inherited ordered-separation framework. The substantive
structural extension established here is to arbitrary column-two support
pseudoforests, including attached trees and unbounded row degrees, and to
more general bounded-separator inverse families. No inspected earlier
source proves that complete extension. This bounded inspection is not a
publication-level priority finding.

No unrestricted arithmetic-circuit lower bound, no Omega(Nr) lower bound
for shared implementations, no general product/interleaving obstruction,
and no barrier for unbounded-separator inverse families follows here.
The old field-general Vandermonde obstruction remains distinct: it covers
fields with a primitive Nth root, whereas the new target comparison uses
complex singular values and flat unitarity.
