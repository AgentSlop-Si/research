# Selected-convolution fusion gates: local compression and pushed-through twiddles

Date: 2026-10-10. Status: self-contained scoped proofs with fresh finite controls; independent audit pending.

## Result

No new exact arbitrary-input sub-`N log N` algorithm is obtained. Two proposed ways of fusing the expensive long-transform stage have precise rejection tests:

1. **Source-local compression cannot reduce the stream count.** The accepted marker set contains a long consecutive frequency interval. Consequently its partial Fourier matrix is injective on every coordinate subspace of dimension at most that interval length, regardless of how those source coordinates are permuted. A block-local linear encoder that replaces each such source block by fewer scalars loses information needed by the selected outputs. Applied to the blocked construction, `r` length-`B` streams require `r>=Q` in the stated range. If these are evaluated by separate ordinary full radix-2 transforms, their existing `Theta(N log B)` work remains.
2. **Moving a twiddle through a long transform produces a Cauchy operator.** Its entries, every square minor, and inverse entries are nonzero. Thus it has no exact bounded-rank off-diagonal representation, finite short convolution stencil, or single sparse-system-inverse representation with monomial endpoint routing. This does not rule out other fast ways to apply Cauchy matrices.

These are applications of classical Vandermonde/full-spark and Cauchy nonsingularity facts, not claims of new general matrix theory or literature novelty. They extend the list of explicitly assessed fusion representations. They do not turn the prior fixed-graph lower bound into a universal arithmetic lower bound.

Appendix A separately verifies the statement and model of an already-known September 2026 external power-saving preprint. Its supplied-root interface is broader than a construction allowed to prepare constants only from the given power-of-two root. That is a direct-import limitation, not a refutation or an impossibility of extension-field simulation.

## 1. Setup, parameters, and cost model

Retain the accepted setting:

    N=2^n, b>=7, L=2^(b-1), L+2b<=n.
    K=min(n,2L+2b), Q=2^K, B=N/Q.

The selected frequencies satisfy

    E={Bv+u : v in A, 0<=u<B},

for a nonempty header set `A`. Its asymptotic density is at least `1/4096`. The marker `t=1,k=1,h=n` gives the explicit contained interval

    I={c,c+1,...,c+M-1},
    c=2^(n-b-1), M=2^(n-2b-1).                         (1)

The hypotheses imply `n>=2b+1`, so `M` is a positive integer. The interval has no wraparound. Only its inclusion, not the density estimate, is needed below.

The condition `B>=Q` is exactly `n>=2K`. A useful sufficient and, in the nontruncated case, equivalent condition is

    n>=4L+4b.                                          (2)

Condition (2) implies `K=2L+2b<n`, `Q<=B`, and `Q<=M`. These extra inequalities are not asserted for every finite triple satisfying only `L+2b<=n`. At the accepted asymptotic choice `b=floor(log2(n/(4 ln n)))`, they hold eventually because `L=Theta(n/ln n)` and `b=O(log n)`.

Inputs are arbitrary field scalars. The field contains a primitive `N`th root `omega` and the normalizing constants. Additions, subtractions, prepared-constant multiplications, scalar reads/writes, and logarithmic-size address work are charged. Constants, schedules, and routing tables are not free. All linear encoders below have input-independent coefficients. Uncharged packing of several scalars into one wider object is excluded. The rank proofs hold over any such field; the later cyclotomic comparison specifically concerns characteristic zero.

The conclusions are exact algebraic ones. They do not establish numerical conditioning, useful approximation ranks, finite-precision error, or bit complexity. A scalar field operation is not a constant-time operation on an arbitrary exact algebraic-number representation.

## 2. Classical interval full-spark lemma

**Lemma 1.** Let `I` be any `M` consecutive Fourier row indices, allowing cyclic indexing, and let `S` be any `d<=M` distinct source indices. Then

    rank((omega^(ell s))_(ell in I,s in S))=d.           (3)

**Proof.** Use the first `d` rows `c,...,c+d-1`. Scale column `s` by the nonzero factor `omega^(-cs)`. The resulting square matrix has entries `(omega^s)^i`, `0<=i<d`. The nodes `omega^s` are distinct because `omega` has order `N`. Its Vandermonde determinant is the product of their pairwise differences, hence is nonzero. The full matrix therefore has column rank `d`. Normalizing rows or multiplying rows/columns by nonzero chirps does not change the rank. QED.

This is the standard full-spark property of consecutive Fourier rows, not a new lemma. Alexeev, Cahill, and Mixon explicitly give the Vandermonde result in Lemma 2 and discuss consecutive Fourier rows after Theorem 4 in [Full Spark Frames](https://borisalexeev.com/pdf/spark.pdf), JFAA 18 (2012), 1167–1194. The proof above is repeated to make the present gate self-contained and field-independent.

For the actual selected operator `F_E`, (1) and (3) imply

    rank(F_E[:,S])=|S| whenever |S|<=M.                  (4)

The same holds for the selected triangular-chirp convolution after its nonzero input and output diagonal scalings. This differs from asserting that every Fourier minor is nonzero. For example, at length 8, rows `{0,2}` and columns `{0,4}` give a rank-one submatrix. A contained interval is what licenses arbitrary source sets here.

## 3. Local-encoder theorem and the exact stream-count gate

**Theorem 2 (block-local information preservation).** Partition the source indices into disjoint sets `S_1,...,S_t`, each of size `d_j<=M`. Suppose an exact implementation first forms

    z_j=P_j x|_(S_j),  P_j in Field^(r_j by d_j),

and every subsequent output is a fixed linear function of the concatenated `z_j`. There is no later reread, uncounted bypass, or other input-dependent state. If all selected Fourier/chirp outputs are computed for every input, then

    rank(P_j)=d_j and r_j>=d_j for every j.              (5)

**Proof.** Let `R_j` be the part of the downstream linear map acting on `z_j`. Restrict the full computation to inputs supported on `S_j`. Then `F_E[:,S_j]=R_j P_j`, up to invertible chirp diagonals. Its rank is `d_j` by (4), while the right side has rank at most `rank(P_j)<=r_j`. QED.

The downstream map can be arbitrarily sophisticated, mix frequencies globally, and use arbitrary constants. The encoder matrices may differ between blocks and may be dense. Thus the theorem does not assume the literal predecessor butterfly graph, identical encoders, or frequency-local output recovery. It only excludes loss of dimension across a specified local-encoding cut. If all summaries are simultaneously materialized, their count is at least `sum d_j=N`; that conditional observation is not a general memory lower bound against streaming/recomputation.

### Corollary 3: fusing channels before the long transforms

Write each input index as `s=a+Qj`, with `0<=a<Q` and `0<=j<B`. Consider any proposed replacement of the `Q` input channels by `r` channels:

    z_rho[j]=sum_(a=0)^(Q-1) P_j[rho,a] x[a+Qj].         (6)

After (6), allow arbitrary processing of the `r` length-`B` arrays, including arbitrary output recombination. Under (2), Theorem 2 applies to every size-`Q` block `{Qj,...,Qj+Q-1}`, and forces

    r>=Q.                                               (7)

There is also a direct proof needing just one selected header and `B>=Q`: restrict to one source block `j` and the first `Q` frequencies `Bv,...,Bv+Q-1` of that header. The coefficient matrix is

    omega^((Bv+u)(a+Qj)), 0<=u,a<Q,

which is an invertibly scaled Vandermonde matrix on `omega^a`. It factors through the `r` summaries at that `j`, so has rank at most `r`.

For the original consecutive source blocks one can state the more general finite bound `r>=min(|E|,Q)`, since arbitrary distinct Fourier rows and consecutive source columns give another Vandermonde matrix. The simple `r>=Q` consequence follows when `|E|>=Q`; `B>=Q` is a convenient sufficient condition, not a necessary one.

If the proposed architecture computes a separate complete ordinary radix-2 `B`-point transform of each of its `r` arrays, the long-transform stage alone uses

    r B log2(B)/2 >= N(n-K)/2                            (8)

butterflies. With the declared scalar and memory accounting this is `Omega(N(n-K))`. At the accepted parameter choice, `K=O(n/ln n)=o(n)`, so (8) is `Omega(N log N)`.

This rejects the specific idea “perform an arbitrary source-local compression first, then run fewer ordinary long FFTs.” It does not show that `Q` long transforms must always be computed independently, that a different batch algorithm costs (8), or that a general factorization cannot avoid this stage. In particular, a nonlocal preprocessing map is outside (6) and must have its own complete cost analysis.

### Cost and storage boundary

At the accepted asymptotic marker choice, the inherited baseline is a matching `O(N log N)` algorithm in this architecture: `Q` full length-`B` FFTs, `N` twiddles, `B` full length-`Q` FFTs, and selection. It uses `N(n-K)/2+NK/2=Nn/2` radix-2 butterflies, plus `O(N)` phase/routing work, `O(QK^3)=N^o(1)` header preparation at that choice, and `O(|E|)` output writes. For other finite parameter triples, the simpler header enumerator need not be cheap; the predecessor's two-trie `O(N+n)` mask builder gives the `O(N log N)` fallback instead. Root/normalization preparation and scalar working storage are `O(N)`. Regenerating the high-time packet bank adds the already accepted `O(NL)` work and its `O(N)` output storage.

Giving (6) and its downstream processing free preparation only strengthens the negative result. Charging their actual preparation cannot invalidate (7) or reduce (8). Exact rank does not imply a floating-point rank lower bound: no lower bound on the smallest singular value is claimed.

## 4. The exact operator created by pushing a twiddle backwards

Set `zeta=omega^Q`, a primitive `B`th root, and let `F_B` have entries `zeta^(uj)`. The blocked identity is

    y[Bv+u]=sum_a omega^(Bav) omega^(au) (F_B x_a)[u],

with harmless normalization suppressed. A proposed fusion may try to move the multiplication `omega^(au)` before `F_B`. The exact source-domain operator required is

    K_a=F_B^(-1) D_a F_B,
    D_a=diag(omega^(au))_(u=0)^(B-1).                  (9)

For `a` not divisible by `Q`, a finite geometric sum gives

    K_a[j,k]=(1-omega^(aB)) /
              (B (1-omega^a zeta^(k-j))).             (10)

Every denominator is nonzero: `omega^a zeta^(k-j)=1` would imply `a+Q(k-j)=0 mod N`, hence `Q|a`. The numerator is nonzero by the same divisibility test.

To see the precise classical structure, put `X_j=zeta^j` and `Y_k=omega^a zeta^k`. The two node sets each have `B` distinct elements and are disjoint. Then (10) is a nonzero constant times

    X_j/(X_j-Y_k).                                     (11)

Thus it is an invertibly row-scaled Cauchy matrix.

**Theorem 4 (total nonsingularity of the pushed-through twiddle).** For `Q` not dividing `a`, every square submatrix of `K_a` is nonsingular.

**Self-contained classical Cauchy proof.** Choose any `d` rows and columns. If a linear combination of the columns of the corresponding Cauchy matrix vanishes, the rational function `sum_k c_k/(X-Y_k)` vanishes at the `d` distinct selected `X_j`. Multiplying by `prod_k(X-Y_k)` gives a polynomial of degree at most `d-1` with those `d` roots, hence the zero polynomial. Evaluating this polynomial at each `Y_k` gives `c_k prod_(h!=k)(Y_k-Y_h)=0`, so every `c_k=0`. The row scalings in (11) preserve nonsingularity. QED.

Cauchy nonsingularity and inversion are classical; a standard historical reference is Samuel Schechter, “On the inversion of certain matrices,” Math. Comp. 13 (1959), 73–77, DOI [10.1090/S0025-5718-1959-0105798-2](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0025-5718-1959-0105798-2). The AMS PDF request returned HTTP 403 in this run, so no claim of having inspected its full text is made. The proof used here is the elementary argument just supplied.

### Consequences for exact fusion implementations

- Every entry of `K_a` is nonzero. An exact ordinary circulant convolution stencil for it needs all `B` cyclic offsets. No short FIR/stencil is obtained by commuting the twiddle through the FFT.
- Every `d` by `d` off-diagonal block has rank `d` whenever it exists. In particular, the top-right half block has rank `floor(B/2)`. Independent row/column permutations and nonzero endpoint scalings preserve total nonsingularity. Any exact off-diagonal low-rank or quasiseparable representation of this single operator must therefore allow that full rank at a balanced cut.
- The identity `K_a K_c=K_(a+c)` follows from (9), so `K_a^(-1)=K_(-a)`. The latter is also dense when `Q` does not divide `a`. If `K_a=P T^(-1) R` with invertible monomial endpoint matrices, then `T=R K_(-a) P` is dense. Consequently one sparse linear-system solve, with only monomial endpoint routing, cannot realize this operator.
- With the positive-exponent convention, `K_Q x[j]=x[j-1 mod B]`; `K_0=I`. The exceptional integral shifts are intentionally excluded from (10) and Theorem 4.

These facts do not lower-bound the general arithmetic complexity of a Cauchy matrix. The same `K_a` is unitary over the complex numbers because the normalized Fourier matrix and `D_a` are unitary. Exact full rank is compatible with good conditioning of the full operator and with useful approximate low-rank blocks. A dense matrix, or one with full-rank off-diagonal blocks, can still have a short circuit in another representation. Sums/products of sparse inverses, nonmonomial endpoint maps, and jointly optimized applications to many channels require separate analysis.

The obvious implementation of (9) uses two complete length-`B` FFTs and `B` twiddles, costing `O(B log B)` once its initial roots are prepared, with `O(B)` roots and storage. Given `omega^a`, the entries in (10) can be prepared in `O(B)` scalar operations if scalar divisions with proved nonzero denominators are allowed in preparation, because the matrix is circulant and only one row is needed. Extracting `omega^a` for `0<=a<Q` additionally costs `O(log N)` by binary powering if it is not in the already charged root table. Cheap preparation of this row does not make its application free. No `O(B)` exact application algorithm is established here.

## 5. Consequence for the open constructive gate

The accepted conditional estimate remains

    cost(high-time packet bank) <= T_E(N)+O(NL+N),

where `T_E` must be a fully charged exact selected convolution cost in the same stated model. The present work does not improve it. The additional necessary distinctions are now concrete:

1. Fewer local summaries are impossible across the source-block cut above.
2. Commuting the phase through a long Fourier stage replaces it by the Cauchy operator (10); it does not erase the phase.
3. Using a small mask automaton does not compute these Cauchy applications, and preparing their kernels is not their application cost.
4. Any candidate that remains after these gates must specify different, nonlocal intermediate forms or a genuinely faster charged batch operation.
5. Early-time packets, causal inverse, and full Fourier recovery remain independent obligations. The established `O(L)` modulation cover cannot be silently treated as a constant-size cover.

FFTW's own [pruned-transform account](https://fftw.org/pruned.html) is a primary implementation reference for the standard strided-transform/twiddle structure and its `O(N log K)` selected-output regime. This report's no-compression statement is a scoped rank argument, not a claim that FFTW proves a universal complexity lower bound.

### A2. The external root resource is explicit

In the official [all-lengths section](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a/preprints/An-explicit-power-saving-for-the-exact-discrete-Fourier-transform-September-25-2026/build/sections/all-lengths.tex), let `R_ell` be the product of the first `ell` odd primes, maximal subject to `R_ell<=2N`. Choose `e` minimally so `W=R_ell 2^e>=2N`; then `2N<=W<4N`. Its stated master-root order is

    D_*=(2N) W 2^ceil(log2(16W)) < 1024 N^3.             (12)

That section explicitly charges three working transforms, including the fixed chirp-kernel transform, and the scalar and routing work. It computes at a coprime-factor working length containing odd primes. ### A3. A precise minimal-root import limitation

Assume, additionally, that the local implementation may initially use only rational scalars, `omega`, and `1/sqrt(2)`, and prepare new scalars by field operations. For `N=2^n`, `n>=3`, these initial scalars lie in

    K_0=Q(zeta_N),

because `sqrt(2)` already lies in `Q(zeta_8)`. Every subsequently prepared scalar stays in `K_0`. This assumption is stronger than merely saying the ambient field contains `omega`; if arbitrary extra constants or a root-of-unity oracle are expressly supplied, this particular guard does not apply.

For odd squarefree `R`, coprimality gives

    [K_0(zeta_R):K_0]=phi(R).                            (13)

Indeed the compositum is `Q(zeta_(NR))`, whose degree divided by `phi(N)` is `phi(NR)/phi(N)=phi(R)`. Thus even one nontrivial odd root such as `zeta_3` is not obtainable by those field operations. The external working roots in (12) cannot simply be treated as powers of the supplied `omega`.

The degree is quantitatively substantial for this specific working-length family. Let the first omitted odd prime be `p_(ell+1)`. Maximality gives `R_ell>2N/p_(ell+1)`. Since the `j`th odd prime is at least `j+2`,

    R_ell/phi(R_ell)
      = product_(j=1)^ell p_j/(p_j-1)
      <= product_(j=1)^ell (j+2)/(j+1)
      = (ell+2)/2.

Therefore

    phi(R_ell) >= 2R_ell/(ell+2)
                > 4N/((ell+2)p_(ell+1)).               (14)

Using the elementary bounds `ell=O(log N)` and `p_(ell+1)=O((log N)^2)` gives `phi(R_ell)=Omega(N/(log N)^3)`. The unoptimized master root in (12) has still larger extension degree: writing `D_*=2^s R_ell`, it is exactly `2^(s-n)phi(R_ell)` over `K_0`. Only the unavoidable odd-root part is needed for the direct-import warning; the large master-root choice need not be minimal.

This does **not** prohibit an extension-field simulation. Such a simulation must specify how extension elements are represented, how input-linear gates and preparation descend to base-field work, and what structure is reused. A generic basis representation has `phi(R_ell)` base-field coordinates per element; specialized algorithms may exploit much more structure. No universal degree-times-runtime lower bound is asserted, and no descended algorithm is constructed here. Likewise, the external exact complex-register model may simply grant the master root, in which case the resource mismatch disappears by an explicit model change.

The appropriate comparison is therefore: an official external power-saving theorem is stated in a broader supplied-root exact-register model; this report neither independently certifies that theorem nor supplies a new power saving, and a minimal-root implementation may not import its constants for free.
