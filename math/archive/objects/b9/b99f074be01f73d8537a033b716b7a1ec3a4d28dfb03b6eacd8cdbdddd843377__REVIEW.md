## Main findings, with actual witness impact

### F01. Literal gate hypothesis needs repair; intended application remains intact

`universal_gate.py:1–14` and the source proof state the gate broadly while relying on a nonzero column coefficient triple. If C is zero, choose all diagonal/column multipliers zero but retain arbitrary U,L. Then C=D=0 imposes no restriction on those retained generators, and a gate of full column rank is possible even for invertible Q.

The preserved independent checker at lines 134–139 and `modular_results.json` explicitly give GF(17), Fourier root 9, U=(1,0,0,8,5,8,2,0), L=(0,8,13,8,6,0,2,13), rank 9, and selected minor 3. This is an existing bounded counterexample to the wording, not a newly executed result. The historical audit repairs the hypothesis to invertible C, or suitable nonzero selected column triples. Its intended C=A^-1 application has that property.

The fourth output column also requires a proof case split: if its upper multiplier vanishes, its nonzero coefficient triple lies in ker(Y4), so rank(Y4)≤2, all cofactors vanish, and the last gate equation is automatic. The independent modular checker deliberately includes that case and the saved controls record 630 accepted invertible samples. No new corner/nonzero fourth-multiplier guard is justified.

Permutation conventions must be kept explicit. The independent checker verifies the coordinate equation `D[:,j]=(Q C)[:,P[j]]` with P=(7,0,6,3,4,1,2,5). This is the concrete relation used to select input columns 1,2,5 for output columns 5,6,7. Bare P versus P^-1 notation depends on whether the tuple denotes a gather or a basis permutation; no orientation failure was established in the stored controls.

### F04. Phase21 algebra and characteristic-zero transfer are sound within their scope

The implementation's root routine supports powers of two N≥4 dividing 998244352, while the accompanying algebraic theorems cover even complex lengths. This domain difference is explicit and is not a false implementation of an all-even API.

For primitive even-order w, T^N=-I gives the denominator 1+alpha^N. Both cyclic Green recurrences correctly close the wraparound scalar equation; the arbitrary-weight version uses 1-product(weights), preserving zero-weight cases. The direct chirp polynomial has the correct quadratic phase and quadratic work. The Hankel matrix factorization into diagonal factors and a distinct-node Vandermonde proves rank N/2. The common-order product cut-rank argument allows zero weights and singular diagonal masks, but is not a shared-evaluation operation lower bound.

The Prony polynomial is monic with cyclotomic-integral coefficients, so nonzero reductions of its derivative and forbidden-pole resultants certify nonzero characteristic-zero resultants. This is a genuine implication for each checked size, unlike inferring complex nonexistence from an empty finite-field search. The first N moments are connected to the roots by the explicitly proved recurrence. The independent checker adds root-order/primality checks, a distinct q-binomial coefficient construction, exact residue arithmetic, and full saved coefficient lists.

Inert checks here verified the byte serialization hashes of all 30 saved coefficient vectors, degree/monicity/nonzero constant terms, the recorded nonzero resultant flags, and the agreement of compact dyadic summaries. They did not recompute Euclidean gcds or execute either checker. At N=8192 the recorded derivative and forbidden resultants are 338814782 and 850988529.

The source `audit.py` imports most of its operations from the implementation under test, so the word “independent” in its header does not denote an independent implementation. The separate `independent_checks.py` is materially independent at source level, but its saved outputs remain historical evidence. Small randomized equalities support an identity; a nonzero residual can refute a candidate, whereas a zero residual on one vector cannot prove operator equality.

### F05. Lift and benchmark results are approximate, bounded controls

`independent_lift_audit.py` reconstructs the exact integer nearest-grid map, uses a high-precision defining Bessel series, forms small dense transforms and covariance operators, and intentionally tests large-size scalar mapping failures and forward-order Bessel instability. Its header correctly disclaims proof by numerical samples. The stored result embeds the exact script SHA-256, which matches this assigned hash.

The finite sweep covers N=1..1000 for the map; 7 ranks on a 21×25 kernel grid; 10 lift lengths through 61; and full random-state enumeration for six covariance lengths through 31. Saved maxima include kernel tail ratio 0.811570185496895 and covariance-bound ratio 0.9948142522336455. Precision is fixed at 90 decimal digits and the series has a stopping heuristic; this is not interval arithmetic. It gives no general end-to-end finite-precision guarantee or uniform cost bound for special functions.

The v11 benchmark fixes rank 10, one random complex input and one state at each of five lengths 511..8191; each method is warmed up once and timed five times. It does not assert reference-versus-streamed equality or a DFT error bound, and writes raw differences from the streamed row. The stored H0 differences range from about 0.254 to 0.0171; H1 from about 0.00181 to 9.95e-6. These are nonzero approximate-fusion differences. The reported speedups are against another row implementation, not a conventional full FFT or a fully certified majority estimator.

The prior lift audit's coefficient-generation, rank-dependent workspace, exact-index, and inherited numerical-constant qualifications remain. The independent checker does not close those source implementation obligations, and the benchmark's two imported implementation identities remain outside this review's closure.

## Costs and testing coverage

- Gate construction is fixed 8×8/11×9 linear algebra; enumerating h uses Θ(p⁴) candidates with data-dependent filtering. Random invertibility acceptance loops have no explicit retry cap. They are audit costs, not a transform algorithm.
- Exact exterior-product determinant accumulation has exponential subset-state growth with matrix size; the selected sizes are bounded (up to 9), with potentially expensive polynomial coefficients. Symbolic resultants/factorization costs are not FFT costs.
- The Bessel checker forms R×N factors, dense N×N kernels and N×N×N covariance arrays; full-state covariance enumeration multiplies dense matrices. It is intentionally outside a linear-work execution schedule.
- The phase19 checker uses four modular matrix sizes, exhaustive permutation tests at N=4 and 8, and floating dense inverse/spectral tests through 128. Exhaustive centralizer checking is factorial in N. Its result is not an all-N numerical proof.
- Phase21 Green application is O(N) field operations and O(N) workspace once prepared; direct chirp application is O(N²). N-pole dictionary preparation uses a dense O(N³) solve and O(N²) storage. Independent execution of N/2 poles is O(N²), and no simultaneous resolver is supplied.
- Rank tests and explicit matrices are dense diagnostic work. The permutation-escape test recomputes `s1(x)` inside a list comprehension for every output index, adding avoidable work but not changing the mathematical example.
- The independent Prony coefficient recurrence has O(h) coefficient steps but modular exponentiation/inversion and an all-node Horner verification cost more; the complete checker is not a linear-time coefficient certificate. Naive polynomial remainders/gcds have quadratic arithmetic behavior. The signed-int64 remainder product/subtraction is safe on the stored reduced residues with p<10⁹; matrix arithmetic uses Python integers.

Phase21 tests cover positive recurrences, repeated poles, noncommuting controls, same-order sums/products, a reordered escape, complex condition numbers, spectral floors, and finite Prony certificates. They do not test every guard branch (singular denominators, noncanonical residues, malformed dimensions, wrong lengths, direct dataclass mutation, or all admissible n). Most low-level helpers assume normalized rectangular residue arrays; all stored uses reviewed here satisfy that assumption. This report does not promote hypothetical unsupported helper inputs to failures of the stored mathematics.
