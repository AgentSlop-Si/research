The 8 most promising research directions
01

Non-affine Green cycles + Weyl-certified FFT
Exact FFT candidate · Highest priority
Construct a small network of \(O(N)\)-work weighted Green resolvents with genuinely non-affine cycle permutations. Rather than optimizing graph ranks alone, require the complete network to satisfy the Fourier shift/modulation identities.
Why promising: Green Phase 26 eliminates a large affine-run family but explicitly leaves non-affine, globally mixing orders unresolved. The next decisive test is an exact Weyl-identity certificate for a growing family of sizes.

02

Superconcentrator graph + algebraic compatibility
Exact FFT candidate · High mathematical interest
Your \(N=8\) two-stage network passes every tested graph min-cut condition, but the matrix family has algebraic dimension 60 rather than 64. This exposes constraints invisible to graph rank.
New attack: derive explicit polynomial constraints, evaluate them on the Fourier matrix, and determine whether the architecture admits a Fourier-specific factorization. Then attempt to lift a successful finite construction to all \(N\).

03

Exact phase-bank fusion before materialization
Exact FFT candidate · Strong algebraic starting point
Hero #1 v13 has an exact identity involving \(M\) complementary phase projections:
\[
F_Nx=\frac1M\sum_{t=0}^{M-1}F_NP_tx.
\]

One phase projection is low-rank; all \(M\) recover the exact transform. Computing them independently loses the hoped-for speedup.
New attack: fuse their shared Green forcing, phases, and reconstruction before building separate full-length output vectors. The decisive result would be a joint executor with \(o(MN)\) work and \(o(N\log N)\) total cost.
Source: fft_hero1_mixedradix_v13_20261010.zip — THEOREM.md, NEXT_GATE.md


04

Bessel/Chebyshev correction as one shared global operator
Exact FFT candidate · Especially relevant to prime lengths
Your all-length lift produces multiple correlated correction channels. Existing scan fusion reduces a subproblem, but separate channel transforms keep an \(R\)-dependent cost.
New attack: represent the entire correction as a polynomial or resolvent-valued forcing and evaluate it through one shared recurrence, without first materializing \(R\) independent Fourier channels.
The obstacle is proving that both channel generation and decoding collapse, with coefficient conversion and precision fully counted.
Sources: fft_all_length_fused_fp_v11.zip, fft_research_master_20261009.zip


05

Tensor-fiber graph union with growing structural rank
Exact for structured inputs · Verified scoped construction
Hero #17 already provides exact \(O_r(N)\) algorithms for several bounded-rank tensor/fiber signal families, including some dense inputs whose reshaped matrices have maximal rank. Its convolution algorithm merges matching frequency labels into a small set of output states.
New attack: find a multiscale representation where the number of surviving union states remains small even as local input descriptions become richer. Generic dense inputs currently fail this compression condition.
Source: fft_hero17_tensor_fiber_graph_union_2026-10-10.zip


06

Ramanujan divisor-spectrum randomized FFT
Approximate FFT · Strongest scoped asymptotic result
Hero #0 and divisor-spectrum v13 establish an \(O(N)\) ideal-arithmetic approximate full-output transform for certain composite-length families, including balanced semiprimes, with proved additive error bounds.
New attack: obtain a similarly sharp covariance bound for prime lengths, or find a low-variance projector that does not depend on a useful divisor.
This is a real approximate-transform research direction, but it does not compute the exact DFT.
Sources: fft_hero0_phase2_composite_green_2026-10-09.zip, fft_divisor_spectrum_v13.zip


07

Signed-atom sparsification + two-level Word-RAM lookup
Approximate FFT · Linear-time proof incomplete
Replace arbitrary sample coefficients by signed real/imaginary atoms, quantize short phasor trajectories, and use two finite-state lookup levels to evaluate all output blocks.
Why promising: the archive sketches \(O(N)\) online word operations for a fixed additive-accuracy contract. However, packed key construction, table preparation, quantization error, and final output handling still need one rigorous end-to-end proof.
Source: SPARSE_SURROGATE_WORDRAM_CHECKPOINT_2026-10-09_v2.md SPARSE_SURROGATE_WORDRAM_CHECKPOINT_2026-10-09_v2.md



08

Sparse-defect escape using globally dense inverse scans
Structural research · Enables direction 01
The newest sparse-defect archive proves a barrier for products whose inverse stages are row-sparse plus bounded-rank corrections. But it also constructs globally dense, linear-time scan operators whose inverses escape that restriction.
New attack: build Fourier-compatible networks from these unrestricted separated-generator scans, using the sparse-defect theorem as a rejection filter rather than returning to tridiagonal inverse stages.
Source: fft_sparse_defect_green_gate_2026-10-10.zip


Where I would concentrate the research
Combine #1 + #2 + #3
I would investigate a Weyl-certified global phase-bank compiler.
The idea is to take the exact complementary phase-bank identity from Hero #1, encode its combined forcing inside non-affine Green scans from Phase 26, and use the algebraic compatibility methods from the superconcentrator branch to verify Fourier-specific phase relationships.
The missing theorem is concrete:
\[
F_N=G_1G_2\cdots G_{t(N)}
\]

with each \(G_i\) executable in \(O(N)\), and either \(t(N)=O(1)\) for linear time or \(t(N)=o(\log N)\) for a genuine sub-\(N\log N\) algorithm. A more general fused circuit could also succeed without materializing these stages individually.
The next decisive milestone is not another benchmark. It is an exact, reproducible factorization on small sizes that simultaneously passes the Fourier Weyl identities, survives non-affine graph-cut tests, and has a plausible scalable construction.

What I would stop repeating
Your earlier audits already give strong reasons to deprioritize plain FFT-subtree caching, \(A-k\) multiplication tricks without reusable structure, ordinary split-radix constant reductions, affine-run Green networks with a bounded number of terms, and treating a single unbiased sketch as an exact transform. There is also an important model boundary: classical \(\Omega(N\log N)\) lower bounds apply to bounded-coefficient or suitably well-conditioned linear-circuit models. A proposed exact linear-time construction must explain precisely which assumptions it changes, without hiding coefficient-size or precision costs.
