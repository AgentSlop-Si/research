# Hero #1 / Phase 21 — reciprocal sparse-rational recovery and a self-reciprocal Hankel resultant

**Date:** 2026-10-10. **Status:** Exact finite-field theorem and reproducible implementation, with an **all-input correct fallback**. Gives `O_s(N)` field operations only on an explicitly described *fixed-sparsity, nondegenerate structured-input family*. Does **not** prove an exact arbitrary-input `o(N log N)` FFT. No priority claim against sparse rational interpolation literature.

## I. Exact Fourier and graph model

Work in a field `F_p`, `p` prime, containing a supplied primitive `N`th root `omega` (thus `N | p-1`); all ring operations, including field inversions, are counted as unit-cost arithmetic. Set

- `F_N x[k] = sum_{j=0}^{N-1} x[j] omega^(kj)` (unnormalized transform),
- `(Sx)[j]=x[(j-1) mod N]`, hence `F_N S = D F_N`,
- `D=diag(1,omega,...,omega^(N-1))`.

Retain the earlier *noncommuting* Green relation `L F_N=F_N P`, where `L=7I+2S+D` and `P=7I+S+2D^-1`. In the tested fields, both are invertible and `L^-1` is computed by a charged `O(N)` cyclic graph solve. Put `r=Px`. It is enough to evaluate `R=F_N r` exactly and return `L^-1 R`.

### Structured promise

Suppose for some unknown sparse cyclic polynomial `Q(z)` and unknown two-term polynomial `H(z)`:

`Q(S)r = H(S)e_0`,  `H(z)=h_u z^u + h_v z^v`, `h_u h_v !=0`, `u != v (mod N)`, `supp(Q) <= s`.

For the graph-union subfamily `Q(z)=prod_{i=1}^t (1-a_i z^(d_i))`, `s<=2^t`, and **none** of the factors, shifts, amplitudes or two defect locations needs to be supplied. Assume `Q(omega^k) !=0` for every k. Then

`R_k = H(omega^k)/Q(omega^k)`.

The promise is input-dependent and highly restrictive for fixed `s`. The fallback computes the DFT correctly without assuming it.

## II. Reciprocal transform and rank test

Write `delta=v-u (mod N)` and `gamma=-h_v/h_u`, so

`H(z)=h_u z^u (1-gamma*z^delta)`.

For `k=0,...,4s-1`, suppose the directly computed `R_k` are all nonzero and define `T_k=1/R_k`. Put `w=omega^delta`. Then

`(1-gamma*w^k) T_k = h_u^-1 omega^(-uk) Q(omega^k)`.

The right-hand side is an exponential sum with at most `s` distinct modes. Therefore its `(s+1)x(s+1)` Hankel matrices have rank at most s. Define, with `m=s+1`, the two polynomials

`P_e(a,w)=det( [T_(e+i+j)*(1-a*w^(e+i+j))]_(0<=i,j<m) )`, `e=0,1`,

and their scalar Sylvester resultant

`T_s(w)=Res_a(P_0(a,w),P_1(a,w))`.

Any true shift root `w=omega^delta` necessarily satisfies `T_s(w)=0`. Each `P_e` has degree <=m in `a`, and naive degree accounting gives

`deg_w T_s <= D_s=m^2(2m-1)=(s+1)^2(2s+1)`.

This gives a correct fixed-sparsity `O_s(N)` discovery route by scanning all N roots and computing a constant-sized polynomial gcd at each. **We do not need to perform a two-shift elimination.**

## III. New algebraic theorem: universal factors and reciprocity

**Theorem (reciprocal Hankel resultant).** For an arbitrary formal sequence `T_0,...,T_(2s+1)` over the integer polynomial coefficient ring, the resultant satisfies the polynomial identities

`w^D_s T_s(1/w)=(-1)^(m^2) T_s(w)`,

and

`T_s(w)=w^v_s (w-1)^(m^2) U_s(w)`,

where

`v_s=s(s+1)(4s+5)/6`,  `b_s=s(s+1)(2s+1)/6`,  `deg U_s<=2b_s`.

Moreover `U_s` is **palindromic of grade `2b_s`**:

`w^(2b_s) U_s(1/w)=U_s(w)`.

Equivalently there is a polynomial `V_s` of degree at most `b_s` such that

`U_s(w)=w^(b_s) V_s(w+w^-1)`.

This is an identity valid also when the resultant vanishes or the formal Hankel matrices specialize to singular matrices. If it vanishes identically, the discovery gate safely falls back; the theorem does not identify a unique binomial.

### Proof overview

1. With `H_e=(T_(e+i+j))_(i,j=0..s)` and `D_w=diag(1,w,...,w^s)`, write `P_e(a,w)=det(H_e-a*w^e*D_w H_e D_w)`. Reversing both `a` and `w` gives `P_e(a,1/w)=(-a)^m w^(-E_e)P_e(1/a,w)`, where `E_e=m e+m(m-1)`. The common degree-m resultant of the reversed polynomials gains sign `(-1)^(m^2)`. Homogeneity yields the reciprocity identity of grade `m(E_0+E_1)=D_s`.
2. Set `w=1+epsilon`, `a=1+epsilon*t`. Each entry `T_k(1-a*w^k)` is divisible by `epsilon`. Both m-by-m determinants have a factor `epsilon^m`. The affine-change formula for a degree-m/degree-m resultant then proves divisibility by `epsilon^(m^2)`, i.e. by `(w-1)^(m^2)`.
3. In the generic coefficient field, Cauchy–Binet gives `ord_(w=0) [a^j]P_e = j(j-1)+e*j`. Consecutive slopes of the Newton polygon are `e, e+2,...,e+2s`; the corresponding roots in `a` have valuations `-e,-e-2,...,-e-2s`. The P0 and P1 root valuations have opposite parity, so their pairwise differences have exact valuations `-max(2i,2j+1)`. The leading coefficients have valuations `m(m-1)` and `m^2`. Thus

   `ord_(w=0) T_s = D_s - sum_(i,j=0)^s max(2i,2j+1)=s(s+1)(4s+5)/6`.

   The coefficient inequality remains a polynomial identity after specialization because it was proved over a generic coefficient field. This gives the universal factor `w^v_s`.
4. Divide by the universal factors and apply reciprocal symmetry. The remaining grade is `D_s-2v_s-m^2=2b_s`, so `U_s` is palindromic. Symmetric Laurent polynomials in `w,w^-1` are polynomials in `w+w^-1`, proving the `V_s` representation.

See [PROOF_ALGEBRA.md](PROOF_ALGEBRA.md) for the full valuation and resultant derivation and the exact experimental comparison for `s=1,...,6`.

For `s=2`, this proves the previously unexplained Phase20 pattern `w^13(w-1)^9` with a palindromic quotient of degree at most 10. For `s=4`, it proves `w^70(w-1)^25` with a palindromic quotient of degree at most 60.

## IV. Certified reconstruction and complexity

For fixed s, compute `4s` Fourier probes `R_k` by direct `O(sN)` dot products and invert them. Then:

1. Evaluate the fixed-size Sylvester resultant `T_s(w)` at **only `b_s+1`** nonzero field elements `w` distinct under `w -> w+w^-1`, with none equal to 1. Divide by the proved factors `w^v_s (w-1)^(m²) w^b_s` and interpolate `V_s` of degree at most `b_s`. The executable makes three independent extra evaluation checks.
2. Reconstruct the palindromic `U_s`. Evaluate it at every Fourier root; this costs `O_s(N)` arithmetic because it has degree at most `2b_s`. If `U_s` is not zero, there are at most `2b_s` admissible nontrivial roots.
3. At each candidate `w=omega^delta`, compute `gcd_a(P_0,P_1)`. If it has degree one, recover the unique `gamma`. Correct the inverse samples by `(1-gamma*w^k)` and use Prony/Berlekamp–Massey to recover an at-most-s-sparse polynomial `A(z)`. Require the reconstructed A to agree with **all `4s` independent probes**, not just the low-rank determinant constraints.
4. Evaluate `A(omega^k)` for **all N roots** and reject any candidate with a zero. Then certify the **entire N-coordinate** identity

   `A(S)r=(I-gamma S^delta)e_0`.

   This check is O(sN) and never assumes the first probes determine an arbitrary input.
5. On acceptance, `R_k=(1-gamma*omega^(k*delta))/A(omega^k)` for every k. Compute all N entries, followed by the O(N) Green solve. If all candidates fail, use the preserved exact full Bluestein FFT fallback.

**Soundness is unconditional within the field model**, because a full cyclic certificate and nonzero spectral denominator guarantee the exact transform. **Completeness on the structured family is conditional** on (a) the first `4s` R probes being nonzero, (b) the resultant `U_s` not vanishing identically, (c) at least one correct shift having a degree-one gcd in gamma, (d) invertibility of the target Q on the N-root subgroup, and (e) enough field nodes for the short interpolation. Cases outside these conditions use a correct fallback.

A critical uniqueness lemma removes repeated full-vector certificates: any two ratios `B/A` with `supp(B)<=2`, `supp(A)<=s`, with nonzero sample denominators, agreeing on `4s` consecutive subgroup roots have identically zero cross product on the full N-root subgroup. The cross product has at most `4s` distinct monomials modulo `z^N-1`, and its first `4s` values form a Vandermonde system in distinct nonzero nodes. This does **not** prove either ratio equals the transform of an arbitrary input, so the full N-vector check is still mandatory.

For fixed s, the entire accepted structured branch is `O_s(N)` field arithmetic (with a large s-dependent constant) including discovery, root enumeration, short interpolation, reconstruction, full validation, forcing, and Green solve. The all-input worst-case is `O(N log N)` because fallback is required. Explicit finite-field root supply, machine-word/bit cost, and numerical stability are separate obligations.

## V. Graph-factor recovery

The valid rational representation recovers the full sparse cyclic Q without knowing any of its original factors. If Q is a product of `t` binomials with **collision-free `2^t` subset-sum support**, its factors can also be recovered (not necessarily uniquely) by enumerating the base exponent and t incident edges of a hypercube among those `2^t` support positions, then checking exact coefficient expansion. For constant t this is `O_t(1)` additional field work. The implementation successfully reconstructs t=2 and t=3 test cases.

## VI. Genuine limitations and model barriers

- For fixed support locations, general 2/s rational functions have at most `s+1` continuous parameters after scaling. The union over finitely many support choices has algebraic dimension <=`s+1`, so it cannot cover generic `C^N` inputs when `N>s+1`. **No arbitrary-input linear FFT theorem results.**
- Input classification can fail or be adversarial. A polynomial perturbation vanishing at the first `4s` Fourier probe roots leaves those probes unchanged while changing later outputs; complete cyclic validation detects it and falls back.
- The cost figures are exact modular **field-operation proxies** with preinitialized root/Green data, not real/complex flop or bit-time speedups. Roots and Green setup require at least O(N) work, and field selection is externally assumed. Numerical Prony and Hankel resultants can be badly conditioned over floating point.
- Lower bounds such as Morgenstern's bounded-coefficient and Ailon's well-conditioned-model results apply to their **specified restricted circuit models**. This project claims neither to overturn those models nor to establish an unrestricted lower/upper bound.
- Sparse rational interpolation, Prony/Hankel rank recovery, cyclic convolution, Sylvester resultants and Fourier/Green conjugation have extensive prior art. The project's contribution here is its exact reciprocal-resultant identity, charged special-purpose implementation and negative/positive audits; historical priority has not been established.
