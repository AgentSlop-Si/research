## 1. Fixed-cut transport and rank-only sharpness

Let every stage have a decomposition `T_i=B_i+E_i`, with each B_i block diagonal relative to the same fixed cut and rank(E_i)≤r_i. The ordered telescoping formula

    T_L ... T_1 − B_L ... B_1
      = sum_i T_L ... T_(i+1) E_i B_(i−1) ... B_1

is correct without commutation or invertibility assumptions. Each term has rank at most r_i. The product of the B_i remains block diagonal, so the target's cross block has rank at most sum r_i. For the consecutive Fourier cut rows 0,...,m−1 and columns m,...,N−1, that cross block is a rectangular Vandermonde at distinct nodes. Its rank is min(m,N−m). The stated lower bound follows.

The correction rank is the rank of the entire E_i. It must not be silently replaced by one directional off-diagonal rank. Likewise the fixed cut cannot change from stage to stage without a new argument. Summing this bound over many dyadic cuts would double-count work unless a separate charging lemma is supplied. Arbitrary free basis changes, permutations moving the cut, auxiliary registers, and general circuits are outside the theorem.

For even N=2m, writing F in four m-by-m blocks A,B,C,D and H=C A^(−1)B gives a rank-m residual after subtraction of `diag(0,D−H)`. All required block invertibilities follow from consecutive-row Vandermonde structure. This proves rank sharpness, not a cheap evaluator: the blocks and dense inverse are computational objects whose cost cannot be erased.

The stronger baseline `diag(tA,D−H/(1−t))` has a rank-m residual for t≠0,1 when its lower block is invertible. Over complex numbers its determinant is a nonzero rational function, so a valid t exists. In a finite field, the polynomial in λ=(1−t)^(−1) has at most m bad roots, while permissible λ form a set of size p−2. The counting argument requires p−2>m. For N≥4 and p≥N+1 this is satisfied.

At N=2, p=3 it is not: F=[[1,1],[1,2]], and the sole allowed t=2 makes the lower baseline block zero. More strongly, every invertible diagonal baseline diag(a,b), a,b∈{1,2}, leaves F−diag(a,b) of rank two. Thus the source's unrestricted finite-field sharpness wording and its `schur_cut_certificate(2,3,2)` expectation need this exception. Our checker exhausts all four baselines without executing the source function.

## 2. Every balanced complementary remainder difference is invertible

Let N=2m and partition the distinct Fourier roots into sets A,B of size m. Let P_A and P_B be their monic root polynomials. For a coefficient vector split as `f=low+z^m high`, define R_A and R_B as the multiplication-by-z^m remainder maps in the monomial quotient bases. Then the two remainder vectors are

    low+R_A high,  low+R_B high.

The coefficient transfer is `T=[[I,R_A],[I,R_B]]`. Evaluation of each remainder at its own m roots factors the row-permuted full Fourier/Vandermonde matrix as `diag(V_A,V_B)T`. The full matrix and both V blocks are invertible; therefore T is invertible. Subtracting its first block row from its second gives the invertibility of R_B−R_A.

This is an exact all-balanced-partition theorem over a field containing N distinct roots, including the complex field. It does not depend on subgroup structure or a numerical rank heuristic. The result excludes a low-rank complementary transfer difference in this interface; it does not exclude a compact circuit for a dense, full-rank difference.

## 3. Unique minimum literal transfer support

Multiplication by z^m is invertible in either quotient, because every root is nonzero. Consequently both m-by-m matrices R_A,R_B are invertible and each contains at least m nonzero entries.

If the combined support equals 2m, both matrices are monomial. The first column of R_A is the negative vector of the lower coefficients of P_A. Its constant component is nonzero, so a one-entry first column forces all other lower coefficients to vanish: `P_A=z^m−c`. Among the roots of z^(2m)−1, the mth powers are ±1; hence A is exactly the even or odd exponent class, and B is its complement. Conversely that split gives R_A=I and R_B=−I, up to exchange.

The bound is therefore attained uniquely by the parity split, up to child exchange. The shared reductions are ordinary sum/difference butterflies. The theorem minimizes matrix entries in the specified monomial bases. It permits no conclusion that every alternative multi-stage arithmetic circuit has at least that many gates, or that a basis change is free.

## 5. Executable branches and charged work

Static inspection confirms that the shared-binomial source evaluator is the classical recursive radix-2 DIF algorithm. Every butterfly executes one field multiplication and two additions/subtractions, including trivial twiddles. For dyadic N it has `(N/2)log2 N` butterflies and an additional N−1 products to build a supplied root's power table. A complete output vector is materialized. The unoptimized count is an attainable implementation ledger, not the minimum possible FFT operation count.

The generic evaluator constructs root polynomials by multiplying linear factors and performs ordinary monic remainder divisions. Its plan wrappers charge the products and additions used in these polynomial constructions. Building all child polynomials at every binary-tree depth requires exactly

    (3/2)(N²−N+N log2 N)

field operations in that literal builder. At N16 this is 456, matching the source plan subtotal. This quadratic setup must not be hidden by reporting only input-dependent divisions. A reusable plan may be amortized only under an explicitly repeated-input model; it remains relevant to the source's fully charged first-transform comparison.

The generic online ledger is data-dependent: zero high coefficients and zero divisor coefficients skip operations. Thus the saved N16 totals are examples for particular inputs and policies, not general worst-case constants or lower bounds. We inspected but did not execute those saved benchmark programs. Root discovery in test fixtures, modular bit complexity, indexing, allocation, and internal memory traffic are not equivalent to scalar field operations. The `inputs` and `writes` counters describe the external input/output scan, not every internal access.

Our independent conventional DIT implementation matches complete direct DFTs at nine dyadic lengths N2 through N512. A separately authored recursive transfer evaluator matches 24 additional complete arbitrary-input transforms under alternating and contiguous root partitions. These controls verify the algebraic evaluators, not source-code timing or a new asymptotic algorithm.

## 6. Ancillary claims and their exact limits

At ordinary FFT stage boundaries, each linear form has support exactly 2^s. Different stages have different support sizes; within one stage the transformation is invertible, so distinct row forms cannot be proportional. The equality-only semantic merge stop rule is correct for these stored stage wires, including the bit-reversed input ordering. It is not a claim about every temporary twiddle product or about circuits synthesizing new shared expressions. Our stage-form controls at N4 through N64 find the predicted distinct projective forms.

The dense-star counterexample is sound: first sum all inputs into one register, then add it to each of the other registers. These are 2(N−1) invertible in-place additions; the resulting dense full-rank matrix is not Fourier. Density and rank alone are therefore insufficient arithmetic lower-bound measures.

For literal complete lookup tables over GF(p), r inputs have p^r possible keys. Since an Nth root requires p≥N+1, a budget of N^c entries bounds r by a constant (indeed r<c for integer c and positive N). This is a statement about complete explicit tables; it excludes compressed or algebraic representations and does not transfer to unrestricted exact-complex arithmetic.

The unitary entropy argument is also correctly scoped. A two-row unitary update preserves the per-column sum of squared magnitudes of the two rows. Its conditional binary entropy contribution changes by at most two bits in total. The normalized Fourier matrix has potential N log2 N, yielding the historical `(N/2)log2 N` two-row gate bound. The source's floating entropy runs are illustrations, not the proof, and the bound cannot be extended to arbitrary nonunitary operations or quantum readout.
