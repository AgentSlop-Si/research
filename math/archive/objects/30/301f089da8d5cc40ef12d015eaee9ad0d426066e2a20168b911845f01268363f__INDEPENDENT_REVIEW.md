# Independent review of common D8 fiber splitting

## Accepted result and scope

The all-size identity-monodromy, common D8 containment, fixed Walsh 1+1+2 route splitting, and endpoint-mask symmetry obstruction are valid under the stated even-grid and pulled-back-bank assumptions. Independent checks reproduce all 17 supplied joint-holonomy instances and their noncommuting witnesses. Equality of the joint group with D8 is accepted for those instances and whenever the explicit outside-C4 test succeeds; it is not asserted at all sizes.

This is an additive review of the deterministic nested four-sheet lift. It does not modify earlier seals or transfer its hypotheses to independently min-spliced orders at each size. In particular, it does not reject the accepted N16 coefficient seed. A common route splitting still leaves a coupled two-state sector and coefficient consistency questions.

## 1 Exact layout and premerge lift

Small vertices are (x,y) with label bx+y, where a=b or a=2b and a,b are even with b>=2. Large vertices have label 2bX+Y and dimensions 2a,2b. Write

    X=x+a epsilon_x, Y=y+b epsilon_y,
    e=2 epsilon_x+epsilon_y in {0,1,2,3}, N=ab.

For each of the eight modular-shear labels, T_S and T_L denote the unspliced covers. Let P_S be any small Hamiltonian cycle, not necessarily a special minimum-spliced one. Set eta=P_S T_S^(-1) and define eta_hat to act on low coordinates by eta while preserving both high sheet bits. The premerge route P0=eta_hat T_L covers P_S.

For a horizontal label with sign s and c in {0,1}, its sheet map is

    epsilon_y'=epsilon_y,
    epsilon_x'=epsilon_x+floor((x+s(2y+c))/a)+(2b/a)epsilon_y mod 2.

For a vertical label it is

    epsilon_x'=epsilon_x,
    epsilon_y'=epsilon_y+floor((y+s(2x+c))/b) mod 2.

The otherwise possible high-x coefficient 2a/b is even. The negative horizontal sign on 2b/a is immaterial modulo two. eta_hat does not alter these sheet maps.

The integer identity sum_(x=0)^(a-1) floor((x+u)/a)=u holds for every integer u, including negative u. Summing the carries over all small vertices therefore gives

    horizontal: s b(b-1+c),
    vertical:   s a(a-1+c).

Both are even. A base Hamiltonian traversal visits every small vertex exactly once. In one horizontal label all maps preserve epsilon_y and have the same shear coefficient, so their product adds the carry sum and N copies of that coefficient; both vanish modulo two. Vertical maps are translations of epsilon_y with even total carry. Hence the premerge sheet monodromy at base zero is identity for every label, independently of the base traversal order.

Each reference sheet then forms one premerge cycle of length N. There are exactly four, and each contains exactly one tail over base zero. Sorted by the declared physical label, those tails correspond to e=0,1,2,3.

## 2 The deterministic merge and common splitting

Let r=(0 1 2 3), with r(e)=e+1 modulo four. Rotating the original heads of the four sorted tails changes the edge map at base zero from phi_0 to phi_0 composed with r on its input. The rest of the base traversal is unchanged. Because its premerge product was identity, the final based monodromy is exactly r. This joins the four N-cycles into a single 4N-cycle.

Let D8 be the eight permutations preserving the unordered partition {{0,2},{1,3}}. This is the order-eight square-dihedral subgroup of S4. Each premerge horizontal or vertical map preserves the y bit up to a constant, so belongs to D8; r also belongs to D8. Every postmerge edge map of every label therefore lies in this same literal-coordinate subgroup.

With Walsh characters chi_c(e)=(-1)^(popcount(c AND e)), D8 preserves chi_0, sends chi_1 to plus or minus itself, and maps span(chi_2,chi_3) to itself. Thus every nested route and its scalar Green resolvent share

    span(chi_0) + span(chi_1) + span(chi_2,chi_3).

This is a fixed common basis, sharpening the generic four-sheet discussion. It does not require an order-dependent gauge merely to obtain these three blocks. The canonical monodromy r has sign +1 on chi_0, -1 on chi_1, and a signed transposition with product -1 on chi_2,chi_3. Accordingly each Green term uses signed cycles of lengths N,N,2N with denominators

    (1-tau^N), (1+tau^N), (1+tau^(2N)).

Their product is 1-tau^(4N), so the original nonzero Green denominator is exactly the required combined guard. The three scans visit 4N coordinates in total. No reduction of asymptotic work or recursive state count follows.

The parity assumption matters. For a=2,b=1, horizontal c=1 has odd carry sum and premerge monodromy (0 2)(1 3), giving two cycles of length 2N. The chosen two-tail merge can even leave the common D8 subgroup. This negative control is outside the theorem, not a contradiction to it; neither the four-precycle conclusion nor the containment proof is silently extended to b=1.

## 3 Exact common-diagonal-gauge criterion

Choose a root and paths in a connected base graph, with transport T_v from the root to vertex v. In the present application a single Hamiltonian base route provides such paths. For an oriented edge v->w define

    g_edge=T_w^(-1) phi_edge T_v.

Let H be the finite permutation group generated by these root holonomies. There is a base-position-dependent complex fiber basis making all edge transports diagonal if and only if H is abelian.

Necessity follows by transporting every closed walk to the root: in the proposed basis, all resulting matrices are diagonal and commute. Conversely, an abelian group of finite permutation matrices consists of commuting unitaries, so it has a common complex eigenbasis at the root. Transporting that basis by the T_v diagonalizes every edge. For a generic merely connected undirected graph, path traversal may use inverse transports; all transports here are invertible. The criterion concerns local four-dimensional fiber gauges, not arbitrary global changes of basis on the entire 4N space.

Here every T_v and phi_edge belongs to D8, so H is contained in D8. The complete first base route is a closed walk with monodromy r, so H contains the subgroup C4 generated by r. Since C4 has index two, H is either C4 or D8. Thus one gauged edge outside C4 proves H=D8 and rules out a common four-scalar fiber gauge. Equivalently, a noncommuting pair of generators is a certificate.

All 17 supplied instances have H=D8. The reviewer independently regenerates the routes, root paths, edge holonomies, entire group closure, and the specific source noncommuting witnesses. The instances comprise eight separately initialized one-level lifts at N4 through N512 and three levels from each seed N4,N8,N16. Some first lifts repeat, so this is a count of supplied instances rather than 17 distinct geometries. A positive C4 control confirms the commuting case. This finite evidence is not promoted to an all-level D8-equality theorem.

For eight fixed labels, path construction and collection of O(N) edge holonomies cost O(N) word work. Group closure is constant size, at most 24 permutations in S4. This is a finite exact diagnostic, not a hidden Fourier transform.

## 4 Endpoint-mask algebra

A physical diagonal mask a(e) in one fiber becomes XOR convolution in the Walsh basis:

    M_a[c,d]=a_hat[c XOR d],
    a_hat[c]=(1/4) sum_e a(e) chi_c(e).

Preserving the fine 1+1+2 decomposition termwise requires preserving the trivial character line. Since diag(a) applied to the all-ones vector equals a, that happens exactly when the four physical values are equal. Thus only fiber-constant masks preserve all fine sectors.

The coarse partition {chi_0,chi_1}+{chi_2,chi_3} is preserved exactly when a_hat[2]=a_hat[3]=0, equivalently

    a(0)=a(2), a(1)=a(3),
    a(e)=alpha+beta chi_1(e).

The physical nonzero guard is alpha+beta !=0 and alpha-beta !=0. It does not require both Walsh coefficients alpha and beta to be nonzero. This coarser mask family may mix the two one-dimensional fine sectors. A generic physical mask mixes the coarse sectors too.

These are termwise algebra statements. A mixed mask alone does not exclude cancellations among several terms, and the argument does not falsely promote termwise nonclosure to a no-go for all sums.

## 5 High-x commutation gate

The high-x swap X acts on sheets by (0 2)(1 3)=r^2 and on global labels by adding 2N modulo 4N. It is central in D8, so every postmerge route P_j commutes with X. Its valid scalar Green inverse also commutes with X.

A two-value mask alpha+beta chi_1 is independent of the high-x bit and commutes with X. Every sum of such masked Green terms, and every product of such layers, consequently commutes with X.

For the prescribed pulled-back active bank, a block at x is active exactly when the paired block at x+a is active. Both copies use identical F_(2b) or identical identity blocks. Thus B also commutes with X. This remains true under identical normalization changes on paired blocks.

The target Fourier matrix fails this symmetry:

    F_(4N)[0,1]=1,
    F_(4N)[2N,2N+1]=-1.

Commutation with X would force these simultaneous-shift entries to agree. Any nonzero overall Fourier normalization preserves the contradiction. Hence this coarse closed mask algebra cannot realize Fourier with the specified symmetric bank. Genuine high-x-dependent coefficients, an asymmetric bank, or another explicitly charged symmetry-breaking factor is necessary.

This clarifies an earlier rank-only control: a pure high-y character mask can supply the full rank required by the scalar-fold defect budget, yet still preserve X and fail this stronger Fourier-specific gate. Meeting a rank budget is not coefficient feasibility.

The accepted N16 seed is not rejected. Its independently chosen routes are not assumed to be these nested routes, and its bank diag(F4,F4,I4,I4) is not the paired pullback pattern from a four-point base: under the high-x shift by eight, its entries B[0,1]=1 and B[8,9]=0 already break the symmetry. A subsequent size lift with a paired bank must be evaluated under that new geometry rather than identifying the two bank patterns.
