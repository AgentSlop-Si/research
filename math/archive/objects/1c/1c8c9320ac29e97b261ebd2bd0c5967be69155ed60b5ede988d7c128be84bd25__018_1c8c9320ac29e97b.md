# Stability regularization for exact sub-`n log n` Fourier algorithms

**Date:** 2026-10-09  
**Status:** theorem-form research note; power-of-two lengths; depends on an effectively printable exact sub-`m log m` local DFT family. OpenAI #130 appears to satisfy the local-family hypothesis. This note does **not** claim an every-length floating-point stability theorem.

## 1. Target model

Let

\[
U_n = n^{-1/2}F_n
\]

be the normalized complex DFT. We measure work in charged scalar linear operations plus logarithmic-word indexing, and we allow extra complex registers initialized to zero.

For numerical conditioning we use a deliberately strong square-state notion. Enlarge the machine state to include:

- the active `n`-coordinate data bank;
- a second `n`-coordinate data bank;
- reusable local scratch.

Every elementary data operation is invertible on this whole state. If `M_t` is the cumulative square linear map on the full state after the first `t` data operations, define

\[
\kappa_t = \|M_t\|_2\,\|M_t^{-1}\|_2.
\]

The extra bank and scratch start at zero for the actual computation and are required to be zero again at the end.

## 2. Abstract local hypothesis

Assume there is one effective printer which, for every power of two `m=2^b`, eventually produces a complete exact linear program for `F_m` together with its scalar-preparation program, and whose charged variable-data work is `A(m)` with

\[
\frac{A(m)}{m\log m}\longrightarrow 0.
\]

The program is input-independent: branches, addresses, and coefficients depend on `m`, not on the input values.

OpenAI #130 supplies an all-length exact DFT algorithm with

\[
A(m)=O\!\left(m(\log m)^\theta(\log\log m)^{4-\theta}\right),\qquad \theta<1,
\]

including scalar preparation and indexing. Hence its variable-data arithmetic can be symbolically unrolled into a linear DAG with `g(m)=O(A(m)+m)` gates.

Append an exact scale by `m^{-1/2}` to obtain a DAG for `U_m`. For powers of two this normalization scalar is effectively preparable; if desired choose even `b`, or obtain `sqrt(2)` from an eighth root of unity.

## 3. Transparentizing a local block

The replay lemma already used in the #130 local compiler converts a linear DAG for a map `M` into an invertible shear word implementing

\[
(x,y,z)\mapsto (x,\ y+Mx,\ z)
\]

for arbitrary initial scratch `z`, restoring that scratch exactly. The shear count is `O(g+a)` for `a` outputs.

Apply this to `M=U_m` and to `M=U_m^{-1}`. Three transparent additions give

\[
\begin{aligned}
y&\leftarrow y+U_mx,\\
x&\leftarrow x-U_m^{-1}y,\\
y&\leftarrow y+U_mx.
\end{aligned}
\]

Algebraically,

\[
(x,y)\mapsto(-U_m^{-1}y,\ U_mx).
\]

The completed two-bank matrix is

\[
W_m=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&-U_m^{-1}\\
U_m&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Since `U_m` is unitary, `W_m` is unitary. Scratch is restored, so the completed map on banks plus scratch is `W_m \oplus I`.

The transparent word has

\[
s(m)=O(A(m)+m)=o(m\log m)
\]

charged data operations.

For the inverse, one may also use `U_m^{-1}=J U_m` because `U_m^2=J` for the usual DFT index-reversal permutation `J`; this avoids needing a fundamentally different local primitive.

## 4. Effective local condition certificate

Every elementary shear has form

\[
E=I+\alpha e_je_i^T,\qquad i\ne j,
\]

with inverse `I-alpha e_j e_i^T`. Therefore

\[
\|E\|_2\le 1+|\alpha|,\qquad
\|E^{-1}\|_2\le 1+|\alpha|,
\]

and

\[
\kappa_2(E)\le (1+|\alpha|)^2.
\]

If the transparent word contains shear coefficients `alpha_1,...,alpha_s`, then the single computable bound

\[
\widehat K(m)=\prod_{i=1}^{s}(1+|\alpha_i|)^2
\]

bounds the condition number of **every** cumulative prefix, because every factor on the right is at least one.

For #130 the prepared coefficients are algebraic expressions in rationals and a specified root of unity. For fixed `m` they can be evaluated symbolically in the cyclotomic field `Q(zeta_D)`. Reduce each coefficient to a rational polynomial in `zeta_D`; the `l1` norm of its rational coefficient vector gives a computable upper bound on its complex modulus. Hence an integer/rational certificate `Khat(m)>=K(m)` is effectively printable. The bound may be absurdly large; only finiteness and computability matter.

## 5. Condition-reset lemma with shared scratch

Consider one radix-`m` macro-stage of a larger transform. Process its disjoint `m`-coordinate fibers sequentially, reusing the same restored scratch.

During one fiber:

- already processed fibers carry completed `W_m` blocks;
- untouched fibers carry identity;
- the current fiber together with shared scratch carries one prefix of the transparent word.

After a coordinate permutation, the cumulative full-state map before the previously completed macro-levels is block diagonal. Multiplying on the right by the unitary map represented by previous completed macro-levels does not change singular values. Therefore

\[
\kappa_2(M_t)\le \widehat K(m)
\]

throughout the fiber.

After the whole macro-stage, every fiber has completed `W_m`; the stage boundary is unitary and scratch is restored. Unit-modulus twiddle diagonals and coordinate permutations are also unitary.

**Conclusion:** bad conditioning is local to the current macro-block. It does not multiply across the number of macro-stages.

## 5.1 No hidden uniform scaling

The full-state word consists of shears (determinant exactly one) and unitary/permutation factors (determinant of modulus one). Hence every cumulative full-state map satisfies

\[
|\det M_t|=1.
\]

Let its singular values be `sigma_1 >= ... >= sigma_d > 0` and suppose

\[
\kappa_2(M_t)=\sigma_1/\sigma_d\le R.
\]

Because their product is one,

\[
1=\prod_i\sigma_i\ge \sigma_1(\sigma_1/R)^{d-1},
\]

so

\[
\|M_t\|_2=\sigma_1\le R^{1-1/d}<R.
\]

Applying the same argument to `M_t^{-1}` gives

\[
\|M_t^{-1}\|_2<R.
\]

Thus the condition certificate cannot be satisfied by hiding an enormous common scaling of the whole state.

There is also a local coefficient consequence. Since

\[
\widehat K(m)=\prod_i(1+|\alpha_i|)^2\le R
\]

and every factor is at least one, each shear separately obeys

\[
|\alpha_i|\le \sqrt R-1.
\]

For the concrete selector `R(N)=O(log log N)`, the data-path scalar magnitudes in the selected transparent word are therefore at most `O(sqrt(log log N))` under this crude certificate.

## 6. Power-of-two macro work

Let

\[
N=2^k,\qquad m=2^b.
\]

Use normalized radix-`m` Cooley--Tukey for `floor(k/b)` full macro-stages, plus fewer than `b` ordinary normalized radix-two levels.

One full macro-stage uses `N/m` transparent `m`-blocks and `O(N)` twiddle/permutation work, hence

\[
O\!\left(\frac Nm s(m)+N\right).
\]

Therefore

\[
\frac{T(N)}{N\log N}
=O\!\left(
\frac{s(m)}{m\log m}
+\frac1{\log m}
+\frac{\log m}{\log N}
\right)
+o(1),
\]

where the final `o(1)` can absorb one-time local program preparation.

Thus any selected radix with

\[
m(N)\to\infty,\qquad \log m=o(\log N),\qquad
\frac{s(m)}{m\log m}\to0
\]

gives

\[
T(N)=o(N\log N).
\]

No relation between multiple inputs, sparse spectrum, partial output, or amortization is used.

## 7. Uniform bounded-printer selector

A naive staircase proof has a uniformity trap: computing the **next** large local block may itself take more work than the current transform. We avoid this using the same bounded-printer principle as the #130 synthesis appendix.

Construct one bit-machine printer which emits records for `m=2,4,8,...`. A record contains:

1. the explicit transparent shear/permutation word;
2. output indices and scratch layout;
3. the scalar-preparation program;
4. the exact shear count `s(m)`;
5. a certified integer condition upper bound `Khat(m)`.

Normalize the printer to emit at most one bit per step.

On input `N=2^k`:

1. run the printer from the beginning for at most `k=floor(log_2 N)` bit steps;
2. among completed records, keep only those with `m <= sqrt(k)` and
   \[
   \widehat K(m)\le R(N),
   \]
   where for a concrete theorem we may take
   \[
   R(N)=\max\{2,\lfloor\log_2\log_2(N+4)\rfloor\};
   \]
3. select the eligible record with largest `m`; if none exists, use the ordinary normalized radix-two FFT.

Because every fixed record has finite print time and finite `Khat(m)`, and because `R(N)->infinity`, every fixed record eventually becomes available and eligible. Hence the selected radix tends to infinity.

The explicit width restriction `m<=sqrt(k)` ensures

\[
\frac{\log m}{\log N}\to0.
\]

Since the selected record index tends to infinity, its local normalized cost ratio tends to zero. Printer execution, parsing, and scalar preparation cost only `poly(log N)` because the visible record has at most `log N` bits/instructions under the explicit-record convention.

Therefore this is **one fixed deterministic uniform algorithm**, not a nonuniform choice of thresholds.

## 8. Main theorem (power-of-two form)

### Theorem
Assume the effectively printable local hypothesis in Section 2. Then there is one deterministic exact algorithm which, for every sufficiently large power of two `N`, computes the normalized length-`N` DFT on an arbitrary fresh dense input and has all of the following properties:

1. **Work**
   \[
   T(N)=o(N\log N).
   \]

2. **Clean extra space**: one extra `N`-coordinate data bank plus `O(log N)` local scratch is initialized to zero and returned to zero.

3. **Square full-state conditioning**: if the computation is extended to arbitrary initial values of both banks and scratch, every cumulative invertible state map satisfies
   \[
   \kappa_2(M_t)\le R(N)=O(\log\log N).
   \]

4. **Unitary macro-boundaries**: after each complete macro-stage, the full data-bank map is unitary and scratch is restored.

More generally, the same bounded-printer construction works for any explicit nondecreasing unbounded condition budget `R(N)` whose value can be computed within `poly(log N)` work.

### Instantiation
OpenAI #130 appears to satisfy the effective local hypothesis: it gives one deterministic exact DFT algorithm at every length with `A(m)=o(m log m)`, counts scalar preparation and indexing, and its local machinery explicitly contains the arbitrary-scratch replay lemma needed for transparentization.

## 9. Alignment with Ailon's extra-space model

Ailon's Section 5 allows an additional memory of size at most `n log n`, initialized to zero. The clean-extra-space theorem assumes the first `n` outputs are the normalized Fourier transform and the extra outputs are zero, and proves an `Omega(n log n / R)` lower bound for `R`-uniformly well-conditioned circuits.

Our construction has, for data length `N`,

- one extra bank of size `N`;
- `O(log N)` selected-record scratch;
- zero initialization;
- exact restoration of the inactive bank and scratch at the end;
- a square full-state condition bound `R(N)`.

For large `N`, `N+O(log N) <= N log N`, so the space condition is satisfied. Complex linear maps may be realified by doubling the dimension; spectral condition numbers are preserved under realification. A fixed-size real simulation of each complex shear changes operation count only by a constant factor; any extra prefix-conditioning incurred by that simulation can be folded into the printable local condition certificate.

Thus the construction is compatible in spirit, and appears compatible line-by-line, with Ailon's **clean-extra-space** setting. It does not contradict the lower bound because `R(N)` diverges and the speedup obtained by the bounded-printer selector may diverge much more slowly than `R(N)`.
