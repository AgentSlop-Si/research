# Phase 11 — Fresh-input multiplication by annihilating filters

**October 9, 2026 — completed prototype and mathematical audit.**

**Claim boundaries:** Exact, fresh-input algorithms for structured polynomial convolution and near-power integer multiplication. A mathematically sound, instance-sensitive improvement, **not** a new worst-case exponent for general complex DFTs, a faster #130 algorithm, or a faster general integer-multiplication bit-complexity bound. No prior FFT/cached computation, hardware threading, or free offline preprocessing is assumed. The finite-field fallback is a conventional NTT.

## 1. How the user's A−k hypothesis generalizes

The identity `AB=(A−k)B+kB` is exact. It only helps if **both** new terms are inexpensive. A particularly valuable integer choice is `k=2^t`, so `kB` is a shift. For `A=2^t−1`, `AB=(B<<t)−B`, a linear bit-operations computation after materializing the operands, versus general multiplication.

For polynomial multiplication, replace subtracting one *number* with multiplication by a small **annihilating polynomial**. Let

`A(z)=Σ_{i=0}^{n-1} a_i z^i`, `B(z)=Σ_{i=0}^{m-1} b_i z^i`, `L=n+m−1`.

Choose two low-degree polynomials `Q_A(z),Q_B(z)` whose constant coefficients are one. Define

`D_A=Q_A A` and `D_B=Q_B B`.

The polynomial identity is exact:

`A B=(D_A D_B)/(Q_A Q_B)`.

Every polynomial with constant term one is invertible as a *formal power series*, so the first `L` coefficients of `A B` can be reconstructed by applying the two recurrences (`Q_A^{-1}` and `Q_B^{-1}`) modulo `z^L`. This remains valid in every commutative ring, including the integers and prime fields. **These are linear convolutions, not cyclic convolutions.**

For a filter `Q=1+q_1 z+...+q_d z^d`, recover `c_i` from `h=Q c` via:

`c_i=h_i−Σ_{j=1}^{min(d,i)} q_j c_{i−j}`.

There is no numerical division by a potentially zero variable. The normalization `Q(0)=1` is essential.

### A complete tiny example

Take `A=[3,3,3,3]`, `B=[2,1,4]`.

`Q_A=1−z` gives `D_A=[3,0,0,0,−3]`.

Compute `D_A*B` by shifting and subtracting: `3B−3z^4 B`.

Recover `A*B` by one cumulative sum (`1/(1−z)`):

`[6,9,21,21,15,12]`.

No FFT or prior cached result is involved.

## 2. Exact complexity theorem for sparse annihilators

Let `s_A=nnz(Q_A A)` and `s_B=nnz(Q_B B)` count all polynomial coefficients, including the trailing `deg Q` boundary terms. Let `d_A=deg Q_A`, `d_B=deg Q_B`.

- Build `D_A,D_B` in `O(d_A n + d_B m)` ring arithmetic.
- Multiply their sparse nonzero terms in `O(s_A s_B)` ring operations. Truncate intermediate coefficients beyond degree `L−1` because they cannot affect the requested output.
- Apply the two inverse recurrences in `O((d_A+d_B)L)` arithmetic.
- Materialize the `L` coefficients in `Ω(L)` and `O(L)` additional memory/time.

Thus,

`T_structure = O((d_A+d_B+1)L+s_A s_B)`

for comparable input sizes, when the two filters are given; fixed-degree detection adds `O(L)` work if it scans a constant-size filter family. A bounded Berlekamp–Massey discovery of order at most D costs `O(LD)` in the worst accepted structured case, and can abort early on high-complexity sequences.

For any length-n input obeying an exact order-d linear recurrence, the polynomial `Q A` has at most the first `d` and last `d` coefficients nonzero, i.e. `s_A≤2d`. So if `d=O(1)`, multiplication by an arbitrary fresh, length-m B uses **O(n+m)** field arithmetic and produces an explicit full output in O(n+m) time. This is an output-optimal *restricted-input* result, not a newly discovered theorem.

The algebra covers:

- constant sequences: `Q=1−z`, `s≤2`;
- arithmetic progression: `Q=(1−z)^2`, `s≤4`;
- quadratic progressions: `(1−z)^3`, `s≤6`;
- geometric progression: `Q=1−rz`, `s≤2`;
- Fibonacci recurrence: `Q=1−z−z²`, `s≤4`;
- piecewise linear recurrences: one Q with a small number of exceptional indices, each exception increases the sparse support.

For exact modular NTT inputs at lengths supported by the chosen prime (the bundled prime 998244353 supports power-of-two lengths up to 2^23), the prototype can also choose an O(N log N) fallback. This finite range by itself does **not** constitute an all-length asymptotic NTT theorem over the same fixed prime. The mathematical selection target is:

`O(L D + min{NTT(L), s_A s_B+(d_A+d_B+1)L})`

for any fixed/filter-scan family of bounded degree D, **only where a valid O(N log N) transform branch is available in the stated model**. The production prototype uses conservative sampling to skip unpromising filters; this can miss a speed opportunity, but it **cannot affect correctness**. Its fallback is an ordinary O(N log N) NTT, not OpenAI #130.

For unbounded integer coefficients, the prototype uses enough NTT primes and CRT to reconstruct exact signed outputs where feasible, then falls back to exact schoolbook convolution for coefficients exceeding the bundled primes. Thus no unrestricted bit-complexity bound follows from this implementation. For huge integers, bit length of intermediate coefficients must be charged.

## 3. Why short-filter sparseness cannot be guaranteed in the worst case

Here is a rigorous barrier (limited specifically to short annihilator filters).

Let `A(z)∈F₂[z]` have n independent uniform binary coefficients. Fix any polynomial `Q(z)∈F₂[z]` of degree ≤d with constant term `Q(0)=1`.

The map `a↦(Q A mod z^n)` is an invertible lower-triangular linear map on `F₂^n` (diagonal entries are all 1). Therefore its output is still a **uniform random binary vector**. Its Hamming weight has distribution `Binomial(n,1/2)`.

Hoeffding gives:

`Pr[weight <= n/3] <= exp(-n/18)`.

There are exactly `2^d` polynomials of degree ≤d with constant term one. A union bound gives:

`Pr[there exists degree<=d Q with weight(QA mod z^n)<=n/3] <= 2^d exp(-n/18)`.

For `d=o(n)`, this tends to zero. Thus with overwhelming probability **no short binary annihilator, even chosen adaptively from all 2^d choices, yields a sparse representation**. Reducing integer coefficients modulo 2 gives the same barrier for integer filters with constant term one applied to random 0/1 sequences: every odd filtered coefficient is certainly nonzero as an integer.

This is a negative theorem about **this family of methods**, not a general Fourier or multiplication lower bound. It does not exclude other nonlinear encodings, unrestricted complex circuits, or entirely different decompositions.

`barrier_audit.py` exhaustively checked bijectivity for n=8, all degree≤3 filters and sampled all degree≤8 filters on a random n=1024 binary array. The latter had minimum transformed Hamming weight 470, versus threshold floor(n/3)=341.

## 4. Why it doesn't improve the #130 universal exponent

OpenAI #130 supplies an exact all-length DFT in a model with unbounded, exact complex registers, unrestricted constant scalars, word-RAM address operations, and an appropriate given root. It is `O(N (log N)^(1−10^−13))`, without numerical-stability or bit-complexity guarantees. That is a different model from a fixed-prime modular NTT or an arbitrary integer's bit complexity.

Our prototype detects zeros and recurrences from the **input values**, using modular arithmetic and branching. It cannot be imported as a linear circuit in #130's precise computation model without separately accounting for permissible zero tests and selection. More fundamentally, the filtered representation remains dense on generic inputs. Thus no new universal exponent is established.

Also, the Fourier transforms of `A−k·1` differ from `FFT(A)` only at DC for a constant array offset. A shift of the *polynomial constant coefficient* instead modifies every Fourier bin. Neither trick removes the generic transform's full work automatically.

The correct comparison is to existing research in polynomial/linear recurrence algorithms, generating functions, Berlekamp–Massey, and sparse convolution—not to a new proof of FFT optimality.

## 5. Reproducible results

Execute:

```bash
python -m unittest discover -s tests -v
python barrier_audit.py
python benchmark.py --sizes 256 1024 4096 8192 --repeats 3
python benchmark_near_power.py
```

The benchmark uses **fresh arrays for both algorithms**, no previous FFT state, one Python process, no threads. The baseline is an exact, plain Python iterative NTT over F998244353. Correctness is tested against exact arithmetic in the same field. Times count detection, algebra, coefficients, and full output. This is not a comparison with native FFTW/GMP or #130.

The last recorded run at 8192 coefficients:

| Fresh input A | Baseline exact NTT | Selected algorithm | Speedup |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Constant | 88.07 ms | 28.36 ms | 3.10x |
| Arithmetic progression | 99.22 ms | 30.18 ms | 3.29x |
| Geometric progression | 102.17 ms | 22.24 ms | 4.59x |
| Fibonacci recurrence | 103.67 ms | 28.54 ms | 3.63x |
| Random binary | 99.93 ms | 108.78 ms | **0.92x (slower)** |

The near-power experiment used arbitrary fresh B and exact Python big integers. For `A=2^262144−1`, the `(B<<262144)-B` path was ~119.5x faster in the local microbenchmark than generic built-in multiplication. For random unrelated inputs, the dispatcher used the ordinary multiplication fallback; it was not faster. Neither is a universal multiplication result, and native libraries may have specialized code for such cases.

## 6. Prior art and originality

The algebraic identities, short linear recurrences, Berlekamp–Massey, finite-difference convolution, sparse multiplication, and NTT are all established mathematics and algorithms. Our contribution **within this research project** is unifying them into a conservative *fresh-input* dispatcher, documenting operation counts and a precise random-input barrier, implementing a working exact version, and testing full preprocessing and output costs. We make no first-ever discovery, lower-bound breakthrough, or priority claim.

## 7. Next falsifiable goal

Search for *new* families of cheaply invertible, **input-independent** transforms whose outputs admit small-work multiplication under a rigorous, all-input guarantee. The short-polynomial annihilator attempt shows that mere sparsity cannot achieve it on random inputs. A next candidate would need a proved compression or computational-sharing property beyond sparse coefficients, with all inverse transforms and preparation charged. An improvement to general exact DFT or integer multiplication would require a uniform algorithm in the relevant model, not just an adaptive special case.

### Optimized native FFT reality check

The same exact Python structured algorithm was compared to native SciPy `fftconvolve` on **bounded small integer inputs only**, where double-precision outputs could be checked by rounding against exact integer results. These are different numerical models: SciPy's FFT has no general exact-integer guarantee. On constant-by-random inputs, single-run medians over 7 repetitions showed:

| Length n | Exact structured Python | Native SciPy float FFT |
|---|---:|---:|
| 1,024 | 3.697 ms | 0.117 ms |
| 4,096 | 14.329 ms | 0.287 ms |
| 8,192 | 26.248 ms | 0.585 ms |

So the Python engine is **far slower than optimized native FFT code** in this comparison. The reduced ring-operation bound is not a hardware performance victory. The optional reproducible test is `benchmark_native.py`; NumPy and SciPy are required only for that optional test.
