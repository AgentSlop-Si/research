# Bundled sparse-kernel/condition lemma

This self-contained reference supplies the lemma used in `PROOF.md`, Section 6. It is extracted and independently restated from the earlier agent-authored low-rank gate; no outside files are needed.

Let F be a flat N-by-N unitary, A invertible, and rank(F-A)<=B. Then

    kappa_1(A) >= N/(B+1)^2.

For B<N, choose B+1 columns of F-A. They are dependent, so there is a nonzero vector x supported on s=B+1 coordinates with Ax=Fx. Flatness gives ||Fx||_infinity <= ||x||_1/sqrt(N) <= sqrt(s/N)||x||_2. Unitarity gives ||Fx||_2=||x||_2. Since ||y||_2^2<=||y||_infinity||y||_1,

    ||Ax||_1=||Fx||_1 >= sqrt(N/s)||x||_2,
    ||A||_1 >= sqrt(N)/s.

The identity F^-1-A^-1=F^-1(A-F)A^-1 preserves the rank bound. Since F^-1 is also flat unitary, the same argument gives ||A^-1||_1>=sqrt(N)/s. Multiply the inequalities. For B>=N the condition-number claim follows from kappa_1(A)>=1. A nonzero scalar multiplying F can be removed without changing a condition number.

If B rank-one perturbations of identity are replaced by I in a product, its difference from the original product has rank at most B, by telescoping the product one factor at a time. If the retained factors are invertible, their product A is invertible. Submultiplicativity then combines any available bounds on their induced-1 condition numbers with this lemma, irrespective of the interleaving order.

For completeness, the earlier bare Green primitive is M=B0^-1+(1/20)T^-1, with T=tridiag(-1,3,-1) and B0 a permutation conjugate of T. The bounds ||T||_1<=5 and ||T^-1||_1<=1 follow by a Neumann series for T=3I-E, ||E||_1<=2. They also hold for B0. Thus ||M||_1<=21/20. Preserving order,

    M^-1=[I+(1/20)B0 T^-1]^-1 B0,

and the perturbation has norm at most 1/4, so ||M^-1||_1<=20/3 and kappa_1(M)<=7. Inverses have the same condition number. This is why the combined bound in `PROOF.md` is 7^s c_N^(2r)(B+1)^2>=N.

These are factor-count bounds. Sparse rank-one factors are not automatically full-array passes, and algebraic fusion requires a separate cost analysis.
