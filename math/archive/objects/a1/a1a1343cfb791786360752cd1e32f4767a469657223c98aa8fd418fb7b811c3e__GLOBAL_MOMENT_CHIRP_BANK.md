# A full jointly summed chirp bank, with an exact scalar-resolvent count

## Result and boundary

There is an explicit full-matrix construction for every odd N≥3, including every N=5q² in the admitted restricted family. It does not use independently fitted 5×5 blocks. It writes the actual forced inverse B_N as two globally coupled chirp-circulant banks and one diagonal term, proving all N² entries at once.

For each of the two chirp circulants, exactly r=(N+1)/2 scalar-resolvent branches are necessary and sufficient in the following precise model:

    C = Σ_(s=1)^r a_s (I−λ_s P)⁻¹,

where P is the natural N-cycle, a_s are scalars, λ_s≠0, and λ_s^N≠1. There is no additional diagonal correction in this per-circulant model. The construction supplies distinct λ_s and nonzero a_s. The two complete chirp banks give N+1 nontrivial Green occurrences plus one zero-edge diagonal occurrence for B_N. This is an upper bound for the full inverse, not its minimum in the much broader arbitrary-endpoint/arbitrary-order grammar.

The construction is finite and exact. A specified polynomial of degree r defines the nodes; an explicit search among the 2N+1 integers 0,…,2N is proved to find a polynomial with distinct, nonzero, nonresonant roots. A root-free quotient-algebra implementation avoids generating those roots. All quotient-algebra operations are expanded into base-field operations: the supplied elementary generator costs O(N³), and each arbitrary-input application costs O(N²). Its growing internal state has dimension r. There is no FFT speedup and no free scalar-field extension.

The finite-moment, Hankel-rank, and formal-orthogonal-polynomial machinery is established mathematics, not claimed new. The deliverable is a project-specific exact specialization, legal-pole certificate, all-entry B_N lift, and fully charged root-free algorithm. Publication-level novelty of even this specialization is unverified.

## 1. Full target and shared chirp endpoints

Work over K=Q(ω) embedded in C, with a supplied primitive odd-N root ω. Set h=2⁻¹ modulo N and define

    F[i,j] = ω^(ij),
    A[i,i] = ω^(h i²),         D=A⁻¹,
    H[i,j] = ω^(−h(i+j)²),
    G = Σ_(k=0)^(N−1) ω^(h k²),     c=NG,
    B = (F⁻¹+A)⁻¹.

The predecessor's cubic identity yields

    B = (F−GH+cD)/(1+c).                                  (1)

Here |G|²=N, so |c|=N^(3/2)>1 and 1+c≠0. One elementary Gauss verification is to expand G times its complex conjugate and use the invertible change of variables (u,v)=(x−y,x+y) modulo odd N; the resulting double character sum equals N. Thus none of the full target's required scalars vanishes.

For σ∈{−1,+1}, define the circulant

    C_σ[i,j] = ω^(σ h(i−j)²),
    f_σ(k) = ω^(σ h k²),      0≤k<N.

Then the exact entry identities are

    F = A C_− A,
    H = D² C_+ D²,
    B = [A C_− A − G D² C_+ D² + cD]/(1+c).               (2)

For example, the exponent in the second identity is

    −i² + h(i−j)² − j² = −h(i+j)² mod N.

No tensor block or fiber coupling is suppressed. Every C_σ is an N×N matrix. Its input and output chirp diagonals are shared across its entire bank.

Let P have entries P[i,i+1 mod N]=1. Since P^N=I,

    (I−λP)⁻¹ = [Σ_(k=0)^(N−1) λ^k P^k]/(1−λ^N).         (3)

The (i,j) entry uses k=j−i mod N. The target's f_σ is even modulo N, so this convention gives exactly C_σ.

## 2. Exact lower bound for the jointly summed scalar bank

Suppose C_σ has Q legal scalar-resolvent branches as in the result. Comparing the N coefficients of the linearly independent matrices I,P,…,P^(N−1) gives

    f_σ(k) = Σ_(s=1)^Q b_s λ_s^k,  0≤k<N,
    b_s = a_s/(1−λ_s^N).                                (4)

Put r=(N+1)/2. The r×r Hankel matrix M_r with entries f_σ(i+j), 0≤i,j<r, uses only these N moments. Equation (4) expresses it as a sum of Q rank-one matrices. Therefore rank M_r≤Q.

But, for every j≤N, the j×j chirp moment matrix factors as

    M_j = diag(f_σ(0),…,f_σ(j−1))
          · [ (ω^(σ i))^k ]_(0≤i,k<j)
          · diag(f_σ(0),…,f_σ(j−1)).                    (5)

The middle matrix is Vandermonde at distinct nodes ω^(σ i). Consequently

    det M_j = (Π_(i=0)^(j−1) f_σ(i))²
              Π_(0≤i<k<j)(ω^(σ k)−ω^(σ i)) ≠ 0.

In particular rank M_r=r, proving Q≥r.

This lower bound permits joint cancellation among every branch; it is not a one-pair-per-block argument. It counts scalar-resolvent branches, not distinct cycle closing weights. The closing weight of λ_sP is t_s=λ_s^N; different λ_s can have equal t_s. Repeated λ_s can simply be combined and cannot evade the rank bound.

Scope is important. This theorem does not permit branch-dependent nonconstant diagonal endpoints, changing P between branches, a separate diagonal correction, products of banks, or an unrelated fused arithmetic circuit. It does not lower-bound the unrestricted minimum for B.

## 3. A constructive matching upper bound with legal roots

Fix either sign and abbreviate f=f_σ. Define the K-linear moment functional

    L(z^k)=f(k),

using the same periodic chirp formula also at k=N. For each 0≤j≤r, let P_j(z) be the monic degree-j polynomial satisfying

    L(z^k P_j(z))=0,  0≤k<j.                            (6)

Set P_0=1. For j>0 its j unknown lower coefficients are found by solving

    M_j · (p_0,…,p_(j−1))^T
       = −(f(j),…,f(2j−1))^T.                          (7)

All these systems are nonsingular by (5). Only P_r and P_(r−1) need to be computed. The extra moment used for P_r is f(N)=1.

There is also a direct coefficient formula, avoiding these linear solves. Define

    V_j(y)=Π_(k=0)^(j−1)(y−ω^(σ k)),
    P_j(z)=f(j) Σ_(l=0)^j ([y^l]V_j(y))/f(l) · z^l.    (7a)

The leading coefficient is one, and L(z^kP_j)=f(k)f(j)V_j(ω^(σk))=0 for k<j. Thus the monic polynomial is exactly the one in (7). Forming the two products gives P_r and P_(r−1) in O(r²) field operations. The checker independently compares this explicit formula with Gaussian elimination of the Hankel systems.

### 3.1 Why the two polynomials are coprime

This part needs no positive measure or real-root theorem. Let Δ_j=det M_j, Δ_0=1. The usual change of monic polynomial basis in the moment Gram matrix gives

    ν_j := L(P_j²) = Δ_(j+1)/Δ_j ≠ 0,   0≤j<r.

The monic polynomials have the algebraic three-term recurrence

    P_(j+1) = (z−α_j)P_j − β_j P_(j−1),
    β_j = ν_j/ν_(j−1) ≠ 0,             1≤j<r.          (8)

For completeness, expand zP_j−P_(j+1) in P_0,…,P_j. Pairing with P_k for k≤j−2 gives zero, because zP_k has degree below j. Pairing with P_(j−1) gives coefficient ν_j/ν_(j−1). Thus only the displayed two lower terms remain. A common zero of P_r and P_(r−1) would propagate backwards through (8) to P_0=1. Hence they are coprime.

### 3.2 A finite search that cannot fail

For τ∈K put

    p_τ(z)=P_r(z)+τP_(r−1)(z).                          (9)

Choose the first integer τ in 0,…,2N such that

    p_τ(0) ≠ 0,
    gcd(p_τ,p_τ′)=1,
    gcd(p_τ,z^N−1)=1.                                  (10)

There is always such an integer. To prove this, write P=P_r and R=P_(r−1), which are coprime.

- A repeated root α of P+τR must satisfy R(α)≠0, τ=−P(α)/R(α), and P′(α)R(α)−P(α)R′(α)=0. The latter polynomial is monic of degree 2r−2. There are at most 2r−2 bad τ values for squarefreeness.
- For each of the N roots ξ of z^N−1 there is at most one τ making P(ξ)+τR(ξ)=0. If R(ξ)=0, coprimality makes that impossible for every τ. Thus at most N values cause resonance.
- At most one value makes p_τ(0)=0.

The union has at most (2r−2)+N+1=2N elements. The 2N+1 distinct integers in the specified search therefore cannot all fail. This is an exact finite algorithm, not a numerical genericity assumption. It works in characteristic zero; the checker also uses prime fields of characteristic greater than 2N.

Let p be the selected polynomial and λ_1,…,λ_r its roots in an algebraic closure. They are distinct and satisfy

    λ_s≠0,       λ_s^N≠1.                              (11)

Since both summands in (9) obey the relevant orthogonality conditions,

    Σ_(j=0)^r p_j f(k+j)=0,  0≤k≤r−2.                  (12)

This is exactly the recurrence needed to extend r initial moments to all 2r−1=N target moments.

### 3.3 Explicit residues and complete moment matching

Write p(z)=Σ_(j=0)^r p_j z^j, p_r=1, and define

    q(z)=Σ_(j=0)^(r−1)
            [Σ_(l=0)^(r−1−j) p_(j+1+l) f(l)] z^j.     (13)

This is the polynomial part of p(z) times Σ_(k≥0) f(k)z^(−k−1). Define

    b_s=q(λ_s)/p′(λ_s),
    a_s=(1−λ_s^N)b_s.                                  (14)

Partial fractions give q(z)/p(z)=Σ_s b_s/(z−λ_s). Its first r Laurent coefficients at infinity are f(0),…,f(r−1) by (13). All subsequent coefficients obey the p recurrence. Equation (12) therefore proves

    Σ_s b_s λ_s^k=f(k),   0≤k<N.                       (15)

Each b_s is nonzero. Otherwise the r×r Hankel matrix in (5) would be a sum of fewer than r rank-one matrices. By (11), every a_s is nonzero as well.

Combining (3) and (15) proves the all-entry identity

    C_σ = Σ_(s=1)^r a_(σ,s) (I−λ_(σ,s)P)⁻¹.            (16)

This attains the lower bound in Section 2. No root is selected numerically; the polynomial and residue rule define the unordered set of branches exactly. If individually expanded algebraic nodes are requested, extracting them incurs an additional cost not hidden in the following root-free implementation.

## 4. The full legal Green representation

Insert the independently generated σ=− and σ=+ banks into (2):

    B = Σ_s [a_(−,s)/(1+c)] A(I−λ_(−,s)P)⁻¹A
        + Σ_s [−G a_(+,s)/(1+c)] D²(I−λ_(+,s)P)⁻¹D²
        + [c/(1+c)]D.                                  (17)

The last term is the allowed zero-edge Green branch, since (I−0·P)⁻¹=I. Every endpoint diagonal in this displayed construction is nonsingular. The nontrivial branches have all edge weights equal to a nonzero λ, and the cycle closure guards follow from λ^N≠1.

The construction is valid for all odd N≥3. In particular it gives a uniform growing-N family at N=5q² with q odd, gcd(q,5)=1, q≡1 mod 241. At q=483 it is a full 1,166,445-dimensional identity with 1,166,446 displayed nontrivial occurrences and one diagonal occurrence. This count is large; it is not a three-Green lift of the five-coordinate restriction.

The construction never asks a coupled 5×5 block to choose one reciprocal pair. Every block receives the sum of the entire global bank. Thus the predecessor's forced single-pair pole invariant is not applicable to (17). The price is explicit: the number of jointly contributing branches grows linearly with N.

## 5. Root-free implementation and complete arithmetic charges

The individually labeled roots may lie in a large splitting field. Treating arithmetic there as unit cost would be unjustified. Instead let

    E=K[z]/(p(z)),          dim_K E=r.

This is a squarefree finite K-algebra, possibly a product of fields. Let z denote its residue class and set

    b=q(z)/p′(z) ∈ E,
    a=(1−z^N)b ∈ E.

Both divisions required here and in the Green inverse are legal by (10). Since p is squarefree, the algebra trace is the sum over all its roots. Thus (16) is equivalent to

    C_σ = Tr_(E/K) [a (I−zP)⁻¹],                       (18)

where the trace is applied entry by entry. This is a succinct exact representation of the sum, not a scalar-width-one algorithm.

### Setup cost in K operations

A sufficient elementary implementation does the following:

1. Generate f(0),…,f(N) by quadratic-phase recurrences in O(N) operations.
2. Form P_r and P_(r−1) by the explicit coefficient products (7a): O(r²) operations. The checker additionally uses independent O(r³) Hankel solves to cross-check this formula; those solves are unnecessary for the algorithm.
3. Test at most 2N+1 parameters. Classical polynomial gcds for degrees at most N cost O(N²) per candidate. This gives O(N³) worst-case work for the certified search.
4. Form q and invert p′ modulo p using the extended Euclidean algorithm: O(r²).
5. Compute z^N modulo p by N companion multiplications, O(Nr), and invert 1−z^N modulo p, O(r²).
6. Precompute the K-linear functional ℓ(y)=Tr_(E/K)(ay). Newton sums give the traces of 1,z,…,z^(r−1) in O(r²), and companion multiplication gives its r coefficients in another O(r²).

Thus S(N)=O(N³) base-field operations is an explicit upper bound. It is not claimed optimal. Two signs change constants only. Chirp diagonals, G, c, and guards cost another O(N). The plan stored after setup uses O(N) K coefficients; setup may use O(N²) temporary storage.

### One arbitrary-input application

To apply (I−zP)⁻¹ to x∈K^N, first compute

    y_0 = [Σ_(k=0)^(N−1) z^k x_k]/(1−z^N) in E.        (19)

Use Horner evaluation for the numerator. Then compute backwards

    y_i=x_i+z y_(i+1),  i=N−1,…,1, with y_N=y_0.         (20)

Output ℓ(y_i) for each i. The first equation y_0−zy_1=x_0 follows from the closure formula (19).

The quotient algebra is not a unit-cost scalar:

- multiplying an E vector by z modulo monic p costs O(r) K operations;
- a general E multiplication by the precomputed closure inverse costs O(r²) using schoolbook arithmetic and reduction;
- evaluating ℓ on an E vector costs O(r).

Therefore the whole application costs O(Nr+r²)=O(N²) K operations and O(N+r) working storage. Equation (17) uses two such passes, chirp scaling, and a diagonal correction. It has the same O(N²) per-application bound. All branch summation and closure work are included.

The total certified setup plus first B application is O(N³). Three B calls give the predecessor's Fourier-recovery polynomial, still O(N³) including setup and O(N²) per input after setup. Alternatively F=A C_−A uses just the negative bank directly, with the same asymptotic conclusion.

A fused implementation may replace the explicitly recovered circulant action by ordinary convolution. That returns to the standard chirp-convolution Fourier reduction; it does not turn the r-dimensional algebra in (18) into constant-width arithmetic. The present work proves neither a faster fused algorithm nor a lower bound against all such algorithms.

These counts are in supplied-root exact arithmetic over K, or for linear application to inputs in a containing field. Computing ω, rational-coordinate bit growth in Q(ω), coefficient conditioning, numerical root extraction, and finite-precision stability are not covered by a bit-complexity or numerical-runtime theorem here. No extra algebraic root extraction is needed for the stated root-free algorithm.

## 6. Relation to the existing cut-rank results

The common-order linear branch constraint is already present in the project's separation-rank/cut-rank corpus. It is not a new claim of this report.

For completeness, if every Green branch uses the same Hamiltonian order and only diagonal endpoints, choose I to be a consecutive half-cycle segment in that order. Every branch has rank at most one on I×Iᶜ, even with zero edge weights. For an invertible matrix, complementary off-diagonal cuts have the same rank in the matrix and its inverse. Therefore

    rank B[I,Iᶜ] = rank F⁻¹[I,Iᶜ]
                  ≥ |I||Iᶜ|/N.                        (21)

The last inequality is the Fourier submatrix Frobenius/operator-norm argument. Thus Q≥ceil(N/4) for odd N, even after choosing any one common order. With the natural coordinate order, the Fourier cut is Vandermonde and has rank (N−1)/2, giving the sharper Q≥(N−1)/2.

This context is consistent with (17). The exact r bound proved here is narrower in its endpoint and shift assumptions but stronger for one chirp circulant. Neither (21) nor Section 2 excludes every bank using independently changing global orders.

## 7. Literature and claimed contribution

Complex moment matching, formal orthogonal polynomials, Hankel systems, quadrature, and minimal partial realization are established connected topics. Pozza and Pranić explicitly treat this general complex setting, including its algebraic moment formulation: [The Gauss quadrature for general linear functionals, Lanczos algorithm, and minimal partial realization](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.11395), especially Sections 2–6. The positive-measure simple-real-root guarantee from ordinary Gaussian quadrature cannot be assumed for the present complex chirp moments; the finite legal-parameter argument in Section 3 supplies the required guard directly.

The modest project-specific delta is the complete combination of:

- the explicit chirp Hankel/Vandermonde determinant;
- minimal legal scalar-resolvent count for the entire circulant;
- a finite 2N+1-parameter guard search avoiding zero, repeated, and cycle-resonant nodes;
- the all-entry full forced-inverse synthesis;
- a root-free, fully charged degree-r implementation and runnable exact controls.

This is a constructive boundary result. It establishes a global identity where the earlier small-block lift was incomplete, while showing why this particular shared-endpoint joint bank still has linear width. It does not claim a new general moment theorem, a publication-level novelty result, a global FFT lower bound, or an improved FFT.
