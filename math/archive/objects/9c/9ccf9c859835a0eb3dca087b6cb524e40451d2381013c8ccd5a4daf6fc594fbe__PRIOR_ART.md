# Phase 20 provenance and priority boundaries

1. **Project lineage.** Phase 20 follows the user's own Phase 12–19 Green/graph research, preserving the complete earlier phase archive in `previous_full_phase19_checkpoint.zip`. It is not an adaptation of CrocSwap or Swapnil-jain's proof strategy for integer multiplication.
2. **Cross-ratio prior art.** Four-entry projective invariants of complex Hadamard matrices are classical Haagerup-style tools. Östergård & Valtonen (2026), *Equivalence of complex Hadamard matrices*, Journal of Algebraic Combinatorics, https://doi.org/10.1007/s10801-026-01568-x, explicitly discusses the invariant. Phase 20's contribution is a two-black-box-call Green-circuit use case and an exact two-column subgroup histogram/complete exponent-grid implementation. Do not claim the original cross-ratio idea is new.
3. **Weyl duality.** The finite Heisenberg/Weyl commutation relations and uniqueness of Fourier as an intertwiner are standard. The gap formula follows from diagonalization of the commutator energy in the orthogonal Weyl basis; its project-specific reproducible bound and verifier are emphasized, not historical priority.
4. **Other classical tools.** The Dirichlet eigenvalues of a path graph, field-rank tests, sparse-matrix support arithmetic, and the real/imaginary Fourier parity decomposition are elementary or well established. The specific restricted architecture statements and explicit audit scripts are this project's derivations.
5. **Existing exact DFT upper bound.** The 2026 OpenAI math materials document an all-length exact-complex-register DFT with `O(N(log N)^(1-10^-13))` arithmetic and unbounded exact complex precision: https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/lean/docs/130.md . No Phase 20 theorem supersedes this complexity bound, nor produces finite-precision or bit-complexity improvements.
6. **Limited negative tests.** Modular rejection of sampled, phase-gauged, independently permuted near-resonant Green sums is exact for those modular instances. It is not an all-parameter theorem. The *stationary* commutator theorem excludes independent permutations and arbitrary intermediate diagonal phases; the *real-mask* theorem excludes additional complex outer or intermediate masks.
