# Local ordinary discriminants of Rogers Szegő polynomials

## Mathematical contribution worth developing

For n = Ad + b, the known root-of-unity factorization creates d clusters of multiplicity A. The separated case has a Hermite leading model. When the odd-degree residual factor shares only the root −1, that cluster instead has multiplicity A + 1 and an explicitly normalized leading polynomial

J(y;τ) = He_(A+1)(y) + A(1−τ)He_(A−1)(y).

Its parameter τ is determined by a finite root-of-unity product and the residual derivative. Under the stated nonvanishing guards, the exact discriminant order changes from dA(A−1)/2 to dA(A−1)/2 + A. The coefficient retains its sign and every residual discriminant and resultant factor. This precise fixed-root statement is the principal candidate contribution.

Two arithmetic consequences give the note substance. In the overlap case, reciprocal root parameters and a characteristic-p selection lemma produce five infinite squarefreeness families. In the separated case, a global integral factorization plus one good residue component gives the conditional valuation

v_p(c_(sp)) = floor(3s(p−1)/8),

for s, p ≡ 1 modulo 4, p prime, and p not dividing s c_s. Here c denotes the separated content, not the paired overlap content.

## What must be credited

The root-of-unity factorization and the q→1 Hermite limit are established. J is a quasi-Hermite combination whose parity reduction is quasi-Laguerre; its standalone discriminant follows from Sawa–Uchida, Theorem 4.8. The Wronskian identity is a hook specialization of known pseudo-Wronskian equivalence and the Grosu–Grosu congruence. The minimum-valuation and cyclotomic-ramification step is standard. The possible contribution is the specific local coefficient and its arithmetic combination, not new versions of these general theories.

## Recommendation and publication gate

Develop one focused note: local coefficients, ordinary discriminants, reciprocal-residue selection, then conditional content valuations. Keep exact finite arrays as supporting evidence and the restricted scalar-bank consequence in an optional appendix. No faster FFT, all-composite theorem, unrestricted inverse minimum, or general repeated-prime result follows.
