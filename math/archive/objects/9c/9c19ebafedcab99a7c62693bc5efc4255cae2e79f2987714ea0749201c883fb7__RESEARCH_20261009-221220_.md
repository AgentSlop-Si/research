# Two-Green parallel-sum and three-tap semiseparable gates for exact Fourier research — v17

**Checkpoint:** 10 October 2026  
**Primary target:** exact all-output DFT of an arbitrary fresh input in `o(N log N)` fully charged sequential arithmetic, ideally `O(N)`.  
**Status:** two new scoped impossibility theorems with exact finite-field verification, plus a nonproof numerical search. **No exact faster FFT is constructed.** The v15 all-length randomized approximate `O(N log log N)` branch is unchanged.

## 1. Why this continuation was chosen

The v16 checkpoint asked for a **full-rank global noncommuting operator** that is applicable in linear or near-linear time and whose Fourier-side companion is also genuinely cheaper than an FFT. A particularly strong candidate is a sum of two independently ordered cyclic Green resolvents:

\[
T_1+T_2,
\qquad
T_r=L_r(I-\alpha_r P_r)^{-1}R_r.
\]

Each term is dense and full rank, its cycle order may be completely non-affine, and it applies in `O(N)` arithmetic by a cyclic first-order scan. Sums of independently ordered terms can have high rank across every balanced cut, so the earlier low-cut-rank objections do not automatically apply.

This checkpoint proves that **two such terms still cannot be an exact prime-length Fourier matrix**, even with arbitrary nonzero left/right diagonal gauges, arbitrary cycle orders, and arbitrary external monomial equivalence.

A second theorem uses the three-tap tail annihilators suggested by the parallel separated-generator research. It excludes a broad, fully variable, nondegenerate two-stage rank-one semiseparable factorization of a prime Fourier matrix, with arbitrary internal and endpoint permutations.

## 2. Algebraic form of a gauged Green term

Let `P` be any permutation matrix, not necessarily affine and not necessarily one cycle. A gauged Green term is

\[
T=L(I-\alpha P)^{-1}R,
\]

where `L,R` are invertible diagonal matrices and `I-\alpha P` is nonsingular.

Its inverse is

\[
T^{-1}=R^{-1}(I-\alpha P)L^{-1}.
\]

Therefore every row of `T^{-1}` is supported on at most two positions:

1. its diagonal position;
2. one position on a weighted permutation graph.

Conversely, the theorem below only needs this support property. The inverse matrices may have arbitrary nonzero row-dependent weights; they need not use a common scalar `alpha`.

---

## 3. Main theorem: two gauged Green terms cannot equal a prime Fourier matrix

Let `N` be prime, `N>=11`, and let `F_N` be the `N x N` Fourier matrix over the complex numbers. The statement remains valid after arbitrary row/column permutations and arbitrary nonzero row/column diagonal scalings of `F_N`.

Let `A` and `B` be invertible matrices such that each is supported on the union of:

- the main diagonal;
- one weighted permutation graph.

Equivalently, `A^{-1}` and `B^{-1}` include every left/right diagonally gauged one-permutation Green resolvent.

### Theorem 3.1 — two-Green parallel-sum obstruction

\[
\boxed{
F_N\ne A^{-1}+B^{-1}.
}
\]

The same conclusion holds for every matrix monomially equivalent to `F_N`.

### 3.1 Convert the sum into a sparse identity

If the equality held, multiplying by `A` on the left and `B` on the right would give

\[
A F_N B=A+B. \tag{3.1}
\]

The right side has at most three possible nonzero positions in each row: one diagonal position and one position from each permutation graph.

### 3.2 Construct a `2 x 2` zero rectangle

Let `sigma_A(i)` be the off-diagonal permutation position in row `i` of `A`; similarly define `sigma_B(i)`. Choose two rows `I={i_0,i_1}` so that

\[
R=I\cup \sigma_A(I)
\]

has size at most three. This is always possible:

- if `sigma_A(i)!=i`, use `I={i,sigma_A(i)}`;
- if a fixed point is available, include it and any second row.

The rows `I` of `A+B` can be nonzero only in

\[
H=I\cup\sigma_A(I)\cup\sigma_B(I),
\]

and `|H|<=5`.

Because `N>=11`, the complement `S` has more than `N/2` elements. Let `tau_B` be the permutation describing the off-diagonal row position in each **column** of `B` (`tau_B=sigma_B^{-1}` under the standard convention). Since both `S` and `tau_B^{-1}(S)` contain more than half the coordinates, they intersect. Hence there are two columns `J` contained in `S` for which

\[
C=J\cup\tau_B(J)
\]

has size at most three.

Therefore

\[
(A+B)[I,J]=0. \tag{3.2}
\]

### 3.3 The Fourier block cannot vanish

Restrict the two rows of `A` to their support `R`, and the two columns of `B` to their support `C`:

\[
U=A[I,R],\qquad V=B[C,J].
\]

Because `A,B` are invertible,

\[
\operatorname{rank}U=\operatorname{rank}V=2.
\]

The identity (3.1) and zero block (3.2) would require

\[
U F_N[R,C] V=0. \tag{3.3}
\]

Chebotarev's Fourier-minor theorem says that every square minor of a prime-size complex Fourier matrix is nonzero. Thus

\[
\operatorname{rank}F_N[R,C]=\min(|R|,|C|).
\]

Here `|R|,|C|` are each two or three. Sylvester's rank inequality gives

\[
\operatorname{rank}(F_N[R,C]V)
\ge
\min(|R|,|C|)+2-|C|.
\]

But (3.3) would force this image into `ker U`, whose dimension is `|R|-2`. For all four possibilities

\[
(|R|,|C|)\in\{2,3\}^2,
\]

the lower bound on the image dimension is strictly larger than `|R|-2`. This is impossible.

### 3.4 Scope

This theorem closes the exact representation

\[
F_N=L_1(I-\alpha P_1)^{-1}R_1
   +L_2(I-\beta P_2)^{-1}R_2
\]

for every prime `N>=11`, regardless of the two permutation orders and all nonzero diagonal gauges.

It does **not** exclude:

- three or more independently ordered Green terms;
- products of Green sums;
- stages whose inverses have more than one off-diagonal permutation;
- approximate representations;
- composite lengths where Fourier minors can vanish.

## 4. Three-tap annihilators for a general rank-one semiseparable stage

Consider the fully variable rank-one semiseparable matrix

\[
S_{ij}=
\begin{cases}
 a_i b_j,&i>j,\\
 d_i,&i=j,\\
 c_i e_j,&i<j.
\end{cases}
\tag{4.1}
\]

Such a stage applies in `O(N)` arithmetic by one prefix and one suffix accumulation.

For three consecutive columns `j,j+1,j+2`, choose a nonzero vector `x_j` in the common nullspace of

\[
(b_j,b_{j+1},b_{j+2}),
\qquad
(e_j,e_{j+1},e_{j+2}).
\]

Then all rows outside `j,j+1,j+2` cancel, so

\[
\operatorname{supp}(Sx_j)\subseteq\{j,j+1,j+2\}. \tag{4.2}
\]

Dually, for three consecutive rows `i,i+1,i+2`, choose `y_i` orthogonal to both local row-generator triples from `a` and `c`. Then

\[
\operatorname{supp}(y_i^T S)\subseteq\{i,i+1,i+2\}. \tag{4.3}
\]

The explicit annihilator is a three-dimensional cross product. Its first and last coordinates are adjacent `2 x 2` minors of the corresponding generator pair.

## 5. Generic two-stage semiseparable obstruction

Call the input side of a stage **adjacent-nondegenerate** if

\[
b_j e_{j+1}-b_{j+1}e_j\ne0
\]

for every adjacent pair. Call the output side adjacent-nondegenerate when

\[
a_i c_{i+1}-a_{i+1}c_i\ne0
\]

for every adjacent pair.

These conditions guarantee that three consecutive annihilator windows are linearly independent: three windows occupy only five coordinates but form a rank-three matrix.

### Theorem 5.1 — nondegenerate two-stage obstruction

Let `N` be prime and `N>=31`. Let `S_1,S_2` be invertible rank-one semiseparable matrices of the form (4.1). Assume:

- the input generator pair `(b,e)` of `S_1` is adjacent-nondegenerate;
- the output generator pair `(a,c)` of `S_2` is adjacent-nondegenerate.

Then for every internal permutation `P`, arbitrary endpoint permutations, and arbitrary nonzero endpoint diagonal scalings,

\[
\boxed{
D_{out}P_{out}F_NP_{in}D_{in}
\ne
S_2 P S_1.
}
\]

### Proof

Take three consecutive input annihilator windows. Stack them as an `N x 3` matrix `X`. They have rank three, occupy a five-coordinate interval `B`, and `S_1X` is supported inside the same interval.

The internal permutation maps `B` to five arbitrary coordinates. For `N>=30`, every five-point set misses some interval `A` of five consecutive coordinates: five points cannot hit all length-five intervals of a path of length at least 30.

Take the three consecutive output annihilator windows inside `A` and stack them as `Y`. They have rank three, and `Y^T S_2` is supported inside `A`. Since `A` is disjoint from `P(B)`,

\[
Y^T S_2 P S_1 X=0. \tag{5.1}
\]

Endpoint monomial transformations preserve ranks and support cardinalities. On the Fourier side, (5.1) would become

\[
U F_N[R,C] V=0,
\]

where:

- `R,C` each contain five coordinates;
- `U` is `3 x 5` of rank three;
- `V` is `5 x 3` of rank three.

Chebotarev gives that `F_N[R,C]` is invertible. Hence `F_N[R,C]V` has rank three, but `ker U` has dimension only two. Contradiction.

### Scope

The theorem is exact but covers an open dense nondegenerate branch, not every exceptional generator assignment. Degenerate adjacent minors can create piecewise-proportional envelopes and require a separate branch classification. It also concerns two stages; deeper products remain open.

## 6. Reproducible exact checks

The implementation uses large prime fields containing primitive roots of the tested prime transform lengths. These modular checks are finite-instance certificates and regression tests; the all-prime complex theorem rests on Chebotarev's theorem and the dimension arguments above.

### Two-Green gate

For each

\[
N\in\{11,13,17,19,29,31\},
\]

40 random pairs of permutation orders and random invertible diagonal-plus-monomial inverse matrices were tested. Every witness had support sizes at most three, the `(A+B)[I,J]` block was exactly zero, and the corresponding Fourier block product had rank at least two in the saved runs.

Total exact tested pairs:

\[
6\times40=240.
\]

### Semiseparable gate

At `N=31`, 30 random pairs of invertible fully nondegenerate semiseparable stages were tested with random internal permutations and random endpoint monomial Fourier gauges.

Every case verified exactly:

- three-tap tail cancellation;
- rank-three annihilator banks;
- a disjoint five-coordinate interval;
- zero semiseparable product block;
- nonzero Fourier bilinear block.

The minimum saved Fourier bilinear rank was three.

### Automated suite

\[
\boxed{6/6\text{ tests pass}.}
\]

The suite also performs thousands of combinatorial witness checks and sampled finite-field Fourier-minor checks.

+
\operatorname{diag}(l_2)(I-\beta Q)^{-1}\operatorname{diag}(r_2).

## 8. Prior-art boundary

Chebotarev's theorem—that every minor of a prime-size complex Fourier matrix is nonzero—is classical. A concise proof is P. E. Frenkel, *Simple proof of Chebotarev's theorem on roots of unity*, 2003, arXiv:math/0312398. Recent work also studies finite-field variants, but this checkpoint only uses finite fields for reproducible finite-instance tests.

Green/resolvent scans, semiseparable matrices, rank inequalities, and sparse-support arguments are established tools. The specific two-Green zero-rectangle theorem and the five-coordinate three-tap composition are project deductions and have not undergone a literature-wide novelty review or independent peer review.

## 9. Honest status and next gate

The exact project has **not** beaten `N log N`.

What v17 closes is one of the most natural survivors of v16:

> Two independently ordered, arbitrarily diagonally gauged, full-rank Green resolvents cannot sum to a prime Fourier matrix once `N>=11`.

It also closes the fully adjacent-nondegenerate branch of a two-stage, fully variable rank-one semiseparable factorization for prime `N>=31`.

The surviving constructive target must now use at least one of:

1. three or more genuinely independent Green orders with a joint evaluator—not separate `O(N)` terms whose count grows;
2. a stage with more than one off-diagonal permutation in its inverse, while retaining linear-time application;
3. a deeper noncommuting product whose exact phase compatibility survives the three-tap gates;
4. a new operator class not reducible to local transcripts, one-cycle moments, shift polynomials, or two-term parallel Green sums.

A credible next candidate must come with an explicit all-`N` identity and a complete arithmetic ledger. Large cross-cut rank or dense Fourier conjugation alone is not enough.
