# Where an exact linear-time FFT could still arise

The proven simultaneous separation-rank theorem excludes a single additive sum of o(logN) arbitrarily permuted rank-one cyclic Green/global scan branches on prime lengths, even with independent diagonal gauges.

**Survivor**: A product of **two or more** sums of several independently ordered scan operators, for example

    M_N = (L1 G1 R1 + L2 G2 R2) (L3 G3 R3 + L4 G4 R4).

Even though each parenthesized factor is individually far from Fourier in operator norm for large N, their product could conceivably be exactly Fourier. Nothing proven here establishes existence or excludes that possibility. Products of single Green inverses alone face the archived inverse-support Ω(logN) stage barrier, so genuine additive mixtures inside stages matter.

**Proposed constructive gate:**

1. Choose explicit families of non-affine cycles and diagonal coefficients depending on N, with O(N) total setup cost and O(N) arithmetic per scan.
2. Derive symbolic identities for `M_N S = D M_N` and `M_N e0 = 1/sqrt N * all_ones`, where `S e_j=e_(j+1)` and `D=diag(omega_N^j)`. They are sufficient and necessary for `M_N = U_N` (for the chosen Fourier sign). No empirical Weyl closeness or finite N optimization counts as proof.
3. For small N=8,16,32, solve the cyclotomic/finite-field polynomial compatibility equations with denominator nonzero branches handled explicitly; avoid overconcluding from failures of numerical optimization.
4. Seek an all-N recurrence for the coefficients and prove uniform O(N) sequential scalar work including all root/weight generation, routing, precision/bit model, memory and output materialization. If not, classify the obstruction precisely.
5. Test against existing Phase26 affine cuts and sparse-defect inverse-support gates, the F8 nonlinear Jacobian witness, and the new additive sum restriction.

**Stop conditions:** If the ansatz becomes a single sum of fewer than its required Green branches, reject it immediately using the theorem. If a stage secretly invokes a full FFT, reject its O(N) claim. If intermediate permutations are uncharged or parameters require Ω(NlogN) preparation, do not claim improvement.

Do not claim this survivor is likely to exist solely because the present lower bound does not apply. A genuine exact O(N) algorithm remains unproved.
