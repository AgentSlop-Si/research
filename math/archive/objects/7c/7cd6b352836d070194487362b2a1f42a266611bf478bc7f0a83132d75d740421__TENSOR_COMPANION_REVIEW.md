## Narrow structural implications independently established

The following results can be justified independently of the missing v24 text, for odd prime p, nondegenerate finite Weil operators on F_p^n, and a coefficient field with the required roots and characteristic unequal to p. They are standard structural algebra used here as independently checked project constraints, not as a declaration that every missing source claim was read or accepted.

Let S=[[A,B],[C,D]] be symplectic and r=rank B. Weyl covariance identifies each column of an intertwining operator U as a joint eigenvector under the transformed modulation subgroup. For b in ker B, the transformed operator is pure modulation by Db. Since S is invertible, D restricted to ker B is injective. These n−r independent modulation constraints restrict support to one affine subspace of dimension r. The symplectic identity B^T D=D^T B shows im B=(D ker B)^perp. The remaining transformed modulations translate by every vector in im B; covariance propagates any nonzero entry across this entire affine subspace with nonzero scalar factors. Because U is invertible, a column is nonzero, so it has exactly p^r entries in its support. The analogous covariance argument on row vectors uses S^−1, whose upper-right block is −B^T, and gives the same row count.

Equivalently, the two parabolic factors in the rank-r symplectic normal form act monomially, and the middle partial Fourier transform has p^r nonzeros per row/column. The support argument avoids inferring an algorithmic bound from that normal form.

For a product ST, B_(ST)=A_S B_T+B_S D_T, hence rank B_(ST)≤rank B_S+rank B_T. Therefore a word reaching full tensor Fourier rank needs the sum of its partial ranks to be at least n. This is a structural rank budget, not an unrestricted arithmetic lower bound.

When B is invertible, the Weil kernel is, up to a nonzero scalar, a quadratic phase whose mixed x,y term is a nondegenerate bilinear form over F_p. Diagonal phases remove the pure x and pure y terms. Every nonzero four-entry cross ratio is consequently a p-th root of unity. When B is singular, the support theorem gives zeros. Cyclic F_(p^n) has no zeros and its submatrix at rows and columns 0,1 has cross ratio a primitive p^n-th root. Row/column permutations only rearrange the collection of cross ratios, while nonzero diagonal scalings cancel in every ratio. For n>1, cyclic F_(p^n) therefore cannot be monomially equivalent to any such Weil operator, whether its B block is singular or invertible. Restrict this conclusion to the odd-prime model reviewed here; the absent full source may contain other qualifications that have not been seen.

A nondegenerate bilinear transform omega^(x^T M y) is simply tensor Fourier with columns relabeled by y→M y. The trace pairing of a finite extension field supplies such an invertible Gram matrix in any basis. Thus an additive extension-field Fourier transform is a tensor/additive-group transform, not the cyclic p^n transform.

## Fresh independent checks

check_tensor_independent.py does not import any uploaded program. It:

- Enumerates all symplectic bases of F3^4 and obtains exactly 51,840 matrices with rank-B counts 1,296, 15,552 and 34,992 for ranks 0,1,2.
- Builds its own chirp, invertible-linear-permutation and coordinate-Fourier generators, independently checks their symplectic forms, and tests 100 fresh words at each (p,n)=(3,2),(3,3),(5,2). All row and column supports equal p^rank(B).
- Checks rank subadditivity at every generated step (1,623 checks total), 2,360 sampled dense cross ratios, exact coordinate-tensor factorization, 60 independently chosen bilinear relabelings, and cyclic cross-ratio orders 9,27,25.
- Recomputes all 30 supplied ledger rows: core dense terms r p N, core writes r N, and row support p^r.

These controls differ deliberately from the source's seed counts and are not a replay of the ten logged source tests. They supplement the algebra above; finite examples alone do not establish the all-parameter propositions.

## Cost and constructive implications

The CSV counts a supplied coordinate partial-Fourier core. At r=0 its zero terms and writes describe the identity core, not the full monomial Weil operator: permutations, phases and output still require work. At fixed p, r=n gives n p N=Theta(N log N) core terms for the displayed coordinate implementation. Rank subadditivity alone does not prove that every implementation must incur this cost. General symplectic decomposition, phase generation, linear relabeling, word length and projective scalar recovery must all be charged separately; no fully charged O(N) arbitrary-Weil evaluator is established by these companions.

For constructive FFT work, a relabeled extension-field/tensor Fourier identity cannot substitute for a cyclic transform. Any cyclic construction through this route must introduce operations that escape the single-Weil/monomial-equivalence class, such as carry-sensitive higher-order twiddles or another explicitly justified non-Weil interaction. This is a necessary change of model, not a claim that such a change is sufficient or cheap. Cross ratios offer a very small exact early rejection test before expensive candidate fitting.

The finite Weil representation, parabolic rank classification, tensor Fourier factorization and monomial cross-ratio invariants are classical. The available prior-art note says as much. No new literature search or publication-wide novelty determination was made here. The missing proof and implementation dependencies remain open even though these narrower implications have been independently supported.
