## 1. The joint middle-monomial test

Write `M=A C B`, with fixed invertible `A,B` and unknown monomial `C=diag(q)P`. The two symmetrized Fourier identities reduce exactly to

`(A^-1 V A)C = C(B T B^-1)` and `(A^-1 T A)C = C(B V B^-1)`.

The same permutation and diagonal ratios must work for both pairs. For any simultaneous diagonal similarity, a product along a closed index walk cancels its diagonal ratios. Thus the six diagonal quantities `X, Y, X^2, XY, YX, Y^2` are a necessary multiset signature. When the signatures are unique they determine the only possible permutation. Given a permutation, nonzero-edge ratios must have consistent products around cycles; propagation on each weak component, with reverse ratios where needed, gives the remaining diagonal gauge up to one scalar per component. Zero-pattern mismatches must also be checked. Duplicate signatures require actual search or an inconclusive result, not rejection by fiat.

This is a fixed-outside-factor gate. It is not a theorem excluding all parameters in a Green family. Furthermore, satisfying both symmetrized identities identifies `F(aI+bJ)`, as already established in the master; a full appropriate column calibration is needed to isolate `F`. Phase20's unsymmetrized pair removes this ambiguity.

Fresh exact checks over GF(97), at N=4,8,16,32, construct random invertible A and an arbitrary middle monomial, define B so that ACB=F, and verify the identity and signature matches. At every tested size the adversary A=F, B=I+2T passes the first identity, fails the second, and has unequal joint signatures. These modular examples are exact controls at their chosen field specialization, not all-parameter complex exclusions.

## 2. The induced-one-norm budget

For stages

`S_j = sum_r L_jr P_jr B_jr^-1 P_jr^-1 R_jr`,

with diagonal magnitudes at most one, let `Gamma_j=sum_r ||B_jr^-1||_1`. Permutations are isometries in the induced one-norm, so `||S_j||_1<=Gamma_j` and `||M||_1<=product_j Gamma_j`. Extra unit-modulus monomials preserve these inequalities. Since `||F||_1=sqrt(N)`, exact Fourier equality requires `product_j Gamma_j>=sqrt(N)`.

For an approximation, triangle inequality followed by `||E||_1<=sqrt(N)||E||_2` gives

`||M-F||_2 >= max(0,1-product_j Gamma_j/sqrt(N))`.

This proof is independent of commutativity and permits each solve's own path permutation. It requires the actual gain budget. Large outside diagonal amplitudes, shrinking denominator scale, or an architecture outside the stated product of sums are not covered.

Strict column diagonal dominance with margin delta implies `||B^-1||_1<=1/delta`. One may prove this directly by applying the maximum-component argument to B^T and then using `||B^-1||_1=||(B^T)^-1||_infinity`; complex entries cause no problem.

## 3. Uniformly conditioned banded inverse bound

For an invertible complex B with half-bandwidth w and singular values in `[a,b]`, set

`c=2/(a^2+b^2)`, `E=I-c B*B`, `rho=(b^2-a^2)/(b^2+a^2)<1`.

Then `B^-1=c sum_(k>=0) E^k B*`. The kth summand has half-bandwidth `(2k+1)w` and norm at most `b rho^k`. At distance d, no term before `K(d)=max(0,ceil((d-w)/(2w)))` contributes. Consequently

`|(B^-1)_ij| <= (b/a^2) rho^K(|i-j|)`.

There are at most `2w+1` entries with K=0 and at most 4w per later shell in each column. Summing gives precisely

`||B^-1||_1 <= (b/a^2)[1+2w(b/a)^2]`.

The proof covers nonnormal and non-Hermitian matrices; w=0 is separately immediate. If a=b, E=0 and the same estimate remains valid, treating the zero-power term in its ordinary series sense.

Thus fixed w,a,b and bounded summand counts force logarithmically many stages under the gain normalization. With variable positive integer counts q_j, the total call count m=sum q_j is also Omega(log N): if `C` is the bound above and `Cplus=max(C,1)`, then `product(C q_j)<=exp((1+log Cplus)m)`. This is a call-count bound only.

When denominators are normalized to largest singular value one and condition number at most K, `||B^-1||_1<=K^2(1+2wK^2)<=(1+2w)K^4`. A t-stage, q-term exact factorization therefore requires

`K >= N^(1/(8t)) / [q(1+2w)]^(1/4)`.

This is necessary, not sufficient. The normalization is substantive.

Fresh numerical checks at N=4,8,16,32,64,128 verify the advertised nonnormal test matrix `I+0.75i T_path+0.2 U_path`: singular values lie within `[0.8,sqrt(3.25)+0.2]`, and its inverse one-norm is below the derived constant. These numerical tests support the algebraic proof but do not replace it.

For the near-resonant test `B_N=I-(1-1/N)T_path/2`, the minimum eigenvalue is exactly `1-(1-1/N)cos(pi/(N+1))=Theta(1/N)`. Since B_N is a symmetric nonsingular M-matrix with row diagonal-dominance margin at least 1/N, `1/lambda_min<=||B_N^-1||_1<=N`, so its inverse one-norm is Theta(N). Clearing the norm budget supplies no Fourier phase identity.

## 4. One Harper middle monomial is excluded

For 4 dividing N and `H=T+V`, a monomial commuting with H must induce a permutation preserving both its cycle support and diagonal cosine values. The unique diagonal maximum at coordinate zero forces that vertex to be fixed. A cycle automorphism fixing zero is identity or reflection. The nonzero unit edge weights then force every diagonal multiplier to be equal. Hence the monomial centralizer is exactly nonzero scalar multiples of I and J.

If `F=f(H) C g(H)` with invertible, well-defined scalar rational factors, all outside factors commute with H. Therefore C does too, and the claimed representation becomes `F=c h(H) J^e`. The two zero modes of H from the previously verified Phase18 theorem have opposite reflection parity and Fourier eigenvalue ratio equal to plus or minus i. But `h(H)J^e` gives a ratio plus or minus one. Contradiction. Invertibility prevents h(0)=0 from being used to evade this argument.

The exact finite-field support check at N=4 and 8 enumerates all permutations, finds 2N cycle automorphisms, and exactly identity/reflection preserve the H diagonal. The all-N complex proof above, rather than these finite enumerations, supports the theorem.

Multiple monomial layers and operators not commuting with H remain outside this exclusion.
