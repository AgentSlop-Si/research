# Exact audit of structured block phases

This package addresses the new structured CountSketch frontier directly. It
proves a counterexample to every integer-polynomial block slope over the natural
dyadic modulus, checks a possible higher-order chirp repair, and establishes an
exact second-moment result for a prime-modulus variant. No universal fast decoder
or priority claim is made. The supplied checkpoint was the only source artifact;
its referenced sketch implementation was not available and is not claimed to
have been tested here.

## Model

Let N=K M, omega=exp(2 pi i/N), eta=omega^K, and use the positive-sign unitary
DFT. Split x into x_(r,t)=x_(rM+t). Form one scalar per block,

    b_r=sum_(t=0..M-1) sigma_(r,t) x_(r,t),
    sigma_(r,t)=eta^(a_r t^2+c_r t).

The natural CountSketch/adjoint decoder is

    Xhat_m=(1/sqrt(N)) sum_r omega^(mMr) b_r
                        sum_t conjugate(sigma_(r,t)) omega^(mt).

The opposite DFT sign changes only phase conventions. The strong accuracy
contract is all-bin additive error at most epsilon*||x||_1/sqrt(N).

## All integer-polynomial block slopes share a dyadic obstruction

Assume M is divisible by 4, K is even, and

    c_r=b+alpha_1 r+...+alpha_d r^d mod M.

No bound on the polynomial degree is needed. Let x_(r,t)=1 on even blocks and
zero on odd blocks. For every even r, c_r has the same parity as b. Pair the
positions t and t+M/2:

    sigma_(r,t+M/2)/sigma_(r,t)=(-1)^(c_r).

The quadratic contribution is one because M divides both M*t and M^2/4.
This holds even if each block has a different quadratic coefficient a_r.
Whenever b is odd, every active block sums to zero, so every sketch entry is
exactly zero. For uniform b this event has probability 1/2, independently of
the other polynomial coefficients or quadratic phases.

Any linear decoder outputs zero on this event, yet the true DC coefficient is
sqrt(N)/2. Its error equals the entire strong scale ||x||_1/sqrt(N)=sqrt(N)/2.
Thus every epsilon<1 fails with probability at least 1/2. On the weaker
epsilon*||x||_2 scale, the normalized error is 1/sqrt(2). This is an exact
high-probability counterexample, not a numerical variance observation.

## Fully dense inputs and nonlinear sketch-only decoders

The zero entries are not essential. Let v=i*t*ones for any positive rational t
and consider the two fixed inputs v+x and v-x. They are fully dense and have
identical coordinate magnitudes, L1 norm, L2 norm, and support. On the same
odd-b event they produce identical sketches, including for a nonlinear decoder
that knows the seed and this shared metadata.

Their DC values differ by sqrt(N). Their common L1 norm is

    (N/2)(sqrt(1+t^2)+t).

The two admissible DC error disks are disjoint if

    epsilon*(sqrt(1+t^2)+t)<1.

For any epsilon in (0,1), it suffices to choose a rational
0<t<(1-epsilon^2)/(2 epsilon). A sketch-only decoder cannot succeed on both
signals during the collision event. Hence one of these two fixed dense inputs
has failure probability at least 1/4. The claim does not grant a decoder arbitrary
additional phase-sensitive information from the original input.

## Increasing the chirp order does not fix polynomial slope coherence

One natural repair replaces eta^(a t^2) by a 2Mth-root chirp:

    sigma_(r,t)=exp(pi i a_r t^2/M + 2 pi i c_r t/M),

with each a_r odd and M a power of two at least 4. This chirp is periodic modulo
M and has zero cyclic autocorrelation at every nonzero shift: the shift-d
correlation is a unit scalar times sum_t eta^(a_r*d*t), which vanishes because
a_r is invertible modulo M. In particular |sum_t sigma_(r,t)|^2=M, so the
previous flat-block zero-sketch argument no longer applies.

A different fixed input defeats the natural decoder: use x_(r,t)=(-1)^t on
even blocks and zero on odd blocks. Its true DC is zero. Exact shift algebra
gives, for every active block,

    (sum_t (-1)^t sigma_(r,t)) * conjugate(sum_t sigma_(r,t))
       = M*(-1)^(c_r+M/4).

All even-block c_r again have the same parity. The estimated DC therefore has
magnitude sqrt(N)/2 for every seed. Adding i*t*ones makes the input fully dense
without changing this error: the added constant component is decoded exactly
at DC by the identity |sum sigma|^2=M. Consequently a single fully dense input
fails deterministically whenever the same small-t inequality above holds.
This last claim concerns the stated natural CountSketch decoder, not every
possible nonlinear decoder.

## Constructive prime-modulus second-moment result

Change the regime: let M be an odd prime, K<=M, use a common a, and take
a,alpha,b independently uniform modulo M, with c_r=alpha*r+b. The natural
decoder is unbiased for every x and every frequency. Its exact variance is

    E |Xhat_m-X_m|^2 = ((M-1)/N) ||x||_2^2.                 (1)

To prove this, expand the error into terms indexed by (r,u,t), u!=t. Each term
has input coefficient x_(r,u), a unit output phase, and the seed character

    eta^[a(u^2-t^2)+alpha*r(u-t)+b(u-t)].

Every character has zero mean because u-t is nonzero. Two terms can correlate
only if their three character labels agree. The b label fixes d=u-t; the alpha
label then fixes r because M is prime and block indices are distinct modulo M;
the a label fixes u+t because 2d is invertible. Together these recover u and t.
Thus distinct error terms are orthogonal in expectation. Each input coordinate
occurs in exactly M-1 terms, proving (1), including all Fourier bins.

This repairs the second moment, not the full target. It supplies no simultaneous
all-bin high-probability bound, and M being an odd prime is not a drop-in
power-of-two-length construction. K>M aliases block labels; an odd composite M
can also alias the within-block character labels. Both are checked negative
controls. A variance bound on the weaker L2 scale does not meet the stronger
L1/sqrt(N) guarantee.

## Joint decoding and the finite chirp boundary remain charged

For affine c_r define

    G_l=sum_t eta^(-a t^2-b t) omega^(l t).

The exact decoder becomes

    Xhat_m=(1/sqrt(N)) sum_r b_r omega^(mMr) G_(m-K alpha r).

Writing m=s+K ell, each fixed residue s uses a length-M cyclic convolution of
G_(s+K ell) with a K-sparse vector placing b_r*exp(2 pi i*s*r/K) at alpha*r mod M.
The shared-phase single K-FFT factorization has disappeared. Direct combination
costs O(NK), even if all G values are already generated in O(N); independent
generic FFT convolutions cost O(N log M). These are candidate implementation
costs, not a lower bound excluding a new jointly factored decoder. No such
faster decoder is implemented or claimed here.

The finite polynomial P(z)=sum_(t=0..M-1) eta^(a t^2+b t) z^t satisfies

    P(z)=1+eta^(a+b) z P(eta^(2a)z)-z^M.

The final term is essential: on z=omega^m, z^M=exp(2 pi i*m/K), not generally one.
Oversampled Fourier bins cannot silently use a complete-grid Gauss formula that
discards this boundary. The audit checks every polynomial coefficient in the
displayed recurrence, including the degree-M cancellation.

## Exact certificates and general architectural boundary

Run `python audit.py`. It uses integer arithmetic, Fraction, modular character
labels, and exact coefficients in Z[z]/(z^(M/2)+1). There is no floating-point
rank test, empirical failure-rate extrapolation, or black-box FFT backend.

The captured checks pass:

- 7,008 exhaustive bad affine seeds for tested dyadic sizes;
- 2,520 higher-degree polynomial cases through degree eight;
- 1,360 exact alternating-input and 1,360 constant-input products for the
  higher-order chirp;
- 26 prime-modulus character-orthogonality cases;
- 482 finite chirp polynomial recurrence cases;
- exact rational dense-pair separation examples at five error levels.

The proofs above establish the all-size claims; the finite certificates audit
their formulas. Independently, the project's new projection argument shows that
any fixed input-independent linear sketch and linear decoder satisfying the
strong contract for every fixed input with probability at least 1-delta needs
rank at least N(1-delta)(1-epsilon^2). Therefore the prime second-moment result
cannot by itself overcome that strong-contract limit with polylogarithmic K.
Input-adaptive sketches, genuinely nonlinear decoders, or an explicitly weaker
error contract fall outside that rank argument. The dyadic collision theorem
still rules out nonlinear sketch-only decoding for this particular polynomial
phase family.
