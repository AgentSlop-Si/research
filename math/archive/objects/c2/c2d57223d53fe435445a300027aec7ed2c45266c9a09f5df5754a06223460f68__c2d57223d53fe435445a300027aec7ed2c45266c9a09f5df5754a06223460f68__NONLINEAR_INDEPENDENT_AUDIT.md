# Independent audit: nonlinear compatibility is a genuine extra gate

9 October 2026. The two new archives were materialized from the supplied Library references, extracted with path/size/symlink checks, and inspected as data. No uploaded Python source was executed or imported. The executable checks below were newly authored for this audit.

## Result

The nonlinear checkpoint's central theorem checks out exactly. Its fixed 8-point, two-stage rank-one scalar scan graph has maximum possible input/output flow for every pair of terminal subsets. Nevertheless, the actual separated-generator matrix family has complex algebraic dimension exactly 60 inside the 64-dimensional matrix space.

Thus passing every graph rank-cut cannot certify that one compatible coefficient assignment realizes a target. This is a limitation of the graph test. It neither excludes nor constructs the Fourier matrix for this architecture.

The word “nonlinear” refers to polynomial dependence on the fixed generator coefficients. Every resulting transform is linear in its fresh input. This is distinct from the nonlinear coordinatewise medians in the v6 approximation branch.

## 2. The characteristic-zero dimension conclusion is justified

For a rank-one stage, the strictly lower matrix has n−1 observable row factors and n−1 observable column factors. A common nonzero scaling is the only generic ambiguity, giving dimension 2n−3. The upper triangle independently contributes 2n−3 and the diagonal contributes n. A stage therefore has dimension 5n−6.

For the product B P A, an invertible diagonal D acts by

    A -> D A,
    B -> B P D^(-1) P^(-1).

This preserves both stage charts and their product. On the open subset where A and B are invertible, the action is free and has dimension n. Consequently the image dimension is at most 2(5n−6)−n = 9n−12, which is 60 at n=8.

The verified nonzero modular Jacobian minor is the reduction of an integer determinant. It is therefore nonzero over Q and C as well, proving complex image dimension at least 60. The upper and lower bounds match. Because the parameter space is irreducible and the invertible locus is a nonempty open subset, including singular or limiting parameter choices does not increase the dimension of the closure of this chart's image.

This does not establish the dimension of every possible alternative quasiseparable representation. It also does not turn the generic-matrix exclusion into a Fourier exclusion: a particular structured matrix may belong to a proper algebraic family.

## 3. A new useful extension: phase masks do not remove the chart constraints

Consider t separated-generator stages, each of rank r, with arbitrary fixed middle permutations, arbitrary nonzero diagonal masks at any interfaces, and arbitrary endpoint monomials. Assume n≥2r+1.

The closure of this family has dimension at most

    min(n^2, n + 2 t r (2n−2r−1)).                  (1)

For r=1 this is min(n^2, (4t+1)n−6t). Allowing all permutations rather than fixing them makes only a finite union and does not change the bound.

### Proof of the single-stage count

On a generic open chart, normalize the first r lower-column generator vectors to the coordinate basis. Row i then contributes min(i,r) observable parameters. Their total is

    r(n−1) − r(r−1)/2.

For each remaining column j≥r, its tail has min(r,n−1−j) observable degrees of freedom. Summing contributes

    r(n−1−r) − r(r−1)/2.

The strictly lower chart thus has dimension r(2n−2r−1). The upper chart is independent, and the diagonal adds n, so one stage has dimension

    d = n + 2r(2n−2r−1).

There are t−1 free diagonal gauge actions, one at each interface, each of dimension n on the invertible locus. Therefore the t-stage image dimension is at most td−(t−1)n, yielding (1).

### Why arbitrary diagonal phases add no missing degrees of freedom here

The separated-generator chart is closed under multiplication on either side by an arbitrary invertible diagonal: scale its row factors and diagonal for left multiplication, or its column factors and diagonal for right multiplication. A diagonal adjacent to a permutation can be commuted across it by permuting its entries. Every interstage diagonal therefore absorbs into a neighboring stage without changing r. Endpoint diagonal masks absorb in the same way.

This remains true even for freely chosen complex phases of arbitrarily high conductor. Such phases can solve an arithmetic field restriction, but they cannot by themselves remove the polynomial compatibility restriction of a fixed rank-r chart family.

A necessary condition for this family to represent a generic n-by-n matrix is consequently

    t >= ceil(n(n−1) / [2r(2n−2r−1)]).

For fixed r this grows like n/(4r). This is explicitly a generic-matrix parameter-count statement, not a Fourier depth or arithmetic lower bound.

## 4. How this fuses with the prior map

### Network and whole-word branches

The earlier N=64 network certificate finds a target submatrix whose Fourier rank exceeds one graph capacity. The new N=8 example demonstrates what happens after every such obstruction disappears: compatible shared coefficients still impose additional equations.

The earlier common-order whole-word theorem gives Fourier-specific quasiseparable rank/degree restrictions. Changing orders can evade that common-order premise and may make all graph cuts vacuous. It does not guarantee coefficient compatibility. The new dimension theorem cleanly separates these two checkpoints without replacing either.

### Phase14 and Haar–Green branches

Phase14 adds a different Fourier-specific invariant: an appropriate Galois conjugate of Fourier is a fixed row permutation, and one-mask base-field factorizations must respect its eigenspaces and multiplicities. Its conductor threshold is independent of graph routing capacity. The absorbed-diagonal observation above explains why satisfying that phase requirement still does not solve separated-generator compatibility.

The Haar–Green entropy gate concerns a different restricted executor: separately applied unitary bounded-local factors. The nonlinear chart admits general nonunitary coefficients, so it is not excluded by that entropy theorem merely because it is built from scans. Conversely, calling a chart nonunitary does not establish a finite exact-width saving or a valid all-length compiler certificate.

### Frequency v5/v6 branch

The rank-deficient retained-measurement obstruction in the frequency synthesis is an information-loss theorem for the input-to-measurement map. The nonlinear-chart dimension bound concerns the family of available full matrices as coefficients vary. A full-rank matrix can belong to a low-dimensional family; these are different notions of rank/dimension. Neither theorem upgrades v6 approximation to an exact arbitrary-input transform.

### Constructive consequence

An exact candidate should proceed from inexpensive necessary checks to exact realization: dependency/cut tests, applicable field/covariance tests, compatible coefficient solving, exact full-matrix replay including scratch roles, and fully charged execution. Passing any early filter is not evidence that the next step has a solution. The existing 16-call Haar-detail target remains a sufficient constructive route; this new checkpoint does not supply its word.

## 5. The all-r lifted-network conjecture remains open

A capacity-r dilation of a superconcentrator correctly gives an aggregate terminal-demand routing relaxation. It does not automatically embed into an ordered scan with r consecutive scalar coordinates per macro coordinate.

The tempting local embedding has a counterexample. Prescribe r units of forward through-flow, no backward boundary flow, and one extra scalar input at the first coordinate that must leave at the last coordinate of a block of length r≥2. The collapsed macro gadget can use its diagonal edge for the extra unit. The ordered expanded block must send r+1 units across an internal forward register of capacity r, which is impossible.

More generally, every expanded block prefix must satisfy

    −r <= a−d+I_q−O_q <= r,

where a is forward inflow at its left boundary, d is backward outflow there, and I_q/O_q are scalar supply/demand in the prefix. The aggregate macro model omits these inequalities.

This refutes only that local proof shortcut. The full two-stage lifted superconcentrator might still hold because its boundary flows and intermediate routing can be chosen globally. The checkpoint correctly describes its r>1 solver findings as computational evidence. See `BLOCK_LIFT_SIMULATION_GAP.md` for the explicit conservation proof.
