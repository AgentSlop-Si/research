# Independent macro review and a stronger scan–broadcast bound

Date: 2026-10-09. This component reviews the supplied bidirectional-macro and
interleaved-scan materials. It supplies one sharper architecture-specific
necessary condition and a representation caveat. It does not supply a faster
general exact FFT, a general arithmetic lower bound, or a priority claim.

## 1. Main additional theorem

Let F be the N-point Fourier matrix, or its inverse, over a field containing a
primitive Nth root, with characteristic not dividing N. Suppose F is a product
of:

- t invertible scalar one-way weighted scans;
- arbitrary invertible diagonal/permutation matrices between any stages;
- invertible updates I+UVᵀ whose total rank budget is B.

For t ≥ 1, the following necessary condition improves the supplied bound:

\[
\boxed{(2B+1)2^{t-1}+B\;\ge N.}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
B\;\ge\;\max\left(0,\left\lceil
\frac{N-2^{t-1}}{2^t+1}\right\rceil\right).
\]

For t=0, B ≥ floor(N/2), as in the supplied material. In particular, for B=0
and every N≥2, not only powers of two,

\[
t\;\ge\;1+\lceil\log_2N\rceil.
\]

This last pure-scan result was already present in the previous project
checkpoint. The additional result here is its boundary-sensitive extension
to interleaved broadcasts.

### Proof: a nonuniform Fourier rigidity inequality

Let S be any N×N matrix. Put u=minᵢ nnz(Sᵢ,:) and s=maxᵢ nnz(Sᵢ,:), and write
r=rank(F−S). Then

\[
\boxed{N\;\le\;u+r(s+1).}
\]

If r≥N, the inequality is immediate. Otherwise choose r+1 cyclic-consecutive
Fourier rows containing a row of S with support u. Their S-support union has
size at most u+rs. If this union had size at most N−r−1, there would be r+1
columns on which S is zero in all chosen rows. On those columns F−S would be
an (r+1)×(r+1) Fourier submatrix with cyclic-consecutive rows. It is invertible:
after factoring a nonzero power from each column it is a Vandermonde matrix
on r+1 distinct roots. Wrapping row indices modulo N does not change these
entries. This contradicts rank(F−S)=r. Therefore u+rs≥N−r.

To apply this inequality, invert the proposed circuit, then replace every
inverse broadcast by identity. The remaining S is a product of t bidiagonal
matrices and arbitrary monomials. The difference from F⁻¹ has rank at most B
by a telescoping expansion, since the inverse of I+UVᵀ differs from identity
by rank at most rank(UVᵀ).

Every row of S has support at most 2^t. More specifically, the leftmost
bidiagonal factor has a boundary row containing only one nonzero. Monomials
to its left only move and scale that row. Multiplying this row through the
remaining t−1 bidiagonal factors increases support by at most 2 each time.
Hence u≤2^(t−1), s≤2^t. Substitution gives
N≤2^(t−1)+B(2^t+1), as claimed.

For t=0, S is monomial, so u=s=1. Thus N≤1+2B and the integer lower bound is
B≥floor(N/2).

## 2. The F8 endpoint-order claim is valid at the cut-rank level

The supplied induction for at most three dyadic characters is sound. If
exponents use both parities, one parity has a single nonzero monomial;
therefore a pair z,−z cannot both be zeros. If all exponents have one parity,
divide the exponents by two after extracting that parity. Distinct exponent
classes stay distinct modulo N/2. The induction reduces the zero count by
two. Thus any nonzero combination of at most three characters vanishes at
no more than N/2 dyadic roots, over characteristic different from two.

For N=8 this makes every 3×5 Fourier submatrix rank 3. Every endpoint ordering
therefore has rank-3 upper and lower off-diagonal blocks at cut 3. The parity
ordering (0,2,4,6,1,3,5,7) has cut ranks

\[
(1,2,3,2,3,2,1)
\]

in each direction; the center rank-2 statement follows from repeated
characters on even/odd subsets. Hence the optimum **quasiseparable order** is
exactly 3, and any literal scan-channel realization needs **at least** three
channels in each direction.

### A necessary distinction: cut rank does not automatically construct channels

The dynamic program minimizes a necessary cut-rank condition. It does not,
by itself, construct three independent scalar scans. General quasiseparable
state-space generators may have matrix transitions and independent output
weights; these are a broader representation than the literal scalar scan
yᵢ=aᵢyᵢ₋₁+bᵢxᵢ used in the supplied primitive definition.

For a small explicit example, the invertible matrix

\[
H=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\1&1&0\\0&1&1\end{pmatrix}
\]

has every strictly lower off-diagonal cut of rank 1. Nevertheless its strict
lower part cannot equal that of a single literal forward scan. Such a scan
would require a₁b₀=1 and a₂b₁=1, which force a₂a₁b₀≠0, contradicting H₂₀=0.
A diagonal addition and a backward scan cannot alter that lower entry.

This is a representation caveat, not a refutation of the F8 lower bound or
of its sharp quasiseparable-order statement. It shows why one should not
silently replace “minimum cut rank 3” with “an explicit three-channel scalar
scan implementation exists.” The supplied F4 implementation is explicit and
does not have this gap.

## 3. Scope of the radix-macro and unitary claims

The supplied complex Fourier submatrix estimate
rank(F[R,C])≥ceil(|R||C|/N) follows from its Frobenius norm and the full Fourier
operator norm. At a balanced cut it gives Ω(N) channel width. Therefore a
complete length-N Fourier block implemented as independent complete-length
scalar scan channels incurs Ω(N²) position visits. Repeating this per radix
block yields Ω(N log N) work under the stated independent-channel accounting.
This argument is complex/unitary; the Frobenius-norm proof is not a general
finite-field rank theorem.

It does not cover shared computation between channels, overlapping global
stages, or arbitrary intermediate permutations across multiple nonunitary
bidirectional-sum stages. Nor does the inverse support theorem apply to sums
of scan outputs or square maps represented with extra state that cannot be
factored into the permitted square primitives.

The supplied unitary-subclass discussion invokes an external Givens
representation theorem. This review checks neither that external theorem's
full hypotheses nor its uniform constants; it does not strengthen that
literature-dependent claim. The new theorem above is self-contained and
does not use it.

## 4. Independent exact verification

Run `python3 check_macro_bounds.py`. No supplied implementation is imported.
The finite-field verifier checks:

- 240 random inverse words with arbitrarily interleaved scans, monomials,
  and broadcasts: row bounds, boundary-row bound, rank telescoping, and the
  data-dependent Fourier rigidity inequality;
- all 6,859 matrices with at most one nonzero per row at N=3 over F7,
  checking the nonuniform rigidity inequality;
- 4,161 cyclic-consecutive Vandermonde minors, including non-dyadic N=3;
- all 6,272 F8 3×5 submatrices over F17 and F97, plus both parity witnesses;
- all 2,401 relevant parameter tuples over F7 for the literal-scan
  representation counterexample.

These exact finite experiments are consistency checks. The displayed proofs
establish the statements for the specified all-N field model. The checker is
not a fast transform implementation, and its verifier work is not claimed
as algorithmic speedup.

## 5. Remaining constructive target

The surviving exact route still needs a uniform factorization into
nonunitary overlapping bidirectional-sum maps, with all scalar operations,
coefficient generation, memory work, and outputs charged. This review adds a
more precise rejection certificate for one-way scan/broadcast proposals; it
does not rule out that broader route or claim an exact sub-NlogN transform.
