## Established and reproducible

1. **Fourier row-sparse rigidity:** for the length-N Fourier matrix and an arbitrary matrix with at most k nonzeros per row, rank(F-P) >= floor(N/(k+1)). Proof: take m consecutive Fourier rows, select m columns absent from the row-sparse matrix; the resulting Fourier minor is Vandermonde and invertible. This is exact at every N, also for F^-1.
2. **Product-of-near-Green-sums obstruction:** for t invertible stages each a sum of diagonally gauged resolvents close in rank to a reference row-d-sparse inverse, arbitrary monomial gates between stages, total denominator and limited gauge-change rank budget R, exact Fourier requires R >= floor(N/(1+product(d_j))). For cyclic bidiagonal Green references, R >= floor(N/(2^t+1)). If per-stage correction ranks stay O(1), depth t >= log_2 N - O(log log N).
3. **Exact ordering diagnostic:** for S=(I-alpha W0)^-1+(I-alpha W1)^-1, assuming invertible, the rank of S^-1-(I-alpha W0)/2 equals N - number of cycles in W0^-1 W1. O(N) computable. No condition-number assumption.
4. **Exact finite-field examples:** two stages with one transposition per cycle at N=64 have correction rank 4 vs Fourier requirement 12; two stages with sparse input/output phase patches have rank 8 vs requirement 12; three ungauged two-Green stages have rank 6 vs requirement 7. All are impossible at those budget caps for *any admissible complex parameters*.
5. **Escaping positive control:** independent random Hamiltonian cycle pairs at N=32,64,256 have permutation difference ranks of order N, outside the new obstruction. Their tested product circuits still fail both exact Fourier Weyl equations. They cost O(N) per forward execution and remain a genuine open construction target.

## Important exclusions and limitations

- This does NOT exclude products of truly independent, rank-distant Green sums, or summand-specific diagonal gauges changing O(N) entries. Avoid presenting it as a general FFT lower bound.
- No worst-case o(N log N) exact FFT exists in the new code; no bit-precision or stability theorem.
- The previous additive-separator obstruction remains valid. Do not return to increasing additive q as the sole strategy.
- Previous Phase 27/28/29/30/31 results excluded many fixed phase fittings; do not repeat random parameter guessing without exact phase invariants.
