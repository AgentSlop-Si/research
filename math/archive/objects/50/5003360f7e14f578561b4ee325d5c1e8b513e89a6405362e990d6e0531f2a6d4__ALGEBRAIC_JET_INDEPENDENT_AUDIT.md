## 1. The regular-patch equivalence is an actual theorem

Write S_ij=L_i V_j for i>j, S_ii=d_i, and S_ij=U_i W_j for i<j. At n=8, a generic stage has 13 lower, 13 upper, and 8 diagonal matrix parameters, totaling 34.

For F=BPA with invertible F, both stages are invertible. The intermediate diagonal action A -> D A and B -> B P D^(-1) P^(-1) preserves the product and the stage class. If all diagonal entries of A are nonzero, it sets diag(A)=1. Internal lower and upper rescalings then set L_A[7]=U_A[0]=1 on their stated nonzero-factor patch. The remaining free coordinates number 6+7+6+7=26. This is a coordinate-variable count; no assertion that every point of this parameterization has an injective parameter-to-matrix map is needed.

Let U=PA and G=F adj(U). On det(U) G[7,0] G[0,7] != 0, the stated 15 lower and 15 upper anchored two-by-two minors vanish if and only if B=G/det(U) has the required separated generators. For example, lower factors are recovered from L_7=1, L_i=B_i0/B_70, and V_j=B_7j. The anchored equations supply exactly the 15 entries not already determined by the first column and last row. The upper argument is identical with its other corner. Diagonal entries of B are unrestricted.

The cofactor indexing in the source is correct. Each cofactor has degree at most 14 in the 26 variables, so each displayed equation has degree at most 28 over Q(zeta_8). This is a valid upper bound, not a claim of exact degree or independent equations after substitution.

New checks, in each of GF(12,289) and GF(65,537):

- 30 separately generated actual products passed all 30 identities.
- Their reconstructed second stages matched every entry exactly.
- The G identities equaled det(PA)^2 times the B identities.
- 900 single-entry mutations each violated exactly its corresponding anchored equation, checking all 30 equation positions on all 30 products.
- 30 unrelated first-stage samples failed the fixed F8 target equations. This last observation is a sensitivity check only.

### A concrete reason the open-patch condition is indispensable

Let B=I+E_(2,1)+E_(4,3), using zero-based indices. It is invertible and all 30 displayed anchored equations vanish: the two corner anchors, the off-diagonal first column, and the off-diagonal last row are zero. Yet B is not globally lower-separated rank one. B_21=1 and B_43=1 force L_4 V_1 != 0, contradicting B_41=0.

Thus the equations alone, without the nonzero-anchor inequalities, genuinely have false positives even among invertible matrices. A symbolic exclusion on the displayed patch should saturate by det(PA) G_70 G_07; the omitted A diagonal/factor patches and B zero-anchor patches still require separate coverage before any universal negative conclusion. Gauge normalization does not make those patches disappear.

## 2. The exact curvature certificate survives independent reconstruction

- Twenty realized second-order jets were accepted by a separately implemented lifting test.

- The affine target jet was rejected, while adding its exact E_(0,7) quadratic correction was accepted.

### Why this lifts to characteristic zero

The original polynomial map has integer coefficients at an explicitly recorded integer point. A nonzero modular determinant is the reduction of a nonzero integer determinant. The earlier algebraic dimension argument bounds this chart image by 60: two 34-dimensional stages minus the 8-dimensional intermediate diagonal gauge. Therefore the complex Jacobian rank is exactly 60, and the verified augmented minor proves E_(0,7) lies outside its complex image.

The 20 independent modular gauge vectors also lift to 20 independent characteristic-zero vectors. As actual parameter symmetries, their vector fields g satisfy Jg=0 identically. Differentiating gives H(v,g)+J(Dg[v])=0, so H(v,g) lies in im(J) for every v. This exact identity, not a finite collection of modular tests alone, makes the projected quadratic term independent of the first-order lift.

For v increasing d_A[7] and d_B[0], the product is exactly M0+t Jv+t^2 E_(0,7). Any other first lift is v+k with k in the 20-dimensional gauge kernel. The projected quadratic term stays the same and is nonzero. Thus no ordinary formal or analytic parameter curve through this specified point realizes the target straight line through second order.

The scope is crucial: this does not exclude another realization point of the same target matrix, produce an explicit global target-only polynomial, or say that Fourier violates the chart's equations. Calling it a local second-order compatibility obstruction is accurate.

## 3. The Cooley–Tukey positive control is exact, including over C

Every supplied entry and every prefix-cut quasiseparable order were rebuilt independently over GF(12,289):

- N=8, radix 2-by-4: stage orders (1,1).
- N=16, radix 4-by-4: stage orders (1,1).
- N=32, radix 4-by-8: stage orders (4,1), not (1,1).

The same identity over C follows directly from index arithmetic. With j=i+a*j2 and k=k2+b*k1, where N=ab,

    j*k = i*k2 + b*i*k1 + a*j2*k2 + N*j2*k1.

The last term contributes one to the root-of-unity factor. The remaining terms are exactly an F_b block stage followed by a shuffle and twiddled F_a block stage. The source's input, output, and middle orderings implement this identity.

For F4 with row and column ordering [0,2,1,3], its only nontrivial two-by-two prefix-cut blocks are [[1,1],[-1,-1]] and [[1,-1],[1,-1]], both rank one. Its other cuts have one row or column. Block-diagonal unions preserve order one, and nonzero column twiddles preserve these ranks.

The saved separated-chart counterexample is also correct: the N=16 first stage has S_10=S_54=1 but S_50=0. A global lower factorization L_i V_j would force S_50 != 0. Quasiseparable transition representations can reset between blocks; one finite global product of row and column generators cannot express this example.

These controls use their displayed CT permutations and endpoint orderings, not the fixed F8 permutation [7,0,6,3,4,1,2,5] in the elimination problem. Consequently they neither supply nor refute a solution to that fixed 26-variable system. They do establish that a finite separated-generator realization condition must not casually be identified with the whole quasiseparable class. The construction is classical and finite-size; no all-length arithmetic saving follows.
