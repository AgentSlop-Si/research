# Phase-12 attachment audit and fusion result

9 October 2026. Source: fft_green_research_phase12_2026-10-09(1).zip.

## Bottom line

The attachment's stronger transform primitive is mathematically useful:
exact unitary structure, linear scalar-arithmetic application and inverse,
order-robust significant cross-cut singular values on the expansion event,
and growing induced 1-condition can coexist. These properties still do not
supply a Fourier factorization.

Combining this new family with the earlier project's sparse/low-rank ideas
gives a stronger obstruction than its supplied five-stage test. A product
of t fixed-strength phase-12 stages, inverses, and unitary monomials needs
t=Omega(log N) to equal a flat unitary. Adding B rank-one Householders still
requires t+B=Omega(log N). The proof approximates the entire stage word by
a matrix with exp(O(t)) nonzeros per row in spectral norm, then uses Fourier
flatness. The constants are deliberately loose. The source's five-stage
gate remains much stronger at realistic finite N.

This is a scoped explicit-stage theorem, not a faster FFT or a general FFT
lower bound. A work lower bound follows only when the displayed stages are
separately charged full-array passes. Whole-word fusion, growing strengths,
arbitrary nonunit diagonal gains, other primitives, and different algorithms
remain outside it. No literature-priority claim is made.

## Verification of the source claims

1. Each Cayley C_a(A)=(I+i a A)(I-i a A)^-1 is unitary for Hermitian A.
   Tree/path elimination has positive real pivots over the complex model.
   The block R=[[0,B],[B*,0]] is a Hermitian involution, so its normalized
   rotation and the complete stage are exactly unitary with linear inverse.
2. The induced-matching and singular-value perturbation arguments are valid:
   crossing E edges give at least ceil(E/32) cross-block singular values at
   least 1/64. The random two-path union bound yields E>N/16 simultaneously
   on all balanced cuts, with the stated probability for N divisible by 16,
   N>=32. This is a probabilistic all-order guarantee, not an assertion that
   every permutation succeeds.
3. The tree root-to-leaf recurrence proves the advertised polynomial norm
   growth for depth L>=1. At L=0 the root is also the leaf and the subtracted
   identity changes the formula: B=I, so the stated lower bound 2 is false.
   This harmless boundary correction does not affect the N>=32 theorem.
4. The five-stage distance-to-monomial argument is valid for the fixed
   small-angle stages, unit phases, permutations, and inverses. It is a
   constant-depth gate, superseded asymptotically, but not numerically, by
   the growing locality gate in this audit.
5. Fourier covariance plus a first-column normalization does identify the
   Fourier matrix. The supplied stage fails covariance; independent exact
   finite-field checks reproduce nonzero defects in every entry at
   N=8,16,32,64,128. A nonzero good-prime reduction disproves the exact
   characteristic-zero rational identity for the corresponding instance.

## A real implementation cost repair

The uploaded constructor validates its permutation using sorted(order).
On arbitrary permutations that costs O(N log N) comparisons. Thus its
literal preparation code does not realize its stated linear setup bound.
This is not an algebraic failure: independent_mixer.py replaces it with a
seen/inverse array, whose validation is deterministic O(N). It otherwise
implements the proved forest/path identities independently and imports no
uploaded executable source.

For N even and T the size of the largest full binary tree fitting N/2,
the modular evaluator's instrumented field-operation counts are exactly:

    preparation: 9N+2T+6,
    forward: (41/2)N+8T-16,
    inverse: (45/2)N+8T-15.

This counts additions, subtractions, multiplications and divisions as unit
field operations. The complex implementation adds O(1) operations to prepare
one N-independent normalization constant. Indexing, validation, copies,
allocation and output writes require separately O(N) work and storage.
The evaluator takes an explicit supplied permutation. Ideal uniform
permutation generation costs O(N) uniform-range draws; rejection-based
randbelow gives expected O(N) random-word draws, rather than a deterministic
worst-case bound without another randomness model or failure budget.

Finite-field mode uses the intentionally unnormalized rotation and its
correct inverse. Its identities do not by themselves prove complex
unitarity; that follows from the algebra. Floating-complex evaluation is
an approximation to the exact arithmetic model. No bounded-word, bit-cost,
backward-stability, or practical speedup claim follows. Exact rational path
pivots and entries can have Theta(N)-bit numerators/denominators. Exact
integer spectral certification is a separate dense audit, not free O(N)
preprocessing for an all-N deterministic successful expander family.

## Independent tests

No uploaded Python or shell code was imported or executed. The new checker
uses the independently authored evaluator, Python's standard library,
NumPy, and three copied source JSON witness/result files as data.

- Exact forward/inverse roundtrips and involution checks: ten even lengths
  from 2 through 1024, three fresh vectors in both inverse directions.
- Independent dense modular Cayley and full-stage controls through N=16,
  including every basis column and invalid-permutation rejection.
- Instrumented exact linear field-operation counts at all ten lengths.
- Exact rational root/leaf recurrence checks at depths 1 through 18.
- Exact integer spectral-gap certificates at N=32,64,128,256; every
  leading principal minor is positive and all archived pivot hashes match.
- Complex numerical unitarity and 32 sampled balanced cuts at each of
  N=16,32,64,128; unitarity error stays below 2e-15 in spectral norm.
- Independent convergent tree-resolvent checks and exact integer/rational
  checks of the new gate's constants.

The all-N statements are established by proofs, not by these finite tests.
The specific finite spectral witnesses certify all balanced cuts for their
own lengths; sampled singular-value tests alone do not.
