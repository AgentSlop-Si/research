# Independent audit: HERO1 mixed-radix v13

9 October 2026. Scope: a bounded mathematical audit of the Library-generated v13 research archive, not a direct user upload and not a new exact fast Fourier transform.

## Verdict

The main exact-arithmetic statements survive independent review:

- The mixed-radix composition is exactly `A_N = F_N P` with the advertised interleaved physical block map.
- With the stated independent column states, the estimator is unbiased and has the stated exact per-output covariance, independent of the odd-factor output coordinate.
- Its rank, fixed-state spectral/Frobenius errors, and expected whole-vector error are correct. They prevent interpreting its near-linear single-row work as an accurate whole-vector or exact transform.
- The complete beta bank reconstructs the Fourier transform exactly. This identity, its sharp information threshold, and its ordinary Cooley–Tukey collapse were already established in the 17:17 exact-bridge report. They are not new v13 findings.
- The arithmetic bounds are valid in the disclosed exact-scalar model with charged setup. The archive does not establish finite-precision bit complexity, same-accuracy performance, or a practical speedup.

Two qualifications matter:

1. The supplied finite-field program reverses the one-row projector phase. This does not falsify its completed-bank identities, but makes those tests insensitive to that sign error and unable to certify the actual one-row executor. The independent checker below closes this gap by checking the recurrence and mixed-radix composition before averaging.
2. “Independence is indispensable” is too strong. Identical full-state reuse can indeed multiply variance by the odd factor, but full state independence is not necessary. A shared random slope and conditionally pairwise-independent beta phases preserve the exact covariance. This yields a small constructive randomness improvement.

A further bounded precision observation is useful: the complete bank is perfectly conditioned as a normalized stacked measurement, and a common downstream twiddle perturbation loses the single-row factor M when the whole bank is averaged exactly. Neither observation solves the scan/FFT arithmetic bottleneck.

## 2. Composition, state, and information

Write N=Gm, with G the largest dyadic divisor and m odd. Let n=mr+s and k=k1+Gk2. Expanding kn/N gives k1r/G+k1s/N+k2r+k2s/m. The integral k2r term vanishes in the exponential, giving the normalized G-transform, diagonal twiddle, then normalized m-transform in the archive's orientation.

For a local block r of length M, define

`s_s(r,u) = exp(2 pi i [a_s u(u-1)/2 + (a_s M r + beta_s)u]/G)`.

Compression uses `sum_u s_s(r,u) x_s[Mr+u]`; reconstruction multiplies that summary by the conjugate phase. Therefore the physical matrix entry is

`P_s[Mr+v, Mr+u] = conjugate(s_s(r,v)) s_s(r,u)`.

This fixes the sign convention unambiguously. If C_s is the block-summary map, P_s=C_s* C_s, C_s C_s*=M I_K, P_s^2=M P_s, and rank P_s=K=G/M. These are scaled orthogonal projections: P_s/M is a projection, while P_s itself is not idempotent unless M=1.

Applying the ordinary mixed-radix transform to the projected interleaved input gives `A_N=F_N P`, with P the permutation-conjugated direct sum of the m local matrices. It has rank N/M and nonzero singular values M. A single row contains only N/M independent complex linear summaries, irrespective of the N displayed outputs.

For r fixed rows, the stacked input map has rank at most rN/M. If r<M, a nonzero vector lies in its kernel; no decoder given only those summaries, even a nonlinear decoder, can reconstruct F_N x for every x. This stronger summary-only information statement was already proved in the 17:17 bridge. The extension to fixed-seed adaptive linear queries based only on previous summaries also already appears there. These are uniform-all-input architecture statements, not unrestricted Fourier lower bounds and not a per-fixed-input randomized failure theorem.

The scalar output-side rank bound also implies spectral distance at least 1 from F_N whenever r<M. Additional access to the original input or an unrelated transform is outside that bound.

## 3. Covariance, including an explicit projector description

Uniform beta makes E[P_s | a_s]=I for every fixed legal slope a_s: all nontrivial local off-diagonal characters average to zero. Thus unbiasedness does not require randomness in the slope. The particular sharp covariance does average over uniformly odd slopes.

For a local output k, let Q_k=E[(A_G-F_G)[k,:]* (A_G-F_G)[k,:]]. Define D_k=diag(exp(2 pi i k i/G)). Directly expanding the coefficients and averaging beta gives

`Q_k = D_k Q_0 D_k*`,

`G Q_0[i,j] = sum_{d in D_u intersect D_v} c_G(d(j-i))`,

where u=i mod M, v=j mod M, D_u={u-t : 0<=t<M, t!=u}, and the normalized dyadic unit-character sum c_G(c) is 1 if c=0 mod G, -1 if c=G/2 mod G, and 0 otherwise.

Here is an independent explanation of the claimed projector spectrum. Put M=2^b and K=G/M, with K>=M.

- If u!=v, the 2-adic valuations of d and j-i are both at most b-1. Their sum is at most 2b-2<=log2(G)-2, so none of the character sums can be nonzero. Q_0 is block diagonal after grouping equal within-block coordinates u.
- Within a fixed u, the K-by-K block indexed by r is circulant. Its Fourier eigenvalue at mode ell is `(1/M) sum_{d in D_u} Pr[a d = ell mod K]`, where a is uniform among the odd residues modulo K.
- For v2(ell)=h<b, the set D_u contains exactly M/2^(h+1) elements of valuation h. Each such d maps uniformly to the K/2^(h+1) residues of that valuation. The eigenvalue is therefore 1/K=M/G.
- For ell divisible by M, the eigenvalue is zero. There are K/M such modes in each of the M coordinate groups.

Consequently Q_k=(M/G) Pi_k, with Pi_k an orthogonal projector of rank G-K for M>1. For M=1, Q_k=0. Explicitly, Pi_0 keeps the block-index Fourier modes not divisible by M separately for each local coordinate u, and Pi_k=D_k Pi_0 D_k*.

The independent checker verifies `(G Q_0)^2 = M(G Q_0)` and its trace using integer arithmetic in 19 legal dyadic cases, then reconstructs covariance by exhaustive complex state enumeration in five cases, at every output frequency.

With independent columns, or the weaker condition in Section 7, cross-column error covariances vanish. The final unitary m-transform thus gives

`Var(error[k1+Gk2]) = (1/m) sum_s x_s* Q_k1 x_s`

`= (M/N) sum_s ||Pi_k1 x_s||^2 <= (M/N)||x||^2`.

The exact expression does not depend on k2. An input consisting of identical copies of an active dyadic covariance mode saturates the bound under an admissible uncorrelated schedule. If the identical full state is reused, the zero-frequency errors add coherently and the variance becomes m times this bound. Independent exhaustive checks reproduce factors 3 and 5.

## 4. What the accuracy and robust claims do, and do not, establish

For each fixed tuple, P has eigenvalues M and 0. Therefore for M>=2,

`||A_N-F_N||_2 = M-1`,

`||A_N-F_N||_F^2 = N(M-1)`.

Also `(P-I)^2=(M-2)P+I`, so E[P]=I implies

`E||A_Nx-F_Nx||^2=(M-1)||x||^2`.

This expected global identity only needs unbiased marginals, not cross-column independence. Averaging r independent unbiased rows divides the expected squared global error by r. Thus this particular linear averaging route needs r>=(M-1)/epsilon^2 for the stated relative mean-square target. It does not imply a lower bound for all approximate Fourier algorithms.

The inherited three-grid estimator is compatible with the covariance bound. Set tau=gamma sqrt(M/N)||x||. Chebyshev bounds each real or imaginary coordinate's bad-row probability by p=1/gamma^2. Independent rows and gamma>sqrt(2) give the binomial/KL majority bound, followed by a union bound over 2N coordinates. The source's `(5/sqrt(2)) tau` complex error bound is conservative for its width-7tau, three-offset grid geometry. At least two grids contain the entire good interval [mu-tau,mu+tau] in a single cell; each such label is a strict majority and survives the majority algorithm. If one grid is bad, its nearby boundary b is within tau of mu and the two good centers are b +/- 7tau/6, so their median with any third value is within 13tau/6 of mu. If all three are good, their middle center is within 7tau/6. Thus even the stronger real bound 13tau/6 applies; the stated complex bound is safely larger. Grid comparisons and labels are part of the exact-arithmetic contract; floating-point tests do not certify behavior at grid boundaries.

For fixed gamma and failure target delta, O(log(N/delta)) rows suffice. This is a sufficient amplification cost, not an independently proved necessity for every possible estimator. With M near log G, the per-coordinate absolute error scale is still of order sqrt(M/N)||x||, not a relative error guarantee on each potentially small Fourier coefficient.

## 5. Arithmetic and precision audit

The disclosed one-row arithmetic bound is

`O(N + (N/M) log(G/M) + N log(m+1))`.

It includes the m local compressions, m short K-FFTs, m length-G scans, twiddle application, G length-m FFTs, and output routing. Choosing a dyadic M comparable to log G makes the local sketch work O(N) in sufficiently large legal G. Fixed m then gives O(N) per randomized row; polylogarithmic m gives O(N log log N); odd N gives the conventional exact length-N transform. None is an all-length worst-case exact sub-FFT bound.

These counts require a clearly delimited scalar model. A supplied primitive root permits O(N) phase/root-table multiplication and routing work, but producing or certifying roots and normalization scalars is a separate obligation. Modular indices, FFT algorithms, and sampling must also be charged. The source's odd-slope rejection sampler has expected O(1), rather than deterministic worst-case O(1), draws per state; directly sampling the d-1 bits of an odd slope at G=2^d removes that implementation detail. Source NumPy calls, trigonometric calls, integer-width limits, and fixed-dtype arithmetic are diagnostics rather than an implementation-independent bit theorem.

The single-root twiddle lemma is valid as a conservative conditional perturbation estimate. With root error eta, relative complex multiplication allowance u, h=eta+u<1/(4N), the two product chains admit a bound of the stated form `(1+h)^(2N+1)-1`. In bounding products, the root and multiplication factors must both be included; the spare exponent and smallness condition absorb their cross terms. Multiplying that maximum table error by M||x|| gives a valid single-row output error bound. For polynomially small target error, O(log N) precision is sufficient for this component under the assumed model. It is not a complete stability analysis of the compression, FFTs, gauged scan, and grid rounding.

No same-accuracy runtime comparison is established against v12. The displayed proxies explicitly compare different error contracts; treating their ratios as speedups would be invalid.

## 6. Completed beta bank: inherited result and sign-blind test gap

For beta_t=beta_0+tK, the actual physical phase vector conjugates the compression phase, so its modulation is `exp(-2 pi i t u/M)`. Reversing this sign merely permutes the complete bank. In either convention character orthogonality gives

`P_t P_l = M delta(t,l) P_t`,

`sum_t P_t = M I`,

`(1/M) sum_t A_t = F_N`.

These statements and their M-state rank threshold duplicate Section 1 of `additions/1717/astra_exact_bridge/EXACT_BRIDGES.md`. The prior report also already batches local summaries as M-DFTs and shows that expanding the aggregate recovers ordinary Cooley–Tukey. For v13, applying that collapse in every dyadic column and then doing the odd-factor transform costs O(N log G+N log m)=O(N log N). The independent-column packaging does not create a new exact frontier beyond the prior genuinely fused-executor question.

The archive's finite-field program uses `phase(v)-phase(u)` for the output-v/input-u entry, whereas its executable sketch uses `phase(u)-phase(v)`. It therefore tests a transposed/conjugated one-row projector. Both complete banks sum to M I, so all reported averaged Fourier entries can still pass. This is a test-coverage/sign-consistency issue, not a failure of the completed-bank theorem. Its wrong-spacing mutation correctly tests a different failure mode.

The independent checker instead constructs the unnormalized dyadic recurrence, including its anti-periodic closure, for each basis input. The gauge at cycle j is `zeta^(a*j*(j-1)/2+beta*j)`; at G it is -1. Thus the recurrence seed is half the total gauged forcing. Each resulting one-row coefficient is checked against the physical projector with the correct phase orientation, then against the mixed-radix F_N P coefficient, before the bank is averaged. Deliberately reversed one-row phases disagree in every nontrivial test case, although the reversed complete-bank sum would still be exact.

## 7. Constructive extension: less randomness, same covariance

### Shared slope with independent phases

Choose one common uniform odd a modulo G and independent uniform beta_s modulo G. Conditional on a, every column error has zero mean, so independent beta_s make every cross-column covariance zero. Each marginal (a,beta_s) has exactly the original law. Therefore the entire exact per-output covariance and all bounds above are unchanged, although the full column states are dependent through a.

This simplest implementation uses `(d-1)+md` random bits per row at G=2^d instead of `m(2d-1)`, with the same O(m) state words and no new algebraic machinery. It needs independent fresh row randomness for the robust tail argument.

### Pairwise phases from a short seed

Full independence of the beta_s is also unnecessary: conditional pairwise independence suffices because the claim is second-order covariance. Let L=max(d,ceil(log2 m)). Fix distinct labels t_s in the finite field GF(2^L), a fixed basis, and a surjective linear map pi from this field onto d bits. Choose A,B independently uniform in the field and set

`beta_s = pi(A t_s+B)`,

interpreting those d bits as an integer modulo G. Take the common uniform odd a independently of A,B. For s!=t, the map `(A,B) -> (A t_s+B, A t_t+B)` is an invertible field-linear map. Its output pair is uniform; applying pi preserves pairwise uniformity. Thus the conditional cross-column covariances vanish and the original covariance theorem follows unchanged.

The random-bit count is `d-1+2L` per row; when m<=G, it is `3d-1`. Higher-order correlations between columns remain, which are harmless for this argument because concentration is obtained by independent whole rows, not by a within-row independence assumption.

This is a randomness reduction, not an uncharged speedup. A concrete field representation must be supplied or constructed and verified. Straight polynomial-basis multiplication costs O(L^2) bit operations, so forming m hashes costs O(m L^2) bit operations with O(L)-bit streaming state. Do not silently count arbitrary-size finite-field multiplication as one scalar operation. The simpler shared-slope construction avoids this extra field setup.

Exact pair-count checks and exhaustive complex evaluations certify two examples:

- G=8, m=3, M=2: 256 complete seed states; 8 bits versus 15 for fully independent full states.
- G=16, m=5, M=4: 2,048 complete seed states; 11 bits versus 35.

Maximum covariance discrepancies are 3.11e-15 and 2.31e-14, respectively, from floating-point evaluation of the full finite ensembles. The pairwise-uniform count tests are integer-exact; the general guarantee rests on the proof above.

## 8. Conditioning and common downstream errors

Each individual A_t has a nullspace when M>1, hence no finite full-space inverse condition number. On its retained subspace its nonzero singular values are all M. In contrast, the completed bank has a tight, well-conditioned stacked representation:

`sum_t A_t* A_t = sum_t P_t^2 = M sum_t P_t = M^2 I`.

Therefore the tall matrix obtained by stacking A_t/M is an isometry and has condition number 1. The raw block summaries stacked as C_t/sqrt(M) are likewise unitary in the square complete-bank case. Good conditioning of this exact representation does not establish numerical stability of the chosen recurrence executor.

For arbitrary per-bank additive output errors e_t, the averaged error satisfies

`||(1/M) sum_t e_t|| <= (1/sqrt(M)) (sum_t ||e_t||^2)^(1/2)`.

A particularly useful special case concerns a *common* approximate twiddle table used for all banks, as in the supplied exact-bank implementation. Write B for the block-diagonal G-DFT, D for exact twiddles, C for the final odd-part transform/routing, and Delta for the diagonal twiddle error. Exact averaging gives

`(1/M) sum_t C(D+Delta) B P_t = C(D+Delta)B`.

Since B and C are unitary,

`||averaged distorted transform - F_N||_2 = ||Delta||_2`.

Thus common twiddle error alone has bound `max|delta_twiddle| ||x||`, with no factor M. In fact the operator-norm equality is exact. Errors introduced independently by local arithmetic, final FFT arithmetic, or bank summation need their own bounds. No full finite-precision theorem is asserted. Three numerical matrix controls check this cancellation and the normalized stacked isometry.
