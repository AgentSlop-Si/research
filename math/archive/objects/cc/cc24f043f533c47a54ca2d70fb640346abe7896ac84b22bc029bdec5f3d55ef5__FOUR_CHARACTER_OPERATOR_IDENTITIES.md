# Exact four-character Fourier, bank, and Green identities

## Status

This note gives reusable operator identities, with all shorter transforms and routing work charged. It does not construct Fourier-compatible Green coefficients or a faster FFT. The four ordinary shorter Fourier transforms below are a radix-four control, not a speedup.

The grid convention is a=b or a=2b, b dyadic, N=ab. Small vertices are v=bx+y; large vertices are2bX+Y. Each large vertex over v has high fiber bits epsilon=(epsilon_x,epsilon_y), with X=x+a epsilon_x and Y=y+b epsilon_y.

Define the four real Walsh characters chi_(u,v)(epsilon)=(-1)^(u epsilon_x+v epsilon_y). To avoid ambiguity, the v in a character label is a bit; a spatial vertex is otherwise denoted by i or t below. Let Q_(u,v) be the signed fiber-sum matrix, and stack these four matrices into W. Then

    W W^T=4I_(4N),
    W^(-1)=W^T/4.

These fixed high-bit characters form the group V4. They must not be confused with the cyclic C4 eigencharacters used later to diagonalize a Hamiltonian sheet holonomy.

## 1. Full Fourier decomposition in the common Walsh basis

Let omega be a primitive4Nth root and zeta=omega^4. The positive-exponent Fourier convention gives

    (Q_(u,v)F_(4N))[bx+y,j]
      = omega^((2bx+y)j)
        (1+(-1)^(u+j))
        (1+(-1)^v omega^(bj)).

For the input residue class j=4t+s,0<=s<4, define

    D_s[bx+y,bx+y]=omega^((2bx+y)s),
    c_s=omega^(bs),
    (R0 f)[bx+y]=f[(2bx+y) mod N],
    (R1 f)[bx+y]=f[(2bx+y+b) mod N].

Then the exact N-by-N block identity is

    (Q_(u,v)F_(4N))[:,4t+s]
      = 0, if s not congruent u modulo2;
      = 2 D_s(R0+(-1)^v c_s R1)F_N, otherwise.

To verify it, omega^((2bx+y)4t)=zeta^((2bx+y)t). The additional factor omega^(b(4t+s)) equals c_s zeta^(bt), and multiplying the Fourier input by zeta^(bt) shifts its selected output row by b. All phases and the factor2 are retained.

For an arbitrary input vector x, first compute the four vectors

    f_s=F_N (x_(4t+s))_(t=0)^(N-1), s=0,1,2,3.

Each output character is then the sum of its two parity-compatible blocks, and the original output is W^T times the four character vectors divided by4. The same four f_s are reused across all four output characters. No sixteen independent transforms are required.

### Cost

This uses exactly four ordinary N-point Fourier transforms on four independent input-residue vectors, plus O(N) word routing, additions, and paid phase multiplications. The four diagonal D_s and four constants c_s can be generated by geometric recurrences with O(N) field operations and O(N) storage; no dense phase table is needed. R0 and R1 are selectors, generally with repeated rows, not asserted to be permutations.

The resulting ordinary recurrence is T(4N)=4T(N)+O(N), giving the standard FFT scaling. This is a correctness and accounting control, not the desired contracting recursive bank identity.

## 2. Active-bank identity in the same character basis

Assume active labels are pulled back: J_large=J_small union(J_small+a). At an active small block x, the large operator is F_(2b) on each of its two x-fiber copies. Hence the x-character bit u is preserved.

Let rho be a primitive2bth root, with the compatible bth root rho^2. Define D_0=I_b, D_1=diag(rho^y), and the signed selectors

    S_(v,w)[t,z]
      = (-1)^(w floor((2t+v)/b))
        1_(z=(2t+v) mod b),
    v,w in{0,1}.

For an active block, the transformed bank block from input character(u',w) to output character(u,v) is

    1_(u=u') D_v F_b S_(v,w).

This follows from Q_v F_(2b)=2D_v F_b E_v and the inverse two-character transform Q_w^T/2. For an inactive block it is simply1_(u=u')1_(v=w)I_b.

### Cost

For each active small block, first form sum_w S_(v,w)z_(u,w), then apply F_b once for each pair(u,v). There are exactly four b-point Fourier applications per active small block, plus O(b) selector, addition, and twiddle work. This matches the cost of two ordinary2b-point transforms split into halves. Four copies on different data are charged even though their matrix is the same F_b.

## 3. A Hamiltonian four-sheet route in the common Walsh basis

Let P_small be an N-cycle and P_large a4N-cycle satisfying pi P_large=P_small pi. Each small directed edge i->P_small(i) induces a permutation phi_i of the four fiber labels. Every permutation of four points is an affine bijection of GF(2)^2: there are4 translations and6 invertible2-by2 binary matrices, accounting for all24 permutations. Thus write

    phi_i(epsilon)=A_i epsilon+t_i.

The edge action in the common Walsh basis satisfies

    chi_c(phi_i(epsilon))=(-1)^(c dot t_i) chi_(A_i^T c)(epsilon).

Consequently W P_large W^(-1) is a signed permutation on the4N pairs(character,small vertex). The trivial character is invariant and gives exactly P_small. The three nontrivial characters form an invariant3N-dimensional sector with a signed-permutation edge matrix depending on the small vertex.

### The nontrivial sector does not split into three ordinary N-cycles

Going once around the small Hamiltonian cycle gives a four-cycle on the four sheets; otherwise P_large would not be Hamiltonian. Its affine linear part has order2, so its action on the three nonzero binary characters fixes one and exchanges two. The fixed character has holonomy sign-1, and the exchanged pair has sign product-1. One way to see the signs is that a four-cycle has eigenvalues1,i,-1,-i, with1 belonging to the trivial character.

Therefore, in the common Walsh basis, the three-character signed route has precisely:

* one signed N-cycle with total sign-1;
* one signed2N-cycle with total sign-1.

This is already a specific failure of naive scalar closure: even one Hamiltonian four-sheet Green term couples nontrivial Walsh channels. It is not four independent copies of the original ordinary-wrap N-point Green term in this common basis.

The signed cycles give a direct common-basis evaluator using three cyclic scans of lengths N,N,2N. Their wrap denominators multiply to the original one:

    (1-tau^N)(1+tau^N)(1+tau^(2N))=1-tau^(4N).

The nontrivial cycles may visit different character labels as the base position changes; they are not assumed to sit in globally fixed individual Walsh channels. Their signed successor arrays can be enumerated in O(N) work. Across eight original terms this route uses24 signed-cycle scans per layer, with total scanned length32N, plus the paid mask/routing work. This is an alternative to the32 N-point scalar streams obtained by the C4 diagonalization below, not an additional uncharged computation.

## 4. Endpoint masks and the closed four-state family

For a large diagonal mask a(i,epsilon), write its exact fiber Fourier expansion

    a(i,epsilon)=sum_c a_hat_c(i) chi_c(epsilon),
    a_hat_c(i)=1/4 sum_epsilon a(i,epsilon) chi_c(epsilon).

In the common character basis, multiplication by the mask becomes the local4-by4 matrix

    M_a(i)[c,d]=a_hat_(c+d)(i),

where character addition is XOR. A pure nontrivial character mask permutes the four channels. A general mask gives an XOR-convolution matrix at each small vertex. Its nonsingularity guard is equivalent to requiring all four original values a(i,epsilon) to be nonzero; it does not require every Fourier coefficient a_hat_c(i) to be nonzero.

For example, the constant mask(1,1,1,1) is nonsingular although three Walsh coefficients vanish. Conversely the mask(0,1,2,4) is singular although its four Walsh coefficients are(7,-3,-5,1)/4, all nonzero over characteristic zero. Testing the Fourier coefficients individually is therefore the wrong guard.

Thus an eight-term large layer has an exact representation

    W H_large W^(-1)
      = sum_(j=1)^8 M_(a_j) (I-tau_j S_j)^(-1),

where S_j is a four-state signed cyclic transport over the small order P_(j,small). This is a closed, reusable **four-state Green/operator family**: each edge has a4-by4 signed-permutation matrix, and each endpoint has a local XOR-convolution mask. It retains O(N) application with the fixed state dimension4, including wrap solves.

It is an enlargement of the scalar eight-order seed model, not proof that the original scalar model closes. Repeating such an enlargement without a proved reduction could grow the state dimension by another factor4 at each level. That growth cannot be silently treated as constant.

## 5. Route-specific diagonalization into four twisted-wrap Green terms

There is another exact representation for each single order. Traverse the base cycle i_0=0,i_1,...,i_(N-1). Carry a reference fiber labeling along ordinary edges: T_0=identity and T_(k+1)=phi_(i_k) T_k. In these transported sheet labels, every ordinary edge is identity on the fiber, while the wrap is one four-cycle M.

Enumerate the reference sheet labels in the order M^c(0),0<=c<4. The ordinary4-point cyclic Fourier transform on this labeling diagonalizes the wrap with eigenvalues

    eta_s=iota^s, s=0,1,2,3,

where iota=omega_(4N)^N is the chosen primitive fourth root. Over the complex numbers this is the usual positive-exponent choice iota=i.

This is a C4 eigenbasis, generally different from the fixed V4 Walsh basis. It is also base-position dependent through T_k and order dependent through phi.

The scalar Green resolvent (I-tau P_large)^(-1) is thereby conjugate to four N-point Green operators, all following P_small, with ordinary edge weights tau and one wrap weight tau*eta_s. The four denominators multiply exactly:

    product_(s=0)^3 (1-tau^N eta_s)=1-tau^(4N).

Thus no denominator guard is dropped. For supplied matching scalar tau, invertibility of the original Green term implies invertibility of all four diagonal channels.

If an ordinary-wrap homogeneous pole is desired, choose beta_s with beta_s^N=eta_s, for example beta_s=omega_(4N)^s. For E_s=diag(beta_s^(-k)) in the base-cycle order and the twisted cycle T_eta_s, the direction is explicitly

    E_s^(-1) T_eta_s E_s = beta_s P_small,
    (I-tau T_eta_s)^(-1)
      = E_s (I-tau beta_s P_small)^(-1) E_s^(-1).

Equivalently, if B_s is the transported positive C4 character map from the large space, then A_s=diag(beta_s^k) B_s satisfies A_s P_large=beta_s P_small A_s. Hence the corresponding homogeneous pole is tau*beta_s, with paid left and right phase gauges.

### Cost and closure consequences

The sheet transport, inverse label maps, and4-point basis conversions take O(N) work and storage per order. Applying the decomposed resolvent costs four N-point Green scans plus O(N) routing and constant-size mixing. There are four phase/wrap channels per original order: eight original terms therefore give32 shorter scalar Green streams per layer, or96 across three layers. This accounting matches the original24 terms applied at size4N; it is not a reduction in total work.

The fixed rational poles1/2 through1/9 are not closed under this decomposition. One must admit either the four wrap phases1,i,-1,-i or the new poles tau_j*omega_(4N)^s and their order-index phase gauges. Moreover, the transport basis depends on the order j. Different Green terms cannot automatically share one diagonalizing basis or one transformed endpoint mask. The common Walsh four-state form above avoids that ambiguity but retains the coupled channels.

## 6. The remaining constructive question

The operator identities identify the required families precisely. A successful lift would have to choose the four-state coefficient masks so that the full Fourier identity in section1, the active-bank identity in section2, all inverse and endpoint guards, and the claimed active recursive mass hold together. It must then prove a uniform coefficient generator and a state/term count that stays within the claimed cost across repeated lifts.

The all-scalar folding-rank gate rules out mere scalar copying, while a nonconstant fiber character supplies rank-compatible freedom. The present note shows how that freedom actually enters the operators; it does not solve the coupled coefficient equations or connect the N16 fitting family to the independently chosen large hierarchical bases.
