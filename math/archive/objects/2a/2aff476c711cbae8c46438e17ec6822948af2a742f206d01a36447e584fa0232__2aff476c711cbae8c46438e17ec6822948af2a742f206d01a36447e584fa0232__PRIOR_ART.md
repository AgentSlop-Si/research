# Bounded primary-source context

Checked 9 October 2026. This is a short contextual check, not a novelty survey.

- I. J. Good, “The Interaction Algorithm and Practical Fourier Analysis,” Journal of the Royal Statistical Society, Series B 20(2), 361–372 (1958). [Original publisher record](https://rss.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.2517-6161.1958.tb00300.x). This is the classical Fourier factorization lineage relevant to the coprime CRT/tensor step; the archive itself already credits Good–Thomas. The publisher abstract/metadata were accessible, not the full article.
- James W. Cooley and John W. Tukey, “An Algorithm for the Machine Calculation of Complex Fourier Series,” Mathematics of Computation 19, 297–301 (1965). [IBM primary institutional record](https://research.ibm.com/publications/an-algorithm-for-the-machine-calculation-of-complex-fourier-series); [paper copy hosted at Johns Hopkins](https://www.cs.jhu.edu/~misha/ReadingSeminar/Papers/Cooley65.pdf). This is the classical mixed-radix/polyphase transform lineage. Sparsity-based pruning of the resulting identity is not evidence of a new universal transform.
- Romuald Dąbrowski and Benji Fisher, “A stationary phase formula for exponential sums over Z/p^m Z and applications to GL(3)-Kloosterman sums,” Acta Arithmetica 80(1), 1–48 (1997), [publisher record](https://www.impan.pl/en/publishing-house/journals-and-series/acta-arithmetica/all/80/11/109556/a-stationary-phase-formula-for-exponential-sums-over-p-m-and-applications-to-gl-3-kloosterman-sums), DOI 10.4064/aa-80-1-1-48. The record confirms established finite-ring stationary-phase literature. It is not used as a black-box proof of our recognition/cost bound.
- Todd Cochrane and Zhiyong Zheng, “Pure and mixed exponential sums,” Acta Arithmetica 91(3), 249–278 (1999), [author-hosted paper](https://www.math.ksu.edu/~cochrane/research/puremix09.pdf), [author publication list](https://math.ksu.edu/~cochrane/research/research09.html). The accessible author copy is dated 2012 and supplies explicit prime-power polynomial exponential-sum analysis, critical-point reductions, and CRT multiplicativity. Its published work substantially predates this archive. The square-fiber derivative congruence is naturally interpreted in this classical stationary-phase setting; this relationship is an algebraic inference, not a claim that this paper states the exact uploaded recognizer.

The identities and charged recognition theorem in our audit are proved independently. This bounded check is sufficient to rule out casually calling the CRT, polyphase, or polynomial-phase reduction unprecedented. It is insufficient to decide publication-level novelty of the exact combination, adaptive recognition strategy or bookkeeping.
