# Independent audit: v10 all-length Bessel/Chebyshev lift

Audit date: 9 October 2026. Source checkpoint label: 10 October 2026.

## Verdict

The near-grid lift, its stated uniform tail, and the covariance-preserving composition with the dyadic v9 sketch are correct in the exact-coefficient model. The construction extends the **randomized, energy-relative approximation theorem** to arbitrary lengths. It does not give an exact arbitrary-input, all-output FFT.

The source's stronger claim that all coefficient generation is already charged in an `O(RN)` algorithm is not established in the strict uniform algebraic/finite-precision model. Exact Bessel evaluation is an additional special-function primitive, and the prototype calls SciPy `jv` without analyzing its work or error. Its actual Chebyshev storage is `Theta(RN)`, contrary to the stated rank-independent `O(N)` implementation storage. These are qualifications to the claimed implementation and cost model, not counterexamples to the lift or covariance identity.

Independently reconstructed numerical controls pass. They do not execute or import any uploaded source or bytecode. A separate Taylor-based construction being investigated elsewhere is outside this audit; no such new repair is claimed here.

## 2. Grid map and deterministic kernel

Let `Q=2^ceil(log2 N)`. For `N>=1`, `N<=Q<2N`. An exact, explicit nearest-grid map is

`j_k = floor((2 k Q + N)/(2N))`, `h_k=kQ-j_k N`, `theta_k=h_k/N`.

The unrounded values are at least one apart, hence the rounded integers are distinct. The largest unrounded value is `Q-Q/N<=Q-1`, so `0<=j_k<Q`: a modulo reduction is unnecessary. Also `2|h_k|<=N`. This supplies integer work and an exact rational residual, assuming sufficiently wide integer words or charged multiprecision arithmetic.

The factorization

`exp(-2 pi i k n/N) = exp(-2 pi i j_k n/Q) exp(-2 pi i (n/Q) theta_k)`

is exact. The normalizer `sqrt(Q/N)` in the lift is necessary and correct because `U_Q` and `U_N` are both unitary-normalized.

For `z=pi s` and `y=2 theta`, Jacobi–Anger gives

`exp(-i z y) = J_0(z) + 2 sum_{r>=1} (-i)^r J_r(z) T_r(y)`.

This follows directly by setting `y=cos(phi)` in the classical cosine/sine expansions, including at the endpoints by continuity. [NIST DLMF 10.12](https://dlmf.nist.gov/10.12)

For completeness, the defining series gives

`|J_r(z)| <= (z/2)^r/r! sum_{ell>=0} (z^2/4)^ell/[ell! (r+1)^ell]`

and thus the source bound `|J_r(z)| <= (z/2)^r/r! exp(z^2/[4(r+1)])` for the nonnegative real arguments used here. For `r>=R`, replace the exponential by its value at `R`; the successive factorial-tail ratios are at most `b/(R+1)`, `b=pi/2`. This proves exactly

`tau_R = 2 exp(pi^2/[4(R+1)]) b^R/R! / (1-b/(R+1))`.

It applies for integer `R>=1`. Consequently `|p_R|<=1+tau_R`, and Cauchy–Schwarz gives `||Xbar-U_N x||_infinity<=tau_R ||x||_2`. No input sparsity is used.

Stirling yields `log(tau_R)=-R log R+O(R)`. The least rank certified by this particular bound for `tau_R<=N^-alpha` is `(alpha+o(1)) log N/log log N`. This is a sufficient-rank result and an asymptotic statement about that certificate, not a lower bound on every possible separable approximation.

## 3. Shared-state covariance: the central claim survives

For each target `k`, set `D_k=diag(p_R(n/Q,theta_k))` on the first `N` coordinates and zero-pad. Reusing a single state across all rank terms gives exactly

`Xhat_k = sqrt(Q/N) [U_Q P_omega D_k x]_{j_k}`.

This is just linearity on the **input** coordinate. The fact that `D_k` varies with the target does not obstruct applying the fixed-input v9 covariance theorem separately at each target. The inputs are deterministic and independent of that row's random state.

With `K=Q/M`, the v9 error covariance at output zero is

`C_0[u,v]=(M delta[u=v]-1[u congruent v mod K])/Q`.

At output `j`, multiply by `exp(2 pi i j(u-v)/Q)`. Thus the lifted error covariance on the original input is exactly

`C_lift,k = (Q/N) D_k^* C_j D_k`,

where the covariance is restricted to the first `N` coordinates. Its operator norm is at most `(M/N)(1+tau_R)^2`. The denominator is correctly the original `N`. This proves the claimed per-output variance bound for every fixed input and unbiasedness around `Xbar`.

Terminology: the actual block operator in the source satisfies `P_omega^2=M P_omega`, not `P_omega^2=P_omega`. It is a scaled block projector. The covariance itself is `(M/Q)` times an orthogonal projector for `M>=2`. For `M=1`, the covariance is zero; the displayed upper bound remains valid.

No independence among the target coordinates or among expansion terms is required. Independent **rows** are required for the majority tail. The result is simultaneous in output coordinates for each fixed input, not one successful random operator guaranteed for every possible input at once.

## 4. Majority probability and the two regimes

The inherited three-grid argument applies to each completed arbitrary-length row, with `sigma=(1+tau_R)sqrt(M/N)||x||_2`. Chebyshev bounds each scalar bad-row probability by `g^-2`. A union over the `2N` real/imaginary coordinates gives the stated KL bound for `g>sqrt(2)` and the elementary fixed-odd-row bound

`failure <= 2N binom(2s-1,s) g^(-2s)`.

Combining its complex radius `(5/sqrt(2))g sigma` with deterministic truncation proves source equation (6.3).

For fixed epsilon, the proposed `g=epsilon sqrt(N/M)/[5sqrt(2)(1+tau_R)]` gives statistical radius `epsilon ||x||_2/2`; with `tau_R<=epsilon/2` the total is at most `epsilon ||x||_2`. The failure constant **15000** is arithmetically correct. The required `g>sqrt(2)` holds only for sufficiently large `N`; this is an asymptotic statement.

For shrinking error, `s>1+a` gives positive `alpha=1/2-(1+a)/(2s)`. The selected `g` and rank produce the claimed `sqrt(log N) N^-alpha` rate and constant row count, subject to the computation-model qualification below.

Small confidence correction: at `a=0`, writing `delta=N^-a` makes `delta=1` and the literal guarantee `1-N^-a=0` vacuous. The source's example of a nontrivial constant success probability needs `delta=c N^-a` for a fixed `0<c<1`; this changes only the constant in `g`. The source helper `fixed_row_g` itself rejects `delta=1`. This is an easily corrected endpoint statement, not an exponent failure.

## 5. What is and is not fully charged

### Conditional arithmetic count

Once each weighted input and each target factor is available in `O(N)` work per rank term, one v9 dyadic row costs `O(Q+(Q/M)log(Q/M))=O(Q)` with `M=Theta(log Q)`. Gathering, multiplying and adding final outputs is `O(N)`. Therefore `O(RN)` per lifted row and constant-row totals are correct under an explicit coefficient-generation contract.

The source does not supply that contract. `prototype.py` lines 204–209 invoke `jv(r,pi*s[:N])` for every rank. SciPy documents this as a special-function implementation using AMOS, rather than a constant-cost algebraic operation. No complexity or certified error bound for those calls appears in the bundle. [SciPy jv documentation](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.special.jv.html)

An exact `J_0,J_1` seed oracle followed by recurrence is an extra nonalgebraic function-evaluation primitive, not a root/coefficient construction in the existing algebraic model. This statement concerns the allowed primitives; it does not assert an unproved transcendence classification for every individual sampled Bessel value.

The work bounds may therefore be reported as exact-coefficient/special-function-oracle arithmetic theorems, or as consequences conditional on a proved bounded-work coefficient generator. They should not be reported as an already closed, fully charged uniform implementation theorem. The fixed-error regime may admit a straightforward bounded-accuracy construction; no replacement construction is proved in this audit.

Dyadic root preparation, short-FFT normalization, exact modular phase arithmetic, norm evaluation, random-state generation, quantization/equality, output writes and growing precision retain the inherited v9 qualifications. Word counts are not bit counts. Fresh dense input and explicit all-output requirements are met only within those qualifications.

### Prototype space differs from the theorem schedule

`_chebyshev_values`, lines 138–147, returns a list of **all R length-N arrays**. Every lifted Bessel row retains it at line 201. Accordingly, the prototype has `Theta(RN)` auxiliary floating-point storage, plus `O(N)` row/majority workspace. With polynomially shrinking error this is not `O(N)` independent of rank.

Keeping only the two previous Chebyshev vectors would implement the stated `O(N)` Chebyshev schedule. A complete `O(N)` algorithm must also supply a coefficient generator with the appropriate workspace; this report does not silently equate the proposed schedule with the current code.

The prototype also calls `np.unique(indices)` for an injectivity assertion. That uses a general uniqueness/sorting path whose cost is not included in the linear map count; it is avoidable after the exact injectivity proof. The saved work proxy is a formal formula, not a timed or complete operation census.

## 6. Finite precision and exact-index failures

Bounded Chebyshev factors and decaying Bessel weights are helpful design features. They do not constitute an end-to-end numerical theorem. Required additional terms include coefficient-generation error, exact residual formation or its certified perturbation, Chebyshev evaluation, weighted inputs, all v9 dyadic row errors, cancellation/rounding in the rank sum, norm/radius error, and exact/certified grid classification. Overflow, underflow and integer range also need a contract.

The previous independent dyadic theorem uses a specified implementation and safe constant 800 with its assumptions. The retained source default constant 64 is still uncertified. Merely composing calls to that prototype does not certify the new lift.

### Forward Bessel order recurrence is not a safe omitted step

The identity `J_(r+1)(z)=(2r/z)J_r(z)-J_(r-1)(z)` is exact. For orders larger than the argument, its forward numerical direction is unstable for `J`; backward recurrence is the appropriate direction according to NIST. The `z=0` point additionally needs separate treatment. [NIST DLMF 10.6](https://dlmf.nist.gov/10.6), [NIST DLMF 10.74(iv)](https://dlmf.nist.gov/10.74#iv)

An independent binary64 experiment, seeded by correctly rounded 90-digit defining-series values, gives at `z=pi/2^20`:

- Forward-computed `J_12`: approximately `-3.6981e49`.
- Reference `J_12`: approximately `2.6663e-79`.

This is a counterexample to naive forward recurrence as a numerical repair. It is **not** a claim that the uploaded SciPy `jv` implementation uses that recurrence or produced this error.

### Float grid construction

The prototype constructs `k` and `k*(Q/N)` in binary64, rounds, and obtains the residual by subtraction. Its small tests do not certify exact mapping for growing length.

The independent scalar reconstruction reproduces these concrete failures without allocating giant arrays:

- `N=2^28-1`, `Q=2^28`, `k=(N-1)/2`: exact nearest `j=134217727`, floating nearest `j=134217728`. The two residuals differ by nearly one, but the grid-index and residual shifts compensate: the **combined frequency defect** is only approximately `1.86e-9`. It would be misleading to claim an order-one kernel error from this case.
- `N=2^53-1`, `k=(N+1)/2`: the scalar float code returns `theta=-1`, outside the required interval.
- `N=2^60-1`, the two adjacent central exact frequencies map to the same floating grid index. The injectivity claim fails in finite precision.

Exact integer quotient/remainder formation avoids these particular issues; its bit cost must still be counted. None of these enormous formal-size checks is a runtime benchmark.

The prototype rejects `Q=1`, hence does not handle its advertised `N=1` case, including the zero input because block selection occurs first. A direct one-coordinate case fixes this. This does not affect the large-N theorem.

`independent_results.json` records the full details and environment:

- Exact rational map checks for every `1<=N<=1000`.
- 90-digit defining-series kernel checks at seven ranks, 2 through 30, including both endpoints. Largest observed tail-bound ratio: `0.8115701855`.

- Enumeration of every dyadic random state at `N=3,7,10,15,17,31`, with `Q<=32`.
- Full operator means, shared-state identity, and every lifted covariance matrix checked, rather than one sample input's variance alone.
- Largest covariance formula discrepancy: `1.1860891893e-15`.
- Largest covariance spectral norm divided by the stated bound: `0.9948142522`.
- Deterministic demonstrations of the floating grid-map and naive forward-recurrence failures.

These are finite numerical controls. The analytical derivations above are what establish the general identities and inequalities. No numerical sweep proves arbitrary-length numerical stability or optimal asymptotic rank.

The source's claims of 12 passing tests, 15 experiments of 1,200 random rows, and 20 robust examples are internally consistent with its saved files. Those source results test numerical correctness at small sizes; they do not prove full cost, finite-precision asymptotics, or practical speed superiority. No original source timing or source test execution was performed in this audit.

## 8. Final boundary and suggested wording

Safe summary:

> The v10 dyadic near-grid composition proves the advertised arbitrary-length randomized energy-relative error bounds in the exact-coefficient model. Its `O(RN)` arithmetic and `O(N)` streaming-space claims require an explicit coefficient-generation and implementation contract. The uploaded prototype instead stores `O(RN)` Chebyshev data, uses uncertified floating grid construction, and leaves the special-function and end-to-end precision work unproved.

The composition is a useful all-length approximate bridge. It neither solves nor excludes the original exact all-output, arbitrary-input, fully charged sub-`N log N` FFT target. The classical near-grid/Jacobi–Anger component does not become a novelty claim through this audit, and the exact composition's publication priority has not been established.
