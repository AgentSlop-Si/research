## Exact rule and usable graph

Let q be a primitive Nth root, N a power of two, and

    f_j = product_(a=1..j)(1-q^a),  H_q(z)=sum_(j=0..N-1) z^j/f_j.

Let A be the lower nilpotent shift, Q=diag(q^j), and T=H_q(A).
The actual middle M=diag(d)R, where d_j=(-1)^j q^(j(j-1)/2)/f_j and R reverses
coordinates, turns A into the weighted upper shift

    B=M A M^-1,    B[i,i+1]=q-q^(-i).

The proved finite identity is

    T B T^-1 = C = B+Q^-1-(q/N)A^(N-1).

Thus T p(B) T^-1=p(C) for every polynomial p. Applying C requires exactly the
charged 2N multiplications and N additions, plus linear preparation. For a fixed
degree d, the implemented p(C) uses 3Nd+N multiplications and 2Nd additions.
This gives a legitimate O(N) replacement when that conjugated subgraph actually
occurs. Adding expensive inverse pairs to a graph solely to cancel them does
not create a saving for the original graph.

The cache demonstration uses the directly equivalent rule T(Bx)=C(Tx). It
computes T x_0 once, then explicitly produces x_j=B x_(j-1) and
y_j=C y_(j-1)=T x_j. It charges both input and output updates and retains all
outputs. An ordinary convolution backend for the first T would give
O(N log N+rN) work for r related updates, subject to compatible supplied roots.
The included executable uses a fully charged quadratic direct T as its oracle;
its operation counts are not a comparison with an optimized FFT.

## Why this does not yet beat FFT

With P=diag(f_j), the correctly ordered Fourier factorization becomes

    F_N = P H_q(C) H_q(A) diag(d) P.

The full H_q(C) has degree N-1. Our honest Horner evaluator costs
3N^2-2N multiplications and 2N^2-2N additions. Cheap application of C does not
make a degree-N polynomial of C cheap.

Two tempting shortcuts are explicitly rejected:

- Dropping the corner changes C^N=0 into (B+Q^-1)^N=I.
- Writing C=U+W with UW=qWU, U^N=I and W^N=-I does not authorize
  H_q(C)=H_q(W)H_q(U). The product has extra terms of total degree at least N;
  at N=2 it already has a nonzero exact error on the first basis vector.

The actual middle also does not collapse to a quadratic chirp. Its successive
diagonal ratios are -q^(-j)(1-q^(j+1)); for N>=4, geometric ratios would require
(1-q^2)^2=(1-q)(1-q^3), but the difference is q(1-q)^2, which is nonzero.
This rules out that specific chirp-word substitution, not general fast circuits.

## Conditioned-macro integration

The source's local prefix bound must include untouched coordinates:

    kappa(P direct-sum I)=max(1,||P||_2) max(1,||P^-1||_2).

It equals kappa(P) when determinant-modulus-one prefixes make the singular
values straddle 1. A scale P=100I gives an exact counterexample otherwise.
The three-addition exchange also requires source and target roles to alternate
between macro-levels. Both issues have exact rational witnesses here.

For the canonical complex root q=exp(2 pi i/N), m=floor(N/8), the actual middle
obeys the proved lower bound

    kappa_2(M) >= N (14/11)^m,       N>=8.

The review gives a stronger rational bound as well. This concerns the explicit
middle-factor interface, not all Fourier algorithms. Finite-field correctness
tests say nothing about complex condition numbers. The macro construction still
needs an independent fast local block; it does not supply that missing block.

The official #130 scope separately states a root-supplied exact-complex uniform
power-saving result, with unrestricted coefficients:
https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/lean/docs/130.md.
That result is a comparator, not an imported proof for this independent route.
Conditioned lower bounds likewise remain model-specific; see
https://arxiv.org/abs/1403.1307.
