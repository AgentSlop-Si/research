# An exact exceptional three-Green-mixer Fourier point at N=4

9 October 2026 UTC. Independent construction against the verified version-13 project baseline. This work executes only the newly authored checker beside this note; no uploaded or inherited program was executed, imported, or compiled.

## Result and scope

There is an explicit exact identity

    W4 = A D B D C

in which A, B, C are entrywise-nonzero, pairwise-noncommuting matrices, each a sum of two diagonally gauged Hamiltonian-cycle resolvents. Every coefficient is an explicitly specified algebraic number obtained from rational numbers and square roots. The matrix equality, both full Weyl identities, normalization, and nonzero denominator guards have exact characteristic-zero checks.

This is a positive finite-size point in the tunable-outer family. It does not contradict earlier all-coupling exclusions, which fixed different outer mixers. Its cycle orders are affine; its diagonal gauges are nonconstant. It does not establish feasibility for arbitrary non-affine orders or a sub-N-log-N algorithm. In fact, the particular reciprocal outer-factor construction used here cannot extend with two poles per outside factor to N>=5. That restricted obstruction is proved below.

The simpler central identity uses scalar-weighted Green terms. It is not automatically a solution of the stricter, unweighted model L[(I-D_a P_a)^-1+(I-D_b P_b)^-1]R, whose denominators have unit diagonal. The three-mixer Phase 28 grammar does allow the scalar term weights used here.

## Conventions

Let q=exp(pi*i/4), omega=q^2=i, Z e_k=e_(k+1 mod 4), Omega=diag(1,i,-1,-i), and W4[j,k]=i^(jk). Put

    D=diag(q^(j^2))_(j=0)^3.

The convention uses the positive-exponent Fourier matrix. Complex-conjugating every coefficient gives the negative-exponent convention. Dividing one outside diagonal by 2 gives normalized F4. The exact Weyl certificate is

    W4 Z = Omega W4,
    W4 Omega = Z^-1 W4,
    W4[0,0]=1.

Write G_a(T)=(I-a T)^-1 whenever a^4 != 1 and T^4=I.

## 1. Central two-resolvent identity

Set t=q^-1=(1-i)/sqrt(2). The cyclic chirp kernel has coefficient polynomial

    g(z)=1+t z-z^2+t z^3.

The chirp identity gives W4=D g(Z) D. Direct multiplication yields

    g(z) Q(z) = P(z) mod (z^4-1),
    Q(z)=1+sqrt(2) z+i z^2,
    P(z)=2-2i+2sqrt(2) z.

The relevant guards are

    Res(Q,z^4-1)=8i != 0,
    discriminant(Q)=2-4i != 0,
    Res(Q,P)=8+8i != 0.

Thus Q has two distinct nonzero reciprocal poles, no root on the fourth roots of unity, and no canceled numerator factor. Let

    delta=sqrt(2-4i),
    alpha=(-sqrt(2)+delta)/2,
    beta =(-sqrt(2)-delta)/2,
    u=2-2i, v=2sqrt(2),
    a=(v+u alpha)/(alpha-beta),
    b=(-v-u beta)/(alpha-beta).

Then Q=(1-alpha z)(1-beta z), and the elementary partial-fraction identity gives

    B := g(Z) = a G_alpha(Z) + b G_beta(Z).

Either square-root choice merely exchanges the two summands. All poles, both residues, and both cycle denominators are nonzero. For a convenient exact field basis, take 1,q,q^2,q^3,delta,q delta,q^2 delta,q^3 delta with q^4=-1 and delta^2=2-4q^2. In this basis the residue expressions also simplify to

    a=1-q^2+(q+2q^3)delta/5,
    b=1-q^2-(q+2q^3)delta/5.

The checker verifies all 16 entries and all anchored projective cross ratios, not sampled vectors.

## 2. Explicit dense noncommuting outside mixers

Define the weighted Hamiltonian cycles

    U=D Z D^-1=q Z Omega,
    V=D^-1 Z D=q Omega^-1 Z.

Both have fourth power I. Their edge weights are nonzero, so each is diagonally conjugate to the natural Hamiltonian cycle Z. Fourier intertwining gives U W4=W4 V.

Use

    r(z)=1/(1-2z)+1/(1-3z)
        =(2-5z)/(1-5z+6z^2).

Its residue-class polynomial is

    r(z)=(-19-41z-91z^2-209z^3)/240 mod (z^4-1).

Its reciprocal on the same four spectral points is

    s(z)=(317-730z+2z^2+5z^3)/609 mod (z^4-1).

The latter also has a two-resolvent representation, since

    s(z)=P1(z)/Q1(z) mod (z^4-1),
    P1(z)=(455-1045z)/874,
    Q1(z)=1+(5/874)z+(3/437)z^2.

The poles are explicitly

    gamma=(-5+sqrt(-20951))/1748,
    eta  =(-5-sqrt(-20951))/1748.

Set u1=455/874, v1=-1045/874 and

    c=(v1+u1 gamma)/(gamma-eta),
    d=(-v1-u1 eta)/(gamma-eta).

Then s(V)=c G_gamma(V)+d G_eta(V). The poles are distinct and nonzero. Q1 is invertible on the fourth roots of unity; for example

    Res(Q1,z^4-1)=583452069375/583506543376 != 0.

The polynomial representation of s and exact modular identity certify r(Z)s(Z)=I. Since r(Z) has nonzero eigenvalues and Q1 is invertible, the displayed C is invertible. No free inverse oracle is used by the evaluator: C is explicitly the two-resolvent sum above.

Now set

    A=r(U)=G_2(U)+G_3(U),
    C=s(V)=c G_gamma(V)+d G_eta(V).

The central B is from Section 1. Then

    A D B D C
      = D r(Z) B s(Z) D
      = D B D
      = W4,

because B, r(Z), and s(Z) are functions of the same Z in the conjugated coordinates, and r(Z)s(Z)=I. Equivalently, A W4 C=W4 follows directly from U W4=W4 V.

This cancellation is the reason the construction is easy. It is an exact exceptional control, not evidence that a generic three-mixer product has Fourier phases.

Every entry of A, B, C is nonzero: the coefficient lists for r, g, s have no zero entries, and conjugating by nonzero diagonals preserves that property. The independent exact checker additionally finds 16 nonzero entries in [A,B], 16 in [B,C], and 8 in [A,C]. Thus pairwise noncommutativity holds in the original coordinate system despite the masked-coordinate cancellation.

## 3. A restricted reciprocal-closure obstruction

Let T have exact order N with minimal polynomial z^N-1, over any characteristic-zero field containing its roots. Suppose

    r(z)=P(z)/Q(z), s(z)=P'(z)/Q'(z),
    deg P,deg P' <= 1,
    Q=(1-alpha z)(1-beta z),
    Q'=(1-gamma z)(1-eta z),

where alpha,beta,gamma,eta are nonzero, and Q(T),Q'(T) are invertible. Scalar-weighted sums of two finite-pole resolvents have this form, including cancellations or vanishing residues. If r(T)s(T)=I, then

    z^N-1 divides PP'-QQ'.

The latter polynomial has degree exactly 4: its quartic coefficient is -alpha beta gamma eta != 0, while PP' has degree at most 2. For N>=5 a nonzero degree-4 polynomial cannot be divisible by z^N-1. Therefore reciprocal closure is impossible.

More generally, proper rational functions formed from p and q nonzero-pole resolvents have numerator degrees at most p-1 and q-1, and denominator degrees p,q. Exact reciprocal closure on an order-N cycle requires p+q>=N. This is a representation bound for this reciprocal same-operator mechanism. It is not a bound on arbitrary three-mixer products, independently ordered cycles, or general shared circuits.

The N=4 construction sits exactly at p+q=N, where quartic wraparound makes reciprocal closure possible. It supplies no growing-N path with two poles at either outside stage. The central chirp's separate q>=N/2 common-cycle term obstruction is inherited from the verified master, not a new theorem here.

## 4. Fully charged application and coefficient ledger

For supplied alpha with alpha^N!=1, the standard cyclic recurrence computes G_alpha(Z)x in 2N-1 scalar multiplications and 2N-2 scalar additions after storing (1-alpha^N)^-1: a Horner pass, one boundary multiplication, and a second recurrence pass. This is an implementation upper count, not an optimality claim.

A generic two-term weighted sum uses two such solves, two scalar term multiplications per coordinate, and one coordinatewise addition. That gives 6N-2 multiplications and 5N-4 additions. A left and right diagonal gauge adds 2N multiplications. Permutation reads/writes, indexing, and outputs are O(N) data operations and O(N) workspace, not free.

The central W4=D B D route therefore has one direct 8N-2 multiplication, 5N-4 addition schedule at N=4, after its fixed coefficients are supplied. A deliberately unoptimized implementation of three independently gauged mixers plus the two D masks has at most 26N-6 multiplications and 15N-12 additions, before field representation costs. Adjacent gauges and term coefficients can be combined; none of these constants claims practical advantage over the ordinary F4 butterfly.

All fixed N=4 coefficients are specified by rational constants, q, and two square roots. Their exact scalar arithmetic lives in a fixed-degree algebraic extension; the proof and checker do not treat an arbitrary growing-degree algebraic number as unit-cost. Specifying fixed radicals is a coefficient construction, not a hidden precomputed dense Fourier matrix. In a bit model, exact rational arithmetic and representation of arbitrary input values must also be charged.

For growing N, factoring large annihilator polynomials or solving dense fitting systems is not free. The reciprocal lower bound requires p+q>=N and thus prevents a constant-term continuation in this particular construction. Evaluating those terms independently has a quadratic-size upper schedule; the term bound alone is not an unrestricted scalar-circuit lower bound and does not preclude a separately supplied shared evaluator.

## 5. Next concrete research question

The useful next target is a product of three sums whose outside mixers are not forced to be reciprocal Fourier conjugates, with at least one genuinely non-affine cycle. The identity above serves as a positive control for exact projective/Weyl recognition and for testing parameter-elimination code; it should not serve as an asymptotic candidate.

A bounded next problem is to tune both outer couplings in a specified N=8 three-mixer layout while allowing the internal masks to vary, and either produce exact admissible coefficients or obtain a saturated all-parameter infeasibility certificate. Fixed-outer Phase 28/29 certificates do not decide that enlarged problem. Finite-field passes without lifting and least-squares fits remain search evidence only. A successful N=8 point would still require a uniform growing-length formula and a fully charged total ledger.
