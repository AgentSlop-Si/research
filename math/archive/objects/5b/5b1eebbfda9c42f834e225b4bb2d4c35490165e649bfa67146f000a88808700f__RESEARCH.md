# HERO #17 — Phase 18: Magnetic Green–Union and Weyl-Line Audit

**Date:** 10 October 2026  
**Status:** mathematically derived exact resolvent fusion, tested implementations, counted arithmetic, and three restricted Fourier obstructions. **Not a faster general FFT; no historical novelty or publication claim.**

## Research question

Phase 17 proved that a single magnetic Green inverse plus low-rank feedback cannot be repaired into Fourier in a specified sparse-graph quotient model. The surviving path was genuinely branching, noncommuting global work sharing. Can a growing number `K` of *different noncommuting* magnetic Green branches be evaluated jointly with `O(N)` work for fresh arbitrary vectors, with every scalar operation counted? If yes, does the construction equal the DFT or does a new obstruction appear?

## Headline result

For `N=K*M`, with `S` cyclic shift, `U` modulation by `q=omega^M` and `US=qSU`, one obtains

`(1/K) sum_(j=0)^(K-1) (I-alpha S-beta q^j U)^-1`

`= ((1-beta^K)I-alpha^K S^K)^-1 sum_(r=0)^(K-1) alpha^r S^r`.

Every inverse exists exactly when `(1-beta^K)^M != alpha^N`. The complete action of the right-hand side on an arbitrary fresh input costs `O(N)` scalar operations *uniformly even for growing K*, plus `O(N)` storage. The branches are generically pairwise noncommuting.

The proof uses the classical root-of-unity quantum-binomial theorem plus a roots-of-unity character filter. The algorithm uses sliding geometric windows and disjoint cyclic recurrences. See `PROOF_CERTIFICATE.md` for full statements and proofs.

## New extensions

- **Exact spectral rank:** the averaged operator is invertible if `alpha^N !=1`, and has rank `N-K+1` if `alpha^N=1`, provided its denominator is invertible. Thus at resonance the average kills exactly `K-1` Fourier modes.
- **Weighted phase sectors:** `R_ell=(1/K) sum_j q^(-j ell) G_j`. Gaussian-binomial polynomial expansion yields an exact `O((ell+1)N+ell^2+K)` executor for fixed `ell`; no dense table and no FFT.
- **Spectral subgroup gate:** every weighted sector commutes with `S^K`. For `K<N`, even their arbitrary linear combination cannot be Fourier; the exact commutator has rank `N-K` or `N` according to the parity of `N-K`. An additive repair requires growing rank when `K` is not too close to `N`.
- **All-direction Weyl support gate:** at odd prime `N`, the Fourier operator has nonzero Weyl coefficients everywhere. Sums of unshifted one-direction resolvent-type operators require at least `N+1` line directions for support coverage; with left/right Weyl monomial gauges, at least `N` affine lines. This is *not* an unrestricted circuit lower bound: products of two Green stages can already have full Weyl support and remain linear-work, without being Fourier.

## Exact independently checked experiment

Over the prime field `F_65537`, `N=65,536`, `K=64`, `alpha=2`, `beta=3`, a random dense input was evaluated two distinct ways:

1. the new collapsed executor (finite window and one strided Green solve);
2. **all 64 original noncommuting Green branches**, solved independently via first-order recurrences and combined, sharing only diagonal setup across branches.

All **65,536** output entries matched exactly.

| Measurement | Fused evaluator | Independent 64-branch reference |
|---|---:|---:|
| Setup and complete execution, counted prime-field scalar operations | **655,693** | **25,231,832** |
| Relative operation count | 1.0x | 38.48x |
| Output entries checked | 65,536 | 65,536 |

The fused executor additionally passed 11 independent selected residual equations. The fused output differed from the **actual DFT** on all 11 independently checked frequency bins; this confirms no accidental claim of full Fourier equality.

Both sides include scalar preparation as implemented and all online scalar arithmetic; reference preparation shares the periodic diagonal coefficients. The field-operation count excludes integer-indexing, array loads/writes, finding the supplied root, and bit complexity. Indexing and array operations are bounded by `O(N)` and explicitly included in the overall RAM complexity. Actual elapsed times appear in `AUDIT_RESULTS.json` but are not asserted as portable benchmarks.

Small exact tests independently reconstructed magnetic Green inverses with modular Gaussian elimination, including zero-diagonal-pivot cases that a naive direct cyclic recurrence cannot solve. There were **121 complete Gaussian-reference cases (2,055 output entries)** and **158 Fourier-weighted Gaussian-reference cases** in the independent audit. All tests use exact modular arithmetic.

The ingredients are not fundamentally new: root-of-unity q-binomial identities, finite Weyl representations, cyclic recurrences, geometric-root filters, quadratic Gauss sums, and the two-dimensional finite Weyl algebra all have extensive prior literature. The particular synthesized proof/executor is a reproducible result of **this research branch**, not a defensible claim of historical priority without a dedicated literature review.

## Explicit failed shortcut

The total phase average `R_0` commutes with `S` and is a circulant. The Fourier matrix does not. Even forming many such averages and adding rank-`o(N)` correction cannot equal Fourier for growing `N`, by Theorem 6. All weighted sectors for fixed `K<N` commute with `S^K`, which also rules out equality even when **all** `K` sectors are summed.

For prime `N`, rotating each single-direction operator through *every* Weyl line does not solve the support budget in fewer than `N+1` unshifted branches (or `N` Weyl-gauged affine branches). Products can leave the line grammar, so this is a precise target rather than a blanket lower bound.

## What Phase 19 must do

Search for a **joint executor of noncommuting products in distinct Weyl directions** whose global operator passes

`M S=T^-1 M` and `M e_0=ones`

for all dimensions and arbitrary inputs. Require a uniform cost recurrence, charge preparation and output writes, test basis-vector equality to exact DFT, and reject representations that are cheap only because they hide an FFT basis conversion. Any new search should use the Phase 18 twirl as a positive control and the Weyl-line support/covariance tests as restricted rejection screens.

**Current verdict:** a real growing-branch `O(N)` fusion for a non-DFT operator, not a faster general DFT or integer multiplication algorithm.
