## Verdict

The new exact-arithmetic projection, energy, Gram-union, stationary Toeplitz, and collision identities are valid in their stated scope. The stationary `q=M` construction is an exact arbitrary-input Fourier factorization. Its actual evaluator remains FFT-scale. The useful bounded-q result is a transform on a known proper subspace, accompanied by an exact-arithmetic, input-dependent full-vector error identity outside that subspace. Neither result improves the unrestricted exact FFT or integer multiplication.

Two independently proved refinements strengthen the source:

1. Every fixed proper orthogonal projection has worst-case relative full-vector error exactly one. The source's `sqrt(1-q/M)` leverage bound is sharp over coordinate impulses in an orthogonal bank, not the minimax error over all inputs.
2. The stationary frame has an explicit uniform conditioning bound in the proposed large-K regime. For `1<=q<=M`, define

   `delta_q = exp(pi (M-1)(q-1)/(2 K M)) - 1`.

   All singular values of `W/sqrt(M)` lie between `1-delta_q` and `1+delta_q`. When `delta_q<1`,

   `kappa_2(Gamma) <= ((1+delta_q)/(1-delta_q))^2`.

   In particular `q=M, K>=4M` has bounded conditioning, and `M=Theta(log G), K=G/M` gives `kappa_2(Gamma)=1+O(M/K)`. This concerns the exact local frame, not the rounding error of the complete scan-based implementation.

The next gate must encompass coefficient formation, the bank of short FFTs, and scan fusion. Eliminating only the M long scans leaves the other FFT-scale work intact.

## 2. Exact projection and decoder audit

Write `N=Gm`, where G is dyadic and m odd, `G=KM`, `K>=M`, and M is dyadic. The antiperiodic scan derivation in this section assumes `G>=2`; the case `G=1` uses the direct odd-transform fallback. The source uses normalized negative-sign Fourier. For a unit a modulo G, set

`S_(a,beta)[r,u] = zeta^(a u(u-1)/2 + (a M r+beta)u)`.

For selected local chirp columns `V=[conj(S_i)]`, full column rank over C makes `Gamma=V*V` positive definite. With `b=V*x` and `c=Gamma^-1 b`, the matrix `P=V Gamma^-1 V*` is exactly the orthogonal projector onto those columns. Consequently

`||F_N x - F_N P x||^2 = ||x||^2 - sum_blocks b* Gamma^-1 b`.

The direct-sum rank is `K sum_s q_s`. This is a complete-vector exact-arithmetic identity, not a finite-precision enclosure or a uniform small-error guarantee. On its range the transform is exact even for physically dense vectors. For a fixed bounded frame, membership can be recognized in the source's exact-arithmetic/equality model by a zero residual at the same asymptotic work. Arbitrarily near-subspace inputs admit no finite-precision exact membership decision without a separation promise.

The inherited scan identity can be checked independently. For a coefficient sequence c and its unnormalized K-point DFT D, define

`f(k) = D[k mod K] - zeta^(-M(k+beta)-a M(M-1)/2) D[(k+aM) mod K]`.

The Fourier output of the chirp-block signal satisfies

`y(k+a) = zeta^(k+beta) (y(k)-f(k))`.

Along `k=aj`, the gauge is `p_j=zeta^(a j(j-1)/2+beta j)`. Since G is even and a odd, `p_G=-1`. Thus the cyclic seed is half the total sum of `f(aj)/p_j`, and one prefix scan determines all outputs. The source's normalization by `sqrt(G)` and final normalized odd transform produce normalized `F_N`. This is an algebraic derivation; a successful modular check alone would not prove the all-size identity.

Orthogonal beta banks use offsets `beta+tK`, yielding local M-point characters and an orthogonal decomposition of the identity. Captured energies add exactly. Sorting the bank-column energies and retaining the largest L is optimal among subsets of exactly L bank-column pairs. This is not optimality among arbitrary chirp frames, rank-L algorithms, or all nonlinear decoders.

An input with equal-amplitude impulses at u=0 in every block has equal energy in every bank. Any adaptive bank selection attaining relative error epsilon must retain at least `(1-epsilon^2)mM` bank-column pairs. This obstruction is correctly scoped to that bank selection architecture.

## 3. Stronger fixed-frame gate

For any proper orthogonal projector P, take a nonzero h in its kernel. Then

`F_N P h=0`, and `||F_N h-F_N P h||/||h||=1`.

Conversely `||I-P||_2<=1`, so the worst-case relative error is exactly one. Any single deficient local block suffices. Therefore a fixed projection with q<M cannot guarantee any uniform relative full-vector error epsilon<1, even when q=M-1.

The source's leverage argument remains correct: `trace(P)=q` implies some coordinate impulse retains at most q/M energy. It is useful because it supplies a coordinate-sparse witness. Its claim of sharpness must be read as sharpness of this coordinate-impulse bound. Adaptive state choice after independent access to x is outside the fixed-frame theorem.

The source's zero-transcript argument also holds: r adaptive linear sketches of rank at most N/M, with future choices determined only by previous observations, cannot recover every x when r<M. Following the zero transcript leaves a nonzero common kernel. Direct extra input reads and randomized quantifiers require separate arguments.

## 4. Stationary full-rank chart

For `a_i=1+iK` and `beta_(s,i)=3s+i(K+1)`, i=0,...,q-1, each a_i is odd. Modulo G, the extra block term `i K M r u` vanishes. Thus

`V_(s,r)=D_(s,r) W`,

`W[u,i]=zeta^(-i E(u))`, `E(u)=K u(u+1)/2+u`.

Every D is unitary diagonal. Therefore `Gamma=W*W` is block-independent and Hermitian Toeplitz. Since `E(u) mod K=u`, all M nodes are distinct, proving the first q columns independent, and the square q=M frame invertible. The local projection is then I, so the assembled evaluator equals Fourier on every input.

The full frame cannot have all commuting rank-one column projectors. Independent commuting rank-one projectors would have orthogonal columns. In the square case this also makes the rows orthogonal. A row inner product is a length-M geometric sum, so every node ratio must be a nontrivial Mth root. That would force every E(u)-E(v) divisible by K, contradicting u-v modulo K. The noncommutation is genuine but supplies no complexity saving by itself.

At clock j, scan destinations `(1+iK)j mod G` have `M/gcd(j,M)` distinct values. Summing dyadic residue valuations gives exactly `K(2M^2+1)/3` distinct (clock,destination) pairs, versus `KM^2` raw visits. The asymptotic 2/3 ratio is only a same-clock collision count. Different streams arriving at the same location need not carry equal values. The count does not bound cross-clock arithmetic reuse or unrestricted circuits.

## 5. Independent conditioning theorem

Let `T(u)=u(u+1)/2 mod M`. For dyadic M, T permutes 0,...,M-1. Indeed, if T(u)=T(v), then `2M` divides `(u-v)(u+v+1)`. The two factors have opposite parity. For distinct u,v in this range, the even factor is nonzero with absolute value strictly below 2M, so its 2-adic valuation is too small, a contradiction.

Consequently the M-by-q matrix

`H[u,i]=exp(-2 pi i T(u)i/M)`

has orthogonal columns of squared norm M. The stationary frame is

`W[u,i]=H[u,i] exp(-2 pi i u i/(KM))`.

Center the indices at `c_u=(M-1)/2` and `c_i=(q-1)/2`. Removing the resulting unit-modulus row, column, and global factors preserves singular values. With `U=diag(u-c_u)` and `I=diag(i-c_i)`, the centered matrix divided by sqrt(M) has the convergent expansion

`H/sqrt(M) + sum_(r>=1) [(-2 pi i/(KM))^r/r!] U^r (H/sqrt(M)) I^r`.

Because `||H/sqrt(M)||_2=1`, `||U||_2=c_u`, and `||I||_2=c_i`, its distance from an isometry is at most

`sum_(r>=1) [pi(M-1)(q-1)/(2KM)]^r/r! = delta_q`.

The singular-value bounds and Gram condition bound in the verdict follow. This also bounds every prefix q frame, not just q=M. For q=1 the bound gives delta=0 exactly. For the full frame K>=4M implies `delta<exp(pi/8)-1<1`; no such bound is claimed here for the entire K>=M boundary regime. Numeric experiments at K=M show visibly increasing Gram condition on some sizes, but do not establish its asymptotic growth.

This removes a local-conditioning uncertainty in the intended M≈log G regime. It does not control root generation, all intermediate states of a fused algorithm, or the complete scan's numerical error, and does not transfer a unitary-circuit lower bound to this nonunitary evaluator.

## 6. Complete arithmetic and memory accounting

All bounds below are scalar arithmetic bounds over a field with the required roots and normalization constants available; comparisons/equalities for adaptive selection are extra stated primitives. They are not bit bounds.

### Known orthogonal bank selection

For L selected bank-column pairs:

`O(N + L(G+K log(K+1)) + N log(m+1))`.

This includes reading the input, phase/compression work, a shared G-root table, one K-FFT and G-scan per selected stream, the final shared odd FFT layer, twiddle generation, energy accumulation, and N output writes. Fixed m, L=O(m), and M≈log G yield linear arithmetic. The input-independent state/mask promise matters.

### Adaptive orthogonal bank selection

Add `O(N log M)` for all local M-point transforms and energy measurements, plus `O(mM log(mM))` sorting. At a small selected L this is `O(N log log G)` for fixed m; it is not linear. The equal-energy adversary can force L≈mM. Root setup must remain counted when analysis and decoding are reported as one fresh-input operation.

### General nonstationary q_s Gram union

A conservative full inventory is

`O(N(1+log(m+1)) + G sum_s(q_s+q_s^2) + K sum_s(q_s log(K+1)+q_s^3))`.

The source's bounded-q/fixed-m linear-work claim is valid for M≈log G. Rank/invertibility is a mathematical precondition. If arbitrary selected families need numerical condition estimation, their SVD work is of the same cubic Gram order but the estimate does not certify invertibility or a rigorous error bound. The vectorized generic implementation stores K local q-by-q Gram matrices per processed column, so its storage for growing q includes O(Kq^2), not uniformly O(N). Bounded q does give O(N) storage.

### Stationary q-frame

Shared W/Gram setup: `O(Mq^2+q^3)`.

Per input: `O(Nq+(N/M)q^2)` for coefficient formation and reused solves, `O(qN)` scans, `O(q(N/M)log(K+1))` short FFTs, and `O(N(1+log(m+1)))` for the shared odd layer, roots/twiddles, input/output and accumulation.

At q=M, `M^3<=NM` from M^2<=G<=N, so total becomes

`O(NM + N log(K+1) + N log(m+1))`.

For M≈log G and fixed m this is FFT-scale. The implemented M scans perform Theta(NM) visits, the short-FFT bank alone is Theta(N log K) for literal radix FFTs, and dense coefficient/Gram work is Theta(NM). These are costs of this supplied implementation, not universal lower bounds. Sharing only scans leaves the latter two costs. A faster candidate must restructure the whole pipeline.

Processing channels and odd columns sequentially gives O(N) words of working storage for the stationary evaluator: roots/stage/output O(N), local coefficient arrays Kq<=G, and W/Gram arrays at most M^2<=G. Word size, exponent range, and N output encodings remain charged. There is no uniform all-integer restricted linear claim when m grows; G=1 falls back to an ordinary length-m FFT.

## 7. Precision and code findings

The exact residual identity can lose all significant digits when evaluated as `||x||^2-captured_energy`. Clamping a negative floating result to zero is a diagnostic convention, not a certified upper error bound. For exact full rank, the mathematical residual is zero even when the computed output has rounding error. A verified implementation should enclose the solve and directly accumulated residual/output error; a proof of local Gram conditioning alone does not do this.

Sequential one-root tables require a certified primitive root. Under an explicit per-complex-multiplication relative error bound nu and root error at most eta, a conservative j-step absolute table error is `((1+eta)(1+nu))^j-1`. Thus fixed target table accuracy can require O(log N) additional accuracy bits as the chain length grows. This bound only addresses a table; scan accumulation, short and odd FFTs, coefficient formation, normal equations, normalization, and output error require a complete contract. The earlier dyadic v9 numerical theorem cannot simply be quoted for the newly added normal equations and the different one-root generation path.

“One root” means one supplied algebraic/complex constant in the exact arithmetic model, not a free exact bit-level root generator. Any bit-complexity claim must specify how roots/normalizers are represented or approximated, and charge input/output bits, arithmetic precision, exponent range and indexing. The inherited code explicitly notes its int64 phase products are only for moderate test sizes; it is not an all-size exact-index implementation.

Static, bounded implementation discrepancies:

- `stationary_states` and `construction` validate G dyadic and K>=M but omit `G % M == 0` / M dyadic. A direct helper call with G=32, M=3, q=3 is accepted by those guards, yet K=floor(32/3)=10 and the omitted `i K M r u` term does not vanish. At i=r=u=1 it is 30 modulo32. The public `stationary_eval` path calls the inherited correct dimension validator, so this helper bug does not invalidate supported evaluator inputs or the theorem.
- `stationary_eval` calls `np.linalg.solve(Gamma,...)` separately for every odd column, so it does not literally retain one factorization globally; the repeated O(mq^3) work is absorbed by O(mKq^2) since q<=K. Its numerical condition estimate adds an O(q^3) SVD. The asymptotic result remains unchanged.
- `evaluate_mask` sets the root-table work field to zero while reporting an analyzed/adaptive operation that already constructed that table. The complete fresh-input accounting must add that O(G) setup back. Again, the asymptotic bound is unchanged.
- Source verification counts and timings remain source-reported. The uploaded test runner and finite-field scripts were inspected as data and were not executed.

## 8. Green forcing gate

If an exact solver satisfies `L y=B x` with invertible L and y=F_N x, then B=L F_N. A nonzero row supported on w coordinates has at least ceil(N/w) nonzero Fourier entries, by Parseval and the infinity-norm bound sqrt(w/N). Consecutive cyclic support improves this to N-w+1 via a degree-(w-1) polynomial evaluated at N distinct roots. For two nonzero coordinates separated by a, the zero count is either zero or gcd(a,N), yielding support at least N-gcd(a,N).

These are sound support obstructions. They do not make dense B expensive in general; a structured dense matrix can have a fast evaluator. The complex support proof is not being inferred from modular numerical tests. Source modular examples are sharpness controls, not a proof of a universal arithmetic lower bound.
