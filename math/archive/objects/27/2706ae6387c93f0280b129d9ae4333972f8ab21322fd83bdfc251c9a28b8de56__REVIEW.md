## Local determinant and geometry audit

A primitive-affine adjacent frequency triple, after legitimate nonzero row/column scalings and relabeling, gives two-vectors

    h(t)=(1-a t, t(1-b t))^T,

at all distinct nth roots. The common difference must be a unit modulo n; otherwise these roots repeat. Independent expansion confirms

    det(h(t),h(u))=(u-t)[1-b(t+u)+abtu].

For b=0 every distinct pair is independent. For b!=0, a dependent pair satisfies t+u-a tu=z=1/b. Two distinct unordered pairs cannot have the same product, since then they also have the same sum and would be the same quadratic root pair. Complex conjugation, using conjugate(t)=t^(-1), now forces a=conjugate(z) if there are two distinct dependent pairs. Consequently each pair lies on a chord line through z.

The two-by-three coefficient matrix mapping (1,t,t^2) to h(t) has rank two for every a,b. Three distinct parameter values form an invertible Vandermonde matrix, so three nonzero columns cannot be proportional. Thus distinct dependent pairs among nonzero columns have disjoint endpoints. Two such lines meet away from any original polygon vertex: a line through two circle points cannot contain a third circle point. Rigby's extended-line theorem therefore permits at most two pairs when n is odd.

Right multiplication by a weighted Hamiltonian cycle can jointly cancel a target two-vector only along a proportional pair of original columns. A Hamiltonian cycle of length at least three uses a given unordered pair at most once. Hence at most two output columns vanish jointly, proving union support at least n-2. If h(t0)=0, necessarily a=b=t0^(-1); all remaining columns are nonzero and pairwise independent. Only the zero target column can then vanish jointly, with zero incoming weight. The exceptional case is retained rather than divided away.

Nonzero monomial endpoint scales merely change effective weights; column permutations relabel the cycle. Zero edge weights remain allowed. This proof is about a Hamiltonian right cycle: an arbitrary permutation with a two-cycle could use both orientations of one unordered pair and needs a separate argument.

## Sharpness and global deductions

For every odd n>=5, the disjoint chords (0,2) and (1,3) are nonparallel because their endpoint products differ. Their intersection is not the origin, since an odd regular polygon has no antipodal vertex pair. The displayed a,z,b formulas are therefore guarded. A zero h-column would make the other nonzero columns independent and cannot coexist with the second disjoint dependent pair, so the proportionality weights are nonzero. Embed the two directed edges in a Hamiltonian cycle and zero its other weights. The first left cycle can also have a zero weight outside the selected pair. Both Green denominators then equal one, and exactly the two selected columns cancel. This proves local sharpness over C; it does not prove global density sharpness.

Summing local union bounds over the cyclic adjacent row pairs gives nnz(T)>=ceil(n(n-2)/2) when every triple is primitive-affine. At prime length, a general three-frequency triple has rank two on every three-column restriction by Fourier full spark. Its nonzero proportionality classes have size at most two; the zero-column case is handled separately. Thus the weaker local bound is ceil(p/2). Summing g strong bounds and p-g weak bounds gives the source's hybrid expression. Arbitrary non-affine cycles at composite lengths do not automatically inherit this prime full-spark step.

For the sparse bridge, the noncommutative order is K=CB and

    A F D=(A+B)(CB)^(-1)(C+D).

Here the outer sums have row degree at most3 and K has row degree at most4. A scalar degree-L polynomial in K has support bounded by the union of its powers, so the bridge has at most9 sum_(r=0)^L 4^r entries per row. Combining with the strong density bound gives

    4^(L+1)>=1+(n-2)/6.

This excludes uniformly bounded scalar-polynomial degree for the specified exact bridge target. It does not exclude an O(N) non-polynomial solve, shared work among powers, other circuit primitives or all two-stage Green products. The strong odd-length form still requires the leftmost frequency triples to be primitive-affine; at general prime cycles only the weaker constant is justified.

## Essential finite-field countercontrol

The complex chord geometry must not be turned into a field-uniform theorem because modular samples happen to pass. Over F19, take N=9, primitive root4, a=1 and b=12. The two-vectors have three disjoint dependent pairs

    (1,8), (2,5), (6,7).

Use the row-sampling right cycle

    beta=[3,8,5,4,1,6,7,0,2]

and weights [0,14,15,0,0,0,10,0,0]. For the left natural cycle use weights [1,12,0,0,0,0,0,0,0]. Both weighted-cycle products are zero, so both inverse guards are one. Exact multiplication of the two denominators with F9 gives jointly zero selected-row columns5,7,8, hence union support6<9-2. This does not contradict the source's explicitly complex theorem, but it invalidates any blanket finite-field extension of that theorem.
