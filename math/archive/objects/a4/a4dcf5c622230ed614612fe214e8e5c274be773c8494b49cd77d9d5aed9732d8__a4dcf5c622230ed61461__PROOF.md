# Whole-word rational fusion: a linear degree and single-solve gate

9 October 2026. Independently derived from the supplied research. These are
representation obstructions, not a general Fourier arithmetic lower bound.
No uploaded executable was run.

## Main results

Let F be the normalized complex N-point Fourier matrix, N >= 2. Put

    beta_N = floor(N^2/4)/N.

1. If T has upper and lower quasiseparable order at most h in some single
   coordinate order, q(T) is invertible, and p/q is a reduced scalar rational
   function of degree d=max(deg p,deg q), then

       qsep(p(T) q(T)^-1) <= h d.

   Consequently, if nonsingular endpoint monomials turn this rational
   function into F, then h d >= beta_N.

2. In particular, a word of t of the prior phase-rotated cycle factors
   K(lambda M), K(lambda M)^-1, K(lambda M*), K(lambda M*)^-1, all using the
   same weighted full cycle M, can equal Fourier up to nonsingular endpoint
   monomials only if

       t >= ceil(beta_N).

   This is a linear, whole-word necessary condition. It strengthens the
   earlier logarithmic stage gate for this common-cycle family. Interior
   separators are allowed precisely when pushing them to an endpoint leaves
   every factor a degree-at-most-one rational function of the same M.

3. If F = Q_L L A^-1 R Q_R, with Q_L,Q_R arbitrary nonsingular monomials,
   and A,L,R are banded-plus-low-rank in one order, then

       (b_A+r_A)+(b_L+r_L)+(b_R+r_R) >= beta_N.

   Here b_X is the half-bandwidth of the banded part, r_X is the rank bound
   for the remaining part, and only A must be invertible. This excludes
   compressing any exact Fourier word to one bounded-bandwidth-plus-bounded-
   rank solve with bounded-width/rank preprocessing and postprocessing.

4. Auxiliary variables do not evade the single-solve gate under local port
   assumptions. Let A be any invertible m-by-m matrix, m >= N, with
   qsep(A) <= h. Inject each input, times a nonzero scalar, into one distinct
   coordinate of the right-hand side. Read each output, times a nonzero
   scalar, from one distinct coordinate of A^-1 times that right-hand side.
   Input and output placements may be different and otherwise arbitrary.
   If the resulting N-by-N map is F, then h >= beta_N. In particular,
   A=A_0+UV^T with half-bandwidth b and rank(UV^T)<=r requires b+r>=beta_N,
   regardless of the number or placement of auxiliary coordinates.

These results do not rule out other sparse graph geometries, nonlocal port
maps, arbitrary changing internal permutations, special high-bandwidth
matrices with fast solvers, or an exact o(N log N) Fourier circuit.

## 1. Elementary cut-rank toolkit

For a matrix X in a fixed order, define qsep(X) as the maximum of the ranks
of X[I,J] and X[J,I] over prefix I={0,...,k-1}, complementary suffix J.
The usual definition using all strictly triangular rectangles is equivalent:
every such rectangle lies inside one of these complementary cut blocks.

For matrices U,V of equal size,

    qsep(U+V) <= qsep(U)+qsep(V),
    qsep(UV)  <= qsep(U)+qsep(V),
    qsep(U+cI)=qsep(U)  for every scalar c.

The product bound follows from

    (UV)[I,J] = U[I,I] V[I,J] + U[I,J] V[J,J].

For any invertible A, and any complementary I,J, even when A[I,I] and
A[J,J] are singular,

    rank(A^-1[I,J]) = rank(A[I,J]).                 (1)

An elementary kernel proof avoids unjustified Schur-complement inversions.
A vector z in ker(A^-1[I,J]) gives
A^-1(0,z)=(0,w), where A[I,J]w=0 and z=A[J,J]w.
Conversely every such w produces z. This map w -> z is bijective: if both
A[I,J]w and A[J,J]w vanish, invertibility of A forces w=0. Both kernel
domains have dimension |J|, so their equal nullities imply (1). Exchange I,J
for the reverse block. Thus qsep(A^-1)=qsep(A).

This is a special case of the classical nullity theorem. A primary source is
Vandebril and Van Barel, [A short note on the nullity theorem, 2004](https://www.cs.kuleuven.be/publicaties/rapporten/tw/TW397.pdf).
The quasiseparable generator background is covered by Pernet and
Storjohann, [Time and space efficient generators for quasiseparable matrices](https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.00396).
No literature-priority claim is made for the underlying tools or corollaries.

If A=A_0+UV^T, A_0 has half-bandwidth b, and rank(UV^T)<=r, then

    qsep(A) <= b+r.                                (2)

Each cut block of A_0 has at most b potentially nonzero boundary rows or
columns, and adding a rank-r matrix increases its rank by at most r.
Equation (1), and hence all our gates, does not assume A_0 is invertible.

## 2. Fourier rank survives arbitrary endpoint orders and gains

For ANY physical row set S and column set T, with sizes s,t,

    rank(F[S,T]) >= s t/N.                         (3)

Proof: the squared Frobenius norm is st/N, while every singular value is at
most 1 because F[S,T] is a restriction of a unitary. Their squared sum is at
most their count. This proof covers every N, including composite/dyadic N;
it makes no false full-spark assumption.

If Q_L X Q_R=F for arbitrary nonsingular monomial Q_L,Q_R, every cut block
of X is a row/column-scaled, independently permuted Fourier submatrix of
the same dimensions. Nonzero diagonal gains preserve rank; we apply the
norm argument to F BEFORE those gains. Therefore, at k=floor(N/2),

    qsep(X) >= k(N-k)/N = beta_N.                  (4)

The endpoints need not be unitary, have bounded entries, share a permutation,
or preserve the internal ordering. All of these freedoms are already paid
for by the uniformity of (3).

## 3. Rational degree is a whole-word cut-rank budget

Let p/q be reduced, a=deg p, b=deg q, and q(T) invertible. The zero function
is irrelevant to Fourier and may be treated separately. Over C, divide and
expand into partial fractions:

    p(z)/q(z) = s(z) + sum_l sum_(j=1..m_l) c_(l,j)/(z-lambda_l)^j.

The denominator's distinct roots are lambda_l, with sum m_l=b. Invertibility
of q(T) ensures T-lambda_l I is invertible for each root. By (1),

    qsep(S_l) <= h, where S_l=(T-lambda_l I)^-1.

The entire group at one pole is a polynomial in S_l with zero constant
term. Horner evaluation uses m_l multiplications by S_l and scalar-identity
additions. Therefore its quasiseparable order is at most m_l h. This is
important: summing separate bounds for each power would incur an unnecessary
sum of 1+...+m_l.

The polynomial part s(T) has quasiseparable order at most h deg s, by the
same Horner argument. If a<b this part vanishes; if a>=b its degree is a-b.
Adding the bounds gives

    qsep(p(T)q(T)^-1) <= h [max(a-b,0)+b]
                        = h max(a,b).             (5)

For b=0 use the polynomial argument directly. The partial fractions are
used only to PROVE the rank bound: root extraction and coefficient generation
are not silently supplied to an executor. The proof also works over a field
extension in which q splits, so its algebraic rank conclusion is compatible
with our finite-field sanity checks.

Combine (5) with (4) for result 1. Any alternative lower-degree rational
representation valid on the spectrum obeys the same bound. Thus cancellation
using the minimal polynomial cannot produce a hidden degree-o(N/h) Fourier
representation in this architecture.

### The prior common-cycle family

Order coordinates around a directed weighted full cycle M. At every cut,
one crossing cycle edge goes each way, so qsep(M)=1. Since M is unitary,
M*=M^-1. Define K(X)=(2I-X)(I-X)^-1. For nonzero lambda,

    K(lambda M)       = (2I-lambda M)(I-lambda M)^-1,
    K(lambda M*)      = (2M-lambda I)(M-lambda I)^-1.

Their inverses exchange numerator and denominator. Each factor is degree
at most one in numerator and denominator. The product of t factors is one
rational function p(M)/q(M) with both degrees at most t. We require that
the originally used factors be defined; the prior assumptions M^N=-I and
lambda^N=1 guarantee this. Formula (5) now gives qsep(word)<=t, hence

    t >= ceil(beta_N).                             (6)

No local unitarity, circle-pairing classification, conditioning assumption,
or separate-pass execution charge enters (6). It applies even when the word
is globally cancelled/fused, and arbitrary endpoint nonunitary diagonal
gains are allowed. It does not cover other commuting matrices merely because
they are simultaneously diagonalizable, or changes of cycle that destroy
the common degree-one rational representation.

## 4. A single banded-plus-low-rank solve

By the product rule and (1),

    qsep(L A^-1 R) <= qsep(L)+qsep(A)+qsep(R).

Apply (2), then (4), to get result 3. This includes both a numerator applied
before a solve and bounded-width/rank output correction, including a bounded
number of Householder wrappers. The matrices must be structured in the SAME
order; changing the internal order is not a free operation under this claim.

This is a direct structural exclusion of the fused matrix. It does not infer
Omega(N log N) from a factor count. A banded matrix with b=Theta(N) or a
rank-Theta(N) correction may have a different, unusually cheap representation
or solver; the gate does not rule that out.

### Local-port auxiliary-variable extension

Write the transfer matrix as C A^-1 B, where every column of B is supported
on its assigned input coordinate, and every row of C on its assigned output
coordinate, with nonzero scalar weights. Input positions are distinct, as
are output positions, but an input and output may share a position.

Take a prefix of the m coordinates containing exactly a=floor(N/2) input
positions. Such a prefix exists because positions are distinct. Let b be
the number of output positions in it. The two cross-side transfer rectangles
have row/column dimensions

    b by (N-a),              (N-b) by a.

They are scaled submatrices of A^-1 across that cut, and so each has rank at
most h. If the transfer equals F, (3) implies

    h >= max[b(N-a), (N-b)a]/N >= a(N-a)/N.

The last inequality follows by b>=a in the first branch and b<=a in the
second. This proves result 4 for EVERY auxiliary dimension m. An arbitrarily
large line-like solve cannot hide Fourier behind rearranged scalar ports.
The theorem does not cover dense input/output encoders, nonlocal feedthrough,
or multiple disconnected orderings each furnished with free shuffles.

## 5. Constructive fusion and the precise limit it reaches

For a common banded base T, commuting rational Cayley/Green factors really
can be fused. If their scalar rational product is p/q, then applying the
whole word is exactly the single equation

    q(T) y = p(T) x.                               (7)

For half-bandwidth b_T, polynomial degrees d_p,d_q give half-bandwidths at
most b_T d_p, b_T d_q. If T=T_0+UV^T, with rank<=r_T, then a degree-d
polynomial in T differs from the same polynomial in T_0 by rank at most
d r_T. To see this, telescope T^k-T_0^k; every resulting column is in the
span of U,TU,...,T^(d-1)U. Thus (7) is a concrete banded-plus-low-rank
fusion, but its widths/ranks can grow with degree. Fixed degree and fixed
bandwidth/rank permit the ordinary linear-arithmetic fixed-width solve
regime when its solver's invertibility/pivot conditions are met.

This statement is not a general O(N) compiler: generating degree-growing
polynomials, storing their bands, factoring q(T), handling pivots, and applying
corrections all need to be charged. Nor does (7) hold by simply multiplying
numerators and denominators of NONCOMMUTING factors. A block lifted system
can encode such a word, but generally changes dimension and graph geometry;
the local-port theorem applies only if its ordered cut ranks remain small.

The previous half-cycle example remains a genuine positive control. For
N=2m, M^N=-I, and all m phases with lambda^m=1,

    product K(lambda M)
       = [(2^m+1)I + (2^m-1)M^m]/2.               (8)

The t=m word has an O(N) scalar-arithmetic evaluator after charged preparation
of its two coefficients and the half-cycle action. It has two nonzeros per
row, not Fourier flatness. Its natural half-cut rank is exactly m: the
off-diagonal block is a nonzero scalar multiple of a signed permutation.
Thus it simultaneously has linear cut rank and a linear-arithmetic evaluator.
The new gate does not prohibit it. Equation (8) also proves why a long
rational word or a high cut rank is not itself an arithmetic lower bound.

## 6. Exactness, preparation, and precision

### The linear rational-degree scale is attainable for Fourier

The unrestricted common-cycle rational class is not empty of Fourier maps.
Here is an explicit all-N polynomial example, using exactly the signed cycle
M e_j=e_(j+1) for j<N-1 and M e_(N-1)=-e_0. Let

    zeta=exp(pi i/N),
    s=1 when N is even, and s=0 when N is odd,
    c_l=zeta^(-l^2+s l),
    C=sum_(l=0..N-1) c_l M^l,
    D_L[k,k]=zeta^(k^2-s k),
    D_R[j,j]=zeta^(j^2+s j).

Then

    D_L C D_R = [exp(2 pi i k j/N)]_(k,j).          (9)

Proof: c_(l+N)=(-1)^(N-s)c_l=-c_l. This antiperiodicity precisely cancels
the minus sign when a power of M wraps around, giving C[k,j]=c_(k-j).
Adding the three exponents in the displayed product gives 2kj. Divide C by
sqrt(N) for normalized Fourier. Every c_l is nonzero, so this polynomial has
degree N-1. Together with result 1, the necessary degree for the unrestricted
common signed-cycle rational class is Theta(N), up to constant factors.

This is a signed-cycle form of the classical quadratic-chirp reduction of
Fourier to convolution, not a new FFT construction. See Bluestein,
[A linear filtering approach to the computation of discrete Fourier transform](https://doi.org/10.1109/TAU.1970.1162132).
Its coefficients can be generated by scalar recurrences in O(N) arithmetic
from a supplied zeta, but applying the resulting dense convolution has not
been made sub-N-log-N here. Formula (9) does not assert that this polynomial
factors into the more restricted K(lambda M) family. It marks exactly where
the gate stops: Fourier exists at linear rational degree; fast execution of
that degree-growing polynomial remains a separate problem.

### Arithmetic representation

The theorems concern exact complex matrices and every fresh input, not
agreement on selected inputs or approximate transforms. They allow arbitrary
exact coefficients and unbounded precision; hence they cannot be bypassed by
large cancellation, ill conditioning, or a dense matrix represented compactly
as the inverse of a low-width matrix. They also do not claim bounded-bit cost.

The positive rational fusion and (8) are scalar-field arithmetic statements.
For example 2^m has Theta(N) bits. Green continuants already exhibit exact
denominators of Theta(N) bits. Roots, coefficient preparation, arithmetic
width, explicit output writes, and indexing remain charged parts of any
algorithmic speedup. The prior residual-certified approximate Green bridge
does not satisfy exact Fourier equality and is neither contradicted nor
upgraded to exactness by these gates.

## 7. Independent verification and relation to the new upload

`check_whole_word.py` uses only newly written standard-library code. It tests
the cut-rank inverse identity including singular principal blocks, the
rational-degree bound on multiple matrix families and degrees, fresh-input
half-cycle fusion, and arbitrary-port inverse restrictions. It also reads the
new scan upload's JSON certificate as DATA and independently reconstructs
the signal graph, its recorded capacity-16 cut, max flow, and both exact
17-by-17 Fourier determinants. Forty additional checks compare actual newly
generated quasiseparable network minors with this graph's max flow. No
uploaded module is imported or executed.

The scan result excludes one rank-(1,1), two-stage, N=64 network for one
fixed triple of orderings. Our all-N result permits all endpoint orders but
restricts the WHOLE compiled map to common-base rational functions or one
low-cut-rank solve. Neither theorem subsumes arbitrary shuffled multistage
networks. See `SCAN_AUDIT.md` and `results.json` for the independently checked
certificate and the explicit limits of the finite tests.
