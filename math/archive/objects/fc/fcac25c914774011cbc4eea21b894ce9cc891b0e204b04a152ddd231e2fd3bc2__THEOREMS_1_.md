# Weyl–Expansion Dual Certificate for Fourier/Green Circuits (WEDC)

**Research date:** 10 October 2026  
**Status:** Self-contained restricted-model mathematical statements, numerical/exact-field verification; *not* an exact O(N) FFT construction. External theorem originality unverified.  
**Source basis:** User's master FFT archive, global Green Phase26, sparse-defect gate, Hero #1/#17, divisor-spectrum, prior SPFSB/additive separation research, and related prior art.

## Goal and scope

The research target is exact, sequential, fresh arbitrary-input DFT application with o(N log N) charged work, ideally O(N). An O(N) application circuit using a fixed O(1)-size network of weighted cyclic Green resolvents would be relevant *only if its complete matrix equals the Fourier matrix* for every length N, including all preprocessing, coefficient generation, storage, and arithmetic model details.

This checkpoint constructs a two-part, falsifiable mathematical test:

1. **Cut/expansion test:** a necessary structural condition for products of sums of cyclic Green inverse operators with common-coordinate conjugacy and diagonal gauges;
2. **Weyl twirl test:** an *exact* all-N identity and a randomized O(N)-cost black-box tester measuring the candidate's distance from a scalar Fourier transform *without applying the Fourier transform*.

A joint theorem gives a **constant numerical-error floor** for certain composite lengths whenever cut expansion is insufficient. None of this is an algorithm computing exact DFT faster than FFT.

## Conventions

Let `N>=2`, `omega=exp(-2pi i/N)` and `U=U_N` the unitary N-point Fourier matrix `U[k,j]=omega^(kj)/sqrt(N)`. Let `S` denote the cyclic shift, `S e_j=e_{j+1 mod N}`, and `D=diag(omega^j)`. Then

    U S = D U,
    U D = S^(-1) U.

Let `W_{a,b}=D^a S^b` for `a,b` in `Z_N`, and `Phi(W_{a,b})=S^(-a) D^b=U W_{a,b} U*`. Frobenius norm `||.||_F` and spectral norm `||.||_2` are distinguished throughout.

## Theorem 1 — exact Fourier–Weyl twirl identity

For every complex N x N candidate matrix `M`,

    (1/(2 N^2)) sum_{a,b=0}^{N-1}
       ||M W_{a,b} - Phi(W_{a,b}) M||_F^2
      = min_{lambda in C} ||M - lambda U||_F^2.                (T1)

The unique minimizing scalar is `lambda_*=Tr(U* M)/N`.

**Proof.** Set `X=U* M`. Unitarity gives

    M W_{a,b} - Phi(W_{a,b}) M = U (X W_{a,b} - W_{a,b} X).

The N^2 Weyl matrices form a Hilbert–Schmidt orthogonal basis and satisfy the twirling identity

    (1/N^2) sum_{a,b} W_{a,b}* X W_{a,b} = Tr(X) I/N.

Expand the squared commutator Frobenius norm and average:

    E_W ||XW-WX||_F^2
      = 2||X||_F^2 - 2 |Tr X|^2/N
      = 2||X-(Tr X/N) I||_F^2
      = 2||M-lambda_* U||_F^2.

Divide by 2 to obtain T1. All identities work also at composite N; neither Chebotarev nor a well-conditioned-circuit hypothesis is involved. QED.

**Novelty boundary:** Weyl twirling and orthogonality are standard group/quantum-information facts. Their specialization as a black-box *phase correctness* gate for this project's O(N)-scan candidate architecture was not found in the searched project reports. Do not claim the twirl identity itself is a globally new theorem.

## Theorem 2 — dimension-independent stochastic estimator

Let an arbitrary fixed complex-linear candidate `M` support applying to a dense vector in at most `C(N)` arithmetic operations. Independently sample `a,b` uniform in `Z_N` and `z` a standard complex Gaussian vector with `E(zz*)=I`. Define

    Z = (1/2) ||M(D^a S^b z)-S^(-a) D^b M(z)||_2^2.       (T2a)

Let `tau^2=min_lambda||M-lambda U||_F^2`. Then

    E[Z]=tau^2,     E[Z^2] <= 8 tau^4,    Var(Z)<=7 tau^4. (T2b)

Thus with `k >= ceil(28/epsilon^2)` independent samples per group, and `h >= ceil(8 log(1/delta))` independent groups, the median of the `h` group means estimates `tau^2` to relative error at most `epsilon` with failure probability at most `delta`. The number of applications of `M` is `2 k h`, so the work is

    O((C(N)+N) epsilon^(-2) log(1/delta))                   (T2c)

with O(N) active vector memory (not including stored `M` coefficients). When `C(N)=O(N)` and error/confidence parameters are fixed, each test is O(N); the count does **not** hide an FFT or an N x N matrix.

**Proof.** Theorem 1 gives the mean after applying `E_z ||A z||_2^2=||A||_F^2`. Let `E=M-lambda_*U`. Because the Fourier component intertwines exactly,

    A=M W-Phi(W)M=E W-Phi(W)E,

so `||A||_F<=2||E||_F=2 tau`. For a complex Gaussian,

    E_z ||A z||_2^4=||A||_F^4+Tr((A*A)^2)<=2||A||_F^4.

Therefore `E Z^2 <= (1/4)*2*(2tau)^4=8 tau^4`. Chebyshev bounds each group mean's relative error by `7/(k epsilon^2)<=1/4`. The standard Chernoff/Hoeffding median-of-means argument gives failure at most `exp(-h/8)`. QED. For `tau=0`, every probe evaluates to zero in exact arithmetic.

**Scope:** This is a randomized *norm estimator*, not a symbolic proof of exact FFT equality. Numerical experiments can have rounding error, and finding small Frobenius distance need not imply exact equality. Real algorithms must account for Gaussian generation, arithmetic precision, and oracle stability.

### Corollary — a genuine sample-complexity advantage over random Fourier columns

A simpler control for any candidate M is to select a random basis index j, generate the corresponding Fourier column U e_j in O(N) work, and compare it with M e_j. Although its expectation estimates `||M-U||_F^2`, it can miss **a single corrupted input column** for Omega(N) samples.

Explicitly take `M=U+(U e_2)e_1^T` at `N>=3`. This has exact seed `M e_0=U e_0`, and `min_lambda||M-lambda U||_F^2=1`. A uniform random Fourier-column test detects the defect with probability exactly `1/N` each attempt. The probability of missing it after t independent probes is `(1-1/N)^t`; getting 90% detection needs `t >= ceil(log(0.1)/log(1-1/N)) = Theta(N)`.

By contrast, the Weyl-twirl estimator has mean **1** and variance at most **7** on this very same adversarial defect, so a constant number of probes suffices for fixed relative accuracy and confidence, independent of N. In the saved N=1024, 128-probe control, random columns detected 0 defects whereas Weyl covariance estimated 0.9841. Neither method alone proves exact all-N correctness, but this shows a mathematically real advantage of the twirl estimator over naive random columns.

## Theorem 3 — seed-correctness to operator-norm accuracy

Let `v=U e_0=(1,...,1)^T/sqrt(N)` and `delta_0=||M e_0-v||_2`. Then

    ||M-U||_2 <= delta_0 + 2 tau.                           (T3)

**Proof.** For `E=M-lambda_* U`, we have `||E||_2<=||E||_F=tau`. Moreover

    |lambda_*-1| <= ||M e_0-v||_2+||E e_0||_2 <= delta_0+tau.

Hence `||M-U||_2 <= ||E||_2+|lambda_*-1|<=delta_0+2tau`. QED.

The **seed correction**

    M_tilde = M + (v-M e_0) e_0^T                           (T3b)

enforces `M_tilde e_0=v` and changes a black-box O(N) candidate by only an additional O(N)-work rank-one operation. The correction does not establish that the original Green ansatz solves FFT.

In particular, the *self-supervised synthesis loss*

    L(theta) = E_{a,b,z} [(1/2)||M_tilde(theta)Wz - Phi(W)M_tilde(theta)z||_2^2]

is zero **if and only if** `M_tilde(theta)=U` in exact arithmetic. It requires no known FFT output samples beyond the all-ones first Fourier column. If the Green parameters are differentiable away from singular denominators, its stochastic gradients are unbiased, and reverse-mode differentiation through an O(N)-step Green recurrence uses O(N) work for all O(N) parameters per sample. This is a *search mechanism*, not a proof that a zero exists.

## Theorem 4 — exact Green cut-rank and cycle expansion

Let `sigma` be any Hamiltonian cycle permutation of `{0,...,N-1}` (including non-affine), `T=diag(w) P_sigma`, and `G=(I-T)^(-1)` with `prod_j w_j !=1`. For any vertex set `A` with complement `A^c`, define

    b_G(A) = #{j in A: sigma(j) in A^c and w_j !=0}.

Then

    rank G[A,A^c] = rank (I-T)[A,A^c] = b_G(A).             (T4a)

Proof: the general complementary-nullity identity for an inverse gives equality of the two offdiagonal block ranks. The denominator's crossing block is a weighted partial permutation, so its nonzero entries occupy different rows and columns and its rank equals their count.

Consider an arbitrary product of finite sums of such Green inverses. Allow independent nonsingular diagonal left/right gauges on each branch, arbitrary monomial conjugations of cycles, and arbitrary *common interstage* monomial shuffles (which can be pushed into conjugated branch cycles). For a fixed cut `A|A^c`, let `b_total(A)` be the sum of all branch crossing counts, in the common global coordinate frame. Then

    rank M[A,A^c] <= b_total(A).                             (T4b)

Indeed rank of an offdiagonal block is subadditive under summation, and

    rank(XY)[A,A^c] <= rank X[A,A^c]+rank Y[A,A^c]

by splitting the intermediate sum through `A` and `A^c`; induction completes the proof.

If `N=p` is prime and the architecture equals `U_p` up to arbitrary outer monomial gauges, Chebotarev's all-minors theorem forces, for every `A`,

    b_total(A) >= min(|A|,p-|A|).                            (T4c)

This is a **necessary edge-expansion condition** for the union of cycles; it does not imply that the phase coefficients are correct. At composite N the rank may be deficient for some cuts; one may instead select a cut known to give a full-rank Fourier block.

**Important scope exclusions:** Independently chosen, unmatched left and right *permutations within each Green summand* are not covered by T4b. Neither are arbitrary dense interstage operators. The theorem restricts matrix *representations*, not all possible arithmetic circuits.

## Theorem 5 — a constant spectral and Weyl-energy barrier

Let `N=2m` where `m` is odd, and let `A={0,2,...,2m-2}`, `A^c={1,3,...,2m-1}`. The block `U_N[A,A^c]` is `1/sqrt(2)` times a unitary matrix, up to diagonal scalings and a row permutation. In particular **every one of its m singular values equals 1/sqrt(2)**.

**Proof.** Index `A` by `2r` and `A^c` by `2s+1`. Then

    U_2m[2r,2s+1] = (1/sqrt(2)) * omega_m^r * omega_m^(2rs) /sqrt(m),

where `omega_m=exp(-2pi i/m)`. Because `gcd(2,m)=1`, multiplication by 2 permutes Fourier row labels, and the remaining phase is diagonal/unitary. QED.

If any `M` satisfies `rank M[A,A^c] <m`, Eckart–Young plus the unitary-block identity implies

    ||M-U_N||_2 >= 1/sqrt(2).                               (T5a)

For a cycle architecture obeying T4b, a sufficient condition is `b_total(A)<m`. Combined with T3 and T1/T2, this yields the project-level **expansion-to-Weyl defect** inequality:

    tau >= (1/sqrt(2)-delta_0)_+/2,
    E_{a,b,z}[ (1/2)||M W z-Phi(W)M z||_2^2 ]
        >= (1/sqrt(2)-delta_0)_+^2/4.                       (T5b)

In particular, if the first Fourier column is enforced exactly, the expected single-probe covariance energy is **at least 1/8**, regardless of Green weights. This is a strong, size-independent *approximation* obstruction on the restricted model, not an unrestricted FFT lower bound.

For prime-length F, T4c gives a separate exact obstruction for **every** nontrivial cut; a uniform spectral gap for arbitrary cuts is not claimed there.

## Theorem 6 — exact finite-field one-sided identity test

Let `q` be a prime power containing a primitive `N`th root `omega`, let `F_N` be the unnormalized Fourier matrix over `F_q`, and let `M` be a linear operator over `F_q`. The conditions

    M e_0 = (1,...,1)^T,
    M S = D M                                                 (T6)

are jointly necessary and sufficient for `M=F_N`. The second condition can be tested without forming an N x N matrix: choose uniform `z in F_q^N` and check `M(Sz)==D(Mz)`. If `M S-DM` is nonzero, its rank is at least 1 and the probability of a false accept on `t` independent probes is at most `q^(-t)`. When `M` supports O(N) field operations per application, each probe requires O(N) field operations.

If a finite-field specialization of a characteristic-zero algebraic candidate has a *nonzero* residual, it can disprove the symbolic identity under appropriate well-defined denominator/specialization assumptions. A finite-field **positive** result does not establish the complex identity for arbitrary parameter values or all lengths.

## What this project contributes / does not contribute

### New within the inspected FFT artifacts

- A *matrix-free, all-length* randomized phase-distance tester based on the **exact** Weyl-twirl norm identity, with dimension-independent relative sample bound.
- A seed-corrected self-supervised objective whose zero loss is **equivalent** to exact Fourier equality; for O(N) Green scans, gradients are O(N) per probe under differentiability/precision assumptions.
- A combined cut-rank + exact Weyl-energy **constant lower bound** at lengths `N=2m`, m odd, for non-expanding Green cycle networks.
- A project-specific finite-field rejection protocol that avoids computing dense Fourier matrices.

### Not established

- No all-N parameter family satisfying the FFT is constructed. No O(N) or o(N log N) exact FFT is proved.
- No proof of publication originality for the underlying twirl, inverse-block, rank, and Fourier-coset ingredients; these have prior art.
- No bit-complexity or numerical-stability guarantee for candidate near-singular Green recurrences. Ailon/Morgenstern bounds constrain well-conditioned models.
- Passing all graph cuts is **not** sufficient for Fourier phase compatibility: a concrete N16 all-cuts-passing four-cycle union in `RESULTS.json` has substantial nonzero Weyl phase energy.

## Reproducible checks

Run in this folder:

    python verify.py
    python optimize_green.py --n 10 --steps 25

`verify.py` performs exhaustive Weyl averages for N=3,4,5,8,11; 2,800 matrix-free stochastic probes for each; checks seed/spectral inequalities; verifies an N18 parity spectral block; enumerates every nontrivial cut at N16; runs 18 exact modular Green inverse block-rank checks at N=7,11,18; and tests the one-sided finite-field gate at q=331, N=11.

`optimize_green.py` is an illustrative PyTorch reverse-mode computation: it differentiates through all four O(N)-scan recurrences, not through an FFT. The tiny N10 validation matrix is materialized **after** training only for independent measurement. Optimization is not evidence that an exact factorization exists.
