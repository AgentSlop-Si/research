# Prior art and novelty boundaries

The project builds on the existing FFT, cyclic Green/resolvent formula, type-II/inverse-orthogonal matrix identities, projective cross-ratio invariance, Bézout's theorem, finite-field reduction, and Macaulay coefficient matrices. None of these underlying tools is claimed as a newly discovered mathematical object.

Relevant classical context:
- Jacques Morgenstern, *Note on a Lower Bound on the Linear Complexity of the Fast Fourier Transform*, JACM 20(2), 1973 (bounded-coefficient model).
- Nir Ailon, *An n log n Lower Bound for Fourier Transform Computation in the Well Conditioned Model*, 2014 (restricted well-conditioned linear circuits), https://arxiv.org/abs/1403.1307.
- Standard algebraic geometry: homogenization, vanishing of forms at projective points, Macaulay coefficient matrices, and nonvanishing minors over good finite-field reductions.
- Prior project phase: `fft_green_phase28_internal_phase_synthesis`, tracing projective moment invariants and exact one-parameter exclusion certificates.

What Phase 29 establishes: exact mathematical non-factorization **for specific seeded and fixed outer Green mixers at specific lengths**, using exhaustive *partial* permutation coverage. It does not establish novelty over the literature without a dedicated prior-art survey; it is not an externally peer-reviewed theorem and not a general arithmetic-complexity improvement.
