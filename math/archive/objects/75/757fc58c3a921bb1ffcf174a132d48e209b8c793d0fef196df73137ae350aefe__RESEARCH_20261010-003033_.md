# Multi-shift Weyl–Green completeness and Bluestein collapse — v21

**Checkpoint:** 10 October 2026  
**Primary target:** exact, full-output DFT of an arbitrary fresh input in fully charged sequential `o(N log N)` arithmetic, ideally `O(N)`.  
**Status:** an exact positive representation theorem for the complete multi-shift Weyl–Green family, followed by a precise collapse to full-width quadratic-chirp convolution. **No exact faster FFT is constructed.**

## 1. Starting point

v20 fixed one nonzero shift step `h` and varied the Weyl slope `r` in

\[
W_{h,r}=X^h Z^r,
\qquad
G_{h,r}(\alpha)=(I-\alpha W_{h,r})^{-1}.
\]

The Fourier character over `r` produced a sparse `O(N)` packet, but every complete weighted packet for one fixed `h` had a quadratic-fiber zero minor. The exact next question was whether **several distinct shift steps `h`** jointly escape that obstruction.

This checkpoint answers that question sharply:

1. for generic `alpha`, the selected packets over **all** `h!=0` form a basis for arbitrary off-diagonal matrix data;
2. after adjoining the identity, they span exactly the matrices with constant diagonal;
3. a simple diagonal gauge of the Fourier matrix lies in that space and, more strongly, uses only **one Fourier mode across each `h` packet**;
4. the resulting `N-1` dense Green terms all lie on one common Weyl line and collapse to a full quadratic-chirp polynomial—Bluestein's convolution in a different gauge.

So multiple shift steps genuinely remove the single-`h` fiber obstruction, but the Fourier-compatible solution does not create a new low-cost evaluator.

## 2. Definitions

Let `N` be an odd prime, let

\[
\omega=e^{2\pi i/N},
\]

and use

\[
X e_j=e_{j+1},
\qquad
Z e_j=\omega^j e_j.
\]

For `h!=0` and `r,s in Z_N`, define

\[
W_{h,r}=X^hZ^r,
\qquad
G_{h,r}(\alpha)=(I-\alpha W_{h,r})^{-1},
\]

with `alpha^N != 1`, and the operator-index character packet

\[
H_{h,s}(\alpha)
=\frac1N\sum_{r=0}^{N-1}\omega^{-rs}G_{h,r}(\alpha).
\tag{2.1}
\]

Since `W_(h,r)^N=I`, roots-of-unity filtering gives the exact entries

\[
H_{h,s}[j+mh,j]
=\frac{\alpha^m}{1-\alpha^N}
\mathbf 1\!\left\{mj+h\binom m2=s\right\},
\qquad 0\le m<N.
\tag{2.2}
\]

For a fixed input or output coordinate, the condition in `m` is quadratic. Therefore every selected packet has at most two nonzeros per row and at most two per column.

## 3. Generic completeness theorem

For a matrix `T`, write its displacement-`delta` diagonal as

\[
t_\delta(j)=T[j+\delta,j],
\qquad \delta\ne0.
\]

Let coefficients `c_(h,s)` form

\[
T=\sum_{h\ne0}\sum_s c_{h,s}H_{h,s}(\alpha),
\]

and define

\[
\widehat c_h(\ell)=\sum_s c_{h,s}\omega^{\ell s},
\qquad
\widehat t_\delta(k)=\sum_j t_\delta(j)\omega^{kj}.
\]

A direct substitution into (2.2) yields

\[
\widehat t_\delta(k)
=\frac1{1-\alpha^N}
\sum_{h\ne0}
\alpha^{[\delta/h]_N}
\omega^{-k(\delta-h)/2}
\widehat c_h(kh/\delta),
\tag{3.1}
\]

where `[q]_N` is the representative in `{1,...,N-1}`.

Set `x=k/delta`. Equation (3.1) splits into `N` independent `(N-1)x(N-1)` blocks

\[
B_x(\delta,h)
=\alpha^{[\delta/h]_N}
\omega^{-x\delta(\delta-h)/2}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

### Theorem 3.1 — generic off-diagonal packet basis

There is a nonzero polynomial `P_N(alpha)` such that, whenever

\[
P_N(\alpha)(1-\alpha^N)\ne0,
\]

the `N(N-1)` packets `H_(h,s)(alpha)` restrict to a basis of the entire `N(N-1)`-dimensional off-diagonal matrix space.

### Proof

It suffices to prove `det B_x` is not the zero polynomial for every `x`.

In a determinant term indexed by a permutation `pi` of the nonzero residues, the total exponent of `alpha` is

\[
E_\pi=\sum_{\delta\ne0}[\delta/\pi(\delta)]_N.
\]

Every summand is at most `N-1`, hence

\[
E_\pi\le(N-1)^2.
\]

Equality requires

\[
\delta/\pi(\delta)=-1
\]

for every `delta`, so the unique maximizing permutation is

\[
\pi(\delta)=-\delta.
\]

Its root-of-unity coefficient is nonzero. Thus `det B_x` has a unique nonzero top-degree monomial `alpha^((N-1)^2)` and cannot vanish identically. The product over all `x` is the required nonzero polynomial `P_N`. QED.

### Corollary 3.2 — constant-diagonal span

Every packet combination has a constant diagonal: only `s=0` contributes there, and its diagonal entry is `(1-alpha^N)^(-1)` at every coordinate.

Theorem 3.1 gives arbitrary off-diagonal data. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{span}\bigl(\{I\}\cup\{H_{h,s}(\alpha)\}\bigr)
=\{A:A_{00}=A_{11}=\cdots=A_{N-1,N-1}\}
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

for generic `alpha`.

This is a genuine positive expressivity theorem: allowing every nonzero shift step removes the individual quadratic-fiber minor obstruction completely, except for the obvious common-diagonal constraint.

## 4. Exact Fourier embedding

Use the unnormalized Fourier matrix

\[
F[i,j]=\omega^{ij}
\]

and the row gauge

\[
D[i,i]=\omega^{-i^2}.
\]

Then

\[
M=DF,
\qquad
M[i,j]=\omega^{ij-i^2},
\qquad
M[i,i]=1.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Thus `M` has constant diagonal and belongs to the span (3.3).

More is true. Along displacement `delta`,

\[
M[j+\delta,j]=\omega^{-\delta j-\delta^2}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Its Fourier transform in `j` is supported only at `k=delta`, i.e. only in the block `x=k/delta=1` of (3.1). Since every block is invertible, all coefficient blocks except `x=1` vanish.

Therefore

\[
\widehat c_h(\ell)=0\quad(\ell\ne h),
\]

so

\[
c_{h,s}=A_h\omega^{-hs}
\]

for some unique `A_h`. Substituting into (2.1) collapses the whole `s`-sum:

\[
\sum_s A_h\omega^{-hs}H_{h,s}=A_hG_{h,-h}.
\]

### Theorem 4.1 — exact `N-1` Green-line Fourier representation

For generic `alpha`, there are unique scalars `lambda,A_1,...,A_(N-1)` such that

\[
\boxed{
DF
=\lambda I+
\sum_{h=1}^{N-1}
A_h\left(I-\alpha X^hZ^{-h}\right)^{-1}.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

The package solves and verifies this identity exactly in finite fields for `N=7,11,13,17`.

This is the first point on this branch where a full Fourier matrix is actually represented by the multi-shift Weyl–Green family. It is an existence/structure result, not a faster executor.

## 5. The representation collapses to one Weyl line

Put

\[
U=XZ^{-1}.
\]

The Weyl multiplication rule gives

\[
X^hZ^{-h}
=\omega^{h(h-1)/2}U^h.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Hence every denominator in (4.3) is a rational function of the **same** monomial operator `U`; all these supposedly different shift steps commute after the phase correction.

The Fourier target itself has the exact polynomial form

\[
\boxed{
DF
=\sum_{h=0}^{N-1}
\omega^{-h(h+1)/2}U^h.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Indeed `U^h` has its only nonzero in column `j` at row `j+h`, with phase

\[
\omega^{-hj-h(h-1)/2};
\]

multiplying by `omega^(-h(h+1)/2)` gives `omega^(-hj-h^2)`, exactly the entry (4.2).

So (4.3) is a rational interpolation of the full-degree chirp polynomial (5.2). It does not produce genuinely independent noncommuting support orders.

## 6. Bluestein collapse

A symmetric diagonal gauge gives the classical chirp-convolution identity

\[
\omega^{ij}
=\omega^{i^2/2}\,
 \omega^{-(i-j)^2/2}\,
 \omega^{j^2/2}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Thus a diagonal scaling of `F` is the circulant convolution kernel

\[
g_d=\omega^{-d^2/2}.
\]

Equation (5.2) is the same phenomenon expressed in the monomial coordinate `U=XZ^(-1)` instead of the plain shift `X`.

Therefore all `N-1` Green-line terms can certainly be fused—but the fused object is exactly the full quadratic-chirp convolution used by Bluestein's DFT reduction.

The coefficient sequence in (5.2) is nonzero at every `h`. Its spectral values are quadratic Gauss sums:

\[
\sum_h
\omega^{-h(h+1)/2+kh},
\]

which are also nonzero at every frequency (over `C`, each has magnitude `sqrt(N)`). Consequently the obvious coefficient domain and the obvious spectral domain are both fully occupied.

This is not an arithmetic lower bound for every possible convolution algorithm. It proves that the present multi-shift Green fusion has reached a classical full-width DFT-equivalent convolution, rather than a new sparse or bounded-state evaluator.

Bluestein's original 1970 paper explicitly presents the DFT as a chirp-filter/linear-filtering computation and uses an FFT to obtain `N log N` work. The current identity is a Weyl–Green reparameterization of that classical reduction, not a claim of priority.

## 7. Gate for individually sparse character packets

Equation (2.2) is quadratic in `m` for every fixed input or output coordinate. Hence a selected `H_(h,s)` has at most two nonzeros in each column and each row.

A sum of `q` selected packets therefore has at most `2q` nonzeros in each column. Every Fourier matrix and every matrix obtained from it by endpoint permutations and nonzero diagonal gauges is dense. Thus

\[
\boxed{q\ge\lceil N/2\rceil.}
\tag{7.1}
\]

Each selected character packet applies in `O(N)`, so evaluating the packets separately costs `Omega(N^2)`.

This gate does not apply to dense weighted packets or to a hypothetical joint packet evaluator. It only closes the idea that a small number of the sparse `O(N)` character aggregates might already produce the dense Fourier matrix.

## 10. Honest conclusion and next gate

We still do **not** have an exact `o(N log N)` FFT.

v21 gives a positive result:

\[
\boxed{
\text{all shift-step Weyl character packets are generically complete for off-diagonal operators.}
}
\]

It also gives an exact Fourier representation:

\[
\boxed{
DF=\lambda I+\sum_{h\ne0}A_h(I-\alpha X^hZ^{-h})^{-1}.
}
\]

But the Fourier-compatible coefficients force every term onto one common Weyl line and collapse to

\[
\boxed{
DF=\sum_h\omega^{-h(h+1)/2}(XZ^{-1})^h,
}
\]

which is a full quadratic-chirp convolution—Bluestein in disguise.

The next exact experiment must therefore avoid a single isotropic/Weyl line. A credible survivor is a **two-layer product using two non-collinear Weyl lines**, tested against:

- the exact Fourier–Weyl intertwining equations;
- the Phase-34 sparse-inverse/low-rank product gate;
- the WEDC twirl certificate;
- full operation accounting for any claimed joint evaluator.

Merely adding more terms on the line `(h,r)=h(1,-1)` will only reparameterize the same chirp convolution.
