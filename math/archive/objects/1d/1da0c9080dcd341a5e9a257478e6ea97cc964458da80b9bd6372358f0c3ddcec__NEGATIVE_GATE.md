# Phase 24 negative gate: certified fast paths do not imply generic FFT savings

1. **Rank defect of short probes.** The `m+1` original-input Fourier outputs used for `m=10` Green probes are an `(m+1)`-row linear map of rank at most `m+1`. For `N>m+1`, they have a nontrivial kernel. They cannot uniquely identify arbitrary `F_Nx`. This is why a complete certificate or a full FFT is mathematically mandatory.
2. **Adversarial first-ten probes.** The inherited Phase 23 example changes a structured residual by `omega^(-19j)` at `N=128`; it preserves its first ten Fourier coefficients and changes another coefficient. The Phase 24 full cyclic check rejects the plausible false candidate and finishes the same native FFT graph. `test_phase24.py` checks this precisely.
3. **Structural family is negligible.** The finite-field counting argument in THEOREM.md bounds accepted input diversity by `(N-1)(p-1) sum_{t=1}^s C(N,t)(p-1)^t`, against `p^N` possible inputs. Uniform generic-input improvement cannot come from this certificate family at fixed `s`.
4. **Crossover with a fair baseline.** The native radix-2 FFT still outperforms the structured Phase 24 evaluator for all tested dyadic sizes. The positive saving against Phase 23 is real, but does **not** beat the best straightforward Fourier baseline.
5. **Cache is not a new asymptotic circuit.** Completing the deferred graph executes the same total field arithmetic as native radix-2. It cannot establish an all-input `o(N log N)` bound by scheduling alone.
