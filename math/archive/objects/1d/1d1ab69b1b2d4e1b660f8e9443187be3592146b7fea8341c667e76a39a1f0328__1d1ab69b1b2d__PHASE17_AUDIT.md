## 1. Exact identity and recurrence

The inherited Weyl identity L F=F P, L=7I+2S+D, is correct for the unnormalized Fourier convention. Consequently computing z=F(Px) and solving L y=z returns F x. The residual support is actually inspected; it is not hidden input-dependent advice.

For nonzero residual coordinates, z_k=sum_j r_j lambda_j^k with lambda_j=omega^j. Its generating function is A(t)/B(t), where B=product_j(1−lambda_j t), B(0)=1 and deg A<K. A balanced sum-of-fractions tree gives the stated product/numerator construction bound when fast polynomial multiplication is available.

Let H be the next power of two at least K. The first H coefficients are A B^{-1} modulo t^H. For a later block starting at s>=H, let h_i=z_(s−K+i). The coefficient of t^(K+j) in B(t) sum_i h_i t^i is precisely the contribution from old samples to the recurrence at s+j. These coefficients vanish once j>=K. Negating and multiplying by U=B^{-1} modulo t^H gives the next H coefficients. The signs and indexing are correct.

At K=1,H=1, the recurrence reduces to z_s=lambda z_(s−1). At K=0 the forcing and output are zero; invertibility of P ensures x=0. The API's main theorem begins at N>=2; a public all-positive-length API would need the trivial N=1 wrapper separately.

The support-dependent denominators, numerator, inverse series and fixed-kernel transforms are rebuilt for every call. Within-call caching reuses the transforms of B and U while charging each variable operand's transform, pointwise product, inverse transform and output work. Short transforms have length at most 2H<=4K. This is legitimate reuse, not reuse of an unrelated input's coefficients.

## 2. Root and operation accounting

The arithmetic bound requires suitable roots for both the N-transform and the short power-of-two polynomial products. N|(p−1) alone is not enough for the implementation. Bluestein also has its separate 2N-root and length-Theta(N) convolution-root requirements. Over exact C these roots exist, but supplying or constructing them is an explicit arithmetic-model contract; existence alone is not a uniform bit-cost compiler.

The source computes each active lambda_j by repeated squaring, costing O(K log N). This is absorbed by O(N log(K+1)) for 1<=K<=N, because log(x+1)/x decreases. It is not an asymptotic defect.

Short NTT planning computes primitive_gen^((p−1)/length) separately for O(log(K+1)) stage lengths/directions. For a growing prime p, the literal implementation adds O(log p * log(K+1)) field multiplications, plus the charged inverse/root scaling work. One must add that term, assume suitably bounded log p, or supply the needed primitive short root and generate subsidiary roots by squaring. Fixed test primes do not by themselves prove a growing-field root/prime construction theorem.

PolyStats omits some scalar negations and subtractions: denominator signs, Newton sign flips and constant correction, and the per-block negated old-history remainder. Restoring O(N+K log(K+1)) such operations preserves the asymptotic bound. Thus the supplied benchmark figures are the implementation's declared forcing-only proxies, not exhaustive counts of every scalar operation. They also omit common P/L setup/application, input/output routing and prime/root discovery. No wall-clock superiority follows from them.

The graph root table, batch inversion, homogeneous recurrence and closure preparation are genuinely linear field work plus a bounded number of field inversions on the source's admissible domain. All storage, addressing, support detection and N output writes remain charged. The independent checker uses naive reference polynomial arithmetic and is not advertised as the fast implementation.

## 3. Finite-field zero-diagonal exception and repair

CyclicSolver first inverts every diagonal entry. This is stronger than nonsingularity of the complete cyclic operator. At p=17,N=16,omega=3, L has a zero diagonal entry at index 3 and P at index 11, yet det L=det P=16 modulo 17, so both are invertible. The source rejects them before closure. The upload already guards its diagonal denominators; preserve that stronger API domain instead of saying it covers every invertible operator over every eligible field.

A complete reverse-direction repair is simple. For (diag(d)+bS)y=r with b nonzero, write

y_(k−1) = b^{-1} r_k − b^{-1} d_k y_k.

Traverse k backwards around the cycle, starting from an unknown boundary value. Compose the scalar affine maps and close the cycle with one scalar division. The closure denominator is 1−product_k(−d_k/b), which is nonzero exactly when the matrix is invertible. This construction permits zero d_k and uses O(N) arithmetic, dividing by b and the closure rather than by every d_k.

Our independently authored reverse solver passes all 32 basis right-hand sides for L and P in the explicit exception. It is a proved algorithmic repair and reference check; no uploaded source was patched. It does not remove the separate short-root requirements or establish floating-point stability. Over C the stated coefficients already have |7+omega^k|>=6 and |7+2omega^(−k)|>=5, so this diagonal exception does not arise.

## 4. Dense inputs, approximation and coefficient growth

For complex x=P^{-1}e_j, every coordinate is nonzero. Starting from x_j, the N−1 nonforced equations propagate forward with nonzero ratios through all other coordinates. If any coordinate is zero, x_j must be zero and the whole vector would vanish, contradicting the forced equation. This supplies the rigorous version of the source's slightly informal zero-propagation wording. Dense input is compatible with K=1; it does not imply coverage of generic dense vectors.

For normalized Fourier and retained residual r_T, exact arithmetic gives y−yhat=L^{-1} F_normalized(r−r_T). Since sigma_min(L)>=4 and ||L||<=10,

||r−r_T||/10 <= ||y−yhat|| <= ||r−r_T||/4.

This is a valid deterministic norm envelope. It excludes rounding errors in residual formation, rational coefficient generation, transforms and the Green solve. Keeping the K largest residual magnitudes minimizes residual truncation error; it does not automatically minimize the final transformed error itself.

The source's clustered-node coefficient warning is correct. An independent finite refinement is available. For support {0,...,K−1}, the phase exponents contributing to denominator coefficient B_h span h(K−h). Rotating them to the midpoint gives

|B_h| >= binomial(K,h) cos(pi*h(K−h)/N)

whenever the angle is at most pi/2. In particular N>=K² implies |B_floor(K/2)|>=binomial(K,floor(K/2))/sqrt(2). The coefficient-to-output scale ratio can therefore be exponentially large in K at explicit finite lengths. This is a coefficient-growth result, not a rounding-error lower bound or proof that every alternate implementation is unstable.

Invertibility of fixed P makes residual coordinates nonzero almost surely under an absolutely continuous complex input law. A finite fixed bank of invertible linear preconditioners also cannot generically create coordinate sparsity. Uniform finite-field input instead gives the stated binomial support distribution. These facts preserve the structured/general distinction.
