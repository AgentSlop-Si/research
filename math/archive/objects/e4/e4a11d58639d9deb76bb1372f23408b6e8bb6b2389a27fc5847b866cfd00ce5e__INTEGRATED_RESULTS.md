## Infinite composite families from a finite lower-order criterion

The ramified-cluster report supplies a sufficient criterion for squarefreeness of the ordinary Rogers–Szego polynomial H_((dp−1)/2)(z;ζ_dp). Here d is odd, p is an odd prime not dividing d, and one prime above p must preserve both the lower residual discriminant and its resultant with z^d+1.

The key step is exact division by a cyclotomic power in Z[q] before reducing modulo p. This avoids the invalid inference that an arbitrary first nonzero p-adic Taylor term controls the value at a ramified root. A separated root cluster has a Hermite leading polynomial; its discriminant coefficient gives a finite lower-order content criterion.

Exact contents c5=5, c9=3^8, and c13=13^4 establish the families N=5p, 9p, 13p. For p congruent to 1 modulo 4, the previously accepted resonance and legal-bank guards give minimum scalar-resolvent bank size (N−1)/2. The exceptional repeated-prime orders 25 and 169 use existing finite certificates checked independently as data.

This is an infinite-family extension of this project's earlier finite composite classification. It is not a uniform all-composite proof, a fast simultaneous evaluator, or a certified novel result in the literature. An independent audit accepts the uniform proof, all fixed-base remainders, both exceptional certificates, and the legal-bank corollary. When dropping the mod-4 condition for squarefreeness alone, retain p not dividing d; in particular, this does not settle N=27 by setting d=9,p=3.

## A short classical proof replaces a long certificate as the main explanation

The five independently audited boundary certificates remain correct, and together with the nonboundary certificate they exclude the entire stipulated adjacent-anchor diagonal orbit. A subsequent priority check found that a simple classical cut-rank argument already proves a stronger statement.

Across S={0,1,2,3} and its complement, the first prescribed cycle has one crossing edge and the second has two. For any invertible matrix Q, the ranks of Q and Q inverse on this same off-diagonal block agree, even when principal blocks are singular. Hence the sum of the two inverse branches has cross-cut rank at most three. Any invertible first factor that preserves this partition also preserves that rank. The Fourier block has rank four by the Vandermonde determinant, giving a contradiction.

This covers every invertible first factor block diagonal for this partition, over any field containing a primitive eighth root. It allows zero edge coefficients and needs no separate nonzero-pivot or nonzero-diagonal assumptions on the inverse branches. The large exact certificates remain preserved as redundant verified proofs, but this particular exclusion is not evidence of a new obstruction method. The short proof and primary antecedent are included in scoped_theorem_priority_0407/CUT_RANK_PROOF.md.

Other support pairs or first factors that mix the cut remain outside this conclusion. No general FFT lower bound follows.
