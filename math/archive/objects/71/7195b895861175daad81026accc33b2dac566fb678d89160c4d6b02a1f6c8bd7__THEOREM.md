# Phase 36 — two-mask Green calibration and quantitative Fourier phase rigidity

**Research date:** 2026-10-10. **Status:** Explicit algebraic deductions, a restricted all-`N` spectral-error theorem, a matrix-free positive calibration algorithm, exact `N=4` certificate, and numerical/executable controls. **No `o(N log N)` FFT algorithm or new integer-multiplication bound.**

## Conventions and architectural scope

Let `N >= 4` be even, let `omega = exp(-2*pi*i/N)`, and write the **normalized** Fourier matrix as

`F[j,k] = omega^(j*k) / sqrt(N)`.

For matching the first row and column, we instead use the unnormalized matrix `F_un = sqrt(N)*F` (its first row and column are all 1). Rescaling one of the unconstrained internal diagonal masks switches between these conventions.

The studied two-mask architecture is

`M = A D1 P1 B P2 D2 C`,

where `D1,D2` are arbitrary invertible *complex* diagonals and `P1,P2` are arbitrary coordinate permutations. None of the phase-rigidity results requires these diagonals to be unit modulus or bounded in norm. The quantitative theorems assume the central `B` is entrywise **strictly positive real**, unless a more general phase-rank hypothesis is explicitly stated. The outer factors `A,C` need not be Green operators for the theorems, but the executable examples are Green-based.

## Theorem 36A — exact reduction of first-cross calibration to positive matrix scaling

Let `A,C` be invertible strictly positive bi-regular real matrices with respective nonzero row/column sums `a,c`, and let `B` be any strictly positive matrix. Put

`r_i = A[0,i]`, `s_j = C[j,0]`, `p_i = r_i/a`, `q_j = s_j/c`.

Then `p,q` are strictly positive probability vectors. There exist strictly positive diagonal matrices `X=diag(x)`, `Y=diag(y)` such that `M=A X B Y C` satisfies **both**

`M e0 = 1` and `e0^T M = 1^T`.

**Proof.** Define `u_i=r_i*x_i`, `v_j=s_j*y_j`. The first-cross equations are equivalent to the row- and column-marginal equations

`u_i (B v)_i = p_i`, `v_j (B^T u)_j = q_j`.

This follows by multiplying the first-column equation on the left by `A^-1` and the first-row equation on the right by `C^-1`, using `A^-1 1=(1/a)1` and `1^T C^-1=(1/c)1^T`. Because `B` is strictly positive and `sum p=sum q=1`, the classical positive matrix scaling theorem provides positive `u,v`, unique up to reciprocal scalar factors. Set `x=u/r`, `y=v/s`. The derived marginal equations give exactly the desired first-cross identities. The converse is the same algebra in reverse. **Nothing has been proved about the other `N^2-(2N-1)` Fourier entries.**

The positive-matrix scaling step is classical (Sinkhorn and Knopp and related matrix-scaling results), not a claim of new priority. Our deduction is its application to this Fourier/Green architecture.

## Theorem 36B — uniformly controlled linear-work *approximate* scaling

Choose a Hamiltonian-cycle permutation `W` and `beta=1-1/N`. Define

`K=(1-beta)*(I-beta*W)^(-1)`.

The geometric-series identity shows that `K` is strictly positive, doubly stochastic, and

`K[i,j]=(1-beta)*beta^d/(1-beta^N)`, where `d` is the forward cycle distance from column `j` to row `i`, `0<=d<N`.

In particular,

`max(K[i,j])/min(K[i,j])=beta^(-(N-1)) < e`.

Both `Kx` and `K^T x` can be computed in `O(N)` arithmetic by the explicit cyclic recurrence in `phase36_two_mask.py`. No dense matrix is materialized in the actual executor.

The Sinkhorn column update is

`v -> q / [K^T(p/(K v))]`.

In Hilbert's projective metric, the positive maps `K` and `K^T` contract by at most `tau=(e-1)/(e+1)<0.463` (Birkhoff contraction; finite positive matrix projective diameter <= `2 log(e)`). Multiplication by `p,q` and componentwise reciprocation are projective isometries. Thus a complete column update contracts by at most `tau^2<0.215`. After the first update, its projective distance to the fixed point is bounded by a constant independent of `N`: all ratios `(v_j/q_j)` have spread bounded by `e`, both at the fixed point and after an update. This gives `O(log(1/epsilon))` iterations to reach prescribed **relative marginal tolerance** `epsilon`, independent of `N`, in the exact real-arithmetic model. Each iteration is `O(N)` arithmetic.

The constructed outer Green-based stages are

`A=(1-eta)*I+eta*K_A`, `C=(1-eta)*I+eta*K_C`, `eta=1/100`,

with independently sampled cycle orders, and `B=K_B` defined similarly. All three have `O(N)` forward and transpose applications. `A^-1` and `C^-1` also have `O(N)` implementations: if `K=(1-beta)(I-beta W)^(-1)`, then

`A^-1 = ( [(1-eta*beta)I - ((1-eta)*beta)W] )^-1 * (I-beta W)`.

The bracketed denominator is another cyclic bidiagonal system. Positive scaling masks also have bounded **relative spreads**: for any positive `v`, the ratio between the largest and smallest entries of `(K v)` is at most `e`, so `max x/min x<=e` and `max y/min y<=e` after calibration, independently of `N`.

**Caveat:** `O(N log(1/epsilon))` is a real-arithmetic, approximate-mask preprocessing result. We make no claim of exact rational/algebraic mask bit complexity, nor of a universal floating-point rounding/stability bound. The calibrated online circuit is `O(N)` arithmetic *given the two masks*.

## Theorem 36C — phase-rank rigidity for an arbitrary complex middle matrix

Let `B` have no zero entries, and define its **entrywise phase matrix**

`Phi(B)[i,j] = B[i,j]/abs(B[i,j])`.

Suppose `rank(Phi(B)) <= r`. Then for all invertible complex diagonal `D1,D2` and all permutations `P1,P2`,

**`||F - D1 P1 B P2 D2||_2 >= (1/2)*sqrt(1-r/N)`.**

**Proof.** Write `T=D1 P1 B P2 D2`; its entrywise phase matrix `U` has all entries of modulus one and rank <=r (left/right unit-modulus diagonal factors and permutations do not change rank). Let `H=sqrt(N)*F`, the unnormalized Fourier matrix. For every coordinate, reverse triangle inequality gives

`abs(H[i,j]-sqrt(N)*T[i,j]) >= (1/2)*abs(H[i,j]-U[i,j])`.

Indeed `abs(abs(sqrt(N)*T[i,j])-1) <= abs(H[i,j]-sqrt(N)*T[i,j])`. Summing, and using the Eckart–Young lower bound for the distance from `H` to a rank-`r` matrix, gives

`||F-T||_F >= (1/(2 sqrt(N)))*sqrt(N^2-r*N) = (1/2)*sqrt(N-r)`.

All singular values of `H` are `sqrt(N)`; and `||F-T||_2>=||F-T||_F/sqrt(N)`. This proves the bound. □

The inequality applies to *any* complex `B` with small entrywise phase rank, even if `B` itself is dense, badly conditioned, or implemented by a complicated linear-time algorithm.

## Theorem 36D — a stronger real-positive-phase bound

For **strictly positive real `B`**, let `g=gcd(2,N)` and define

`c_N = max{ (1/2)*sqrt(1-1/N), sqrt((1-sqrt(g/N))/2) }`.

Then, for all complex nonzero internal masks and arbitrary permutations,

**`||F - D1 P1 B P2 D2||_2 >= c_N`.**

The first term follows from Theorem 36C, since the phase matrix of `B` has rank 1. To derive the second, write each complex-masked entry as

`s[i,j]*u_i*v_j/sqrt(N)`, with `s[i,j]>0` and `|u_i|=|v_j|=1`.

Let `theta[i,j]` be the phase of `omega^(ij)/(u_i v_j)`. Distance to the nonnegative real axis gives

`N*abs(F[i,j] - T[i,j])^2 >= sin(theta[i,j])^2`.

Now

`sum_{i,j} sin(theta[i,j])^2 = (N^2 - Re(sum_{i,j} omega^(2ij)*conj(u_i)^2*conj(v_j)^2))/2`.

The matrix `H2[i,j]=omega^(2ij)` has operator norm exactly `sqrt(g*N)` (its repeated-row structure is determined by `g=gcd(2,N)`). Since the two vectors of squared phases have Euclidean norms `sqrt(N)`, the magnitude of the double sum is at most `N*sqrt(g*N)`. It follows that

`||F-T||_F^2 >= (N-sqrt(g*N))/2`

and thus `||F-T||_2^2 >= (1-sqrt(g/N))/2`. □

For powers of two `N>=4`, `g=2`; the Fourier spectral error floor tends to `1/sqrt(2)` as `N` grows. The bound is uniform over **all magnitudes and phases** of the diagonal masks; unlike a positive-matrix nonnegativity argument, it also excludes sophisticated complex phase choices.

## Theorem 36E — stable outer sandwiches preserve a constant spectral error

Let `A,C` be arbitrary invertible complex matrices satisfying

`||A-I||_2<=delta_A<1`, `||C-I||_2<=delta_C<1`.

Let `T=D1 P1 B P2 D2`, with `B` positive. Then

**`||F-A T C||_2 >= [ (1-delta_A)*(1-delta_C)*(1+c_N) -1 ]_+`.**

More generally, for complex `B` with `rank Phi(B)<=r`, replace `c_N` by `(1/2)*sqrt(1-r/N)`.

**Proof.** Let `a=delta_A/(1-delta_A)`, `c=delta_C/(1-delta_C)`. The Neumann estimate yields

`||A^-1-I||_2<=a`, `||C^-1-I||_2<=c`.

Using `||F||_F=sqrt(N)`,

`||A^-1 F C^-1 - F||_F <= (a+c+a*c)*sqrt(N)`.

Theorem 36D gives `||F-T||_F >= c_N*sqrt(N)`, and the singular values of `A,C` are at least `1-delta_A,1-delta_C`. Therefore

`||F-A T C||_2 >= (1-delta_A)*(1-delta_C)*[c_N-(a+c+a*c)]_+`.

Substituting `a+c+a*c=1/[(1-delta_A)(1-delta_C)]-1` gives the advertised expression. □

For `A=(1-eta)I+eta*K_A` and the analogous `C`, every `K` is a convex combination of permutations, so `||K||_2<=1`. It follows that `delta_A,delta_C<=2*eta=1/50` at `eta=1/100`. The lower bound then approaches

`(49/50)^2 * (1+1/sqrt(2)) -1 ~= 0.63951`.

This is a **nonvanishing operator-norm error floor**, not simply a no-exact-equality theorem.

### Necessary near-full entrywise phase rank

For `delta_A=delta_C=delta`, write `epsilon=(1-delta)^(-2)-1`. An *exact* factorization through any entrywise-nonzero complex middle `B` requires

`(1/2)*sqrt(1-r/N) <= epsilon`, hence

**`r >= N*(1-4*epsilon^2)`.**

At `delta=1/50`, `epsilon=1/(0.98^2)-1`, so `r/N >= 0.993199...`. This is a **necessary phase-rank condition**, NOT a sufficient one and NOT a computational lower bound for producing high rank. The next theorem shows why.

## Theorem 36F — one cyclic Green solve can have phase rank N but only three cross-ratios

Let `W` be any `N`-cycle, let `alpha=rho*exp(i*theta)` with `rho>0`, `alpha!=0`, `alpha^N!=1`, and define

`G_alpha=(I-alpha W)^(-1)`.

In cycle-position coordinates `p_i`,

`G_alpha[i,j]=alpha^((p_i-p_j) mod N)/(1-alpha^N)`.

Ignoring row/column unit phases and one global phase, its entrywise phase matrix is

`H[i,j] = zeta` if `p_i<p_j`, and `1` otherwise, where `zeta=exp(i*N*theta)`.

Subtract each row from its successor in position order. This triangularizes `H` and proves

**`det H=(1-zeta)^(N-1)`.**

Thus its phase rank has a sharp dichotomy:

- **rank 1**, when `zeta=1` (the complex Green is diagonal-phase conjugate to a positive real Green);
- **rank N**, when `zeta!=1`, even though applying the Green solve takes only `O(N)` arithmetic.

Nonetheless, **every nonzero `2x2` cross-ratio** of the Green matrix belongs to

**`{1, alpha^N, alpha^(-N)}`**.

To see this, expand the four exponent distances; all row and column position terms cancel, leaving `N*Delta`, where the difference of two interval-indicator functions gives `Delta in {-1,0,1}`. Multiplication by arbitrary external nonzero diagonals cancels from these ratios, and external row/column permutations only relabel them.

For an `N`-point Fourier matrix with `N` divisible by 4, these cross-ratios attain **all N distinct Nth roots of unity**: row difference one and column differences `1,...,N-1` give the nonidentity roots, and row difference two with column difference `N/2` gives 1. Therefore a single cyclic Green matrix cannot be monomially equivalent to Fourier for any `N` divisible by four.

**Especially instructive control:** `alpha=exp(pi*i/N)` gives `alpha^N=-1` and

- all `N^2` Green entries have the **same magnitude** `1/2`;
- the entrywise phase matrix has **rank N** (`|det H|=2^(N-1)`);
- the cross-ratios take **only** the values `1,-1`;
- the Green transform and its inverse still take `O(N)` arithmetic.

This definitively shows why we must not interpret near-full phase rank alone as evidence of Fourier phase coherence.

## Computational controls and exact certificate

`phase36_two_mask.py` supplies the matrix-free `O(N)` positive Green applications, exact algebraic `O(N)` inverse of the weak outer, Sinkhorn calibration, and analytic spectral bounds. `phase36_phase_rigidity.py` supplies the phase-rank controls, complex Green gauge identities, the `alpha=exp(pi*i/N)` counterexample, and an **exact rational `N=4` Fourier projective cross-ratio certificate** in `Q(i)` via SymPy. That certificate explicitly has a nonzero imaginary part and so rules out an arbitrary real positive middle bridge for those exact outer matrices and **any** complex masks.

All numerical full-matrix spectral comparisons were completed for `N=4,8,16,32,64`. First-cross calibration passed through `N=65536` with numerical error less than `2e-14`. These numerical checks are sanity controls; the all-size theorems rely on the algebraic proofs above.

**Scope and next target:** No improvement over worst-case `O(N log N)` FFT complexity. Two-mask positive Green bridges can be calibrated, but Fourier phases face a constant spectral gap. Complex cyclic Green operators may have maximal entrywise phase rank at linear cost while still lacking the required cross-ratio spectrum. Phase 37 must synthesize an internal noncommuting **complex** network with the right Fourier cross-ratios and Weyl covariance, not just density, conditioning, or phase rank.
