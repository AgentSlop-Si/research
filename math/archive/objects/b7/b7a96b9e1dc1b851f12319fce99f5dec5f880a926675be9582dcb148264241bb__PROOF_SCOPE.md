## D1. Support geometry, cosets, and the one-parent graph theorem

For radix-2 decimation in time, depth-d affected subproblems are precisely the
occupied residues modulo 2^d; each contributes N/2^d output cells. Therefore
W(S)=sum_d (N/2^d)|S mod 2^d|. Bounding the residue count by min(2^d,k) yields
the stated N(ceil(log2 k)+2) bound, capped by N log2 N. This is graph influence,
not a count of all nontrivial arithmetic or a lower bound on other algorithms.
It is a nonnegative weighted coverage function, so monotonicity/submodularity
follow termwise. Independently traced iterative butterfly influence agrees for
all 256 supports at N8; all 65,536 support pairs satisfy submodularity.

For a correction on r+g*t, with g dividing N and L=N/g, direct substitution
gives delta_hat[q]=omega_N^(rq) z_hat[q mod L]. Finding the maximal common g by
gcd and constructing the L-vector costs work; arbitrary random support often
gives L=N. This is compact correction storage, not free base or dense output.

Under fixed nonnegative symmetric additive edge costs, one parent per output,
and retained outputs usable as parents, an acyclic schedule plus its fresh-source
edges is an augmented spanning tree. Conversely a tree oriented from the source
is a valid schedule. Thus MST optimizes *that supplied objective*. Cache limits,
changing edge costs, shared subkernels and arbitrary algebraic circuits are not
covered. Chain count 1+...+64=2080 versus64 is correct but not a runtime ratio.

## D2. Packing, related-input linearity, and collision barriers

For real a,b, Hermitian symmetry of their transforms gives
A[k]=(Z[k]+conj(Z[-k]))/2 and B[k]=(Z[k]-conj(Z[-k]))/(2i), where
Z=F(a+ib). Padding before squaring Z gives imag(F^-1(Z^2))/2=a*b for linear
convolution. This is real-linear packing and uses conjugation of variable
results; it is not automatically an allowed strictly complex-linear primitive.
Two arbitrary complex vectors cannot be injectively encoded by one fixed linear
map into N complex scalars.

Known linear families, shifts, affine index maps, complements and sparse edits
obey the stated DFT substitutions. Recognition, phase preparation and explicit
outputs remain charged. Fixed rank gives O(rNlogN+MrN), not a guarantee that
arbitrary dense inputs have fixed rank.

Exact hash-consing's butterfly count is sum_m(m/2)U_m. A fixed period bounds
U_m by p. For iid binary inputs, disjoint level blocks are independent uniform
samples among 2^m strings, giving E U_m=2^m[1-(1-2^-m)^(N/m)]. The collision
union bound at m>=3log2N is <=1/(2N) per level. The affine-index/complement orbit
has at most2m phi(m)=m² elements for dyadic m>=2, giving the same threshold via
N²/2^(m+1). Thus almost all large levels remain unreusable in these algorithms.
This is not a lower bound for nonlocal linear networks, nonlinear encodings or
other computational models. Huge prepopulated tables are a distinct time/space
assumption; empty memory is not a free answer oracle.

## D3. Dense-safe rank, covers, and exact skeleton restrictions

The 2n-1 output coefficient bilinear forms of polynomial multiplication are
linearly independent, giving a variable-product rank lower bound2n-1. Enough
distinct evaluation points attain it, while naive evaluation/interpolation
still costs quadratic arithmetic. Karatsuba's three-product identity is exact
and has its familiar field-operation recurrence; neither improves FFT order.

For M affine binary templates of dimension<=r with k-sparse residuals, union
size is at most M 2^r sum_{j<=k} C(n,j). For complex inputs, a finite union of
affine spaces enlarged by k coordinate directions remains a union of proper
affine spaces if r+k<n. The moment-curve argument in row19 correctly produces
a witness outside that finite union. Neither argument excludes input-dependent
subspaces or infinitely parameterized families whose actual costs are charged.

The integer additive-cover criterion is sound: |S+K|<=|S||K|, and both finding
the decomposition and computing the correction must be counted. The signed-term
inequality w(A)<=w(A-k)+w(k) follows by concatenating representations, independent
of the cited NAF optimality theorem. It constrains flat independent shift sums,
not general addition chains or shared/factored arithmetic.

For Fourier rows/columns in arithmetic progression, invertible row/column
scalings remove offsets and leave a Vandermonde base of order N/gcd(N,st).
Rank is min(a,b,N/gcd(N,st)). Independent modular controls checked405 cases.
Grouping consecutive r+1 columns then proves
nnz(F-L)>=(N-r)floor(N/(r+1)) for rank(L)<=r: otherwise r+1 unaffected rows
would agree with an invertible Vandermonde minor. The direct independent-dot
skeleton cost lower bound follows only under its explicitly unshared decoder
and entrywise residual evaluation contract.

For periodic Fourier-column subspaces, each row residue can match at most
gcd(j,q) target values. Summing dyadic valuations gives
sum_{j<q}gcd(j,q)=q(1+log2(q)/2), so the sharp residual count is
N²-Nr(1+log2(q)/2). Thirty independently constructed controls attained it.
The dense half-rank residual still factors as ordinary radix2, so density
cannot be promoted to total arithmetic lower bound.

The bounded-coefficient determinant argument is valid for the unnormalized
DFT and two-input linear gates. Each newly available row increases the maximum
N-row minor by at most2C. The final determinant has modulus N^(N/2), giving
g>=Nlog2N/[2log2(2C)]. It does not apply unchanged to normalized Fourier or
unrestricted coefficient growth.

## D4. Grouped sparse transforms and external-backend composition

Writing N=bm, input t=r+b*l and output q+m*j yields
Y[q+m*j]=DFT_b(V[q,:])[j], where V[q,r] accumulates delta[t]omega_N^(qt).
For b the next power of two above positive k, km<=N; allocation, twiddles,
placement and dense output cost O(N). Thus total work is O(N+(N/b)A(b)),
conditional on an actual length-b exact backend of cost A(b) in the same model.
The new checker verifies132 cases/5,586 output coordinates over F257 using
an independently authored direct DFT, not the external #130 algorithm.

The proposed combined supplied-root order is polynomial if the external
length-b root hypothesis has its stated polynomial bound. This is a valid
conditional reduction, not a validation of the external theorem, its formal
scope, or an implementation. With SciPy inner FFTs the prototype remains
O(N(1+log(k+1))). Constant sparsity with explicit dense output is Theta(N).

## D5. Linearization and exact paired-bit phase states

For a polynomial straight-line circuit, constant and degree-one homogeneous
parts propagate through a product as (c_a c_b,c_a l_b+c_b l_a). Consequently
an s-gate circuit computing a linear map as a polynomial identity gives a
linear circuit of at most3s gates, plus O(s) coefficient preparation. At a
regular rational basepoint, quotient derivatives similarly use at most three
linear gates; preparing reciprocal-derived coefficients also costs O(s), not
merely reading the original gate values. Finding that point or a generic path
can remain nonuniform. Finite equality on binary inputs or F_p does not imply
the polynomial identity, as x²=x or x^p=x demonstrates. The new controls check
150 basis-direction cases of nonlinear-k cancellation; the all-N result is
the algebraic argument, not finite sampling.

For the paired-bit unfolding in row69, removing nonzero row/column phase factors
leaves duplicated w^(ac), a Vandermonde matrix of rank2^min(s,L-s). Every linear
chain state must carry at least this rank. Walsh has unfolding rank one.
At interior cuts s,q>=2, the scaled Walsh left/right vectors lie outside the
Fourier column/row spaces because they vary along duplicated coordinates,
so subtraction increases rank by exactly one. Independent F257 controls at
15 cuts for N4..64 confirm composition and ranks. This is an exact prescribed
chain-width theorem, not a lower bound on all FFT graphs. The quoted dense
contraction cost describes one implementation, not an unavoidable consequence
of rank or an argument against approximate low-bond methods.

## D6. Persistent convolution, projected jets, and cache accounting

The four-kernel fixed-pair update is exact: update A first, then update B using
the newly updated complement. Expansion includes d_a*d_b*R. Gains and shifts
commute with convolution in the displayed way, without dividing by possibly
zero gains. One hundred eighty new exact-integer controls cover signs, zeros,
shifts, sparse collisions and empty-complement identity cases. For many editable
factors the subset-kernel family costs exponentially many maintained kernels;
the two-factor saving does not remove that dimension dependence.

Formal Hasse coefficients of a polynomial in a commutative convolution ring
require no factorial division. Substituting one or two impulse edits yields
the stated shifts and all mixed terms. This validates the formula, not floating
symbolic canonicalization or generated/native machine code. Dense/time versus
selected-frequency outputs and warm versus fresh-process costs remain distinct.

Balanced-tree affected ancestors satisfy sum_l min(2^l,p), hence
O(p(1+log(m/p))) for 1<=p<=m, conditional on relevant cached boundary values.
General DAGs and unbalanced appends do not inherit that bound. Division-free
gap products and disjoint sparse tables preserve zero safety; index storage,
construction and copies are charged separately. Projected Horner jet evaluation
costs O(q(k+d)) after its coefficient projections exist, not free cold setup.
