## 2. Rigidity and the noncommuting inverse-stage gate

If S has at most s nonzeros in each row, put r=floor(N/(s+1)). In r consecutive Fourier rows, at most rs columns are touched by S; at least r columns remain untouched. The resulting r-by-r Fourier minor is Vandermonde on distinct roots, hence nonsingular. Therefore

rank(F_N-S)>=floor(N/(s+1)).

The inverse Fourier matrix has the same result, by changing the root to its inverse and scaling by 1/N. Independent row/column monomial factors preserve the argument by transporting the selected row labels. The bound also covers s=0 and vacuous r=0 cases. It is maximum per-row degree, not merely total sparsity.

For T_i^(-1)=S_i+E_i, put d=product d_i and R=sum r_i. The all-S product has row degree at most d. The correctly ordered telescoping identity compares it to the true inverse product using summands with one E_i and arbitrary factors on either side. Rank subadditivity gives rank(defect)<=R without requiring any factors to commute and without requiring the sparse S_i to be invertible. Applying the preceding Fourier bound gives

R>=floor(N/(1+d)), equivalently N<(1+d)(1+R).

The source's strict integer inequality is correct. Woodbury supplies a valid example of the required inverse decomposition for an invertible low-rank correction to an invertible Green layer; the middle Woodbury matrix is invertible under those assumptions.

For fixed d_i<=d>=2 and r_i<=r, the resulting N<(1+d^L)(1+rL) rules out L=O(log log N), and implies L=Omega(log N). This is a restriction on the specified inverse-representation grammar, not on unrestricted arithmetic circuits or all possible Green sums.

The stronger guess rank(F_N-S)>=N-s is genuinely false. The submitted length-four rank-one subtraction leaves exactly two nonzeros per row; our exact field control confirms it.

## 3. All-square construction: exact work and field scope

For N=A^2>=4, take S to swap tensor indices, C to be an N-cycle, D=diag(1+1/(2N),1,...,1), a=1/(100N), b=1-1/N, epsilon=1/(10^6 N^2), and

G1=(I-aS)^(-1), G2=(I-bDC)^(-1), T=G1+epsilon G2.

The construction and norm claims are over the real/rational, hence complex, numbers. Finite-field regression checks do not inherit positivity, magnitudes, or condition numbers.

Since S^2=I, G1=(I+aS)/(1-a^2) applies in O(N). The weighted Hamiltonian G2 applies by scalar cycle closure followed by back substitution in O(N), with no dense matrix construction. Setup of the weights, cycle product, swap indexing, and closure denominator is O(N) exact scalar work. All scans and output writes must be charged. The source correctly disclaims bit complexity: exact rational intermediate values need not fit fixed-size words.

S has fixed points and two-cycles and is not an N-Hamiltonian cycle. Therefore this is a legitimate sum of two general permutation-Green solvers; it is not a supplied two-Hamiltonian-Green-plan layer. Expanding G1 produces two monomial terms, each representable by a zero-mask Hamiltonian Green plan with suitable endpoints, and G2 supplies a third plan. This gives an immediate three-plan representation, not the stricter two-plan result. No general two-plan non-membership is claimed.

## 4. All-size analytic checks

The weighted cycle B=bDC has row and column absolute sums below one. Its geometric inverse has one positive path contribution in each matrix entry. For N>=4,

b^(N-1)>1/3, b^N<3/8, and D_00<=9/8,

which justify the source's conservative bounds 1/3<(G2)_ij<3. Thus its 1-, infinity-, 2-, and Frobenius-norm upper bounds of 3N are valid.

Let A0=I-aS and H=A0 G2 A0. Expanding entries gives H_ij>1/3-6a>1/4, and ||H||_infinity<=12N. The inverse expansion is

T^(-1)=A0-epsilon H+R,
||R||_infinity<=64 N^2 epsilon^2<epsilon/8.

The order is correct. It follows by expanding (I+epsilon A0G2)^(-1)A0, with ||epsilon A0G2||_infinity<4N epsilon<1/2. Every off-diagonal inverse entry is negative: its base is zero or -a, the first perturbation is below -epsilon/4, and the remainder is smaller than epsilon/8. Every diagonal entry exceeds 1/2. Therefore all inverse entries are nonzero and their magnitudes are at least epsilon/8.

The displayed condition bound

(1/(1-a)+3N epsilon)(1+a)/(1-4N epsilon)<2

is also valid. It follows from the spectral norm bounds directly, not from an unjustified equality of spectral and infinity norms.

For the realignment map, R(S) is a permutation and R(S)^T R(I)=vv^T, with v^T v=A. Thus the minimum singular value of R(G1) is exactly a/(1-a^2). The perturbation has spectral norm at most epsilon||G2||_F<=3N epsilon, smaller than that gap. Hence R(T) is nonsingular and balanced operator-Schmidt rank is exactly N. This proof remains valid for every square length, including non-dyadic squares.

Finally G1 maps ones to a constant, whereas G2 cannot: a constant putative solution of (I-bDC)y=ones contradicts the unequal diagonal entries of D. Thus T ones is nonconstant.

All five advertised properties of this particular source family are accepted. They are structural properties of a cheap operator, not evidence of Fourier phase compatibility.

## 5. The near-identity limitation is decisive for copies of this family

The direct estimate is

||T-I||_2<=a/(1-a)+3N epsilon=O(1/N).

A product of L copies, or of factors satisfying the same norm bound, differs from I by at most (1+O(1/N))^L-1. For L=O(log log N), this tends to zero. By contrast, the normalized Fourier transform differs from I by at least its effect on e0, namely sqrt(2-2/sqrt(N))>=1 for N>=4. Thus such short products cannot give the normalized Fourier transform for growing N.

This argument does not apply after inserting arbitrary unaccounted endpoint maps that destroy the near-identity premise. The source appropriately leaves a substantially different Fourier-compatible construction open.

## 6. A stronger independent sparse-plus-rank-one countercontrol

Even the conjunction of all five structural properties above does not prove an inverse-rigidity separation. This can be shown uniformly for every square N>=4.

Retain S and a=1/(100N), set epsilon=1/(10^6 N^2), u=ones+e0, and define

A0=I-aS,
T_star=(A0+epsilon u ones^T)^(-1).

Then T_star^(-1) is exactly row-2-sparse plus rank one. Nevertheless:

- Every inverse entry is nonzero. Diagonals are positive. Off-diagonals are epsilon u_i or -a+epsilon u_i, and epsilon u_i<=2epsilon<a.
- Sherman-Morrison gives T_star by one G1 action plus a rank-one correction, with O(N) setup and fresh-input application.
- Writing delta=a+2N epsilon bounds ||T_star^(-1)-I||_2<=delta. Hence kappa_2(T_star)<=(1+delta)/(1-delta)<2 and ||T_star-I||_2<=delta/(1-delta)=O(1/N).
- T_star ones is nonconstant. Otherwise ones would be a scalar multiple of T_star^(-1)ones=(1-a)ones+epsilon N u, which is nonconstant.
- The Sherman-Morrison perturbation from G1 has Frobenius norm at most 2N epsilon/(1-a)^2<4N epsilon. This is smaller than a/(1-a^2), so the same realignment-gap proof gives Schmidt rank exactly N.

This is a counterexample to an inference about the structural certificates, not a claim that the source's specific G1+epsilon G2 has a rank-one inverse defect. Its purpose is to demonstrate what remains unproved: a bound against every sparse baseline requires an actual rank-rigidity argument, not just dense support or full realignment rank.

The source sentence saying the theorem does not apply to sums of Green solvers is too broad. It applies to any invertible layer for which the required inverse sparse-plus-low-rank decomposition exists. Such a decomposition is simply not guaranteed by the summed-Green syntax. The particular source construction has not been proved far from every fixed-degree sparse-plus-fixed-rank baseline.

## 7. Exact next-search identity

For invertible A=G1^(-1), B=G2^(-1), the identity G1+G2=F_N is equivalent to A F_N B=A+B. The order is correct: A(G1+G2)B=B+A.

For row-permutation denominators, the source's four-root residual uses sigma^(-1)(j) on the right factor and the associated weight b_(sigma^(-1)(j)); the indexing is correct. The reviewer independently compared all entries with full matrix multiplication. This is an exact algebraic verification/search gate, not an all-length solution or a proof that all parameter choices fail.

Scalar-weighted permutation Green sums/products map ones to a constant and therefore cannot equal F_N for N>1. Weighted denominators avoid that elementary invariant but do not establish the Fourier certificate.
