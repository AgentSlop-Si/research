# Paired Fourier rank gates and a denominator-free dual — proof certificate

**Date:** October 10, 2026. **Model:** fixed `AFFINE_LAYOUT.json`, size `N=256`, exact complex arithmetic, normalized DFT `U=F_256/16`, four arbitrary *invertible* two-sided order-one quasiseparable factors, with zero entries/generators permitted. No coefficient boundedness or unitary assumption except where expressly indicated.

## 1. Setup and the paired rank theorem

Suppose

\[
U=S_4P_3S_3P_2S_2P_1S_1.
\]

As `U` is invertible, every factor `S_j` is invertible. Let `C4=S4^-1`, `C1=S1^-1`, and

\[
H=P_3^{-1}C_4UC_1P_1^{-1}=S_3P_2S_2.
\]

For every nonzero eight-bit parity mask `m`, write `g_m(i)=1` if the parity of the set bits of `i&m` is even, and define

\[
C_m=\{i:g_m(i)=1\},\quad
R_m=\{i:g_m(P_2[i])=0\}.
\]

These sets have 128 elements each. The proven moving-boundary rank bound for a product of two quasiseparable order-one stages gives

\[
\operatorname{rank}H[R_m,C_m]\le b_m:=d(g_m)+d(g_m\circ P_2),
\]

where `d` counts natural-order membership transitions. Replacing `g_m` by its complement does not change either transition count. It exchanges **both** selected row and column sets with their complements. Therefore

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}H[R_m,C_m]\le b_m,\qquad
\operatorname{rank}H[R_m^c,C_m^c]\le b_m.}
\]

Exactly 32 masks have `b_m<128`, giving **64 necessary matrix-block conditions**. The previous archive recorded one block for each mask, not both. The strongest mask is `m=64`, with `b_m=3+13=16`.

This proof holds for the entire quasiseparable-order-one class represented by scalar forward/backward recurrences. No cancellation of nonzero generator values or implicit division is used. The verifier includes general recurrent generators with exact zeros, not merely the globally separated chart.

## 2. An invertibility consequence for the cross-blocks

Reorder the rows of `H` as `(R,R^c)` and the columns as `(C,C^c)`, obtaining

\[
\widetilde H=\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\C'&D\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\operatorname{rank}A,\operatorname{rank}D\le b.
\]

Since `H` is invertible, each set of 128 columns of `\widetilde H` is independent. The left 128 columns imply

`128 = rank([A;C']) <= rank(A)+rank(C')`, hence `rank(C')>=128-b`.

The right 128 columns similarly give `rank(B)>=128-b`. At mask 64, **both opposite cross-blocks have rank at least 112**. This is a requirement of any *complete* Fourier factorization (not a claim for singular middle-stage controls).

## 3. The two normalized Fourier blocks have identical singular values

Let

\[
H_0=P_3^{-1}UP_1^{-1}.
\]

`H_0` is unitary. Use exactly the same row/column partitions and write its four blocks as `(A0,B0;C0,D0)`. Unitarity implies

\[
A_0^*A_0+C_0^*C_0=I,\quad
C_0C_0^*+D_0D_0^*=I.
\]

The two square matrices `C0*C0` and `C0 C0*` have identical eigenvalue multisets; therefore so do `A0*A0` and `D0 D0*`. Consequently, the singular-value lists of **A0 and D0 are identical**. This is a special case of the classical cosine–sine decomposition of a unitary matrix.

Each selected block is 128×128, has entries of modulus `1/16`, squared Frobenius norm `128²/256=64`, and spectral norm at most 1. The old exact rational moment certificate gives the stronger, *strict* bound for mask64:

\[
\sigma_{17}(A_0)=\sigma_{17}(D_0)>3\sqrt2/5.
\]

## 4. New paired Frobenius- and trace-norm obstruction

For arbitrary diagonal unitary `D4,D1`, let

\[
E=C_4UC_1-D_4UD_1.
\]

After the fixed permutations, the baseline `D4 U D1` is `H0` with row and column phases, so its **two** selected diagonal blocks have exactly the singular values just proved. The candidate matrix `H` has rank at most 16 in **both** blocks.

The Eckart–Young singular-value approximation theorem yields, for either selected block,

\[
\|\Delta_{\rm block}\|_F^2\ge\sum_{j=17}^{128}\sigma_j^2
\ge\sum_{j=1}^{128}\sigma_j^2-16\ge64-16=48.
\]

The two blocks occupy disjoint sets of matrix entries, so their squares add. As row/column permutations do not change unitarily invariant norms,

\[
\boxed{\|C_4UC_1-D_4UD_1\|_F^2\ge96,\quad
\|C_4UC_1-D_4UD_1\|_F\ge4\sqrt6.}
\]

For the nuclear/trace norm `||.||_*`, pinching a 2×2 block matrix to its two diagonal blocks is contractive: the pinching equals `(X+JXJ)/2` with `J=diag(I,-I)` unitary. The trace norm of either diagonal-block error is at least `sum_(j>16) sigma_j` by the nuclear-norm Eckart–Young–Mirsky inequality; this is at least `sum_(j>16) sigma_j²>=48` because the Fourier block's singular values are in `[0,1]`. Thus

\[
\boxed{\|C_4UC_1-D_4UD_1\|_*\ge96.}
\]

These norms are different from the prior operator-norm bound `>3sqrt2/5`: the paired argument strengthens the Frobenius/trace-norm restrictions, **not** the general operator-norm threshold.

For the single paired rank constraint alone, the numerical best rank-16 truncation of both Fourier diagonal blocks gives squared Frobenius error approximately `96.60764190`; this is a non-proof numerical observation, and the proven bound is `96`.

## 5. Exact global energy-relaxation saturation: a useful negative result

Treat each of the 64 necessary block-rank statements **only** through the elementary scalar energy inequality

\[
\sum_{(i,j)\in R_m\times C_m}|E_{ij}|^2\ge (64-b_m)_+
\]

and its complementary analogue, expressed in common outer row/column coordinates. These are necessary inequalities; the relaxed `|E_ij|²` are merely nonnegative formal cell energies.

The strongest pair at mask64 involves disjoint 128×128 rectangles and already forces total cell energy `>=96`.

A **matching relaxation witness** assigns cell energy exactly `3/1024` to every entry of these two rectangles and zero elsewhere. There are 32768 marked cells; the total assigned energy is exactly `96`. Each of the other **62** mask rectangles intersects the marked support in exactly **8192** cells, so it receives exactly `24` units of assigned energy. Its scalar minimum requirement is at most `17` (only seven masks even have a positive requirement).

Thus **all 64 scalar Frobenius-energy inequalities are simultaneously feasible at total energy 96**. Merely weighting/summing these scalar inequalities can never prove a larger lower bound. Any further progress must use **singular-vector directions, minors, joint coefficient equations, or cross-block geometry**. This relaxation witness is not a valid four-stage Fourier factorization.

## 6. Denominator-free inverse-block duality

By the standard complementary nullity/Jacobi theorem, for every invertible square `H` and equally sized half-sets `R,C`,

\[
\operatorname{rank}H[R,C]
=\operatorname{rank}H^{-1}[C^c,R^c].
\]

Since

\[
H^{-1}=P_1S_1U^{-1}S_4P_3,
\]

the 64 previous equations admit an **equivalent dual necessary system** in the *original outer stages*, without matrix inverse variables:

\[
G(S_1,S_4):=P_1S_1U^{-1}S_4P_3,
\]

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}G[C_m^c,R_m^c]\le b_m,\quad
\operatorname{rank}G[C_m,R_m]\le b_m\quad(m\text{ among 32}).}
\]

`G` is **bilinear in the actual matrix entries of S1 and S4**, with fixed cyclotomic coefficients. Each rank statement is equivalent to the vanishing of all `(b_m+1)`-minors. These are polynomial equations without dividing by any stage generator. Quasiseparability of `S1,S4` can be imposed separately using their standard recurrent scalar-generator representation, which allows zero transfer coefficients; invertibility remains a necessary open condition. This system is **necessary, not sufficient** for the full four-stage factorization.

At the identity outer stages, both 17×17 minors of this dual system are nonzero modulo each of `12289` and `65537`; the exact residues are in `NEW_CERTIFICATES.json`. Therefore these constraints are nontrivial over characteristic zero. A nonzero evaluation modulo a prime with a primitive 256th root certifies nonzero evaluation over `Q(zeta_256)`.

## 7. Additional *conditional* unitary-outer-stage geometry

This is **not an assumption in the main research model**. If *both* `C4` and `C1` happen to be unitary, then `H` is unitary. Let `V=D4 U D1` be any diagonal-unitary baseline. The block `H[R,C]` has rank at most 16, so its kernel inside the 128-dimensional input coordinate subspace has dimension at least 112. By the min–max principle for `V[R,C]`, that kernel contains a unit vector `x` with `||P_R Vx||>=sigma_17(V[R,C])>3sqrt2/5`. Yet `P_R Hx=0` and `||Hx||=||Vx||=1`.

The closest unit vector supported in `R^c` to `Vx` has Euclidean distance at least

\[
\sqrt{2\left(1-\sqrt{1-\|P_RVx\|^2}\right)}
>\sqrt{2-2\sqrt7/5}\approx0.970412.
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{\|C_4UC_1-D_4UD_1\|_2>\sqrt{2-2\sqrt7/5}.}
\]

If the two *unitary* outer factors both lie within `epsilon` of diagonal unitaries, the triangle inequality bounds the left side by `2 epsilon`, forcing

\[
\boxed{\epsilon>\tfrac12\sqrt{2-2\sqrt7/5}\approx0.485206.}
\]

This is a stronger radius than the earlier `0.359606` **only in the additional exactly-unitary outer-stage subclass**. General nonunitary outer stages remain unresolved.

## 8. Verification and limitations

`verify_paired_gate.py` validates:

- 64/64 Fourier primal block ranks and 64/64 Jacobi-dual block ranks in **each** of two exact finite fields;
- 64/64 primal–dual rank equalities for a synthetic *invertible* middle product in each field, using fully independent scalar quasiseparable recurrence generators with explicit zeros;
- 14/14 zero-generator/identity-stage complementary rank checks in each field;
- exact nonzero characteristic-zero witnesses via 17×17 modular determinants on both primal and dual blocks;
- exactly 62 overlap counts, each 8192, as the energy-relaxation witness;
- the singular-value pairing at mask64 numerically as a non-proof sanity check;
- the sharp 2×2 rotation sanity check for the conditional unitary gap.

**Not proved:** Fourier nonexistence for the full four-stage architecture; simultaneous complex algebraic infeasibility of the dual polynomial system; new all-length or sub-`N log N` Fourier transform; floating-point numerical speedup; optimal stage depth in a general computational model.
