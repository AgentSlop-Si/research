# Independent interval-window audit and bounded extension

9 October 2026. Source: `fft_window_rank_gate_2026-10-09.zip`, Library ID `[private-id]`, current materialized version 0. The uploaded programs were read as source and never executed or imported. The independently authored checks use the Python standard library only.

## Accepted result and exact endpoint scope

The general three-sided window lemma is correct. The all-length consequence `N <= 9r^2+9r+3` is also correct, **only** when Fourier input columns follow a natural or affine coprime-stride order; output rows may be arbitrarily permuted. The source correctly supplies and explains this limitation. No exact arbitrary-input faster transform is established.

Write `B=M C[:,pi]` with invertible C. For a B-column window I=[u,v], let t=|I|, J=pi(I), a=min J, b=max J, H=[a,b], and R=complement(I). Let cu,cd and bu,bd bound the upper-right/lower-left quasiseparable ranks of C and B. Put beta=(u>0)bu+(v<N-1)bd.

For any support interval K containing J, the map L which stacks restriction outside K with M restricted to rows R has kernel dimension `|K|-rank(M[R,K])`. Its restriction to the t-dimensional column space C[:,J] therefore has rank at least `t-|K|+rank(M[R,K])`. The two stacked images have rank at most beta plus the appropriate outside-C rank. Taking K=[a,N-1], [0,b], or [a,b] proves the three source formulas. This derivation actually does not need M to be invertible; invertible C and the stated rank budgets suffice.

For `M[i,k]=omega^(rho(i)(c+d*k))` with gcd(d,N)=1, each M[R,K] has consecutive exponents and distinct row bases, so its rank is min(|R|,|K|) over any field containing a primitive Nth root. The formulas become

- min(t,a) <= beta+(a>0)cu
- min(t,N-1-b) <= beta+(b<N-1)cd
- min(t,N-1-b+a) <= beta+(a>0)cu+(b<N-1)cd

Scalar Fourier normalization does not affect these ranks. Inverse factors preserve the same oriented quasiseparable ranks by the complementary-block inverse rank identity, so a two-invertible-stage factorization can be rewritten in this form. Outside nonzero diagonal gauges preserve ranks; arbitrary endpoint input permutations do not preserve the Vandermonde substitution.

## Source counting argument

Let m=cd+bu+bd+1. Every interior B window of m columns must meet the last m C indices; otherwise the tail inequality fails. There are N-m-1 such windows when this is positive, and each of the m indices belongs to at most m windows. Thus N<=m^2+m+1. The head argument uses cu instead of cd. With all four bounds <=r this yields the source's N<=9r^2+9r+3. Small sizes with no such windows satisfy the displayed bound automatically.

## Stronger independent consequence: N <= 9r^2+7r

Assume r>=1 bounds all four ranks and the same Fourier endpoint condition holds. Let m=3r+1 and let S be the positions in pi of the last m C indices. If N<=m the claimed bound is immediate, so suppose N>m. There are m marked positions s1<...<sm.

1. The initial window of length 2r+1 must meet the last 2r+1 C indices, by the tail inequality with beta<=r. Consequently s1<=2r.
2. The final window of length 2r+1 gives sm>=N-2r-1.
3. Every gap between consecutive marked positions is <=m. Otherwise m unmarked consecutive positions strictly between those markers form an interior window violating the tail inequality.

Hence `N-4r-1 <= sm-s1 <= (m-1)m`, or `N <= 9r^2+7r+1`.

If equality held, all inequalities would be equalities, forcing the marked positions to be exactly

`2r, 2r+m, ..., 2r+(m-1)m`.

The identical head argument forces the positions of the first m C indices to be the very same set. At this equality size N>2m, the first and last m label sets are disjoint, so their position sets cannot coincide. Equality is impossible. Therefore

`N <= 9r^2+7r`.

For r=1 this gives N<=16, improving this source's cutoff N<=21. The source permutation

`[8,0,13,4,9,3,15,5,6,1,12,11,10,2,14,7]`

passes every rank-one window inequality at N=16. Thus 16 is the largest length allowed by the complete rank-one window filter. **This is sharpness of the necessary filter, not a construction or an existence theorem for a two-stage factorization.** No publication-priority claim is made. The result does not supersede different stronger restrictions that may hold for narrower stage families.

## Exact linear-time all-window screening

The uploaded screen iterates O(N^2) windows. For fixed oriented rank bounds it is exactly equivalent to at most six endpoint-window checks and three sliding-window passes:

- A head violation with beta fixed occurs precisely when both t and a exceed beta+cu. It therefore has a violating subwindow of length beta+cu+1.
- A tail violation similarly has a violating subwindow of length beta+cd+1.
- If the label hull meets either label endpoint, the both-sided inequality is identical to its one-sided counterpart. Otherwise a both-sided violation has a violating subwindow of length beta+cu+cd+1, since shrinking the window cannot enlarge its label hull.

For prefix windows retain a prefix of the indicated length (beta=bd); for suffix windows retain a suffix (beta=bu); for interior windows take an interior subwindow (beta=bu+bd). If a required length is larger than its window class permits, that class cannot violate that test. Sliding minima and maxima computed by monotone deques take O(N) comparisons and O(N) worst-case storage over the three interior passes. The six endpoint scans also total O(N). The algorithm works for arbitrary nonnegative constant-per-cut rank bounds, including zero. This is a faster **candidate filter**, not a Fourier evaluator.

The independent checker agrees with direct all-window enumeration on all 40,320 N=8 permutations, another 1,080 random cases at sizes 2 through 10 and varying asymmetric ranks, and 120 larger random cases, totaling 41,520 exact equivalences. Those experiments are controls; the subwindow argument proves the reduction for all lengths.

## Arbitrary endpoints and exact positive controls

An arbitrary input permutation requires actual ranks in `t-|K|+rank(M[R,K])`. The source's N=8 and N=16 Cooley–Tukey positive controls were reconstructed independently from

`j=s+a*t`, `k=q+b*ell`, and `omega^(jk)=omega^(s*q) omega_a^(s*ell) omega_b^(t*q)`.

The checks verify every matrix entry over GF(12289), both oriented ranks equal one for the two stages and the inverse stage, and every window inequality (108+408=516 exact inequalities). In the initial three-column windows, actual ranks are 4 and 8, whereas the inappropriate full-Vandermonde replacements would be 5 and 9. The corrected lower bound is 2 and equals the rank budget; the naive lower bound 3 would falsely exclude valid classical factorizations. These controls are not new Fourier algorithms and do not have the claimed endpoint scope of the simplified theorem.

The independent N=8 enumeration reproduces 28,584 old-screen survivors, 15,728 full-window survivors, and 12,856 additional rejections. No new-screen survivor fails the old screen. Additional controls include 128 rectangular Fourier ranks with affine inputs and arbitrary outputs over two fields, and all windows of 16 independent invertible diagonal-plus-rank-one stage pairs. See CHECK_RESULTS.json for the exact count.

## Consequence for the active construction problem

In the stated natural/affine input endpoint model, two rank-one quasiseparable stages cannot scale beyond N=16. Reusing an N=8 or N=16 solution as a universal two-stage template is therefore blocked before solving its coefficients. A construction must enlarge rank, increase stage count, use a genuinely non-affine input order, or leave this architecture. Arbitrary endpoint reorderings remain subject to the actual-submatrix-rank test and full Fourier equality; passing a rank gate does not demonstrate efficient application.

These are exact field/rank statements. Root preparation, coefficient representation, inversions and bit precision are separate costs. The audit's dense exact Gaussian eliminations are verification work and make no fast-evaluator claim. The O(N) filter works on a supplied permutation/rank tuple using comparisons and indexing; it neither discovers stage coefficients nor executes their transform. No unrestricted FFT lower bound, uniform sub-N-log-N evaluator, or exact integer-multiplication improvement follows.
