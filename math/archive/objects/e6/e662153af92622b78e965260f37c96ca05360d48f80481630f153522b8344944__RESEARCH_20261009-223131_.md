# Simultaneous-order rank gate for exact Green banks — v18

**Checkpoint:** 10 October 2026  
**Primary target:** exact all-output DFT of an arbitrary fresh input in `o(N log N)` fully charged sequential arithmetic, ideally `O(N)`.  
**Status:** a new scoped impossibility theorem for sums of independently ordered cyclic Green resolvents, with exact finite-field certificates. **No faster exact FFT is constructed.** The v15 randomized approximate all-length branch is unchanged.

## 1. Starting point

v17 excluded a sum of **two** independently ordered, arbitrarily diagonally gauged Green resolvents at prime transform lengths. The strongest surviving variant was

\[
F_N=\sum_{r=1}^{t}L_r(I-\alpha_rP_r)^{-1}R_r,
\]

with several independent cycle orders `P_r`. Every term is dense, full-rank and applicable in `O(N)` arithmetic by a cyclic first-order solve. A constant-size bank would therefore give an exact `O(N)` DFT if the identity existed.

v18 replaces the two-term-specific sparse identity by a rank rectangle that works for **all `t` simultaneously**.

---

## 2. Cyclic Green kernels

Let `P` be an `N`-cycle, and let `rho(i)` be the position of coordinate `i` around that cycle. For `alpha^N != 1`,

\[
G(P,\alpha)=(I-\alpha P)^{-1}
=\frac1{1-\alpha^N}\sum_{m=0}^{N-1}\alpha^mP^m.
\]

Thus, up to the harmless convention for the orientation of `P`,

\[
G(P,\alpha)_{ij}
=\frac{\alpha^{d_P(i,j)}}{1-\alpha^N},
\]

where `d_P(i,j)` is the directed cycle distance from `j` to `i`.

Suppose row set `A` and column set `B` are separated in the cycle order: either

\[
\rho(a)<\rho(b)\quad\text{for all }a\in A,b\in B,
\]

or the reverse inequality holds everywhere. Then the wrap decision in `d_P(a,b)` is constant on the whole rectangle. Consequently

\[
G(P,\alpha)[A,B]=u_Av_B^T
\]

or is identically zero when `alpha=0`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}G(P,\alpha)[A,B]\le1.}
\]

Arbitrary nonzero diagonal gauges on the left and right do not change this rank bound.

## 3. Simultaneous separation lemma

### Lemma 3.1

Let `prec_1,...,prec_t` be `t` total orders on the same `N`-element set. There exist disjoint sets `A,B`, of equal size

\[
\boxed{|A|=|B|\ge\left\lfloor\frac{N}{2^t}\right\rfloor,}
\]

such that, for every order `r`, either every element of `A` precedes every element of `B`, or every element of `B` precedes every element of `A`.

### Proof

Use the first order to split the set into a lower half `A` and an upper half `B` of equal size.

Assume equal-size `A,B` are already separated in the first `r-1` orders. Sort `A union B` in order `r` and take its first half. If this prefix contains `x` elements of `A`, then the suffix contains exactly `x` elements of `B`. The opposite orientation has `m-x` elements on both sides, where `m=|A|=|B|`. Keep the larger orientation, of size at least `ceil(m/2)`. If the labels `A,B` are swapped, all earlier separation orientations merely reverse and remain valid.

After all `t` orders, the retained size is at least `floor(N/2^t)`. QED.

This proof is constructive and is implemented by `simultaneous_separated_sets`.

## 4. Main theorem: an exact Green bank needs almost `log_2 N` terms

Let `N` be prime and let `F_N` be the complex `N x N` Fourier matrix. The statement is invariant under arbitrary endpoint row/column permutations and arbitrary nonzero endpoint diagonal scalings.

### Theorem 4.1 — Hamiltonian Green-bank obstruction

Suppose

\[
M=\sum_{r=1}^{t}L_r(I-\alpha_rP_r)^{-1}R_r,
\]

where every `P_r` is an `N`-cycle, `alpha_r^N != 1`, and every `L_r,R_r` is invertible diagonal. If `M` is monomially equivalent to `F_N`, then

\[
\boxed{\left\lfloor\frac{N}{2^t}\right\rfloor\le t.}
\]

Equivalently, if

\[
\boxed{N\ge2^t(t+1),}
\]

then no such representation exists.

### Proof

Apply Lemma 3.1 to the `t` cycle-position orders. Obtain separated sets `A,B` of size

\[
s\ge\left\lfloor\frac{N}{2^t}\right\rfloor.
\]

Every Green summand has rank at most one on `A x B`, hence

\[
\operatorname{rank}M[A,B]\le t.
\]

For prime `N`, Chebotarev's Fourier-minor theorem says every square minor of `F_N` is nonzero. Endpoint permutations and invertible diagonal scalings preserve this property. Therefore

\[
\operatorname{rank}M[A,B]=s.
\]

If `s>t`, contradiction. QED.

### Corollary 4.2 — number of required orders

Any Hamiltonian Green-bank representation must satisfy

\[
N<2^t(t+1).
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{t\ge\log_2N-\log_2\log_2N-O(1).}
\]

In particular, a constant number of independent Green orders cannot scale to an unbounded prime-length Fourier family.

### Concrete consequences

- `t=3` is impossible for every prime `N>=37`.
- `t=4` is impossible for every prime `N>=83`.
- `t=5` is impossible for every prime `N>=193`.
- `t=8` is already impossible from prime `N=2309` onward.

The exact universal threshold is the displayed `2^t(t+1)` condition; the prime examples use the first prime at or above it.

## 5. Permutations with a bounded number of cycles

Let `P_r` have `c_r` cycles. Partition the coordinate set by the tuple of cycle labels across all `t` permutations. One cell has size at least

\[
\frac{N}{\prod_{r=1}^{t}c_r}.
\]

Inside that cell, each permutation supplies one total cyclic order. Applying Lemma 3.1 gives a rectangle of size at least

\[
\left\lfloor
\frac{N}{2^t\prod_rc_r}
\right\rfloor.
\]

Every term again has rank at most one there.

### Theorem 5.1 — bounded-cycle extension

A monomial Fourier representation by `t` diagonally gauged permutation Green terms requires

\[
\boxed{
\left\lfloor
\frac{N}{2^t\prod_rc_r}
\right\rfloor\le t.
}
\]

If every `c_r<=c` for a fixed constant `c`, then

\[
N<(2c)^t(t+1),
\]

so the number of terms still satisfies `t=Omega(log N)`.

The theorem weakens when individual permutations contain a growing number of cycles. It makes no claim for a bank whose cycle-count product is itself exponentially large.

---

## 6. Arithmetic implication and its exact scope

A literal executor that applies every dense Green term independently performs `Theta(N)` arithmetic per term. Theorem 4.1 then implies

\[
\boxed{
\Theta(tN)=\Omega(N\log N)
}
\]

up to the lower-order `N log log N` correction in the term count.

Therefore an exact Fourier speedup cannot arise by merely summing and separately evaluating a bank of independently ordered Hamiltonian Green scans.

This is **not** a lower bound against a genuinely joint evaluator. A future construction could, in principle, process `Theta(log N)` cycle orders with shared arithmetic costing `o(N log N)`. v18 proves only that the representation cannot use a constant or sublogarithmic number of independently ordered Green terms.

## 7. Exact verification

### Deterministic construction tests

`test_checkpoint.py` checks the separation lemma across random order families through six simultaneous orders and sizes through 257.

For two orders at `N=8`, the first order is fixed and **all 40,320** second orders are exhausted. The smallest separated pair has size exactly two, matching `floor(8/4)`.

### Finite-field rank certificates

For `t=2,...,8`, exact modular certificates were generated at:

| `t` | prime `N` | certificate field | separated size | Green-bank rank | Fourier rank |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2 | 13 | 53 | 3 | 2 | 3 |
| 3 | 37 | 149 | 6 | 3 | 6 |
| 4 | 83 | 167 | 7 | 4 | 7 |
| 5 | 193 | 773 | 8 | 5 | 8 |
| 6 | 449 | 3593 | 9 | 6 | 9 |
| 7 | 1031 | 2063 | 13 | 7 | 13 |
| 8 | 2309 | 32327 | 13 | 8 | 13 |

Every individual Green rectangle had exact rank one; every summed rectangle had rank at most `t`; every selected Fourier determinant was nonzero in its certificate field.

The finite-field nonvanishing is an independent reproducibility check for the selected instances. The all-prime complex theorem itself uses Chebotarev and does **not** infer complex nonvanishing from those finite fields.

`independent_verifier.py` reimplements the permutation generation, simultaneous separation, Green kernel and modular elimination without importing the main module. It reproduces all seven rank certificates exactly.

### Bounded-cycle checks

Additional deterministic checks cover banks with:

- two permutations, three cycles each at `N=109`;
- three permutations, two cycles each at `N=257`;
- four permutations, two cycles each at `N=1297`.

The observed common cells and separated rectangles were substantially larger than the universal lower bound.

## 8. Limitations

v18 does **not** establish:

- an unrestricted FFT lower bound;
- a lower bound for products of Green stages;
- a lower bound for sums with a growing cycle-count product;
- an obstruction to approximate Fourier representations;
- a bit-complexity or numerical-stability theorem;
- impossibility of a joint evaluator for `Theta(log N)` Green terms;
- a theorem for every composite transform length.

The result is exact for prime complex Fourier matrices because every prime Fourier minor is nonzero.

---

## 9. Next exact gate

The constant-term Green-sum route is now closed asymptotically. The remaining viable Green direction is much narrower:

> Construct a genuinely joint program for `Theta(log N)` noncommuting cycle orders whose total arithmetic is `o(N log N)`, or prove that their separated rank-one rectangles force an arithmetic bottleneck beyond mere representation count.

The other surviving direction is an `O(N)`-solvable inverse with multiple off-diagonal monomial graphs, followed by a product architecture that is not reducible to a bounded Green bank.
