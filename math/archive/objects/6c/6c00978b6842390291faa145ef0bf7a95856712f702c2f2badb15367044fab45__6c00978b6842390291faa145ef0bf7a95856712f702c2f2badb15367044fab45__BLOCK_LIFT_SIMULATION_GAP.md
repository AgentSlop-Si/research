# Block-lift superconcentrator: a precise obstruction to the local proof route

Independent static audit, 9 October 2026. No uploaded code was executed or imported.

## Finding

The all-r claim in the new nonlinear checkpoint remains open in this audit. A capacity-r dilation of the 8-coordinate superconcentrator is not automatically simulated by replacing each macro coordinate with r consecutive scalar coordinates in the uploaded ordered scan graph. The natural local-simulation lemma is false.

This is a gap in one possible proof, not a counterexample to the complete two-stage block-lift conjecture. A complete proof could globally reroute its flows so that the bad local configurations below never occur.

## Explicit local counterexample

Take any block of r consecutive coordinates with r >= 2. Its forward and backward registers have capacity r, exactly as in `prior_checkpoint/scan_flow.py`. Prescribe the following local boundary and scalar-port flows:

- r units enter the forward register at the block's left boundary;
- r units leave the forward register at its right boundary;
- no flow enters or leaves either backward boundary;
- one further unit enters the scalar input at the first coordinate;
- one unit must leave the scalar output at the last coordinate;
- every other scalar input/output carries zero flow.

The collapsed capacity-r macro-coordinate gadget can carry these flows: send all r forward units straight through and connect the additional scalar input to the scalar output along the local diagonal edge.

The expanded ordered block cannot. Across any interior prefix that contains the first coordinate but not the last, there are r+1 units of supply and no scalar demand. Backward crossing cannot help carry positive net flow to the right. Consequently at least r+1 units must cross the forward register at that prefix, whose capacity is only r.

Equivalently, the pertinent local source-to-sink cut has capacity r while the required flow is r+1. The result already holds at r=2: two through-flows plus a first-to-last local transfer require three units across the internal forward register of capacity two.

## General necessary prefix inequality

Let a be forward flow entering the left boundary, d backward flow leaving that boundary, I_q the scalar input flow introduced in a block prefix of length q, and O_q the scalar output flow removed from that prefix. If f_q and b_q are the forward and backward flows crossing its right boundary, conservation gives

    f_q - b_q = a - d + I_q - O_q.

Because 0 <= f_q,b_q <= r, every realizable expansion must satisfy

    -r <= a - d + I_q - O_q <= r

at every interior prefix. The collapsed macro gadget checks no such interior inequalities. In the counterexample the middle expression is r+1.

Thus any constructive proof based on a dilated 8-node routing must additionally choose or reroute that routing so that these prefix demands are feasible inside every expanded block of both stages. Endpoint capacity counts alone do not establish this.

## What remains valid

- Scaling a superconcentrator's capacities by r gives the appropriate aggregate routing relaxation.
- The supplied finite-r optimization results remain computational evidence for the actual expanded graph.
- Neither the failed local-simulation lemma nor its explicit counterexample disproves universal superconcentration of the full lifted two-stage network: interstage flows and boundary loads are free variables there.
- The original checkpoint correctly leaves the all-r theorem unproved. No Fourier feasibility or arithmetic-complexity conclusion follows from this gap.
