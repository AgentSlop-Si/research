# FFT Green research — Phase 23

**Goal:** continue the user-directed research into Fourier computation sharing and Green networks, without claiming that a special-input shortcut beats the worst-case FFT.

**Main result:** from Phase 22's exact covariance `C Z=R C`, a single `O(N)` block Green solve evaluates the **real orthogonal Fourier residual** on any geometric sequence. A sum of `r` such sequences costs `O(rN)` field arithmetic, including all outputs. An exact `O(N)` detector chooses a Green path for rank <= 2 exponential input or <= 2 cyclic changes/kinks (first or second differences), and otherwise executes the usual `O(N log N)` FFT fallback.

**Main barrier:** sums of `r` geometric sequences have Hankel rank at most `r`. Over binary inputs, the classical exact finite-field Hankel count shows that at least half of uniform inputs require at least `N/2` terms in any such representation over complex numbers, despite abundant short-block collisions. This limits this architecture; it is not an arbitrary FFT lower bound.

**Additional result:** the inverse real residual `C^T` has `N-1` nonzeros in every odd-index row, yielding a restricted sparse-Green-product depth bound. This is an extension of a familiar Phase 15 argument to the Phase 22 residual.
