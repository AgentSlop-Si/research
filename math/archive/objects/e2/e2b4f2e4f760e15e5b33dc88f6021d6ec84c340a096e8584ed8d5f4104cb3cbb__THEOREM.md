# A binary-tree counterexample to uniformly bounded ordered separation rank

10 October 2026 UTC. New research addressing the explicit structural strictness gate. This document is a proof proposal with independent audit pending; it does not change any earlier checkpoint disposition or certify publication priority.

## Result

**There is no uniform constant bound.** For every integer h ≥ 0, let T_h be the complete rooted binary tree of height h, with N_h = 2^(h+1) − 1 vertices. Index rows and columns by its vertices and define

    P_h[u,v] = 1 if u is the parent of v, and 0 otherwise,
    Q_h = I − P_h,                 B_h = Q_h^(-1).

Over every field, Q_h is invertible, each column has at most two nonzero entries, and each row has at most three. For the independently row-and-column optimized, two-sided ordered separation rank defined below,

    floor((h+1)/4) ≤ sr(B_h) = sr(Q_h) ≤ h.               (1)

In particular sr(B_h) = Θ(log N_h). Taking h = 4s+3 gives an admissible inverse with sr(B_h) ≥ s+1, for any prescribed constant s ≥ 0. This witnesses strict coverage beyond every fixed ordered-separation budget. It does not say the earlier theorem fails for growing rank budgets, nor is it an unrestricted Fourier arithmetic lower bound.

The lower constant in (1) uses the published Adler–Kanté forest theorem. Section 8 gives a wholly elementary, weaker lower bound that already proves unboundedness independently of that theorem.

## 1. The precise parameter and inherited definition

For row and column orders r_1,…,r_N and c_1,…,c_N, independently chosen, put

    R_k = {r_1,…,r_k},       C_k = {c_1,…,c_k},
    s_(r,c)(A) = max_(0≤k≤N) max{
        rank A[R_k,C_k^c], rank A[R_k^c,C_k]
    },
    sr(A) = min_(r,c) s_(r,c)(A).

The rank of an empty matrix is zero. Complements are within the respective row and column index sets. All ranks of A in this definition are over its coefficient field.

This is exactly the inherited theorem's definition using *all strictly separated rectangles in either direction*. Indeed, every displayed cut block is strictly separated. Conversely, a rectangle whose row positions all precede its column positions is contained in A[R_k,C_k^c] for a cut k between those positions. The reverse orientation is contained in A[R_k^c,C_k]. Restriction cannot increase rank.

Both orientations matter. A one-sided definition would not answer this gate: in a common parent-before-child order Q_h and B_h are upper triangular, so all strictly lower blocks vanish. The claimed obstruction concerns the two-sided parameter actually used by the inherited theorem.

## 2. Inversion preserves the independently optimized parameter

For any invertible N-by-N matrix A, the complementary inverse-rank identity is

    rank A^(-1)[I,J]
      = rank A[J^c,I^c] + |I| + |J| − N.              (2)

The complements are swapped. A direct proof works over every field: for a vector y supported on J put z=A^(-1)y. The condition z_I=0 is equivalent to z being supported on I^c and (Az)_(J^c)=0. This bijects the kernels of the two rectangular maps, whose domain dimensions are |J| and N−|I|. Rank-nullity gives (2).

An ordering pair (r,c) for A induces the **swapped** pair (c,r) for A^(-1). For every k, apply (2) with I=C_k and J=R_k^c, and then with I=C_k^c and J=R_k. The cardinality correction is zero in both cases, giving

    rank A^(-1)[C_k,R_k^c] = rank A[R_k,C_k^c],
    rank A^(-1)[C_k^c,R_k] = rank A[R_k^c,C_k].

Consequently s_(c,r)(A^(-1))=s_(r,c)(A). Swapping pairs is a bijection of all independent orderings, so

    sr(A^(-1)) = sr(A).                              (3)

No principal-block invertibility or common row/column order is assumed. Equation (2) is classical nullity theory, not claimed as new; see Vandebril–Van Barel, Corollary 1.

## 3. The admissible family and its explicit inverse

Ordering parents before children makes P_h strictly upper triangular. More specifically, P_h^(h+1)=0. Thus

    B_h = I + P_h + ⋯ + P_h^h.

There is exactly one directed parent-to-descendant path when one exists, so

    B_h[u,v] = 1 if u is an ancestor of v, including u=v;
               0 otherwise.                         (4)

These formulas remain valid in characteristic two: −1 becomes 1 but is still nonzero, and each ancestral path is unique. The root column of Q_h has only its diagonal entry; every other column has its diagonal 1 and the parent entry −1. Each row has its diagonal and entries at its at most two children. In particular, the example does not rely on unbounded degree or tuned/generic coefficient choices. No bounded-condition-number claim is made.

Let G_h be the simple bipartite support graph of Q_h, with one row vertex ρ_u and one column vertex γ_v, adjacent exactly when Q_h[u,v]≠0. Every nonroot γ_v subdivides the tree edge between ρ_parent(v) and ρ_v. The root γ_root is an additional leaf attached to ρ_root. Hence G_h is a tree of maximum degree at most three, on 2N_h vertices. Deleting γ_root and contracting the subdivision edges gives T_h as a minor.

In the row-support multigraph convention used by the separator theorem, the same matrix has the tree T_h together with a singleton-column loop at the root. Its symmetric bipartite lift is the tree G_h and therefore has treewidth one. The accepted separator theorem applies with q=2, even more restrictively than its general column-two q=3 corollary.

## 4. Forest support removes all field/cancellation ambiguity

**Lemma.** If a rectangular matrix M has nonzero entries precisely at the edges of a bipartite forest F, then over its coefficient field

    rank M = ν(F),

where ν is maximum matching size. In particular the rank is the same as the rank over GF(2) of F's unweighted zero-one biadjacency matrix.

**Proof.** A nonzero t-by-t minor has a nonzero determinant term and hence a matching of size t, so rank M≤ν(F). Conversely take a maximum matching of size t and select its t row endpoints and t column endpoints. In the resulting induced forest that matching is perfect. A forest has at most one perfect matching: the symmetric difference of distinct perfect matchings would contain an alternating cycle. The determinant thus has exactly one nonzero term, equal up to sign to a product of nonzero edge weights. It is nonzero over every field. Therefore rank M≥t. The same argument applies to unit weights over GF(2). ∎

Every Q_h submatrix has forest support, so this lemma applies to every cut block under every independent ordering. No generic-coefficient assumption and no inference from modular nonvanishing to arbitrary complex ranks is being made.

## 5. Independent orders give an ordinary graph linear layout

Fix any independent orders (r,c) for Q_h of separation width s. Form the interleaved ordering of the bipartite graph vertices

    ρ_(r_1), γ_(c_1), ρ_(r_2), γ_(c_2), …,
    ρ_(r_N), γ_(c_N).                                 (5)

At the cut after 2k vertices, the graph's GF(2) cut-adjacency matrix, after permuting its rows and columns, is block diagonal with blocks

    supp Q_h[R_k,C_k^c],
    (supp Q_h[R_k^c,C_k])^T.

By Section 4 its rank is exactly

    rank Q_h[R_k,C_k^c] + rank Q_h[R_k^c,C_k] ≤ 2s.     (6)

Passing from one prefix to the next removes one column and adds one row to the cut matrix, so its rank can increase by at most one. Thus every odd cut in (5) has rank at most 2s+1. With linear rank-width defined as the minimum, over all graph vertex orders, of the maximum GF(2) cut rank, this proves

    lrw(G_h) ≤ 2s+1,
    lrw(G_h) ≤ 2 sr(Q_h)+1.                           (7)

This direction is sufficient. We neither assume that optimal graph layouts alternate bipartite halves nor assert equality between the two optimization problems.

Adler and Kanté prove in their Theorem 2 that lrw(F)=pw(F) for every forest F. Applying that result and minor-monotonicity of pathwidth gives

    2 sr(Q_h)+1 ≥ pw(G_h) ≥ pw(T_h) = ceil(h/2).        (8)

Since sr is integral, (8) is exactly the lower bound in (1):

    sr(Q_h) ≥ ceil((ceil(h/2)−1)/2) = floor((h+1)/4).

The proof of the binary-tree pathwidth identity is supplied next. The graph theorem is used in its usual GF(2) sense, and Section 4 explicitly bridges that rank to Q_h over every field.

## 6. An elementary derivation of pw(T_h)=ceil(h/2)

A path decomposition is a sequence of vertex bags covering all vertices and edges, such that the bags containing any fixed vertex form a nonempty interval. Its width is maximum bag size minus one. Two basic facts follow directly from this definition.

**Path removal lemma for trees.** If a nonempty tree T has pathwidth k, there is a simple path P in T such that every component of T−P has pathwidth at most k−1. Conversely, for k≥1, existence of such a path implies pw(T)≤k.

For the forward implication take an optimal decomposition with nonempty first and last bags; choose a vertex x in its first bag and y in its last bag. Along the unique x-to-y path P, consecutive vertices have overlapping bag intervals because each path edge is covered. The union of their intervals is therefore an interval containing both endpoint bag indices, and meets every bag. Removing P from every bag decreases each bag size by at least one; restricting to any component yields width at most k−1.

For the converse list the vertices p_1,…,p_t of P in path order. Each component of T−P has a unique attachment vertex p_i, since T is a tree. For each p_i, concatenate decompositions of its attached components, adding p_i to every bag. Insert singleton bags {p_i} and connecting bags {p_i,p_(i+1)} as necessary. The resulting decomposition has maximum bag size at most k+1, covers every edge, and retains the interval property.

For h≥2, any simple path P in T_h leaves some entire rooted copy of T_(h−2) in a component of T_h−P. If P avoids the root, it lies in one root-child subtree, so the other is untouched. If P contains the root, its portion in either child subtree can use only one of that child's two downward branches; at least one grandchild subtree is untouched. The path removal lemma and subgraph monotonicity therefore give

    pw(T_h) ≥ pw(T_(h−2))+1.

Conversely choose a leaf-to-leaf path through the root that continues down one child at each successive branch. Every component left off that path has height at most h−2. The converse lemma gives

    pw(T_h) ≤ pw(T_(h−2))+1.

The base values pw(T_0)=0 and pw(T_1)=1 prove pw(T_h)=ceil(h/2) by induction.

For completeness, minor-monotonicity used in (8) also follows directly from path decompositions. Vertex/edge deletion cannot increase width. Contracting an edge uv replaces u and v by one vertex; their bag intervals overlap because a bag contains the edge, so their union remains an interval, and bag sizes do not increase.

## 7. A matching logarithmic upper bound

Use the same depth-first preorder for Q_h's rows and columns. Parents precede descendants, so all lower cut blocks vanish. At any upper cut, a processed vertex can have a nonzero entry into an unprocessed column only if it has an unvisited child. Such vertices are on the active depth-first root-to-vertex stack and number at most h. The diagonal contributes no crossing entries. Thus every upper cut has at most h nonzero rows, yielding sr(Q_h)≤h. Equation (3) gives the same bound for B_h.

Together with (1) and N_h+1=2^(h+1), this establishes Θ(log N_h), not merely an unfavorable choice of order.

## 8. A fully elementary unboundedness proof, without the forest rank-width theorem

This section replaces the invocation of Adler–Kanté by a weaker bound. It is included to make the class-separation conclusion independent of any unproved graph-width theorem in this document.

A tree of maximum degree at most three admits a proper edge coloring with three colors: root each component, color root-child edges distinctly, and then at each vertex color its at most two child edges with colors different from its parent edge. Each color class is a matching. A crossing subgraph of G_h consequently has at most three times its matching number edges. Section 4 identifies that matching number with cut rank.

The interleaved order (5) therefore has at most 3(2s+1)=6s+3 crossing edges at every cut. For any graph vertex order v_1,…,v_m, let F_i be the processed vertices with a neighbor after position i. Each F_i vertex is incident to a distinct crossing edge if one edge is selected per processed endpoint, so |F_i| is no greater than the number of crossing edges.

The bags

    F_(i−1) ∪ {v_i},             1≤i≤m,

form a path decomposition: an edge from v_j to v_i with j<i is covered at bag i, and v_j stays in the bags from its own position through its last later neighbor. Width is at most max_i |F_i|. Hence

    pw(G_h) ≤ 6s+3,
    sr(Q_h) ≥ ceil((ceil(h/2)−3)/6).                  (9)

One may replace the right side by its maximum with zero. The derivation of pw(T_h) above and the forest-matching lemma complete this proof. Bound (9) alone diverges with h; the primary graph theorem improves it to (1).

## 9. What this resolves, and what it does not

- It resolves the precise mathematical alternative posed in the publication assessment: the whole class of invertible column-two matrices does **not** have uniformly bounded optimized inverse separation rank.
- The independent row and column permutations are fully quantified over. The proof does not confuse common permutations with independent ones or two-sided width with triangular one-sided width.
- The counterexample has uniformly bounded degrees, a tree lift, coefficients in {0,1,−1}, and an explicit inverse. High degree and a natural ordering are not used as strictness evidence.
- The accepted weighted-separator framework has constant q on this family, while every fixed ordered-separation budget eventually fails for a single branch. Thus its structural hypothesis covers families not covered by a uniform fixed-rank premise of the inherited theorem.
- The inherited theorem still applies with a growing rank budget. Nothing here excludes decompositions of B_h into other structured terms or improves a lower bound for unrestricted FFT circuits, shared implementations, products, or interleavings.
- This resolves a correctness/coverage gate, subject to independent audit. It does not establish that the strictness observation or the separator-sum application is externally new. Nullity, inverse rank preservation, graph rank-width, and tree pathwidth are established mathematics. No priority, submission, or acceptance claim is made.
