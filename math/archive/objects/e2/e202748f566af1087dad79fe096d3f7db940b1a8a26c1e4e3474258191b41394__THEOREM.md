# Hero #1 — Phase 26: high-arity and nonunitary graph tolls

**Date:** 2026-10-10. **Status:** proved elementary identities, exact tested implementations and restrictions; **not** a general sub-`N log N` FFT or independent historical novelty claim.

## 1. Separate the computational domains

This checkpoint makes two logically distinct statements:

1. **Exact modular-field implementation:** for prime `p=2013265921` (and independently `p=754974721`) and powers of two `N | p-1`, it compares two different full-input Fourier recursions and a nonunitary shear realization. A modular multiplication by any scalar other than `1`, including an element satisfying `i²=-1`, counts as **one multiplication**; each addition/subtraction as **one addition**. The same primitive roots and primitive-field arithmetic are used throughout. The Good–Thomas extension handles `N=3·2^t`.
2. **Complex circuit lower-bound diagnostics:** entropy and determinant inequalities for in-place linear gates over **complex numbers**, with explicitly restricted gates, coefficients, and workspace. These are consequences of known methods by Morgenstern and Ailon; **do not transfer them to finite fields** or unrestricted algebraic circuits.

Both objects are fully accounted for below.

## 2. Two different exact Fourier recursions have the same charged scalar cost

For an unnormalized discrete Fourier transform over an odd-characteristic field, define

\[
F_N(x)_k = \sum_{j=0}^{N-1} x_j\omega^{jk},\quad \omega^N=1,
\]

where `ω` has exact order `N`. For `N=2^t`, conventional radix-2 decimation-in-frequency has a node operation

\[
u_j=x_j+x_{j+N/2},\quad v_j=\omega^j(x_j-x_{j+N/2}).\]

Its exact scalar inventory, skipping multiplication by one, is

\[
\boxed{A_2(N)=N\log_2 N,\quad M_2(N)=\tfrac N2\log_2N-N+1.}
\]

Now define a **different** split-radix DIF circuit, for `N>=4`. Let `h=N/2`, `q=N/4`, and

\[
 s_j=x_j+x_{j+h},\qquad d_j=x_j-x_{j+h}\quad (0\le j<h).
\]

For `0<=j<q` and `i=ω^q` (`i²=-1`), form

\[
 c_j=\omega^j(d_j+i d_{j+q}),\qquad
 e_j=\omega^{3j}(d_j-i d_{j+q}).
\]

The even-frequency transform is `F_h(s)`; outputs at `4r+1` are `F_q(c)_r`, and outputs at `4r+3` are `F_q(e)_r`. All maps are exact over the field: this is the classical split-radix decomposition with a project-specific **fully paid field-operation accounting**.

The top split-radix node performs `3N/2` additions (sum and difference for each input pair, then two adds for each `j<q`) and `3N/4−2` scalar multiplications (`q` multiplications by `i`, and `2(q−1)` nonidentity twiddles). It recursively calls one `N/2` and two `N/4` transforms. Hence

\[
\begin{split}
A_s(N)&=A_s(N/2)+2A_s(N/4)+3N/2,\\
M_s(N)&=M_s(N/2)+2M_s(N/4)+3N/4-2,
\end{split}
\]

with `A_s(1)=M_s(1)=0`, `A_s(2)=2`, `M_s(2)=0`. Induction gives

\[
\boxed{A_s(N)=A_2(N),\qquad M_s(N)=M_2(N).}
\]

The equality holds **separately** for additions and multiplications, for every dyadic `N`, not just in the leading coefficient. The independent implementations verify the identities for full Fourier bases through `N=256` across two suitable finite fields, and for randomized dense vectors through `N=4096`, with a larger benchmark at `N=16384`.

**Memory caveat:** this split-radix prototype is recursive and allocates intermediate arrays, unlike the optimized in-place Phase 25 radix-2 graph. Its exact field-arithmetic equality is not a claim of equal data traffic, cache efficiency or wall-clock performance.

**Mixed-radix extension:** replace the three dyadic rows of Phase 25's Good–Thomas `3·2^t` circuit by the split-radix evaluator. Exact outputs and the per-output full circuit count remain unchanged. The independent Phase 25 evaluator is used as a cross-check. The Phase 26 demo currently builds a full `N`-entry root table, rather than Phase 25's `G`-entry table; that *additional cold setup is explicitly charged*, not described as an improvement.

## 3. Exact nonunitary butterfly lifting adds a cost

Over any odd-characteristic field, put

\[
H=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&-1\end{pmatrix}
 =\underbrace{\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\1&1\end{pmatrix}}_{L}
 \underbrace{\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-2\end{pmatrix}}_{D}
 \underbrace{\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix}}_{U}.
\]

The right-to-left application computes `t=u+v`, scales `v` by `−2`, and computes `t−2v`, exactly the ordinary butterfly `(u+v,u−v)`. The direct butterfly uses two additions; the lifted one uses two additions **plus one extra multiplication**. Every dyadic `N`-point FFT has precisely `(N/2)log₂N` butterflies, so the experiment's nonunitary lifting causes

\[
\boxed{T_{lift}(N)-T_{radix2}(N)=\tfrac N2\log_2N.}
\]

This is a negative result about the **specified lift**, not a lower bound against all nonunitary factorizations. An implementation may replace multiplying by `−2` with another addition, which still increases the ordinary butterfly cost by one field operation.

## 4. High-arity unitary entropy toll (complex, no extra workspace)

Consider an `N×N` unitary linear transform built from in-place unitary row gates `U_g`, each touching `b_g` of the original `N` registers. Let

\[
 \Phi(A)=-\sum_{i,j}|A_{ij}|^2\log_2|A_{ij}|^2.
\]

For `A=I`, `Φ=0`. For the **normalized** `N`-point Fourier matrix, `Φ=N log₂N`.

Within a `b_g`-row gate, the combined column mass in the affected rows is invariant. Per column, the entropy contribution of redistributing mass among `b_g` rows can change in absolute value by at most that mass times `log₂ b_g`. Summing all columns gives total affected mass `b_g` because the existing rows have squared norm one. Thus

\[
 \boxed{|\Delta \Phi|\le b_g\log_2b_g.}
\]

Telescoping over the circuit yields the necessary budget

\[
\boxed{\sum_g b_g\log_2b_g\ge N\log_2N.}
\]

For `b_g=2`, this reduces to the established `N log₂N/2` bound. With all gate arities bounded by a constant `B`, the number of such unitary gates is necessarily `Ω(N log N)`. If arities grow with `N`, one cannot count an arbitrary `b×b` Fourier gate as one *scalar operation*. Its actual implementation must be expanded and charged.

This is a straightforward arity-dependent form of Ailon's known matrix-entropy argument, not a new unrestricted lower bound.

## 5. Higher-arity bounded-entry determinant toll (complex, including nonunitary)

For the **unnormalized** complex DFT, `F_N* F_N=N I`; hence

\[
 |\det F_N|=N^{N/2}.
\]

Suppose `F_N` is factored into in-place invertible linear gates `G_1,...,G_m`, with arities `b_g` and **every local matrix entry** having complex modulus at most `C>=1`. Each determinant is at most the product of Euclidean row norms, giving Hadamard's bound

\[
|\det G_g|\le(C\sqrt{b_g})^{b_g}.
\]

Taking logarithms of the determinant of the complete composed circuit yields

\[
\boxed{\frac N2\ln N\le\sum_g b_g\big(\ln C+\tfrac12\ln b_g\big).}
\]

This bound **does not require local gates to be unitary**. If there are at most `G` gates of arity at most `B`, it implies

\[
\boxed{C\ge B^{-1/2}N^{N/(2GB)}}
\]

whenever the right side exceeds one. In particular, `G<=N`, `B=2` requires `C>=N^{1/4}/√2`. Thus an `O(N)` in-place gate count with bounded arity cannot compute the unnormalized complex Fourier transform using uniformly bounded matrix entries. Large coefficients (or a different model) are necessary to escape *this restriction*; they are not alone sufficient to obtain a speedup.

For fixed `B,C`, the determinant inequality implies `Ω(NlogN)` *gate count*, but provides no unqualified lower bound for circuits with large workspace, variable/unbounded coefficients, nonlinear operations, data-dependent branching, or finite-field arithmetic. It is a consequence of Morgenstern's classic determinant argument, with a per-gate Hadamard estimate.

### Saturation by the existing Good–Thomas graph

For `N=3G`, `G=2^t`, Phase 25 uses `3Gt/2` unnormalized `2×2` Fourier butterflies and `G` unnormalized `3×3` Fourier codelets. Each has entries of complex modulus one. Their aggregate **maximum allowed determinant-volume growth** is

\[
 \frac{3Gt}2\ln2+G\frac32\ln3
 =\frac{3G}2\ln(3G)=\frac N2\ln N.
\]

The arithmetic circuit therefore **saturates this particular gate-volume bound**; replacing its two-input gates with larger uncharged blocks merely changes the units used for the count. Likewise, using normalized `2×2` and `3×3` unitary Fourier gates gives total entropy budget exactly `Nlog₂N`. A calculation checker reproduces these equalities over a range of values of `G`. This is a model-bound audit, not a scalar-operation optimum theorem.

## 6. Consequence for Phase 27

Any input-independent claim of exact `o(NlogN)` scalar operations should provide: an explicitly defined algebraic field and operation model; a complete scalar program for any high-arity gate; exact full-basis correctness for new lengths; a root/constant construction and memory ledger; and a proof that its savings are not only caused by packaging existing butterflies into a single black box. If complex in-place bounded-arity gates are used, the coefficient-growth or entropy toll must be addressed. If finite fields are used, complex analytic lower bounds cannot be imported without separate justification.
