# Bounded independent extension: arbitrary-modulus covariance

Date: 2026-10-09. This note derives new bounds from the displayed mathematics in v9. It does not execute or import code from the uploaded archives. The sharp proposed bound below is still a conjecture. The weaker bounds in Sections 3–4 have self-contained proofs.

## Result

Let N=KM with 2≤M≤K, with no prime-power assumption. Use the same unit-step row, a uniform unit modulo N, and an independent uniform beta modulo N. For every output frequency m, the covariance is unitarily similar to Q=Q^(0). Put

- G={g:g divides N, 1≤g<M},
- D(N,M)=|G|,
- L=ceil(log2 M),
- c_g(u)=#{d:u−M+1≤d≤u, d≠0, gcd(N,d)=g},
- C(N,M)=sum over g in G of max_u c_g(u)/phi(N/g).

Then

    ||Q|| ≤ C(N,M) ≤ (M−1) D(N,M) / phi(N),

and, independently,

    ||Q|| ≤ 2(M−1)(L+1)^2 / phi(N).

Consequently the following is a proved, explicit variance bound:

    B(N,M) = min{C(N,M), 2(M−1)(L+1)^2/phi(N)}.

The trace bound M−1 can optionally be included in this minimum. A simpler displayed bound is

    ||Q|| ≤ [(M−1)/phi(N)] min{D(N,M), 2(L+1)^2}.

This report retains the main scope 2≤M≤K with N=KM. In that scope 2M−2<N, so averaging beta cannot identify distinct signed displacements modulo N. The covariance proof must not be extended to M=N from the displayed formulas: displacement aliasing would need separate treatment. The original decoder and unbiasedness do hold for composite N in the stated scope.

Numerical evidence supports the stronger conjecture

    ||Q|| ≤ M/phi(N),

but this note does not prove it. In particular, the exact prime-power projector identity must not be substituted for the composite covariance.

## 1. Exact covariance from normalized Ramanujan sums

Write n=rM+u, with local coordinate u in {0,...,M−1}, and let

    D_u={u−t:0≤t<M,t≠u}.

The beta average leaves only identical displacements. Therefore

    Q[n,n′] = (1/N) sum_{d in D_u intersect D_u′} c_N(d(n′−n))/phi(N),

where c_N denotes the Ramanujan sum. All entries are rational. In the fresh implementation it is evaluated by

    c_N(q) = phi(N) mu(N/gcd(N,q))/phi(N/gcd(N,q)).

The trace is M−1 because every diagonal has (M−1)/N. For a nonzero output m, the extra factor exp(2 pi i m(n−n′)/N) is diagonal unitary conjugation, so the spectrum is unchanged. The parity twist cancels in the same-displacement product.

For each nonzero d with |d|<M define the diagonal mask E_d[n,n]=1[d in D_(n mod M)]. Let g=gcd(N,d), and let P_g be the orthogonal Fourier projector onto

    S_g={k in Z_N:gcd(k,N)=g}.

Multiplication of a uniform unit a by d is uniform over S_g. The reduction map of units modulo N to units modulo N/g is onto (by the Chinese remainder theorem), with equal fiber sizes. Since |S_g|=phi(N/g),

    Q_d = E_d P_g E_d / phi(N/g),
    Q   = sum_{0<|d|<M} Q_d.                         (1)

This normalization is important: P_g has kernel (1/N) sum_{k in S_g} exp(2 pi i k(n′−n)/N). Distinct P_g are orthogonal, but the E_d generally do not commute with them.

## 2. Why the sharp shortcut is not a proof

For arbitrary composite N, applying different masks on the two sides of different Fourier-shell projectors destroys the direct orthogonality argument. The identity Q=(M/N) times a projector already fails at N=12,18,60 in the supplied audit, and the fresh implementation reproduces those examples.

The domination phi(N)/phi(N/g)≤g alone is insufficient to obtain M/phi(N): one must still control the interaction of the different masked shells. The proofs below keep the cost of that interaction explicit.

## 3. Direct divisor bound

Fix g. Since 0≤P_g≤I and each E_d is an orthogonal coordinate mask,

    sum_{d:gcd(N,d)=g} E_d P_g E_d
        ≤ sum_{d:gcd(N,d)=g} E_d
        = diag(c_g(n mod M))
        ≤ (max_u c_g(u)) I.

Sum over g in (1) to obtain ||Q||≤C(N,M).

For fixed u, the permitted positive displacements have lengths 1,...,u and the permitted negative magnitudes have lengths 1,...,M−1−u. Both must be multiples of g. Thus

    c_g(u) ≤ floor(u/g)+floor((M−1−u)/g)
           ≤ floor((M−1)/g).

For every divisor g of N, Euler's product formula gives

    r_g := phi(N)/phi(N/g) ≤ g.

Indeed r_g equals g times the product of (1−1/p) for exactly those prime factors of N removed entirely in N/g. It follows that

    C(N,M)
      = [1/phi(N)] sum_g r_g max_u c_g(u)
      ≤ [1/phi(N)] sum_g g floor((M−1)/g)
      ≤ (M−1)D(N,M)/phi(N).

This is a fully explicit divisor-dependent bound, with no numerical premise.

## 4. Universal logarithmic mask bound

This bound removes the divisor count at the expense of a logarithm squared in M.

Let U be a unitary N-point Fourier matrix. Define an unmasked analysis operator T whose rows are indexed by pairs (d,k), where 1≤d<M and k lies in S_g for g=gcd(N,d). Its entries are

    T[(d,k),n] = sqrt(r_g) U[k,n],
    r_g = phi(N)/phi(N/g).

Let c_g^+ count positive displacements d with gcd(N,d)=g. The shells are orthogonal before masking, so

    T* T = sum_g r_g c_g^+ P_g,
    ||T||^2 = max_g r_g c_g^+ ≤ M−1,                (2)

because c_g^+≤floor((M−1)/g) and r_g≤g.

The actual positive-displacement operator T_+ is obtained by retaining the entry in column n=rM+u precisely when d≤u. For negative displacement −d, the corresponding operator T_− retains precisely d≤M−1−u. Formula (1) becomes

    Q = (T_+* T_+ + T_−* T_−)/phi(N).             (3)

We now give the required elementary triangular-truncation estimate. Pad row labels d and column labels u with zeros to a power-of-two index set of size 2^L. The relation d≤u is the disjoint union of:

1. the diagonal d=u;
2. for each of L dyadic levels, rectangles where d is in the left child and u in the right child of the same dyadic interval.

At any single level, these rectangles use mutually disjoint row groups and mutually disjoint column groups. Each retained rectangle is a compression of T, so has norm at most ||T||. Their direct sum also has norm at most ||T||. The diagonal part has the same property. The triangle inequality therefore gives

    ||T_+|| ≤ (L+1)||T||.

The reflected order on the column labels gives the identical estimate for T_−. Combining (2) and (3),

    ||Q|| ≤ 2(M−1)(L+1)^2/phi(N).

This proves the logarithmic bound without assuming any shell orthogonality after masking.

## 5. Approximation consequence and its limits

Every unit-step row still has O(N+(N/M)log(N/M)) arithmetic work and O(N) storage. Replace the v9 scalar variance proxy M/N by B(N,M). Its same robust-majority argument then gives, for independent rows,

    Pr[||X_tilde−U_N x||_infinity
          > (5/sqrt(2)) g sqrt(B(N,M)) ||x||_2]
       ≤ 2N exp(−R D(1/2 || 1/g^2)),  g>sqrt(2).

This statement uses v9's aggregation argument; this note independently derives its new variance input, not a new aggregation proof. In finite precision, add the previously proved row error to the scalar radius as in v9. No composite sharp-constant stability claim is made here.

Relative to the prime-power variance scale M/N, the proved inflation is at most

    (N/phi(N)) min{D(N,M), 2(L+1)^2}.

Thus composite lengths that have a divisor M of order log N retain linear work per row, with a controlled extra factor in the error radius. For example, the logarithmic mask bound multiplies the original standard-deviation scale by at most

    sqrt(2 N/phi(N)) (1+ceil(log2 M)).

This does not establish a uniform all-integer-length fast theorem. A modulus with no useful divisor near the desired M still has the block-size selection obstruction; choosing M=1 makes the row exact but requires the full N-point FFT. It also does not establish a practical speedup over FFTW or certify binary64 errors.

## 6. Independent numerical and rational-entry checks

Fresh file: independent_composite_probe.py. It uses only the displayed covariance and gcd-shell formulas, plus NumPy eigensolvers. It never imports or executes any uploaded program.

Two independent assembly paths were compared:

- Full N-by-N covariance with exact Fraction entries from the normalized Ramanujan formula.
- M-by-M block-Fourier matrices using the periodic local-coordinate masks.

The second path removes a harmless diagonal gauge. For block frequency ell in Z_K, its global Fourier labels are ell+sK, s=0,...,M−1. Define

    W_g[u,v] = #{d:0<|d|<M, gcd(N,d)=g, d in D_u intersect D_v}.

Its reduced matrix is

    B_ell[u,v]
      = sum_g W_g[u,v]/phi(N/g)
          * (1/M) sum_{s:gcd(ell+sK,N)=g} exp(2 pi i s(u−v)/M).

The full covariance norm is max_ell ||B_ell||. This formula is exact; diagonalization is floating point.

Completed checks:

- All 146 pairs with 4≤N≤100, 2≤M≤sqrt(N), M divides N: exact-rational entry assembly versus reduced spectral norm, with agreement within 2e−12. Symmetry and trace M−1 also checked.
- All 16,723 pairs with 4≤N≤5000, 2≤M≤sqrt(N), M divides N: no numerical violation of the conjecture ||Q||≤M/phi(N).
- The largest normalized ratio ||Q||phi(N)/M in that sweep was 0.9850746268656727 at N=4489=67^2, M=67 (a prime-power case).
- The separate fresh check_bound_certificates.py passed all 511 dyadic partition checks for 2≤M≤512, exact count/phi-ratio inequalities for all 2,550 admissible pairs through N=1000, and 11 explicitly constructed analysis-operator Gram checks. Each dyadic level was also checked to have norm at most the unmasked operator. These are implementation checks of the proof objects, not a substitute for the argument in Sections 3–4. ## Bottom line

A controlled arbitrary-modulus covariance bound is available without the false projector extension. The strongest simple tested candidate, M/phi(N), remains conjectural. The proved divisor/log-squared bounds are sufficient to extend the approximate-row guarantee to composite factorizations with a useful block divisor, with an explicit loss in accuracy. They do not remove the remaining all-length factorization issue.
