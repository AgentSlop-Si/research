## Guards and implementation limitations
The prime-field precondition is assumed, not proved. Rank/window/nonzero-center and most internal invariants are assertions. Polynomial operations expect normalized, well-formed coefficient lists; exact_divide assumes inverse_lead is the genuine inverse. These preconditions hold in the supplied test caller. geometric_values verifies q^order=1 after nonzero folding, but not exact order; a smaller-order q still gives the requested evaluations, although a separate distinct-point/candidate-count argument would need exact order. Its zero-fold early return bypasses root validation, which matters only for invalid q/order inputs. No such input occurs in stored tests. This is a validation gap, not a stored-result defect.

## Costs
Bareiss performs sum(k^2,k=1..r) exact divisions, O(r^3(D+1)^2) schoolbook field work, at most r−1 scalar inversions and O(r^2(D+1)) storage. Toeplitz performs O(r^2) polynomial products/additions plus r Newton inversions, for O(r^2(D+1)^log2(3)) arithmetic and O(r(D+1)) live coefficient storage. Full intermediate products up to 2D do not change those bounds. Geometric scanning costs O(len(coefficients)+order^log2(3)) and O(order) linear-convolution storage. Kernel counters do not count every Python allocation, assertion, modular bit operation or exponentiation; they are not wall-clock measurements or exact whole-program instruction ledgers.
