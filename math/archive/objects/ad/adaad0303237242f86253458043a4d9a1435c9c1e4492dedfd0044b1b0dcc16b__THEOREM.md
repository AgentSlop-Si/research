# Phase 16 — Entropy-optimal Galois FFT and an all-strength Haar–Green stage obstruction

**Date:** October 9, 2026  
**Research goal:** exact Fourier computation in strictly sub-`N log N` *charged* work by graph/Green/Haar computation sharing, without adopting the OpenAI/CrocSwap architecture.  
**Status:** new project-specific deductions and reproducible tests, built upon a **previously known entropy lower-bound technique**. **No new sub-`N log N` Fourier algorithm and no unconditional FFT lower bound.**

## 0. The correct theorem/model distinction

The underlying entropy argument is due to **Nir Ailon (2013)**, *A Lower Bound for Fourier Transform Computation in a Linear Model Over 2x2 Unitary Gates Using Matrix Entropy*, arXiv:1305.4745. His related well-conditioned linear-circuit work (2014/2016) proves results in different restricted models. We are **not claiming this lower-bound method as new**.

Our additions are (i) recognizing that the exact Phase-15 Galois residual `R` and its two Fourier banks saturate the 2-coordinate-unitary-gate lower bound **individually and together**, and (ii) **constructively compiling the Phase-13 path-Cayley/Haar architecture** into at most `(11N/2)-8` two-coordinate unitaries, giving a strength-independent product-length obstruction for that specific architecture. The finite complex audits support the identities; the all-size claims rely on the algebraic proofs below.

We work over `C` with `N` a power of two and normalized Fourier matrix

`Fhat_N[k,j] = exp(2πijk/N)/sqrt(N)`.

A *unitary 2-coordinate gate* left-multiplies the current `N×N` computation matrix by a matrix acting unitarily on two selected rows and as the identity elsewhere. Coordinate permutations and unit-modulus coordinate phases are allowed without charged entropy gates, because they preserve matrix entropy. These are restrictive circuit assumptions. Arbitrary nonunitary circuits, large-workspace algorithms, nonlinear/tabulation operations, high-arity primitives, or algorithms with differently costed coefficients are not covered by this theorem.

## 1. Two bits of matrix entropy per unitary 2-coordinate gate

For a unitary matrix `U`, define its row Shannon matrix entropy in **bits**:

`Phi(U) = - sum_(i,j) |U_ij|² log2(|U_ij|²)`, with `0 log 0 = 0`.

Every row of a unitary matrix has squared-modulus total `1`. In particular,

`Phi(I)=0`, `Phi(Fhat_N)=N log2 N`.

Suppose a gate replaces rows `p,q` by a unitary mixture. For each column `j`, the quantity `r_j = |U_pj|² + |U_qj|²` is conserved, and the contribution from the pair of entries to matrix entropy is

`-r_j log2 r_j + r_j h_2(|U_pj|²/r_j)`,

where `h_2(s)=-s log2 s-(1-s)log2(1-s)` is binary entropy. Since `0<=h_2<=1`, the **change** in this column's contribution is at most `r_j`. Sum over `j`: `sum_j r_j=2`. Therefore

**`Delta Phi <= 2 bits per 2-coordinate unitary gate`.**

In an in-place circuit of `g` such gates, possibly with free monomial unitaries,

**`g >= N log2 N / 2`.**

This is the established Ailon-type entropy lower bound specialized to this setting. It is *not* an unrestricted linear-arithmetic lower bound.

## 2. Phase-15 factorization is entropy-optimal at every single gate

Phase 15 established `N = B L`, with `8<=B<N<=B²`, and an exact unnormalized factorization `F_N = B_orbit Q R D_B P_in`. Make its pieces normalized:

- `U_B = (I_L ⊗ F_B)/sqrt(B)`, the `L` independent base Fourier banks.
- `R_u = diag(sqrt(d_e/L)) R`, the normalized Galois dyadic folding factor, where each residual output corresponds to an orbit of length `d_e`.
- `U_orbit = ⊕_(orbits) F_d/sqrt(d)`, the normalized disjoint orbitwise Fourier matrices.
- `Q`, `P_in`, `P_out` are unit phases and permutations.

Then exactly

**`Fhat_N = P_out U_orbit Q R_u U_B P_in`.**

Every component is unitary. In particular, `R R* = diag(L/d_e)`, previously proved in Phase 15.

### 2.1 The folding factor has an exact entropy identity

Each row of `R_u` corresponding to an orbit of length `d` has exactly `g=L/d` nonzeros, **all of magnitude** `1/sqrt(g)`. This follows from the dyadic folding construction: the output accumulates exactly `g` distinct input frequency registers, each with coefficient of unit modulus and normalization `1/sqrt(g)`.

Therefore the row entropy equals `log2 g`, and

`Phi(R_u) = sum_(orbits) d log2(L/d)`.

For the power-of-two Galois orbits used in Phase 15, there are `B` length-1 orbits and `B/2` orbits of each length `2,4,...,L`. Hence the sum evaluates to the **exact identity**

**`Phi(R_u) = N-B`.**

Phase 15 executes `R` with `(N-B)/2` butterflies; after normalization every one is a two-coordinate unitary butterfly. So `R_u` **meets the 2-coordinate-gate entropy lower bound exactly**. There is no replacement of `R_u` using fewer two-coordinate unitary gates, no matter which pairings or unitary coefficients are chosen.

### 2.2 Both Fourier banks are separately optimal

`Phi(U_B)=N log2 B`, requiring at least `(N/2)log2 B` gates. The base radix-2 bank uses exactly this many.

The orbit bank has entropy

`Phi(U_orbit)=sum_(orbits) d log2 d = B+N(log2 L-1)`,

requiring `B/2 + (N/2)(log2 L-1)` gates. The orbit bank uses exactly this many.

Adding the three entropies yields

`Phi(U_B) + Phi(R_u) + Phi(U_orbit) = N log2 N = Phi(Fhat_N)`.

Their gate counts also add to `(N/2)log2 N`. Thus **every individual gate of the entire construction must achieve the maximum 2-bit entropy gain**, since the total available gain equals the target exactly. Concretely, every butterfly combines two rows with disjoint support of unit total squared mass, and balanced mixing adds one binary choice over their combined support. The `entropy_gate_audit.py` program independently constructs the complete complex transform gate by gate and confirms this through `N=512`, with gate increments `2 ± 3e-14` bits and matching all `N²` DFT entries.

**Interpretation:** Phase-15 butterfly conservation is not a computational accident. The three modules are each optimal inside the pairwise-unitary arithmetic model. Their phase coherence is correct; eliminating butterflies by reordering/rephasing within the same model is impossible. This does *not* prevent algorithmic speedups in a different computational model.

## 3. A strength-independent Givens compilation of path Green Cayleys

Now consider our Phase-13 path Cayley

`C_alpha(A) = (I+i alpha A)(I-i alpha A)^(-1) = 2(I-i alpha A)^(-1)-I`,

with `A` the real symmetric adjacency of a (possibly weighted or relabeled) path and `alpha` real. The matrix `C_alpha(A)` is unitary for every real finite `alpha`.

**Green inverse formula.** Let `T=I-i alpha A`, tridiagonal with nonzero off-diagonals, let `theta_j` be its leading principal determinants, `phi_j` its trailing principal determinants, and `b_j=T[j,j+1]`. For `i>j`, the inverse has the form

`(T^-1)[i,j] = (-1)^(i+j) (b_j ... b_(i-1)) theta_j phi_(i+1) / det(T)`.

The right side factors as `u_i v_j`: its strictly lower-triangular entries are *globally rank-one semiseparable*. All principal minors of `I-i alpha A` are nonzero, because for a Hermitian principal block `A'`, every eigenvalue of `I-i alpha A'` is `1-i alpha lambda`, never zero. The identity term in `C` does not alter strictly off-diagonal entries. If `alpha=0` or the path splits at a zero edge, apply the argument to its independent blocks.

**Lemma.** Any `N×N` unitary matrix with strict-lower entries `U[i,j]=u_i v_j` for all `i>j` can be represented using at most `2N-3` two-coordinate unitary Givens gates, plus one diagonal unitary. Under ordinary nondegeneracy; zeros permit gates to be skipped or limits applied.

**Constructive proof:**

1. For `k=N-1,N-2,...,2`, use a unitary 2-row rotation on rows `(k-1,k)` that zeroes the lower entry in column `k-2`. The rank-one lower generator guarantees that the *same rotation simultaneously zeroes all entries in that lower row in columns `0,...,k-2`*. Previously eliminated rows are untouched. This costs `N-2` gates and produces a unitary upper-Hessenberg matrix. (Using the *nearest* shared column `k-2` avoids numerical underflow from exponentially tiny first-column entries.)
2. Use the standard Hessenberg QR sweep on adjacent rows `(j,j+1)` for `j=0,...,N-2`, eliminating its single remaining subdiagonal. This costs `N-1` gates and leaves an upper-triangular unitary matrix, which must be diagonal.
3. Reverse the rotations to obtain `U`. Total: `(N-2)+(N-1)=2N-3` unitary gates plus a diagonal phase matrix. QED.

Relabeling a path by an arbitrary permutation merely conjugates the resulting circuit by a coordinate permutation.

`green_givens_compiler.py` verifies the constructive two-sweep factorization against independently formed Cayley matrices for weighted paths of sizes 4,8,16,32,64,128 and strengths `alpha=1/32, 1/2, 2`, recovering outputs to approximately `3e-15` max absolute error. Its dense coefficient computation is an **audit**, not a claimed `O(N)` parameter-generation algorithm. The theorem concerns the existence/gate count, which suffices for the next lower bound.

### 3.1 Haar itself uses `m-1` unitary gates

The size-`m` Haar synthesis matrix has a binary-tree implementation with precisely `m-1` balanced two-register unitary transforms. The input registers consist of the scaling coefficient and one coefficient at each internal node. A parent coefficient is combined with the corresponding wavelet coefficient via `(a+b)/sqrt2,(a-b)/sqrt2`; the resulting two registers seed the two children, after which the leaves are permuted into sample order. Analysis reverses the gates.

`haar_green_givens.py` constructs and independently validates this gate schedule against the original Phase-13 Haar-synthesis/analysis code.

## 4. A stronger no-go for the COMPLETE Phase-13 Haar–Green stage

Phase 13's stage is

`S_H = V_H C_1 C_2`, with `V_H = (I+i eps R_H)/sqrt(1+eps²)`,

`R_H = [[0,H_m],[H_m*,0]]`, `m=N/2`.

Define `W=diag(H_m,I_m)` and `J=[[0,I_m],[I_m,0]]`. Then **`R_H=W J W*`** and

`V_H = W (I+i eps J)/sqrt(1+eps²) W*`.

The middle factor is a direct sum of `m` disjoint 2-coordinate unitary rotations. The two Haar factors `W,W*` use `2(m-1)` gates. So `V_H` has a `3m-2` gate decomposition. Each path Cayley has at most `2N-3` gates by the preceding lemma.

Altogether, **one complete Haar–Green stage** and its inverse have a unitary pair-gate representation with no more than

`2(2N-3) + (3N/2-2) = 11N/2-8`

2-coordinate gates, in addition to free phases/permutations. No bound on `alpha`, `eps`, path permutation, or `N`-dependence of those parameters is necessary as long as the indicated operators remain unitary.

Suppose the exact normalized Fourier matrix is factored into `t` such stages or their inverses interspersed with arbitrary *unit-modulus* diagonal phases/permutations. This hypothetical factorization would yield a unitary pair-gate circuit of no more than `t(11N/2-8)` gates. But Fourier entropy requires at least `(N/2)log2 N` gates. Therefore

**`t >= N log2 N / (11N-16) = Omega(log N)`.**

This is a **uniform, coupling-strength-independent stage-count obstruction** for the Phase-13 Haar–Green product architecture, stronger in parameter range than the earlier Phase-13 fixed-small-coupling `ell_p` obstruction. Importantly, the constant `1/11` is weaker than the earlier fixed-strength numerical constant; the improvement is in **generality, not the strength of the constant**.

This conclusion concerns exact factorizations by this stage family. It does **not** prohibit exploiting cancellations via a separately priced *nonunitary* implementation of a product, using more general global sum-of-resolvents, or any unrestricted FFT scheme.

`haar_green_givens.py` reconstructs the full Phase-13 implementation by independently compiling both path operators and the Haar sandwich, comparing random dense complex vectors at `N=16,32,64,128` with maximum errors below `4e-15`.

## 5. A quantitative explanation of the misleading "global mixing" signal

For the isolated Haar involution rotation `V_H`, let `p=eps²/(1+eps²)` and `h_2(p)` be binary entropy. Each row has probability mass `1-p` on its own coordinate and mass `p` distributed according to one row of `H_m` or `H_m*` across the opposite half. Hence

`Phi(V_H) = N h_2(p) + 2p Phi(H_m)`.

The Haar basis satisfies the exact identity `Phi(H_m)=2(m-1)`, since its `m-1` tree butterflies each increase entropy by exactly two bits. Therefore

**`Phi(V_H)=N h_2(p)+2p(N-2) = O(N)`** for every fixed `eps`.

That rotation can send a specific basis vector to a globally supported vector and can make induced `ell_p` norms grow, yet its **total Fourier-style entropy is only linear**. In this sense, high cross-cut rank, growing `ell_p` norm, and dense outputs do not imply that the transform is close to a DFT in matrix-entropy complexity.

At Phase-13's original `eps=1/512`, for `N=128`, `Phi(V_H)` is about `0.010455` bits. The whole numerical Haar–Green stage has entropy approximately `19.0213` bits, whereas `Phi(Fhat_128)=896` bits. Those are finite-size diagnostic values, **not** a stand-alone all-size impossibility theorem; the gate-compilation argument in §4 is the all-size theorem.

## 6. What remains legitimately open in this project

Our direct target `o(N log N)` Fourier work has **not** been met. Phase 16 rejects two concrete lines of attack:

- rearranging, caching, or replacing the Phase-15 factors with fewer in-place pairwise unitary gates; each factor already saturates the restricted entropy bound;
- using `o(log N)` arbitrary-strength Phase-13 Haar–Green stages separated only by unit-modulus monomials to obtain the normalized Fourier matrix.

A genuinely different family must be considered. Candidates include nonunitary shared intermediate graphs, noncommuting **sums** of resolvents whose *inverse* is not in a linear-size pair-unitary class, higher-arity primitives with their actual scalar cost counted, and models using memory/precomputation with explicit access charges. None is automatically a faster FFT.

Before claiming a new algorithm, independently verify at every growing size:

1. **Fourier covariance** `AZ=DA` and output normalization, not just graph cut rank or low residual norm.
2. **Exact Galois covariance** for algebraic roots of unity when applicable.
3. **All arithmetic work**, twiddle/root generation, preprocessing, memory reads/writes, numerical precision and bit complexity where integers are the goal.
4. **Escape from the actual lower-bound model**; either provide a valid nonunitary/high-arity mechanism or show which assumption of the existing lower bound does not apply.

**Research priority:** Investigate noncommuting sum-of-Green inverse modules and a fully charged nonunitary fusion executor, not another unitary butterfly/Givens rewire. This is a research *proposal*, not evidence of a new upper bound.
