# Proof certificate — simultaneous Fourier constraints and outer-inverse separation

**Date:** 2026-10-10  
**Model:** Unnormalized Fourier matrix `F_256[i,j] = zeta^(ij)`, normalized unitary `U=F_256/16`; exact complex arithmetic, four arbitrary two-sided order-one quasiseparable stages, **fixed shuffles** in `AFFINE_LAYOUT.json`.

## 1. The fixed four-stage identity

If `U=S4 P3 S3 P2 S2 P1 S1`, all factors are invertible because `U` is. Define `C4=S4^{-1}` and `C1=S1^{-1}`. Under the saved convention `P_j x=x[p_j]`,

`H=P3^{-1} C4 U C1 P1^{-1}=S3 P2 S2`.

In particular, singular or zero *generator entries* in S2 and S3 are fully permitted. No division by any generator occurs. This identity is an exact consequence of the factorization.

## 2. Thirty-two necessary rank conditions

For every nonzero bitmask `m` on eight bits, set `g_m(i)=1` when `popcount(i&m)` is even. Define

`C_m={i:g_m(i)=1}`, `R_m={i:g_m(p2[i])=0}`.

Both contain exactly 128 coordinates. Let `d(g)` count natural-order zero-one transitions. Order-one quasiseparable crossing ranks and rank subadditivity give

`rank H[R_m,C_m] <= b_m = d(g_m)+d(g_m[p2])`.

For exactly **32 masks**, `b_m<128`. They are listed in `EXACT_AND_NUMERIC_CERTIFICATES.json`. Their seven smallest bounds are:

| mask | d(g) | d(g[p2]) | b | rank of H0 block |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 64 | 3 | 13 | 16 | 128 |
| 168 | 25 | 22 | 47 | 128 |
| 156 | 46 | 3 | 49 | 128 |
| 128 | 1 | 51 | 52 | 128 |
| 232 | 26 | 27 | 53 | 128 |
| 220 | 45 | 14 | 59 | 128 |
| 40 | 24 | 37 | 61 | 128 |

Here `H0=P3^{-1} U P1^{-1}` (corresponding to `C4=C1=I`). Its selected block has rank 128 over the complex numbers for **all 32 masks**. `verify_outer_stability.py` independently computes the ranks modulo both 12289 and 65537 (both fields contain primitive 256th roots), giving 64 nonzero modular full-rank certificates. Nonzero determinants under an exact cyclotomic specialization imply nonzero complex determinants. The verifier also checks the crossing-rank bounds using independent random order-one stages and a singular/zero-generator control.

The 32 rank conditions are **necessary, not sufficient**. There may be unknown outer-stage coefficients satisfying all of them.

## 3. Elementary all-N spectral bound

Let `U_N=F_N/sqrt(N)` be unitary, let `R,C` each contain `N/2` coordinates, and put `K=U_N[R,C]`. Then:

- Every entry has squared modulus `1/N`, hence `||K||_F^2=N/4`.
- `||K||_2<=1` because K is a compression of a unitary.
- For singular values `s_1>=...>=s_{N/2}`, for any integer `b<N/4`,

`s_(b+1)^2 >= (N/4-b)/(N/2-b)`.

Proof: `N/4=sum_j s_j^2 <= b+(N/2-b)*s_(b+1)^2`. This is an ordinary trace/energy inequality, not a new general Fourier theorem.

For `N=256,b=16`, it gives `s_17>=sqrt(3/7)`. The *next* certificate strengthens this strictly to `s_17>3 sqrt(2)/5` for the saved layout.

## 4. The exact, stronger fourth-moment certificate

For mask 64, take its actual Fourier row and column sets `R0,C0` after the saved endpoint permutations. Let `K=U[R0,C0]`; it is 128×128. Put `lambda_j=s_j(K)^2` in descending order, so `lambda_j in [0,1]` and `sum lambda_j=64`.

Define the fourth singular-value moment

`M=tr((K*K)^2)=sum_j lambda_j^2`.

Let `A_d` and `B_e` be the numbers of **ordered pairs** in `R0` and `C0`, respectively, whose modular differences equal `d,e` in `Z/256Z`. Then the exact cyclotomic identity is

`M = (1/256^2) sum_(d,e=0)^255 A_d B_e zeta^(de)`.

Collect powers of `zeta`; because `zeta^128=-1`, reduce to 128 integral coefficients `a_k`. Conjugation gives `a_(128-k)=-a_k` for `1<=k<=63`, `a_64=0`. Consequently

`M = (a_0+2 sum_(k=1)^63 a_k cos(pi k/128))/65536`.

All 128 exact integer coefficients are recorded in `RATIONAL_MOMENT_CERTIFICATE.json`; `verify_moment_rational.py` reconstructs them from scratch with standard-library integer loops.

The **entire inequality** is certified with rational arithmetic:

- `pi=16 arctan(1/5)-4 arctan(1/239)` (Machin identity).
- Alternating rational arctangent partial sums enclose each arctangent, hence enclose pi.
- Each cosine is evaluated by a rational Taylor polynomial at the rational midpoint. The global `1`-Lipschitz property of cosine and the Lagrange Taylor remainder bound enclose the true cosine rigorously.
- Using the integer coefficients with outward interval operations yields

`M > 1264/25 = 50.56`, **with no floating point in the proof**.

The exact fraction endpoints are saved in the JSON certificate. Their non-proof decimal displays are both approximately `50.567230584337...`.

Now, because `0<=lambda_j<=1` and `lambda_j<=lambda_17` for `j>=17`,

`M=sum_(j<=16)lambda_j^2+sum_(j>=17)lambda_j^2`
`<=sum_(j<=16)lambda_j+lambda_17 sum_(j>=17)lambda_j`
`<=16+48lambda_17`.

Therefore

`lambda_17 > ((1264/25)-16)/48 = 18/25`, and thus

**`sigma_17(K) > 3 sqrt(2)/5 ~= 0.8485281374`.**

For context only: floating-point SVD estimates `sigma_17(K)~=0.9247603386`. This last number is **not** a certified spectral threshold and is not used in any proof.

## 5. A quantitative theorem for outer inverse stages

Fix any diagonal unitary matrices `D4,D1`. The selected block of `P3^{-1} D4 U D1 P1^{-1}` differs from `K` only by diagonal phases, so its singular values equal those of K.

For every putative exact factorization, `H[R_64,C_64]` has rank at most 16. By Eckart–Young's operator-norm rank-approximation theorem,

`3 sqrt(2)/5 < sigma_17(K) <= ||C4 U C1-D4 U D1||_2`
`<= ||C4-D4||_2 ||C1||_2+||C1-D1||_2`.

Hence the necessary **strict inequality**:

**`||C4-D4||_2 ||C1||_2+||C1-D1||_2 > 3 sqrt(2)/5`.**

If both distances to some diagonal unitaries are at most `epsilon`, then `||C1||<=1+epsilon`, and exact factorization forces

`epsilon(2+epsilon) > 3 sqrt(2)/5`, equivalently

**`epsilon > sqrt(1+3 sqrt(2)/5)-1 ~= 0.3596058758`.**

This excludes a specific **operator-norm neighborhood** of every diagonal-unitary outer-inverse pair, even though the two middle stages remain arbitrary (including zero generators).

### Arbitrary invertible diagonal baselines

For any invertible diagonal `D4,D1`, let `m4` be the smallest absolute diagonal entry of D4 used by the selected 128 Fourier rows and `m1` the corresponding minimum for D1 on the selected 128 columns. The same argument yields

`||C4-D4||_2 ||C1||_2+||D4||_2 ||C1-D1||_2 > m4*m1*3 sqrt(2)/5`.

This includes nonunitary diagonal gauge baselines and exposes precisely how poorly scaled diagonal entries weaken the bound.

### Approximate FFTs

Let `M=S4 P3 S3 P2 S2 P1 S1`, and define `delta=||U-M||_2`. If `delta<1`, then M and all its square stages are invertible. With the corresponding C4,C1, the necessary inequality is

`delta*||C4||_2*||C1||_2 + ||C4-D4||_2*||C1||_2 + ||D4||_2*||C1-D1||_2 > m4*m1*3 sqrt(2)/5`.

For unitary diagonal baselines and both distances <=epsilon, a quantitative lower bound is

`delta >= [3 sqrt(2)/5 - epsilon(2+epsilon)]_+/(1+epsilon)^2`,

where the endpoint can be non-strict for a convenient weaker corollary. If `delta>=1` the displayed lower bound is trivial. If epsilon is zero, the Fourier approximation error is at least `0.848528...` in normalized spectral norm.

## 6. Scope and research limit

These are new **verified project-specific coefficient restrictions**, not proof that the fixed four-stage architecture fails and not an FFT arithmetic lower bound. A general order-one quasiseparable inverse can be far from any diagonal, while remaining applicable with O(N) arithmetic. Such generators are not excluded. The original goal of constructing an exact Fourier factorization with o(N log N) work remains unsolved by this checkpoint.
