# HERO #0 — Phase 16: Gauge curvature, Fourier monomial centralizers, and the single-Green support gate

**Checkpoint date:** 10 October 2026  
**Status:** Complete algebraic proofs for the stated restricted classes; reproducible finite-field and symbolic certificates. **No exact all-input `O(N)` or `O(N log log N)` FFT has been found.** These theorems are not an unrestricted arithmetic-circuit lower bound. Independent mathematical novelty is **not established**.

## 0. Provenance and problem

We continue Phase 15's surviving question: can strongly vertex-asymmetric, mixed-axis sums/products of `O(N)`-applicable weighted-permutation Green resolvents equal the Fourier operator on **every fresh length-N input**, without hidden precomputation or parallel execution?

Write `F_N[i,j]=omega_N^(ij)` with primitive `omega_N=e^(-2 pi i/N)`, and `U_N=F_N/sqrt(N)`. A weighted permutation matrix is `P[i,pi(i)]=w_i`, and its Green solver is `G=(I-P)^(-1)` where the inverse exists. For `N=s^2`, use the four Margulis affine permutations in the prior Phase 15 package. For even `s` they all commute with the involution `tau(x,y)=(x,y+s/2)`.

This phase proves two independent architecture-specific impossibility families, a conditional approximation bound, a gauge defect gate, and an exact **N=4 positive control**. None of these results implies that a universal faster FFT exists or does not exist.

## 1. Full classification of monomial matrices commuting with F_N

**Theorem 16.1 (complex monomial centralizer).** Let `N>=2`, let `tau` be any permutation of residues modulo N, and `Q e_j=a_j e_(tau(j))` with all `a_j != 0`. Then `F_N Q=Q F_N` if and only if there are a unit `u` modulo N, a residue `b`, and nonzero complex scalar `c` satisfying

```
tau(j) = u*j+b (mod N)
u^2=1 (mod N),  2*b=0 (mod N), b^2=0 (mod N)
a_j = c*omega_N^(u*b*j).
```

For odd N, b=0. For N divisible by 4, b may equal N/2 in addition to 0. If N is 2 modulo 4, only b=0 is permitted. The same proof works over a field containing a primitive Nth root whose characteristic does not divide N.

**Proof.** Since `Q` is monomial, the (i,j) entry of the commuting equation is

```
a_j * omega^(i*tau(j)) = a_(tau^-1(i)) * omega^(tau^-1(i)*j).     (1)
```

At j=0, setting i=tau(k) yields `a_k=a_0*omega^(tau(k)*tau(0))`. Substitute into (1) and cancel nonzero factors:

```
i*(tau(j)-tau(0)) + tau(0)*tau(j) - j*tau^-1(i) = 0 mod N. (2)
```

At j=1, with `u=tau(1)-tau(0)` and `b=tau(0)`, (2) says `tau^-1(i)=u*i+b*tau(1)`. A bijective affine function has invertible slope, so u is a unit. Since tau itself has slope `tau(1)-tau(0)=u` and inverse slope u, `u^2=1`. Hence `tau(j)=u*j+b`. Substituting this into (2) gives `b^2+2*u*b*j=0` for every j. At j=0 and j=1 these imply `b^2=0` and `2*u*b=0`, equivalent to `2*b=0` since u is invertible. Finally `a_j=a_0*omega^(b*(u*j+b))=a_0*omega^(u*b*j)` because `b^2=0`. Conversely substitution into (1) verifies the equation for every i,j. QED.

**External prior art warning.** Pure permutation centralizers of DFTs have been studied for decades (e.g. P. J. S. G. Ferreira's *A Group of Permutations that Commute with the Discrete Fourier Transform*). The weighted/monomial classification is derived here for this project; global priority or novelty is NOT asserted.

## 2. Fully asymmetric *pure-gauge* Margulis networks are impossible

For each Margulis generator `pi_t` and scalar `c_t!=0`, choose a common collection of nonzero vertex potentials `d_i` and edge weights

```
w_t(i) = c_t*d_i/d_(pi_t(i)).                                      (3)
```

Put `D=diag(d_i)` and let `P_t^0[i,pi_t(i)]=c_t`. Then

```
P_t(w)=D P_t^0 D^-1,
(I-P_t(w))^-1 = D (I-P_t^0)^-1 D^-1.                        (4)
```

Thus *every* scalar-weighted sum and arbitrary ordered product of such resolvents is `T=D*T0*D^-1`, where T0 is the corresponding constant-weight network. These weight arrays can have strong asymmetry at every vertex.

**Theorem 16.2 (nonunitary-gauge exact no-go).** For every even `s>=2` and `N=s^2`, no such network with a common invertible complex diagonal gauge D equals F_N or U_N, assuming all constituent Green inverses exist.

**Proof.** All four reference permutations commute with the half-grid permutation `Pi_y`, so T0 commutes with Pi_y. Hence T commutes with `Q=D*Pi_y*D^-1`, a monomial matrix with the same underlying permutation. For `s>=4`, this permutation is **not affine modulo N**: its values at 0,1 imply translation by s/2, but its value at s/2 disagrees with that translation. Theorem 16.1 therefore excludes commuting with F_N. For `s=2`, the permutation is `tau(j)=1-j mod 4`, which *is* affine, but `b=1` violates `2*b=0 mod 4`, so the same theorem excludes it. QED.

**Scope:** Arbitrary nonzero *complex* D, including very ill-conditioned gauges, is covered for **exact** equality. The theorem requires one common D for every resolvent occurrence; unrelated vertex weights escape it. A product can contain any number of noncommuting layers.

## 3. Uniform approximation barrier for unitary gauges

Assume, in addition, that `|d_i|=1`, so Q is a unitary involution. Let `h=s/2` and `tau=Pi_y` for even `s>=4`.

**Theorem 16.3 (all-size phase-gauge spectral gap).** Every `T=D*T0*D*` with `T0 Pi_y=Pi_y T0`, where D is diagonal unitary, satisfies

\[
\boxed{\|U_{s^2}-T\|_2\ge
\frac12\sqrt{2-\frac{2\sqrt2}{s\sin(\pi/(2s))}}.}
\]

The bound is positive for all even `s>=4` and approaches `0.223252...` as s tends to infinity. It is a **proved lower bound**, not a claim that a Green network attains it.

**Proof.** Write `Q e_j=a_j e_(tau(j))`, so `|a_j|=1` and `a_j a_(tau(j))=1`. Acting on e0,

\[
([U,Q]e_0)_i=\frac{a_0\omega_N^{ih}-a_{\tau(i)}}{\sqrt N}.
\]

Group indices into involution pairs `i=s*x+y`, `tau(i)=i+h` with `0<=y<h`. For a pair, the two *desired* unit phases are `t_i=a0*omega^(ih)` and `t_tau=a0*omega^((i+h)h)`. Their product is `-i*a0^2*exp(-2 pi i y/s)`. The two available phases must instead multiply to 1. Minimizing the two squared phase discrepancies subject to that product constraint gives at least

```
4*(1-abs(cos(theta-pi*y/s)))
```

for one common angle theta. Summing over the s choices of x and h choices of y, and using the fact that all angles `pi*y/s` lie inside an interval shorter than pi/2, gives

\[
\max_\theta\sum_{y=0}^{h-1}|\cos(\theta-\pi y/s)|
=\left|\sum_{y=0}^{h-1}e^{i\pi y/s}\right|
=\frac{1/\sqrt2}{\sin(\pi/(2s))}.
\]

Consequently `||[U,Q]e0||_2^2 >= 2 - 2sqrt(2)/(s sin(pi/(2s)))`. Since `[T,Q]=0` and Q is unitary, `||[U,Q]||_2 <= 2||U-T||_2`. Combine with `||[U,Q]||_2 >= ||[U,Q]e0||_2`. QED.

For s=2 the displayed argument gives zero; exact noncommutation is nevertheless covered by Theorem 16.2. General complex, nonunitary D is also covered by Theorem 16.2 for exact equality, but **not** by this uniform approximation constant.

## 4. A gauge-defect rank barrier (generic potentials)

**Theorem 16.4.** Fix the Margulis permutations, scalar stage coefficients, and an invertible pure-gauge reference T_g. Change exactly k **individual vertex-weight occurrences**, counting the same edge weight again whenever it appears in a second resolvent stage. Let T be the modified network, assuming the changed inverses exist. Then

\[
\operatorname{rank}(T-T_g)\le k,\qquad
\operatorname{rank}([T,Q])\le2k,
\quad Q=D\Pi_yD^{-1}.
\]

This follows by telescoping the resolvent identity and sums/products, precisely as in Phase 15, but the baseline is now an arbitrary diagonal gauge of a symmetric reference.

For **Zariski-generic** nonzero potentials D, `rank([F_N,Q])=N` at even square N. To prove genericity, observe that the commutator determinant is a rational function of the d_i and is nonzero at D=I (the independent Phase 15 Pi_y Fourier commutator theorem). Its numerator is therefore a nonzero polynomial; the determinant can vanish only on a proper algebraic subset. Hence, for such generic D,

\[
\boxed{T=F_N\implies k\ge N/2.}
\]

This is **NOT** claimed for every D. The rank test for an exceptional D must be performed separately. Random tests over GF(65537) at N=16,64,256 gave full rank for every saved sample, which is evidence only, not the genericity proof.

### Linear-work gauge and holonomy recognizers

With constants c_t supplied, `recover_common_gauge` traverses the generator multigraph and solves `d_(pi_t(i))=c_t*d_i/w_t(i)` using a spanning forest. It either returns a valid common gauge or a contradiction. The work is `O(E)` field operations for E provided directed edges, including all inverse operations and comparisons. `spanning_gauge_residuals` returns a forest-normalized gauge plus the list of non-tree edge constraints (holonomies) that fail. This is an **early structural rejection gate**, not an FFT algorithm or a polynomial-time solution of the unrestricted Fourier compatibility system. If the c_t are themselves unknown, extra algebraic work is required.

## 5. A separate exact no-go: one Green layer plus any rook-supported layer

Let `P` be an arbitrary weighted permutation with `I-P` invertible. Suppose `F_N=(I-P)^(-1)*X`. Then `X=(I-P)F_N`, whose ith row is

\[
X_{i,j}=\omega_N^{ij}-w_i\omega_N^{\pi(i)j}
=\omega_N^{ij}(1-w_i\omega_N^{(\pi(i)-i)j}).
\]

If `pi(i)!=i`, this row has either zero or `gcd(N,pi(i)-i)` zero entries: the monomial equation `omega_N^((pi(i)-i)*j)=w_i^-1` has either no solutions or exactly the size of its kernel. If pi(i)=i, invertibility forces `w_i!=1`, so the entire row is nonzero. Thus every row has at least

\[
\boxed{N-\frac N{p_{\min}}}
\]

nonzero entries, where `p_min` is the smallest prime divisor of N. An analogous statement holds for each column of `F_N(I-P)`.

**Theorem 16.5 (fully asymmetric single-resolvent/rook impossibility).** Let `N=s^2`, `s>=3`, and let `R_s` be the vector space of all matrices supported on the rook pattern (same x **or** same y coordinate). Every row and column of a rook matrix has at most `2s-1` nonzeros, but `2s-1 < N-N/p_min` for every s>=3. Therefore

\[
\boxed{F_{s^2}\ne (I-P)^{-1}X\quad\text{and}\quad
F_{s^2}\ne Y(I-P)^{-1}}
\]

for **every** weighted permutation P with invertible I-P and **every** rook-supported X,Y, irrespective of all complex weights. This strictly concerns a *single Green solver on one side*, not a sum of several such solvers on the same side. The prime factor bound and the N=16 half-shift sharpness example were tested exactly over suitable finite fields.

### Optional numerical approximation extension

Assume P is **fixed-point-free**, `||P||_2 <= rho`, `X` has at most d nonzeros per row, and `alpha=d/N<1/2`. Write `T=(I-P)^(-1)X`. Then

\[
\boxed{\frac{\|U_N-T\|_F}{\sqrt N}\ge
\frac1{1+\rho}\sqrt{\frac{1-2\alpha}{1-\alpha}}.}
\]

**Proof.** Distinct rows of U are orthonormal, so each row of `(I-P)U` has squared norm `1+|w_i|^2`, with each entry magnitude at most `(1+|w_i|)/sqrt(N)`. Retaining at most d entries leaves squared residual at least `1+t^2-alpha(1+t)^2` for `t=|w_i|`. Its minimum over `t>=0` is `(1-2alpha)/(1-alpha)`. Sum over N rows, then use `||(I-P)(T-U)||_F <= (1+rho)||T-U||_F`. QED. This bound is independent of whether X is invertible; it is a restricted numerical approximation lower bound.

## 6. Small-size positive control: an exact two-layer Green-sum Fourier circuit at N=4

A network can escape Theorem 16.2 by having **genuine gauge curvature** and can escape Theorem 16.5 by having a **sum** rather than a single Green solver on each side.

`positive_small_factor.py` constructs unnormalized F4 exactly in cyclotomic arithmetic. First choose an explicit invertible 4x4 x-fiber matrix V and set

```
X = V^-1,
Y = F4 * V.
```

Both X and Y have rook support, and `Y X=F4` is an exact symbolic identity. The script then decomposes **each** rook matrix into a sum of four invertible weighted-permutation Green resolvents on the s=2 Margulis permutations `(id, xflip, id, yflip)`.

For every swap pair (i,j), desired off-diagonal values a,b can be obtained from a two-cycle Green solver by choosing `t` such that `t^2-t-ab=0`, then weights `w_i=a/t`, `w_j=b/t`. Two arbitrary diagonal Green resolvents supply the remaining diagonal entries. Therefore

\[
\boxed{F_4=(G_0+G_x+G_1+G_y)_{\rm out}
              (G_0+G_x+G_1+G_y)_{\rm in}}.
\]

**All eight inverses and the matrix product are checked exactly by SymPy**, not merely to floating-point precision. The phase-dependent weights have nonzero gauge curvature (otherwise Theorem 16.2 would exclude the construction). This is only a **fixed N=4 positive control**; it has **no asymptotic implication**, and it does not establish new optimal arithmetic counts.
