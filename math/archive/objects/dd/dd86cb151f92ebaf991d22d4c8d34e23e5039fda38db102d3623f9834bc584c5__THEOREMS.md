# HERO #0 — Phase 11: Fourier sparse-inverse rigidity and a constructive Green-sum escape

**Date:** 2026-10-10  
**Goal:** an exact, fresh, arbitrary-input Fourier transform in sequential `O(N)` or `O(N log log N)` arithmetic work.  
**Status:** A proved restricted network lower bound and an explicit infinite family of `O(N)`-applicable, high-rank, globally mixing candidate **layers**. **No exact universal sub-`N log N` Fourier algorithm.** No independent novelty claim.

## 0. Computational and evidentiary contract

Work over a field containing a primitive `N`-th root of unity; the main all-length construction uses rational/real exact scalars. Arithmetic complexity counts scalar additions, multiplications, inversions and address arithmetic in a model where exact scalar operations cost one. A rational such as `(1-1/N)^N` can have `Theta(N log N)` representation bits: **nothing below proves a bit-complexity or fixed-word implementation**, and the exact transformation matrix is **not** computed by the escape construction. Finite-field regression tests use `GF(65537)` with generator `3` and transform sizes dividing `65536`.

The prior Phase 23 checkpoint already showed that **pure** products of sparse-inverse Green blocks require logarithmic depth, by support propagation. The new contribution here is the **low-rank-correction extension** through a Fourier-specific rigidity bound, together with an explicit counter-construction demonstrating why **sums** of Green solvers are outside that lower bound. Fourier rigidity, operator-Schmidt rank and sparse matrix factorization have prior literature; we do **not** claim this theorem is new to the mathematical community.

## 1. Theorem A — per-row sparse Fourier rigidity

Let `F_N` be the unnormalized DFT over a field containing a primitive `N`-th root `zeta`, with `(F_N)_{j,k}=zeta^{jk}`. Suppose an `N x N` matrix `S` contains at most `s` nonzero elements **per row**. Then

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}(F_N-S)\ge \left\lfloor\frac{N}{s+1}\right\rfloor.}\tag{A}
\]

The same statement holds for `F_N^{-1}`, and under arbitrary independent row/column permutations and nonzero diagonal row/column rescalings, provided the row labels in the proof are transported through the permutations.

**Proof.** Set `m=floor(N/(s+1))`. Consider the first `m` consecutive Fourier rows, indexed `0,...,m-1`. Their corresponding `m` rows of `S` have at most `ms` occupied columns in total. Thus at least `N-ms >= m` columns are untouched. Select any `m` of them, with distinct Fourier-root labels `j_1,...,j_m`. On this `m x m` submatrix, `F_N-S` equals the Fourier submatrix

\[
  \begin{pmatrix}
  1 & \cdots & 1\\
  \zeta^{j_1} & \cdots & \zeta^{j_m}\\
  \vdots&&\vdots\\
  (\zeta^{j_1})^{m-1}&\cdots&(\zeta^{j_m})^{m-1}
  \end{pmatrix}.
\]

This is a Vandermonde matrix with determinant `product_{p<q}(zeta^{j_q}-zeta^{j_p}) != 0`, since the roots are distinct. Thus `rank(F_N-S)>=m`. For the inverse Fourier matrix, replace `zeta` with `zeta^{-1}` and multiply by the nonzero scalar `1/N`. Row/column permutations and diagonal scalings do not destroy the determinant. QED.

**Sharpness warning.** The stronger guess `rank(F_N-S)>=N-s` is false: for the length-4 unnormalized Fourier matrix, let `E=ones(4) outer [1,1,-1,-1]`. Then `rank(E)=1` while `S=F_4-E` has exactly **two** nonzero entries in each row. The bound (A) correctly gives only `floor(4/3)=1`.

## 2. Theorem B — sparse-inverse plus low-rank depth tradeoff

Suppose `F_N=T_L ... T_1` exactly, each `T_i` invertible, and

\[
T_i^{-1}=S_i+E_i,
\qquad \operatorname{rowdegree}(S_i)\le d_i,
\qquad \operatorname{rank}(E_i)\le r_i.
\]

No requirement is imposed that the matrices commute or that their sparse graph edge patterns share an ordering. Define

\[
s=\prod_{i=1}^L d_i, \qquad R=\sum_{i=1}^L r_i.
\]

Then necessarily

\[
\boxed{ R\ge \left\lfloor\frac{N}{s+1}\right\rfloor,
\quad\text{and hence}\quad N<(1+s)(1+R).}\tag{B}
\]

**Proof.** Since `F_N` is invertible, its inverse is the reversed product of the `T_i^{-1}` factors. After relabeling their order, let `A_i=S_i+E_i` and `A=A_1...A_L=F_N^{-1}`. Let `S=S_1...S_L`. The number of nonzero entries per row in a matrix product cannot exceed the product of the per-row maximum degrees, so `rowdegree(S)<=s`. The telescoping product identity gives

\[
 A-S=\sum_{i=1}^L(S_1...S_{i-1}) E_i(A_{i+1}...A_L).
\]

Every summand has rank at most `r_i`, whence `rank(A-S)<=R`. Apply Theorem A to `A=F_N^{-1}`: `R>=floor(N/(s+1))`. Rearrangement of the integer floor gives the strict inequality in (B). QED.

**Application to a low-rank correction to a Green layer.** If `G_i^{-1}=S_i` is sparse and `T_i=G_i+U_iV_i^T` is invertible with `U_i,V_i` having `r_i` columns, the Woodbury identity shows

\[
T_i^{-1}=S_i-S_iU_i(I+V_i^TS_iU_i)^{-1}V_i^TS_i,
\]

so its correction has rank at most `r_i`. The same theorem applies to a fully arbitrary set of per-layer permutations and input-independent coefficients.

**Corollary.** If `d_i<=d` and `r_i<=r`, for fixed constants `d>=2,r>=0`, then an exact Fourier factorization requires

\[
\boxed{N<(1+d^L)(1+rL).}\tag{C}
\]

A choice `L=O(log log N)` fails for sufficiently large `N`, because its right-hand side is polylogarithmic. For constant degree and constant correction rank, **depth must grow at least on the order of `log N`**. This is a rigorous obstruction for this layer grammar, **not a lower bound for arbitrary arithmetic circuits**.

**Exact length examples.** With inverse support degree `d=2` and rank-one correction per layer: `N=16,L=2` is impossible (`(4+1)(2+1)=15<16`); `N=32,L=2` impossible (`15<32`); `N=64,L=3` impossible (`(8+1)*4=36<64`); `N=128,L=4` impossible (`(16+1)*5=85<128`). The test suite also independently certifies a nonvanishing Fourier minor for each sampled graph product.

## 3. Why this is stronger than the Phase 10 Schmidt-rank gate

The SWAP permutation between two equal-dimensional registers has maximal operator-Schmidt rank `N`, but its inverse is itself, with exactly **one** nonzero element per row. Thus maximal Schmidt rank alone does not imply a Fourier circuit is computationally difficult; and a layer can have high Schmidt rank yet contribute almost no inverse-support expansion. The new theorem is insensitive to endpoint permutations and combines inverse row sparsity with global low-rank corrections.

It still does **not** apply when a full layer is the **sum of multiple** independently computed Green solvers, because the inverse of the sum can be dense and far from any sparse-plus-constant-rank matrix.

## 4. Theorem C — an explicit infinite escape family of cheap noncommuting layers

For **every square** `N=A^2>=4`, on the tensor index `(i,j)` in `{0,...,A-1}^2`, define `S` as the SWAP permutation `(i,j)->(j,i)`. Let `C` be the length-`N` cyclic shift, and

\[
D=\operatorname{diag}\left(1+\frac{1}{2N},1,\ldots,1\right), \quad
b=1-\frac1N,\quad a=\frac1{100N},\quad \epsilon=\frac1{10^6N^2}.
\]

Define two Green solvers and their sum:

\[
G_1=(I-aS)^{-1},\qquad
G_2=(I-bDC)^{-1},\qquad
\boxed{T_N=G_1+\epsilon G_2.}\tag{D}
\]

The following hold **for every such N**, not only for the tested examples:

1. Applying `T_N` to any vector needs **O(N) sequential exact scalar operations**, after `O(N)` scalar preparation. Because `S^2=I`, `G1=(I+aS)/(1-a^2)` takes one permutation and two scalings; because `C` is a single weighted cycle, `G2` is solved by one cyclic geometric recurrence in linear work.
2. `T_N` is invertible and satisfies the uniform complex spectral condition-number bound **`kappa_2(T_N)<2`**.
3. **Every entry of `T_N^{-1}` is nonzero**, and in fact

   \[
   \boxed{\min_{i,j}|(T_N^{-1})_{ij}|\ge\frac{\epsilon}{8}=\frac1{8\cdot10^6N^2}.}\tag{E}
   \]
4. Its operator-Schmidt rank across the balanced `A|A` cut is **exactly N**.
5. `T_N*1` is not a constant vector: the elementary common-eigenvector obstruction present in scalar-weighted permutation Green sums is avoided.

**Proof, weighted cycle and work.** Put `B=bDC`. Its maximum row and column absolute sums are below one. The inverse is the **closed-form finite geometric sum**

\[
G_2=\frac{I+B+\cdots+B^{N-1}}{1-b^N\prod_iD_{ii}}.
\]

Because `C` is one full cycle, each entry of the numerator gets exactly one positive term. The inequalities `(1-1/N)^{N-1}>1/e>1/3` and `(1-1/N)^N<=1/e<3/8`, together with `1<D_{00}<=9/8`, give the convenient componentwise bounds

\[
\boxed{\frac13<(G_2)_{ij}<3\quad\text{for every }i,j.}
\]

Thus `||G2||_infty,||G2||_1,||G2||_2<=3N` and `||G2||_F<=3N`. A cycle solver for `y-B y=x` uses its per-cycle product denominator and forward/back substitution, hence `O(N)` arithmetic.

**Proof, dense inverse.** Let `H=(I-aS)G2(I-aS)`; by the preceding entry bounds,

\[
 H_{ij}\ge \frac13-6a>\frac14,\qquad ||H||_infty\le 12N.
\]

Expand the inverse of `T_N` around `G1`:

\[
 T_N^{-1}=(I-aS)-\epsilon H+R,
 \quad ||R||_\infty\le64N^2\epsilon^2<\epsilon/8.
\]

The remainder inequality uses `||\epsilon(I-aS)G_2||_infty <4N\epsilon<1/2` and the Neumann series. For `i!=j`, `(I-aS)_{ij}` is either zero or `-a`. Since `H_{ij}>1/4`, every such inverse entry is negative and has magnitude at least `epsilon/8`. For diagonal entries, `(I-aS)_{ii}>=1-a`, while the total perturbation is below `12N epsilon+64N^2epsilon^2`, so they are positive and larger than `1/2`. This proves (E). The same norm inequalities and

\[
||T_N||_2\le\frac1{1-a}+3N\epsilon,
\qquad ||T_N^{-1}||_2\le\frac{1+a}{1-4N\epsilon}
\]

prove `kappa_2(T_N)<2` for all `N>=4`.

**Proof, maximal operator-Schmidt rank.** For the realignment map `R`, `R(S)` is a permutation matrix. Further,

\[
R(S)^T R(I)=vv^T,\quad v_{(i,j)}=1_{i=j},\quad v^Tv=A.
\]

Therefore

\[
\sigma_{\min}(R(G_1))=\frac{a}{1-a^2}.
\]

Meanwhile `||epsilon R(G2)||_2<= epsilon ||G2||_F <=3N epsilon < a/(1-a^2)`. The perturbation cannot make the realignment singular. Thus `SchmidtRank(T_N)=N` exactly.

**Proof, nonconstant response.** `G1*1=(1-a)^{-1}*1`. If `G2*1` were constant `c*1`, then `(I-bDC)(c*1)=c(1-bD_{ii})` would need to equal `1` at every coordinate, impossible because `D_{00}!=D_{11}`. Therefore `T_N*1` is nonconstant.

**IMPORTANT LIMITS.** This `T_N` is **not** the Fourier matrix, nor a new FFT. Indeed its chosen parameters make `||T_N-I||_2=O(1/N)`; products of only `O(log log N)` copies of such near-identity matrices cannot equal the normalized Fourier transform, which is a constant distance from the identity. To pursue Fourier, the next grammar must permit **substantially mixing** Green sums and justify a complete Fourier factorization. The scalar rational *parameters* above have `O(log N)`-bit descriptions, but the exact cycle products can have much larger bit length. The `O(N)` accounting is **exact scalar-arithmetic only**, not a bit-complexity theorem or proved floating-point method.

## 5. Exact necessary and sufficient polynomial identity for the next escape search

For invertible `A=G1^{-1}` and `B=G2^{-1}`, the statement that a single sum `G1+G2` equals the DFT `F_N` is exactly equivalent to

\[
\boxed{A F_N B=A+B.}\tag{F}
\]

For `A=I-diag(a_i)P_pi` and `B=I-diag(b_i)P_sigma`, let `q(j)=sigma^{-1}(j)`. Then the `(i,j)`-th residual, which must vanish, is

\[
F_{ij}-a_iF_{\pi(i),j}-b_{q(j)}F_{i,q(j)}
  +a_ib_{q(j)}F_{\pi(i),q(j)}-(A+B)_{ij}.
\]

Every entry involves only four Fourier roots and a quadratic polynomial in two weights. This is a **precisely specified algebraic search gate**; `two_green_sandwich_gate.py` evaluates it exactly and independently checks it against full matrix products. No candidate currently solves (F) for all N.

One caveat that matters: if both Green solvers are `(I-aP)^-1` with a single scalar `a` each, *every* such solver maps the all-ones vector to a multiple of itself. Any sum or product of these scalar-weight Green solvers has that invariant, while `F_N*1=N e_0`. So the weighted `D` and other noncommuting mechanisms are necessary even to avoid that elementary obstruction.

## 7. Research prior art and precise originality boundary

This is a deliberately narrow theorem within a rich existing literature. The consecutive-row Fourier full-spark/Vandermonde fact is classical (e.g. Achanta et al., *The spark of Fourier matrices*, Digital Signal Processing, 2017). Matrix-rigidity and sparse-factorization research includes Kumar–Volk, *Lower Bounds for Matrix Factorization*, 2019/2020, and extensive earlier Fourier-rigidity work. The Phase 23 project **already proved the pure inverse-sparsity support-propagation restriction**, without rank corrections. We should refer to Phase 11 as a verified **synthesis and extension within the project**, not a globally new theorem until a full literature audit confirms novelty.

The published uniform exact Fourier algorithm of OpenAI Math #130 claims `O(N (log N)^(1-10^-13))` in unrestricted-coefficient exact complex arithmetic. Nothing in Phase 11 improves that result, gives a new integer-multiplication bound, or supplies a novel all-input FFT:

## 8. Phase 12 continuation gate

Work with **substantially mixing, weighted, independently permuted, noncommuting sums of at least two Green solvers**, not pure inverse-sparse products or rank-one tweaks. For a single sum, solve the sparse sandwich identity (F) over exact small finite fields, including singular branches; a numerical near-match is insufficient. For two or more summed-Green stages, seek a uniform exact identity for *all* N (ideally any fresh input) and charge (i) coefficient generation, (ii) cycle setup, (iii) every Green solve, (iv) all input/output scans and (v) indexing. Validate beyond one or two finite lengths.

**Do not recycle** Phase 7 shifts, Phase 8 short cycles, Phase 9 sparse innovations, Phase 10 low-order rational input recurrences, Phase 23 pure inverse-sparse products, or the Phase 10 one-shot Schmidt low-rank attempt. A verified full Fourier factorization, rather than a merely expressive cheap operator, is the missing theorem.
