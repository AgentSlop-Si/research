### Old shift scan vs new resultants

An initial Phase21 shift scan (`reciprocal_union.py`) already gave an O_s(N) *conditional* theorem, but performed two (s+1)x(s+1) determinant interpolations for every candidate delay until finding one. Its finite-time work varies strongly with the hidden defect separation, e.g. a true delay requiring 1,112 shift trials at N=4096 cost about 2.17 million field ops. A different test with a true relative delay 50 required only about 438 thousand. The new `compressed_resultant.py` computes a **global scalar polynomial once**, then enumerates only subgroup roots of its short quotient. This is less sensitive to the order of the hidden delay, and gives the above positive N>=4096 structured benchmarks.

For a N=4096 structured instance, the earlier *uncompressed* 226-sample degree-225 resultant route cost about 997,495 field operations; the compressed 31-sample route cost 464,912 on a comparable explicit two-factor/two-defect input. Arithmetic order remains O_s(N) either way; this improvement is finite constant-factor and algebraic sample compression.

## Exact charged algorithm components for fixed s

1. Green preconditioner `P`: O(N), recorded by the module.
2. First `4s` direct Fourier samples: `O(sN)`, every addition and root multiplication charged, no N-point FFT hidden.
3. Batch-invert nonzero samples: `O(s)` field operations and one field inverse.
4. `b_s+1` resultant determinant evaluations and three independent witnesses: only O_s(1) input-independent-size matrices. Reconstruct symmetric quotient `V` with O(b_s^2) arithmetic and charged inversions, then `U` with O(b_s^2) field operations.
5. Evaluate `U` on the Nth roots with the preserved sparse-Fourier kernel: `O_s(N)` arithmetic for fixed s, including short convolution/NTT or sparse-output logic and all root generation the helper charges. Nonzero `U` has at most `2b_s` subgroup roots for testing.
6. At most `2b_s` candidate polynomial gcds and Prony calls; each exact subgroup root scan costs `O(sN)`. A full cyclic certificate is charged. Candidate uniqueness on the first 4s probes avoids repeated full certificates for globally invertible alternatives.
7. All-N spectral inversion: batch inversion + O(sN) short denominator evaluation. Cyclic Green solve: O(N).
8. If certification fails: a **fully charged** all-length Bluestein fallback. Generic total O(N logN) in the declared field model.

In the exact field-operation model, inversion is counted as one field primitive. Implementing inversion by modular exponentiation, and integer field multiplication on arbitrary-precision words, can have additional bit costs; this does **not** establish an arbitrary-precision integer multiplication or real/complex FFT upper bound.

## Input families are measure-zero at fixed s

For fixed polynomial supports, after normalizing a nonzero denominator coefficient, `H/Q` has at most `2+(s-1)=s+1` free field/complex coefficients. With finitely many support choices for each N, it is a finite union of sets of dimension <=s+1. For N>s+1, it cannot contain a generic complex N-vector. This remains true if the sparse polynomial Q is represented as a constant number of hidden graph factors. Automatic *discovery* for the promised family is useful, but does not change its codimension.

## Important distinctions

- Asymptotic: for fixed s with supplied roots and nondegenerate structured input, O_s(N) **field arithmetic**; unrestricted all-input algorithm is O(NlogN) due fallback.
- Numerical: no floating-point backward/forward-error certificate; inverse Fourier samples and Hankel roots can be poorly conditioned.
- Experimental: finite-field operation counters and exact DFT comparisons support the implementation; small examples do not prove a general upper bound.
- Prior art: Prony, sparse rational interpolation, fast cyclic convolution, resultant methods and Fourier/Green conjugation predate this checkpoint. No worldwide novelty claim.
