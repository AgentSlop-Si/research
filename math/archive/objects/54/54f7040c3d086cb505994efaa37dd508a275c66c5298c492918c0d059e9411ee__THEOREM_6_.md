# Projective Fourier Gauge Rigidity and Exact Fixed-Stage Feasibility

**Date:** 10 October 2026  
**Research status:** Exact elementary theorems, deterministic finite-field/complex tests, matrix-free linear-work loss and gradient for *fixed stages*. **No exact arbitrary-input o(N log N) FFT algorithm. No global originality claim.**

## 0. Contract

Take the **unnormalized** DFT matrix `F_N[i,j]=omega^(i*j)` with `omega=exp(-2πi/N)` and `Delta=diag(omega^i)`. Fix `N×N` matrices `A,B`. The motivating case is two Green sums, `A=G1+G2` and `B=G3+G4`, with fully independent permutations/diagonal gauges, and each Green branch applicable in O(N) arithmetic once its parameters are given.

We ask whether `F_N=L A D B` for **some arbitrary invertible diagonal** matrices `L,D`. The choice of `A,B` is fixed during every test. Arbitrary changes to Green permutations, branch weights, or other parameters are separate outer optimization variables and are **not** eliminated by these theorems.

Let `b_j=B e_j`, define `C_j=A diag(b_j)`, and `K_j=C_(j+1 mod N)-Delta C_j`. Then the j-th Fourier covariance column is `K_j d=0` when `D=diag(d)`.

## Theorem I — Complete rank-only test for *all* output/middle gauges

Construct the stacked linear matrix

`H_all = [K_0 ; K_1 ; ... ; K_(N-1)]` of shape `N²×N`, and set `H=C_0`.

Over an infinite field containing a primitive N-th root (in particular over C), the following are equivalent:

1. There are invertible diagonals `L,D` with `LADB=F_N`.
2. The nullspace `W=ker H_all` is nonzero and **none** of these `2N` functionals vanishes identically on W: `d -> d_i` for each i, and `d -> (H d)_i` for each i.
3. `rank H_all < N`, and for every i
   
   `rank([H_all ; e_i^T]) > rank H_all`,
   
   `rank([H_all ; H[i,:]]) > rank H_all`.

**Proof.** Fourier covariance of `M=A diag(d) B` is `MS=Delta M`, where `S e_j=e_(j+1 mod N)`. Column j of this residual equals `K_j d`. Thus `H_all d=0` iff every row of M is `c_i (1,omega^i,...,omega^((N-1)i))`, with `c=H d`. The diagonals are invertible and normalizable exactly when `d_i` and `c_i` are all nonzero; then take `L=diag(c_i^-1)`. For a linear subspace W over an infinite field, finitely many proper linear hyperplanes cannot cover W; therefore a common d avoiding all 2N zero constraints exists if and only if none of the corresponding linear functionals vanishes identically on W. A row functional vanishes on `ker H_all` iff it is in the row span of H_all; this is exactly the rank-augmentation test. □

**Finite-field variant.** The same conditions are sufficient over GF(q) when `q>2N`, by the union bound: each proper hyperplane occupies at most a `1/q` fraction of W. A random vector in W succeeds with probability at least `1-2N/q`. Necessity holds over every field; when `q<=2N`, the rank tests can be insufficient for simultaneous nonzero coordinates.

**Deterministic witness construction over C.** Given a basis `v_1,...,v_r` of W, consider `d(t)=sum_(k=1)^r t^(k-1) v_k`. Every forbidden coordinate and output row is a *nonzero polynomial* of degree at most `r-1` under the rank-augmentation hypotheses. Their product has at most `2N(r-1)` roots; hence one of `2N(r-1)+1` distinct t-values yields a valid d. This is a finite constructive proof, not an O(N) algorithm for generating Green parameters.

**Cost.** Building `H_all` explicitly takes N full operator-column evaluations and polynomial-time elimination (possibly superquadratic). This is a *synthesis feasibility test*; it does not beat FFT operation counts. Classical diagonal-similarity via `X=BSB^-1`, `Y=A^-1 Delta A` is an alternative complete criterion when A,B are invertible and was already derived in this project's Phase 18/19.

## Theorem II — Complete two-column feasibility condition

Replace `H_all` by the single `N×N` pencil `K=K_0=C_1-Delta C_0`. The exact same rank-augmentation conditions with `K` and `H=C_0` are **necessary and sufficient to match the FIRST TWO Fourier columns** using some invertible middle and output diagonals. They do *not* certify any later Fourier column.

Consequences:

- `det K != 0` rejects all middle/output gauges (the prior project phase-pencil result).
- `det K=0` alone is not sufficient. A nonzero vector in `ker K` may be forced to have a zero middle gauge entry, or to produce a zero output first column.
- For `rank K=N-1`, the middle diagonal is unique up to scalar when feasible, and can be built from any nonzero cofactor column of `adj(K)`, or by nullspace elimination. This eliminates N free middle-gauge variables from subsequent symbolic search; remaining Green weights and cycle permutations still require solving.

The `GF(17), N=4` verifier contains separate witnesses for: (i) a true Fourier factor, (ii) singular K but forced-zero d, (iii) singular K and all-nonzero d possible but unavoidable zero seed row, and (iv) first two Fourier columns feasible but all-column compatibility impossible.

## Theorem III — Quantitative projective spectral gap

Now work over C. Define

`C0=A diag(b0), C1=A diag(b1), K=C1-Delta C0`,

`Q=C0* C0+C1* C1` (where `*` denotes conjugate transpose).

If `K` is nonsingular, `Q` is positive definite. Define

`gamma² = min_(d != 0) (||K d||₂²)/(d* Q d)
        = lambda_min( Q^(-1/2) K* K Q^(-1/2) ) > 0`.

**For EVERY invertible diagonal `L,D`**,

`||L A D B - F_N||₂ >= gamma / sqrt(2)`.

**Proof.** Write `D=diag(d)`, `u=C0 d`, `v=C1 d`, and `z_i=omega^i`. The first two columns of `LADB` have entries `(l_i u_i,l_i v_i)`, whereas those of F are `(1,z_i)`. Elementary orthogonal projection in C² gives, when `s_i=|u_i|²+|v_i|²>0`,

`inf_(l_i in C) (|l_i u_i-1|²+|l_i v_i-z_i|²)
      = |v_i-z_i u_i|²/s_i`.

If `s_i=0`, the row error is 2, and the nonnegative lower bound below still holds. Put `T=sum_i s_i=||u||²+||v||²=d*Qd`. Since each `s_i<=T`, summing the rowwise lower bounds yields

`||(LADB-F)[:,{0,1}]||_F² >= ||v-Delta u||² / T
                                 = ||K d||²/(d*Qd) >= gamma²`.

The Frobenius norm of two selected columns is at most `sqrt(2)` times the spectral norm of the entire matrix. This gives the result. □

**Important scope**: The bound is for the **unnormalized** Fourier matrix. For `U_N=F_N/sqrt(N)` and arbitrary output gauges, the induced bound is `gamma/sqrt(2N)`, not a size-independent constant. These inequalities are *restricted approximation obstructions*, not a general worst-case complexity lower bound.

**Numerical certification**: The formula for gamma is an exact mathematical statement. Reported floating-point gamma values are numerical diagnostics only; rigorous decimal lower bounds would require certified interval singular-value computations. Exact modular nonzero determinants certify exact infeasibility, but cannot by themselves certify a positive *numerical* gamma for a chosen complex parameter lift without further magnitude bounds.

### A fully exact, rational, genuine four-Green example

For N=2 let `P=[[0,1],[1,0]]`, `G_+=(I-P/2)^-1`, `G_-=(I+P/2)^-1`. Both are invertible weighted two-cycles with nonzero weights. Direct inversion over Q gives

`(3/8)G_+ + (3/8)G_- = I`.

Use this pair for each of the two Green-sum stages, so `A=B=I`. With omega=-1, `K=diag(-1,1)`, `Q=I`, and `gamma=1`. Hence for **every** invertible L,D,

`||L A D B - F_2||₂ >= 1/sqrt(2)`.

Indeed the best possible diagonal approximation has spectral error exactly 1. Every claimed formula is rational except the final square root, so this is a genuine exact example without numerical inference.

## Theorem IV — Eliminate the output gauge during optimization in O(N) per two-column probe

For fixed A,B and middle gauge d, the optimally row-scaled *two-column* loss is

`P_2(d)=sum_i |(K d)_i|²/(|(C0d)_i|²+|(C1d)_i|²)`

on the open domain where every row denominator is positive. The minimizing output gauge for row i is

`ell_i=[conj(u_i)+conj(v_i) omega^i] / (|u_i|²+|v_i|²)`.

If an optimal ell_i happens to be zero, the displayed minimum is the infimum over **invertible** diagonals (approached by nonzero ell_i); for a genuine Fourier equality solution all ell_i are nonzero.

The Wirtinger gradient `g=∂P_2/∂conj(d)` is

`r0=ell*u - 1; r1=ell*v - omega_powers;`

`g=conj(b0) ⊙ A* [conj(ell)⊙r0]
  +conj(b1) ⊙ A* [conj(ell)⊙r1]`.

This follows from the envelope theorem for the exact rowwise least-squares minimizers. **If A is a constant sum of O(N)-apply Green branches**, both the loss and its exact analytic gradient are evaluable in `O(N)` sequential arithmetic **for a supplied d and supplied stage parameters**, using four forward/adjoint recurrences. There is no claim of global optimizer convergence, no claim that a few rows imply full Fourier covariance, and no claim that the Green stage parameters can be found in linear total time.

Finite-difference tests at N=8,16,32 checked the gradient against a separate directional numerical derivative to less than 4e-9 absolute discrepancy.

## Originality and limitations

The project Phase 18/19 already has Fourier covariance, diagonal similarity and cocycle methods. The immediately previous project round already introduced `det K_0` and the full-spark `GF(65537)` rejection witness. Classical linear algebra already has orthogonal projection, least squares, nullspace-rank tests, finite union of proper hyperplanes, generalized singular values, and adjugate elimination. This note's **project-specific additional result** is the complete nonzero-aware inverse-free rank criterion, quantitative all-gauge spectral gap, and linear-work row-gauge-eliminated objective/gradient for cyclic Green sums.

This is **new relative to inspected project checkpoints**, not certified globally novel for publication. It has not solved the existence of `O(N)`-cost exact Green parameters for arbitrary-length DFTs. It also does not contradict publicly reported all-length `o(N log N)` exact algorithms under a permissive exact complex arithmetic model.
