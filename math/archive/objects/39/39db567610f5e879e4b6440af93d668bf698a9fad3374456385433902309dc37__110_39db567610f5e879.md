# Phase 10 — Fresh-input Fourier graphs and translated multiplication

**Answer to the main question: no. Our project has not established a new
`o(N log N)` algorithm for arbitrary fresh full-output Fourier transforms or
a new integer-multiplication bound.** This round investigates input-independent
sharing, independently of the linked kappa construction.

The supplied Phase 9 compiler is useful for prepared single-edit convolution
streams. Its saved transforms and polynomial kernels depend on the old data.
This package moves the research question to fresh inputs and exact linear graphs.

The user's new `AB=(A-k)B+kB` proposal is implemented and tested too. It gives
large special-case gains near powers of two, including detector and correction
cost, but the current family does not cover every input. Read
`TRANSLATION_RESEARCH.md` first for that proposal, its positive example,
universal-cover criterion, finite coverage results, and signed-digit limitation.

The substantive result is a proved limitation of two natural sharing schemes:

- An evenly spaced set of at most `N/2` Fourier modes cannot explain the full
  Fourier matrix with a sparse entrywise correction: at least `N²/4`
  correction entries remain. This applies to every choice of encoding using
  that mode subspace, for dyadic `N` and dyadic subspace dimension.
- The implemented general Fourier-column skeleton architecture, when decoded
  by independent direct dot products and an entrywise correction, has an
  `Omega(N^(3/2))` scalar-operation lower bound. This is an architecture-specific
  result, **not** a lower bound for all Fourier algorithms.

Factoring and sharing the dense correction recovers the ordinary FFT in the
half-rank example. Therefore a dense residual alone is not a reason to reject
a more sophisticated graph. The missing result is a recursively cheaper
factorization of the decoder and residual together.

## Run

```bash
python3 -m unittest discover -s tests -v
python3 experiment.py
python3 verify_certificates.py
python3 translation_experiment.py
python3 audit_uploaded.py
```

Run from this directory. The input audit needs NumPy/SciPy and optionally Numba,
as the supplied compiler does. It preserves the uploaded ZIP and runs its
unchanged contents in a temporary directory. In the recorded environment,
21 of its tests passed and 2 native-backend tests errored because Numba was
absent; its separate exact audit passed. The 18 new Phase 10 tests all passed.

The Fourier graph search gives no speedup. Integer special-case timings are
machine-specific comparisons to Python's bigint operation, not an FFT benchmark.
No asymptotic improvement, originality of the underlying linear-algebra or
signed-digit ideas, or integration with the external integer-multiplication
theorem is claimed. This round narrows the search with proofs and leaves
reusable exact checkers for stronger candidates.
