# Independent review: fixed binary-affine counter-cycle banks

9 October 2026 UTC. The statement reviewed is the construction track's `FIXED_GF2_AFFINE_BANK_OBSTRUCTION.md`. The proof below is independently reconstructed. No publication-priority claim is made, and no constructor or uploaded code is executed.

## Accepted theorem and important counting distinction

Let `N=2^k`, `r=floor(k/2)`, `m=2^r`, and let `Z` be addition by one modulo N in the binary coordinate labeling. Suppose the allowed cycle orders are `P_j=sigma_j Z sigma_j^-1`, `j=1,...,d`, where each `sigma_j(x)=A_j x+b_j` is an invertible affine map over `GF(2)^k`. The maps may depend on N. Let every inserted internal bank and every endpoint monomial preserve each contiguous block of m coordinates.

For a product of sums of weighted Green terms using these orders, let **M count every Green occurrence in the full expression**. This is not the number d of allowed orders and not merely the number L of layers. Each defining Green inverse must exist; weights may vanish. Put `u=floor((k-r-1)/d)`. Every exact realization of a complex flat unitary target scaled to entry magnitude one must satisfy

`M >= 2^(u-1)`.

The same conclusion holds for Fourier matrices over their cyclotomic field and any characteristic-zero extension: the Fourier minors have the same rank after scalar extension. No arbitrary-positive-characteristic version follows from this proof.

If each of L layers contains at most d terms, then `M<=Ld`, so the necessary consequence `Ld>=2^(u-1)` is valid. Reusing an order reduces neither M nor the resulting rank charge. Thus fixed L and fixed d are excluded asymptotically in this representation class, even with size-dependent affine maps.

## Independent proof

Choose a linear parity form f with its first r low coefficients zero. Also impose that each `A_j^T f` has its first u low coefficients zero. These are homogeneous linear constraints on the k coefficients of f, at most `r+du<=k-1` in number. Regardless of dependencies, a nonzero solution exists.

The set `S={x:f(x)=0}` has size N/2 because f is nonzero. It is constant on every contiguous m-block because the first r bits do not occur. All permitted internal banks and endpoint monomials therefore preserve S.

Pulling S back through sigma_j gives a balanced affine parity set with nonzero linear mask `g_j=A_j^T f` and a possible constant `f(b_j)`. The first u low bits of g_j vanish. During the counter transition `x -> x+1`, its parity can change only at a carry through the low u bits. Exactly `N/2^u` transitions are candidates, including wrap. Every cyclic binary-valued sequence has equal numbers of 0-to-1 and 1-to-0 transitions, so at most `N/2^(u+1)` transitions enter this parity set. Adding an affine constant swaps the two transition orientations and does not change that count.

For an invertible matrix B, the complementary inverse cross-block identity gives `rank B^-1[S,S^c]=rank B[S,S^c]`. Taking `B=I-D_w P_j`, the cross-block is a weighted partial matching. Its rank is at most the count of incoming cycle edges, hence at most `N/2^(u+1)`. Endpoint operations preserving S cannot increase this budget. Cross-rank is subadditive under sums and under square products, and an S-preserving bank contributes zero. Thus the full expression has cross-rank at most `M*N/2^(u+1)`.

For the target T, `T*T=N I` and every entry has magnitude one. The N/2-by-N/2 cross-block C has squared Frobenius norm `N^2/4` and squared operator norm at most N. The singular-value bound `||C||_F^2 <= rank(C)||C||_op^2` yields `rank C>=N/4`. Comparing the two bounds gives exactly `M>=2^(u-1)`.

The analytic singular-value argument is a statement over complex numbers. For the Fourier matrix, the resulting nonzero algebraic minors retain their nonvanishing over characteristic-zero scalar extensions; this is the reason the Fourier statement extends. No notion of a flat unitary matrix over a completely unspecified abstract field is required.

## Exact crossing formula and small controls

For a nonzero mask g, a non-wrapping counter transition that flips h low bits occurs `2^(k-h)` times for `h=1,...,k`. The wrap flips all k bits once more. It follows that

`e(g) = [sum_(h=1)^k 2^(k-h) parity(g & (2^h-1)) + parity(g & (2^k-1))]/2`.

The extra final parity term is essential. Omitting wrap would make the h=k contribution incorrect. The independently authored checker verifies this formula for every nonzero mask at every k from 1 through 10. It also constructs invertible affine maps by elementary binary row operations, independently solves the simultaneous parity constraints, checks block compatibility and balance, computes actual conjugated-counter crossings, and distinguishes the allowed-order count d from the total-occurrence bound M. These are controls for the proof, not a numerical proof of the asymptotic statement.

## Optional sharper allocation with the natural order

Different low-bit budgets u_j are permitted whenever `r+sum_j u_j<=k-1`. If the natural order is included, its pulled-back parity already has r low zeros, imposing no additional constraints. For d>1 one may spend `u=floor((k-r-1)/(d-1))` on each other order. A layer with one term on each order then has budget

`N/2^(r+1) + (d-1)N/2^(u+1)`.

For d=2 this is `q/2+m`, where `q=N/m`, for both exponent parities. Comparing with the generic flat-unitary lower bound yields `L(q/2+m)>=N/4`. The separate natural/bit-reversal striped-cut theorem uses a special full-rank Fourier rectangle and can replace N/4 by N/2 there. These statements have different witnesses and are consistent.

## Relation to the expander construction

For fixed d the common parity cut has total incoming count at most `d*N/2^(u+1)=o(N)`. The MGG-derived eight-cycle bank instead has a positive constant times N incoming edges across every balanced cut for all sufficiently large N. Thus its eight orders cannot all be binary-affine conjugates of the binary counter at arbitrarily large dyadic sizes. This is an asymptotic assertion, not an assertion about every individual order at each small size.

Neither the affine exclusion nor the MGG capacity escape establishes Fourier synthesis or an unrestricted arithmetic-circuit lower bound. Both concern explicitly specified representations and common-subset cut accounting.
