# Deterministic discovery of a sparse recurrence defect

Implementation: `detected_fourier.py`. Focused audit: `audit_detector.py` and
`detector_results.json`. Root source review and the independent adverse-case
and operation-count review are complete, with no unresolved issue within the
stated contract. This note supplies the exact acceptance theorem and accounting.

## Contract and theorem

Supply a primitive Nth root q in an exact field, dyadic N>=4, all N coordinates
of x, and a nonnegative integer support cap s. The operator E is the upper
bidiagonal operator in the package README, and F is the complete unnormalized
positive-sign DFT. The evaluator uses no nonzero Fourier anchor and does not
require a supplied shift.

It accepts exactly the following union of input families:

1. x has at most 2s nonzero coordinates.
2. There exists a scalar lambda with lambda!=0 and lambda^N!=1 for which
   `(E-lambda I)x` has at most s nonzero coordinates.

On acceptance it returns every coordinate of F x exactly in O(N(s+1)) field
and word work and O(N) live field-element storage. It can decline outside
this union after O(N) field and word work. Thus it does not accelerate every
fresh dense input. Primitive-root construction, exact field-element bit sizes,
and floating-point conditioning are outside this arithmetic model.

## Discovery proof

First scan x, recording its m nonzero coordinates. If m<=2s, direct sparse
Fourier evaluation returns all N outputs in O(Nm+ m log N + N) work. Required
phase roots are constructed by charged repeated squaring, so the sparse branch
does not need an uncharged table or transform. This includes the zero vector.

Otherwise m>2s. Compute a=E x, including a full root-power table, in linear
work. At each index with x_i!=0 consider the ratio a_i/x_i. If an admissible
lambda has at most s defects, at least m-s>m/2 of these ratios equal lambda.
Thus that lambda is the unique strict majority of the ratio multiset.

Boyer-Moore cancellation finds this candidate deterministically. Removing
two unequal labels preserves any strict majority. Repeated cancellation
therefore leaves that majority as the surviving candidate, if it exists.
The implementation compares ratios a_i/x_i and A/B as `a_i*B == A*x_i`.
All denominators are nonzero; this equality is exact in a field. Each such
comparison costs two field multiplications. No ratio hashing, ordering, or
one-division-per-coordinate assumption is used. At the end, one paid inverse
and one multiplication form the surviving candidate lambda.

A zero candidate or a candidate satisfying lambda^N=1 is declined. If an
admissible shift with <=s defects existed, it would be the unique majority
and hence the candidate, so these declines cannot discard an input in the
promised recurrence family. A zero final cancellation balance likewise
excludes a strict majority. Lambda=0 is excluded by the current target-solver
representation, even though the mathematical source operator E is invertible.

A surviving candidate is only a proposal: the code computes **every** residual
coordinate `a_i-lambda*x_i`, including positions where x_i=0, and verifies
support <=s. A majority among nonzero coordinates alone would be insufficient.
The audit includes a dense one-defect vector whose first input coordinate is
replaced by zero: its nonzero-coordinate ratios still have the same majority,
but the added residual at the zero position causes the required decline.

After acceptance, FE=JF implies

    F x = (J-lambda I)^-1 F((E-lambda I)x).

The residual Fourier vector is evaluated by s phase recurrences, reusing the
already paid root powers. The existing cyclic solver then returns all N
coordinates. No anchor transform or ordinary FFT is called. Conversely,
acceptance through this branch verifies an admissible shift and the full
residual support, so it is sound and completes the stated acceptance theorem.

## Explicit costs and arithmetic model

For the recurrence branch, let L=log_2 N, R be the number of ratio comparisons,
and d<=s be the verified residual support. Accepted operation totals are:

| Stage | Scalar field operations |
|---|---:|
| Root powers and E action | 6N-4 |
| Ratio cancellation | 2R |
| Candidate division and lambda^N test | L+3 |
| Complete residual formation | 2N |
| Cold rational solver preparation | 21N+7 |
| Complete sparse-residual Fourier vector | (2N-1)d |
| Target cyclic solve | 9N |

Thus the total is `38N+2R+(2N-1)d+L+6`. The existing solver deliberately
rebuilds some root/source data; this duplicate preparation is charged rather
than optimized away. For the dense one-defect audit inputs, R=N-1, yielding
exactly `42N+L+3`. The returned metadata retains every stage ledger.

The sparse-input branch costs `(2N-1)m+P`, where P is the explicitly counted
repeated-squaring work for its phase roots, plus O(N+m) word work. A decline
retains the costs of all detection stages already executed; it never reports
unsuccessful discovery as free. Input/support comparisons, cancellation index
work, copying, allocation, and all N output writes obey the stated linear
bounds but are not counted as field gates or exhaustive CPU instructions.

The inherited counter key `constant_inverse` is a historical label. Here the
candidate inverse divides by a query-dependent x_i, and solver preparation
depends on the discovered lambda. **All are paid field inversions in an
adaptive rational arithmetic model.** This detector is not an input-independent
linear circuit, and the label must not be interpreted as providing constants
or divisions for free. Exact equality comparisons are also part of the
adaptive algorithm and have their recorded linear word-work bound.

## Focused verification and limitations

The audit passes 51 accepted exact complete outputs, 40 decline controls and
four invalid-argument controls across ten small prime/cyclotomic configurations
and four larger prime-field examples. Small outputs are compared with an
independent dense DFT matrix. Declined fixtures independently enumerate every
potential ratio to verify absence of an admissible low-defect shift. Every
stage ledger sums to the returned total, and the linear-stage formulas above
are checked directly. The larger outputs match an ordinary radix-two DFT.

These sampled cold evaluations do **not** beat the ordinary comparator's
gate count: at N=8192 the detector costs 344,080 versus 167,949 including the
ordinary transform's setup. The useful result is a deterministic linear-work
method for a precisely verified structured family, with favorable-shift
discovery included; constants and the exact same-target comparisons are
reported honestly. No practical speedup, novelty claim for majority voting,
universal exact FFT result, or extension to arbitrary independent inputs is
inferred.
