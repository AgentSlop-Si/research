# HERO #17 — Phase 9: Root-Exchange Union and an invariant reflection defect

**Research date:** October 10, 2026  
**Result:** An exact low-rank correction and Cauchy inverse for *one* nonstandard polynomial-CRT split; a basis-invariant commutator-rank formula. Fully charged prototype and independent finite-field audits. **No universal exact O(N) FFT and no new integer-multiplication upper bound.**

## Why this branch is worth testing

Phase 8 showed that splitting a Fourier root set into nonperiodic halves generally creates very dense polynomial-remainder transfer matrices, and that the classical even/odd split uniquely minimizes the **literal nonzero matrix entries** of the two transfers. But density is not a direct arithmetic-circuit lower bound: the same dense matrix can be the sum of a diagonal matrix and a rank-one update.

Phase 9 develops this missing case rigorously. Instead of choosing arbitrary mixed root sets, exchange t roots across the two classical parity classes, then search for shared algebraic corrections and a cheap inverse. This reuses our own graph/union structure without relying on repeated input vectors or cached spectra from other requests.

## Main result A: dense transfer, low-rank correction

For N=2m with t exchanged roots between even and odd Fourier-root groups, both modified remainder maps are exact generalized reflections:

\[
R_A^2=R_B^2=I, \qquad
\operatorname{rank}(R_A-I)=\operatorname{rank}(R_B+I)=t.
\]

The joint correction has rank `min(m,2t)`. This rank is achieved by explicitly assembled Lagrange updates. Thus one **pair of N/2-sized remainders** can be computed from arbitrary fresh coefficients in `O(mt+m)` arithmetic, after a fully charged `O(N+mt+t³)` preparation and `O(t²)` field inversions.

At N=16 and t=1 over F17, `R_A` and `R_B` each have **64/64 nonzero entries**. Nevertheless each differs from ±I by rank one. Literal matrix sparsity badly overestimates the cost of applying these transfer maps.

## Main result B: the gauge-invariant commutator theorem

The stronger result is

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}(R_A R_B-R_B R_A)
=\operatorname{rank}(R_A R_B+I)
=2\min(t,m-t).}
\]

The same rank equals `rank(R_A+R_B)`; meanwhile the Phase 8 theorem says `rank(R_A-R_B)=m` for every balanced partition. This exactly distinguishes a root-exchanged pair from a commuting parity pair.

Unlike the transition-mask rank from Phase 5, the commutator rank survives a **common invertible conjugation of the coefficient basis**. That makes it a mathematically robust obstruction or diagnostic in this specific paired-remainder grammar.

In a quantum-inspired interpretation, `R_A` and `R_B` are involutions/generalized reflections; the rank above measures their noncommutativity. This does not amount to a quantum FFT or to an arbitrary-circuit lower bound. See `PROOF_CERTIFICATE.md` for a field-independent proof.

## Main result C: exact backward recovery via two small Cauchy systems

An independent inversion of a dense m×m remainder bridge would require high cost. Here the 2t correction evaluations can be grouped through the two Cauchy matrices

\[
C_{ij}=\ell_{B,e_j}(o_i),\qquad D_{ji}=\ell_{A,o_i}(e_j).
\]

Both are nonsingular; the exact inverse uses **two t×t systems** (the inverses are constructed once with a charged plan). Applying the prepared inverse takes `O(mt+t²)` arithmetic.

Thus both forward and inverse *local transfers* are linear for fixed t, with no hidden FFT. The algorithm is not limited to structured input values—the root partition is structured, while the degree-N input polynomial is arbitrary.

## Implementation and independent audit

The self-contained `phase9.py` implements root preparation, synthetic division, cardinal polynomial generation, exact Cauchy inverse construction, a forward pair, inverse pair, an adaptive smaller-swap orientation, a one-swap closed-form decoder, and an intentionally fully charged end-to-end transform comparison.

Tests are over exact finite prime fields with a primitive Nth root. The field fixture search is *test setup*, not a claimed free universal algorithm for choosing arithmetic domains. For an explicitly supplied primitive root, the executor charges every root used. There are no third-party numerical dependencies.

### Confirmed checks

- **15/15 Phase 9 test groups pass.** Previous Phase 8 **13/13** also rerun.
- Independent audit: **60** complete fresh-input remainder pairs; **1,744** child-root polynomial evaluations independently compared to the original full polynomial.
- **101** separate correction-rank certificate checks and **34** independent involution/commutator tests using a second Gaussian-elimination implementation.
- **12,870** balanced partitions of N=16 counted combinatorially. Histogram by the number t of exchanged parity roots: `[1,64,784,3136,4900,3136,784,64,1]`.
- **15** smaller complete hybrid DFTs checked, plus one length-**65,536** complete DFT compared bin-by-bin against a separate radix-2 implementation. **38** of the large output bins were also compared against a direct length-N polynomial evaluation.

### Fully charged end-to-end comparison

At N=65,536 with one root exchange, the **complete** nonstandard root-partition evaluator counted **2,981,902** additions/multiplications including preparation. The classical radix-2 implementation counted **1,638,399** under the same basic scalar ledger. They produced identical full spectra, and the selected independent O(N)-per-bin direct checks passed. Thus the nonstandard evaluator is **slower**, though still Θ(N log N).

At N=16 and the exact same noncoset root split A=`[0,1,2,3,4,8,10,12]`, the new hybrid used **801** charged scalar operations, versus **883** for the saved Phase 8 generic recursive-division executor. Ordinary radix-2 used **111**. These are implementation-specific arithmetic counts, not benchmarks of optimized codelets and not an asymptotic improvement.

## Why this cannot yet beat FFT

We have accelerated the **first branching bridge**. But the full transform still needs the two child evaluation maps. Our hybrid supplies them with standard length-m FFTs, so the work remains Θ(N log N). For t growing with N, plan cost contains an additional O(t³) term; random partitions usually have t around m/2 and offer no sparse-root-exchange advantage.

The commutator invariant is a robust mathematical diagnostic, not a proof that every possible multistage circuit must pay that rank at every layer. Basis changes applied differently to different stages, sharing across nodes, unconventional nonunitary scalar gates, and the specialized #130 exact arithmetic network remain outside this lower-bound grammar.

## Recommended next target (Phase 10)

Stop optimizing isolated polynomial root partitions: their complete evaluations are just the same Fourier map with different middle coordinate systems. The next useful conjecture must give a **joint multilevel evaluator** that cancels work *across* sibling CRT interfaces, and whose end-to-end arithmetic count—not intermediate transfer rank—is smaller than O(N log N) on every fresh input. A valid candidate should be represented as explicit scalar linear forms over original input symbols, prove the end-to-end identity on all basis inputs, and include a charge ledger for preparation, coefficient conversion, intermediate computations, and output writes.

To avoid previously disproved paths: do not treat exact-value collisions, a fixed t root exchange, free conjugation, large uncharged lookup tables, a precomputed spectrum, or fewer named stages as an asymptotic speedup.

## Publication and historical-priority boundary

Lagrange interpolation, barycentric node updates and inverse formulas for Cauchy matrices have extensive prior art. For example, the classical Cauchy determinant and Lagrange-inverse discussion is documented by Thomas Alberts (University of Utah), and fast polynomial interpolation via structured matrices was studied long before this project. The particular packaged commutator-rank identity was proved here for this project and checked over finite fields, but **priority over existing literature has not been established**.

For a current comparison, OpenAI Math Result #130 documents a *uniform exact complex-arithmetic* bound O(N (log N)^(1-1e-13)), with nontrivial model assumptions. This checkpoint does not compete with that universal bound.
