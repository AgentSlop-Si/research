# Fourier–Segre Gauge Compatibility and Commutator Certificates

**Research checkpoint: 10 October 2026.**  
**Status:** Exact fixed-stage theorems, independently rerun modular certificates for selected actual 4-/6-Green wires, and a derived nonzero numerical-error bound. **No all-length exact sub-FFT algorithm and no publication-priority assertion.**

## 0. The unambiguous computational target

Fix a field \(K\), a primitive \(N\)-th root \(\omega\in K\), and the *unnormalized* Fourier matrix
\(F_N=(\omega^{ij})_{0\le i,j<N}\). Let \(A,B,C\in K^{N\times N}\) be **fixed** invertible stages. Consider all choices of *invertible diagonal* matrices \(L,D,E\) in

\[
F_N=L A D B E C.\tag{1}
\]

- **Four-Green application:** \(A=G_1+G_2\), \(B=G_3+G_4\), and \(C=I\). Thus (1) permits *free output, middle, and input diagonals*, strictly more freedom than the previous fixed-input-gauge phase-pencil test.
- **Six-Green application:** \(A=G_1+G_2\), \(B=G_3+G_4\), \(C=G_5+G_6\). Both inner diagonals and the output diagonal remain free.
- Each \(G_i=(I-D_{w_i}P_{\sigma_i})^{-1}\), possibly with independently fixed left/right monomial gauges. Applying a supplied Green operator takes \(O(N)\) arithmetic once its parameters are supplied, but the **certificate** below is not an \(O(N)\) method for discovering those parameters.

The theorems below are about fixed stages and specified fields; they do **not** quantify over every possible choice of Green permutations and weights.

## 1. Exact Hadamard–Segre lifting theorem

**Assume every entry of the fixed middle stage \(B\) is nonzero.** Put

\[
H=F_N C^{-1},\qquad Z(c)_{uv}=
\frac{(A^{-1}\operatorname{diag}(c)H)_{uv}}{B_{uv}}
\quad(c\in K^N).\tag{2}
\]

**Theorem A.** The Fourier factorization (1) exists **if and only if** there is a vector \(c\in(K^*)^N\) such that \(Z(c)\) has rank one and **all entries of \(Z(c)\) are nonzero**.

**Proof.** If (1) holds, let \(c_i=L_{ii}^{-1}\), \(d_u=D_{uu}\), and \(e_v=E_{vv}\). Multiplying (1) by \(A^{-1}\operatorname{diag}(c)\) on the left and \(C^{-1}\) on the right yields

\[
A^{-1}\operatorname{diag}(c)F_N C^{-1}=DBE.
\]

Entrywise division by \(B\) therefore gives \(Z(c)_{uv}=d_u e_v\): a rank-one matrix with no zero entries. Conversely any such rank-one matrix can be factored \(Z(c)=de^T\) with \(d,e\) entirely nonzero. Set \(L=\operatorname{diag}(c^{-1})\), \(D=\operatorname{diag}(d)\), \(E=\operatorname{diag}(e)\). Reversing the displayed identity proves (1). \(\square\)

**Important:** rank one by itself is insufficient if some entry of \(c\) or \(Z(c)\) vanishes. For sparse \(B\), the divisions in (2) are invalid; the corresponding generalization requires zero-pattern constraints and a *masked* rank-one completion problem, which is not settled here.

Geometrically, (2) asks whether the \(N\)-dimensional linear family \(\{Z(c):c\in K^N\}\) intersects the open, nonzero-coordinate part of the **Segre rank-one variety**. The Segre interpretation is an application of established algebraic geometry, not invented terminology for a new FFT.

## 2. Common-eigenvector elimination

Let \(b_j\) be column \(j\) of \(B\), and \(h_j\) column \(j\) of \(H=F_NC^{-1}\). Define the explicit linear maps

\[
P_j=\operatorname{diag}(b_j^{-1}) A^{-1}\operatorname{diag}(h_j),
\qquad Z(c)[:,j]=P_jc.\tag{3}
\]

Suppose \(P_0\) is invertible (equivalently, \(A\) is invertible and every coordinate of \(b_0,h_0\) is nonzero). Set

\[
T_j=P_0^{-1}P_j.\tag{4}
\]

**Theorem B.** (1) holds **if and only if** there is a common eigenvector \(c\) of \(T_1,\dots,T_{N-1}\), with

- all \(c_i\ne0\);
- all \((P_0c)_i\ne0\);
- all eigenvalues \(\lambda_j\ne0\).

**Proof.** By Theorem A, a valid factorization gives \(P_jc=\lambda_jP_0c\), where \(\lambda_j=e_j/e_0\), hence \(T_jc=\lambda_jc\). Conversely the joint eigenvector and nonzero conditions ensure the columns of \(Z(c)\) are \(P_0c,\lambda_1P_0c,\ldots,\lambda_{N-1}P_0c\); this is an everywhere-nonzero rank-one matrix. Apply Theorem A. \(\square\)

For **two stages with free input gauge** (\(C=I\)), \(h_0={\bf1}\), \(h_j=(\omega^{ij})_i\), and

\[
\boxed{T_j=A\operatorname{diag}(b_0/b_j)A^{-1}\Delta^j},
\qquad \Delta=\operatorname{diag}(1,\omega,\ldots,\omega^{N-1}).\tag{5}
\]

This gives a direct formula using the first three columns of the two fixed Green-sum stages. It does not silently fix the right input diagonal.

## 3. A one-determinant necessary condition

For \(N\ge3\), form the commutator

\[
\mathcal C=[T_1,T_2]=T_1T_2-T_2T_1.
\]

**Theorem C (exact gauge-independent obstruction).** If

\[
\boxed{\det\mathcal C\ne0},\tag{6}
\]

then (1) is impossible for **every** choice of the unknown invertible diagonal matrices \(L,D,E\).

**Proof.** A common eigenvector of \(T_1,T_2\) lies in \(\ker[T_1,T_2]\). An invertible commutator has zero kernel. Apply Theorem B. \(\square\)

**Not sufficient in reverse.** If the commutator is singular, a common eigenvector may not exist. Even when a common eigenvector exists, it may have forbidden zero entries. A rigorous saved counterexample takes \(A=C=I\), \(B=I+{\bf1}{\bf1}^T\) at \(N=4\). All \(T_j\) are diagonal and commute, so the commutator is zero; nevertheless \(Z(c)=\operatorname{diag}(c)(F_N\oslash B)\) has rank at least two for every all-nonzero \(c\). Both statements are checked over \(\mathbb F_{97}\) by `verify.py`.

Common-eigenvector and commutator methods are classical; in particular **Shemesh (1984)** already supplies stronger algebraic common-eigenvector criteria. The novelty boundary here is the project-specific Fourier/Green Hadamard lifting and its explicit all-three-gauges application, not the existence of a commutator test in linear algebra.

## 4. Positive operator-norm separation, not just impossibility

Work over \(\mathbb C\), with fixed \(A,B,C\) satisfying the above assumptions and invertible \(\mathcal C\). Define positive constants

\[
\begin{gathered}
 s=\sigma_{\min}(\mathcal C),\quad m=\sigma_{\min}(P_0),\quad
 a=\|T_1\|_2,\quad b=\|T_2\|_2,\\
 M_j=\|P_j\|_2\quad(j=1,2),\\
 \delta=\min\left\{\frac m2,\;
 \frac{s m}{4\sqrt{a^2+b^2}\sqrt{(1+M_1/m)^2+(1+M_2/m)^2}}\right\}>0.
\end{gathered}\tag{7}
\]

**Theorem D (uniform all-gauge spectral gap).** For every three invertible diagonal matrices \(L,D,E\),

\[
\boxed{
 \|LADBEC-F_N\|_2
 \ge
 \frac{\delta\,\min_{uv}|B_{uv}|}
 {\sqrt N\,\|A^{-1}\|_2\,\|C^{-1}\|_2}>0.
}\tag{8}
\]

Here \(F_N\) is unnormalized. The same derivation works for a unitary-normalized target if all \(P_j\) and the resulting constants are computed with that target.

**Proof.** We first show that for each \(\|c\|_2=1\), \(Z(c)\) is at least \(\delta\) away in Frobenius norm from *every* rank-one matrix. Write \(z_j=P_jc\), and \(\rho_j=\min_{\lambda\in\mathbb C}\|T_jc-\lambda c\|_2\). Let \(\lambda_j=c^*T_jc\) be the minimizer. Since \(|\lambda_j|\le\|T_j\|_2\), expanding the commutator in terms of \(T_jc=\lambda_jc+e_j\) yields

\[
 s\le\|\mathcal Cc\|_2
 \le 2\sqrt{a^2+b^2}\sqrt{\rho_1^2+\rho_2^2}.\tag{9}
\]

Let \(R\) be any rank-one matrix and \(\varepsilon=\|Z(c)-R\|_F\). If \(\varepsilon\ge m/2\), the desired lower bound holds. Otherwise, \(\|R[:,0]\|\ge\|z_0\|-\varepsilon\ge m/2\), and columns of \(R\) satisfy \(R[:,j]=\alpha_j R[:,0]\), with \(|\alpha_j|\le 1+2M_j/m\) for \(j=1,2\). Thus

\[
\|z_j-\alpha_jz_0\|_2\le 2\varepsilon(1+M_j/m),\qquad
\rho_j\le(2\varepsilon/m)(1+M_j/m).\tag{10}
\]

Substituting (10) into (9) implies \(\varepsilon\ge\delta\). This holds for every rank-one \(R\) and every normalized \(c\).

Now let \(M=LADBEC\), and exploit the exact overall scaling gauge \(L\mapsto tL\), \(D\mapsto D/t\) to arrange \(\|c\|_2=1\) for \(c_i=L_{ii}^{-1}\). Then

\[
Z(c)-de^T=
\bigl(A^{-1}\operatorname{diag}(c)(F_N-M)C^{-1}\bigr)\oslash B,
\]

where \(d,e\) are diagonal entries of the appropriately rescaled \(D,E\). Since \(\|\operatorname{diag}(c)\|_2\le1\),

\[
\delta\le\|Z(c)-de^T\|_F
\le \frac{\|A^{-1}\|_2\|C^{-1}\|_2}{\min_{uv}|B_{uv}|}\|F_N-M\|_F
\le \frac{\sqrt N\|A^{-1}\|_2\|C^{-1}\|_2}{\min_{uv}|B_{uv}|}\|F_N-M\|_2.
\]

Rearrange to obtain (8). \(\square\)

The value in (8) may be extremely small when the fixed stages are ill conditioned. Reported decimals in `NUMERICAL_RESULTS.json` are **floating-point diagnostics**, not rigorous rounded lower-bound certificates. The positivity follows from the exact nonvanishing hypotheses.

## 5. Exact witnesses and a valid complex lift

The supplied `verify.py` works over \(\mathbb F_{97}\), which contains primitive \(N\)-th roots for \(N=3,4,6,8\). Each fixed stage is the sum of **two actual weighted-cycle Green inverses**, with separately chosen row/column permutations and nonzero diagonal gauges. It accepts a witness only when all required stage inverses and the base matrix \(P_0\) exist, and the calculated commutator determinant is nonzero.

Saved selected witness determinants (modulo 97):

| N | Four-Green \((A,B,C=I)\) | Six-Green \((A,B,C)\) |
|---|---:|---:|
| 3 | 8 | 38 |
| 4 | 3 | 34 |
| 6 | 71 | 55 |
| 8 | 90 | 70 |

The script reports 8 independently sampled nonzero witnesses per architecture/length combination: **64 witnesses total**; no success is counted for a singular stage or singular \(P_0\). Six separately constructed positive exact-factorization controls pass, and the singular-commutator-but-infeasible control passes.

`CERTIFICATES.json` records the *signed integer* cycle weights, monomial gauges, permutations, aggregate stage matrices, primitive root, and determinant for one selected witness of each type. `independent_verify.py` uses SymPy's independent modular inversion and reconstruction from these original parameters; all eight saved witnesses pass.

**Soundness of a modular rejection over \(\mathbb C\).** For the recorded *integer/rational* Green parameters, the Fourier-root-dependent commutator determinant is a rational function in a primitive \(N\)-th root of unity with integer coefficients and nonvanishing denominators. A nonzero value under a valid map \(\mathbb Z[\zeta_N]\to\mathbb F_{97}\), sending \(\zeta_N\) to the recorded primitive root, proves the cyclotomic number is **not zero**. Hence it is nonzero in the complex realization for those fixed parameters. This is a sound nonzero certificate, **not** proof that every parameter choice or cycle wiring fails.

For a selected fixed wiring, the same nonzero integer specialization establishes that the rational commutator determinant is not identically zero as a function of complex branch weights: on its admissible parameter space, generic continuous coefficients therefore fail. Exceptional algebraic coefficient choices remain unexcluded.
