# Annihilator-Closed Fourier Feedback (ACFF)

**Date:** 10 October 2026  
**Research status:** A proposed synthesis architecture, proved algebraic correctness and execution theorems, architecture-specific obstruction theorems, and exact executable audits. **No uniform sub-`N log N` Fourier factor family is constructed in this document.** In particular, the cheap candidate maps have not simultaneously satisfied the Fourier certificate and a sublogarithmic annihilator degree for growing lengths. External publication novelty has not been established.

## Abstract

The previous project proposal, Recursive Fourier-Mass Contraction, placed its unproved step in a replacement for a mixed-radix Fourier bank. This proposal moves the unproved step to a different object: the loop operator in a full-rank feedback network. Two linear-time maps `A_N,B_N` define `u=x+A_N y`, `y=B_N u`. The output is `(I-B_N A_N)^(-1)B_N x`. If the loop `K_N=B_N A_N` has a uniformly available degree-`d_N` annihilating polynomial `mu_N`, with `mu_N(1) != 0`, its inverse action is exactly a degree-`d_N-1` polynomial, evaluated by `2d_N-1` map applications. There is no infinite iteration, no assumption of convergence, and no recursive Fourier call.

A cubic matrix identity and one seed identity certify that this network equals the full Fourier transform without inserting a dense Fourier matrix into the certificate. Consequently a uniform family of `O(N)`-work maps satisfying that certificate with `d_N=O(log log N)` would give an exact `O(N log log N)` transform; bounded `d_N` would give `O(N)`. The candidate maps are sums of two independently ordered weighted Green scans each. We prove that using only one Green scan per map is impossible for `N>8`, and that the proposed paired-Green parameter space generically has loop minimal-polynomial degree `N`. Thus short closure must be an exceptional Fourier-compatible algebraic construction, not an effect of random mixing or graph density.

The polynomial inverse and feedback identities use established linear algebra. The project-specific proposal is their joint use with a Fourier phase certificate, noncommuting paired-Green maps, a fully charged evaluator, and explicit rejection gates. The universal construction is a conjecture, not a theorem of this package.

## 2. Model and Fourier conventions

Let `E` be a field whose characteristic does not divide `N`, containing a specified primitive `N`-th root `omega`. The main intended arithmetic model is exact complex scalar arithmetic. Define

\[
F_N[k,j]=\omega^{kj},\qquad S e_j=e_{j+1\bmod N},\qquad
D=\operatorname{diag}(1,\omega,\ldots,\omega^{N-1}).
\]

Then

\[
F_NS=DF_N,\qquad F_Ne_0=\mathbf1.
\]

All maps in the candidate family are input-independent. Every arithmetic operation, coefficient-generation step, root-power stream, index operation, read, and output write must be charged in the declared model. The root is supplied; the `N` successive powers can be generated in `O(N)` multiplications. An arbitrary exact field element is one scalar in this arithmetic model, not one bit. The uniform generator uses the declared field operations, the supplied root, the integer length, and fixed program constants. It cannot assume an N-dependent advice table, precomputed Fourier rows, or an uncharged algebraic equation solver. Any additional supplied algebraic constants would have to be declared as an additional model assumption; none are asserted available by the conjecture here.

No bounded-coefficient, floating-point stability, bit-complexity, quantum-computation, or integer-multiplication improvement follows automatically. The finite fields in the verifier are test fields, not a fixed-field asymptotic claim for all lengths.

## 3. The feedback architecture

For `A,B` in `E^(N x N)`, use the equations

\[
u=x+Ay,\qquad y=Bu.
\]

Set

\[
K=BA,\quad H=I-BA,\quad J=I-AB.
\]

If `H` is invertible, the unique output is

\[
T x=H^{-1}Bx.
\]

The standard push-through identity gives

\[
T=H^{-1}B=BJ^{-1},\qquad J^{-1}=I+AH^{-1}B.
\]

The inverse `B^(-1)` is **not** assumed or computed. In particular, certification must not discard potentially singular `B` branches prematurely. Fourier correctness will imply invertibility of `B` as a consequence.

These equations are a specification. Merely writing a feedback loop is not an algorithm for solving it cheaply. Section 5 supplies the finite evaluator under a separate, explicit hypothesis.

### Rank-preservation gate

Because `H^(-1)` is invertible,

\[
\operatorname{rank}T=\operatorname{rank}B.
\]

Therefore exact Fourier output requires `rank B=N`. No feedback map `A` can restore information destroyed by a rank-deficient input map `B`.

A single rank-one-per-block Fourier sketch cannot be made into an exact full transform by appending this feedback. Keeping all `N` input coordinates available through a full-rank `B` is essential.

## 4. A cubic Fourier certificate

### Theorem 1: phase certificate

Suppose `H=I-BA` is invertible. Then `T=H^(-1)B` equals `F_N` if and only if both identities hold:

\[
\boxed{(I-BA)DB=BS(I-AB)}\tag{C1}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{(I-BA)\mathbf1=Be_0.}\tag{C2}
\]

`C1` is cubic in the unknown matrix entries of `A,B`. `C2` is a vector identity of degree at most two. The identities contain the shift and modulation operators, not the dense Fourier matrix. After substituting Green resolvents, these are rational identities in the scan parameters; clearing denominators need not leave degree three in those underlying weights. The cubic description is a matrix-variable certificate, not a promise of low-cost parameter solving.

#### Proof

Using `HT=B` and `TJ=B`,

\[
HDB-BSJ=H(DT-TS)J.
\]

Both `H` and `J` are invertible. Thus `C1` is equivalent to

\[
DT=TS.
\]

Likewise `C2` is equivalent to `Te_0=1`. For every `0<=j<N`,

\[
Te_j=TS^je_0=D^jTe_0=D^j\mathbf1.
\]

Its kth entry is `omega^(kj)`, exactly the jth Fourier column. Conversely `F_NS=DF_N` and the seed identity imply `C1,C2` by the displayed factorization. This proof does not assume `A` or `B` invertible. QED.

### Why the seed is indispensable

Every scalar multiple `c F_N` satisfies the phase relation `DT=TS`. More generally, arbitrary diagonal row scalings of `F_N` satisfy it. The seed fixes those row scalings. The verifier checks that `2F_N` passes `C1` and fails `C2`.

Even `B=0` makes `C1` vanish. It fails the seed. This is a deliberately included singular negative control, not a case to remove by an unproved determinant assumption.

### Certificate complexity is separate from execution complexity

A direct dense finite-instance check can cost cubic work. It is a research oracle, not part of the claimed fast transform. A uniform construction must prove `C1,C2` symbolically from its parameter formulas. If it instead discovers coefficients by expensive optimization or dense elimination for each new `N`, that discovery cost has to be included and can destroy the advertised bound.

## 5. Exact termination by an annihilator

### Theorem 2: finite polynomial closure

Let

\[
\mu(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{d}\mu_jz^j,\qquad \mu_d\ne0,\qquad d\ge1,
\]

satisfy

\[
\mu(K)=0,\qquad \mu(1)\ne0.
\]

Define

\[
p(z)=\frac{\mu(1)-\mu(z)}{(1-z)\mu(1)}.
\]

The apparent quotient is a polynomial of degree at most `d-1`, and

\[
\boxed{(I-K)^{-1}=p(K).}\tag{5.1}
\]

#### Proof

The numerator vanishes at `z=1`, so division by `1-z` is exact. Moreover

\[
(1-z)p(z)=1-\frac{\mu(z)}{\mu(1)}.
\]

Substituting `K` gives `(I-K)p(K)=I`; the two factors commute since they are polynomials in the same matrix. Hence `p(K)` is the inverse. QED.

The polynomial need not be minimal. Any valid annihilator meeting the nonresonance condition suffices. A chosen annihilator with `mu(1)=0` does not certify this inverse; that alone does not prove that `I-K` is singular, because a nonminimal annihilator might have an unnecessary factor `z-1`.

### Coefficient construction

Write `p(z)=sum_(r=0)^(d-1) q_r z^r`. Then

\[
\boxed{q_r=\frac{\sum_{j=r+1}^d\mu_j}{\mu(1)}.}\tag{5.2}
\]

All coefficients are obtained using suffix sums, one inversion, and `O(d)` scalar arithmetic. There is no polynomial factorization or root-finding step here. Obtaining the valid `mu` in the first place is a separate synthesis obligation.

### No analytic convergence assumption

The spectrum of `K` need not have modulus below one. The formula is an exact polynomial identity, not a truncated Neumann approximation. For example, `K=2I` has annihilator `z-2`, and `(I-K)^(-1)=-I` is computed in constant polynomial degree despite the divergent geometric series `sum K^j`.

## 6. The conditional sub-`N log N` execution theorem

### Theorem 3: charged algorithm

For every length in a declared family, suppose a deterministic uniform generator provides maps `A_N,B_N` and a polynomial `mu_N` of degree `d_N>=1` such that:

1. `A_N` and `B_N` each apply in at most `cN` operations and use `O(N)` memory, for a constant `c` independent of `N` and the input.
2. The complete generator, root preparation, numeric parameters, index arrays, polynomial coefficients, and scan setup cost `O(N d_N)` work and use `O(N+d_N)` memory.
3. `mu_N(B_N A_N)=0` and `mu_N(1)!=0` are proved exactly for the generated family.
4. The generated maps satisfy `C1,C2` exactly.

Then the full fresh-input Fourier transform has an exact algorithm using

\[
\boxed{O(Nd_N)\text{ work and }O(N+d_N)\text{ memory}.}\tag{6.1}
\]

In particular:

| Annihilator degree | Conditional total work |
|---|---|
| `d_N=O(1)` | `O(N)` |
| `d_N=O(log log N)` | `O(N log log N)` |
| `d_N=O((log N)^alpha)`, fixed `0<alpha<1` | `O(N (log N)^alpha)` |
| Any `d_N=o(log N)` | `o(N log N)` |
| `d_N=Theta(log N)` | `O(N log N)`; no exponent improvement from this bound |

#### Algorithm and proof

Construct `q` from (5.2). Evaluate:

```
u = B(x)
v = q[d-1] * u
for r = d-2, ..., 0:
    v = B(A(v))
    v = v + q[r] * u
return v
```

This is Horner evaluation of `p(BA)B x`. There are `d-1` applications of `A`, `d` applications of `B`, and `O(Nd)` vector operations. A fixed number of `N`-vectors is sufficient. Theorem 2 gives `p(BA)B=H^(-1)B`, and Theorem 1 gives `H^(-1)B=F_N`. Adding preparation proves the stated cost. QED.

This proves an implication, not the existence of its hypotheses. The same short polynomial must work for **all** input vectors at that length. An annihilator fitting one vector or one scalar output sequence is insufficient.

### Why a short Fourier cycle alone does not solve FFT

Let `R e_j=e_(-j mod N)`. The geometric-sum identity gives

\[
F_N^2=N R,\qquad F_N^4=N^2 I.
\]

Thus the Fourier matrix itself already has an annihilating polynomial of degree at most four. That fact does not make its first application cheap. Choosing `K=F_N` would simply hide the transform in every loop action.

The ACFF hypothesis is stronger in a different direction: `K=BA` must be assembled from explicitly linear-time maps, **and** its polynomial output must have the exact Fourier phase certificate. Neither low degree nor phase correctness alone is an algorithmic breakthrough.

## 7. The concrete candidate: two independently ordered Green scans per map

Define

\[
G=L(I-\operatorname{diag}(a)P_\pi)^{-1}R,
\]

where `L,R` are diagonal scalings and `pi` is a Hamiltonian cycle on all `N` coordinates. The candidate family is

\[
\boxed{A=G_{A,0}+G_{A,1},\qquad B=G_{B,0}+G_{B,1}.}\tag{7.1}
\]

The four cycle orders may be different and non-affine. The diagonal scalings and edge weights may depend on `N` and `omega`, but not on the input. No finite fixed-pole or root-free pencil restriction is assumed. All parameter-generation cost must nevertheless meet Theorem 3.

This is a specific synthesis search space with four Green plans, not an assertion that four always suffice. Independent noncommuting orders are allowed precisely because existing common-order/cut-rank and additive two-Green gates eliminate several simpler constructions.

### Linear-time Green evaluator

Let `c_0,...,c_(N-1)` list the cycle, with `(P_pi z)[c_t]=z[c_(t+1)]`, using cyclic indices. For `v=Rx`, solve

\[
z_{c_t}-a_{c_t}z_{c_{t+1}}=v_{c_t}.
\]

Set

\[
\rho_0=1,\quad \rho_t=\prod_{s=0}^{t-1}a_{c_s},\quad
\Delta=1-\prod_{s=0}^{N-1}a_{c_s}.
\]

If `Delta!=0`,

\[
z_{c_0}=\Delta^{-1}\sum_{t=0}^{N-1}\rho_t v_{c_t}.
\]

Recover the remaining coordinates in descending cycle order from

\[
z_{c_t}=v_{c_t}+a_{c_t}z_{c_{t+1}}.
\]

Finally return `Lz`. This never divides by an individual edge weight, so zero edge weights are valid. Only the cycle-closure denominator must be nonzero. Prefix coefficients are generated once per plan, not by separately exponentiating a root at every entry.

### Instrumented operation ledger

The following exact counts correspond to the intentionally simple straight-line kernel in `verify.py`. An addition to an initially zero accumulator is counted; no zero/one coefficient specialization is taken.

| Kernel | Scalar multiplications | Additions/subtractions | Inversions |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| One Green plan setup, supplied parameters | `N` | `1` | `1` |
| One endpoint-scaled Green application | `4N` | `2N-1` | `0` |
| One paired-Green application | `8N` | `5N-2` | `0` |
| Horner kernel, polynomial length `d` | `(17d-8)N` | `(11d-6)N-4d+2` | `0` |

Four plans add `4N` multiplications, four subtractions and four inversions to setup. Generating their parameters and cycle orders is not included in those small setup counts and remains explicitly charged in Theorem 3. The root stream, array validation/addressing, data traffic, and `q` preparation add `O(N+d)` work under a valid uniform generator.

These are scalar instruction counts, not timing results or a claim to outperform a native FFT at tested lengths.

## 8. A particularly sharp construction target: quadratic closure

The first genuinely nontrivial constant-degree target is

\[
\boxed{(BA)^2=\alpha BA+\beta I,\qquad 1-\alpha-\beta\ne0.}\tag{Q1}
\]

Here `alpha,beta` are global scalar parameters, not input-dependent fitted values. Using

\[
\mu(z)=z^2-\alpha z-\beta,
\]

Theorem 2 gives

\[
\boxed{
F_N=\frac{BAB+(1-\alpha)B}{1-\alpha-\beta}
}\tag{Q2}
\]

whenever `C1,C2,Q1` hold.

Thus four Green plans satisfying these identities would compute the full transform with one initial `B` call, then one `A` and one `B` call, and linear vector combinations. The instrumented Horner count is `26N` scalar multiplications and `16N-6` additions, plus preparation and addressing.

**No growing family satisfying Q1 and the Fourier certificate has been found here.** Q1 is an especially restrictive optional target. The more flexible conjecture allows degree `O(log log N)` instead of degree two.

Degree one is not a useful shortcut by itself: if `BA=lambda I`, then `T=B/(1-lambda)`. Fourier correctness would already place a scalar Fourier transform inside `B`. It does not explain a cheap Fourier map.

## 9. What graph union actually means in this theory

Expanding the paired maps in

\[
p(BA)B=\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}q_j(BA)^jB
\]

produces up to

\[
\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}2^{2j+1}=\frac{2(4^d-1)}3
\]

formal Green words before coincidences or cancellations. The Horner program never materializes these words separately. It shares the vector passing through the same composed operator and uses the annihilator to make feedback closure exact.

The expanded word count is explanatory, not the source of an asymptotic theorem: any factored expression can be wastefully expanded. The actual savings require a proved small `d` and the cost ledger of Theorem 3. Products of independently ordered Green maps are not individual one-cycle Green resolvents, so a lower bound for a parallel sum of one-cycle resolvents cannot be applied to these formal words without an additional argument.

This gives a precise interpretation of the project's desired cycle/reuse principle:

> Repeated graph shape is not enough. A reusable exact operator relation `mu(BA)=0` can remove repeated algebraic work, provided the resulting operator still has the exact Fourier phases.

No thread-level parallelism, quantum superposition, or value-based cache collision is counted as a scalar-work saving.

## 10. Obstruction I: pure Green feedback fails above eight points

### Theorem 4

Let `P,Q` be invertible matrices each supported on its diagonal plus one weighted permutation graph. Set

\[
A=P^{-1},\qquad B=Q^{-1}.
\]

Whenever the feedback inverse exists, its transfer matrix cannot equal `F_N` for `N>8`.

This includes nonzero diagonal endpoint scalings of a single weighted-cycle Green map, since such scalings can be absorbed into an inverse denominator with the same diagonal-plus-permutation support. It does not assert the same threshold for arbitrary independent endpoint permutation gauges.

#### Proof

If `T=F_N`, then

\[
F_N^{-1}=T^{-1}=B^{-1}-A=Q-P^{-1}.
\]

Multiplication by `P` gives

\[
PF_N^{-1}=PQ-I.\tag{10.1}
\]

Every row of `PQ-I` is supported on at most four positions: the diagonal, either permutation position, and their composed position. The diagonal location is already included, so subtracting `I` does not add a fifth possible position.

A row of `PF_N^(-1)` is a nonzero combination of at most two distinct Fourier characters. A one-character combination is nonzero everywhere. For two characters, zeros solve

\[
\omega^{(r-s)j}=c
\]

with `r!=s mod N` and nonzero `c`. This has either zero solutions or exactly `gcd(r-s,N)` solutions, at most `N/2`. Therefore every such row has support at least `ceil(N/2)`. It cannot be the zero combination because `P` is invertible and the Fourier rows are independent.

Equation (10.1) requires `ceil(N/2)<=4`, hence `N<=8`. QED.

For prime lengths the same proof strengthens to `N-1<=4`, excluding primes at least seven. This is an architecture-specific obstruction, independent of any bounded-coefficient or numerical-stability assumption.

**Consequence:** Simply putting two pure linear-time Green solves into a feedback loop cannot deliver the claimed transform. The sums inside the candidate maps change a real hypothesis, but are not themselves evidence of success.

## 11. Obstruction II: short loop closure is nongeneric

### Theorem 5

Fix any four Hamiltonian cycle orders and let the weights and diagonal endpoints in (7.1) vary over complex parameters for which all Green denominators are nonzero. On a nonempty Zariski-open subset of this parameter space, the loop `BA` has minimal-polynomial degree `N`.

Here “Zariski-open” means outside the zero set of a nonzero polynomial, after the already required denominators are excluded. The claim concerns the ambient candidate family, **not** the unconstructed Fourier-compatible subfamily.

#### Proof

Consider the determinant of the `N x N` Krylov matrix

\[
\mathcal K=[\mathbf1,K\mathbf1,\ldots,K^{N-1}\mathbf1].
\]

It is a rational function of the Green parameters. To show it is not identically zero, choose all edge weights zero, so each Green map becomes diagonal. Let both `A` branches equal `I/2`, and both `B` branches equal `diag(lambda_0,...,lambda_(N-1))/2`, where the `lambda_i` are distinct and nonzero. These choices are valid for any of the fixed cycle orders, with all closure denominators equal to one. Then

\[
A=I,\quad B=K=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_i),
\]

and `mathcal K` is a Vandermonde matrix. Its determinant is

\[
\prod_{i<j}(\lambda_j-\lambda_i)\ne0.
\]

Consequently its rational numerator is not the zero polynomial. On its nonzero locus the `N` Krylov vectors are independent. An annihilator of degree below `N` would make them dependent, so none exists. Cayley–Hamilton supplies an annihilator of degree at most `N`; the degree is exactly `N`.

Requiring every edge weight to be nonzero still leaves a nonempty open set: it intersects the nonempty open set just proved over the infinite field of complex numbers. The zero-weight witness is a way to establish nonvanishing of a polynomial, not a restriction of the generic claim to diagonal maps. QED.

### Empirical control

The verifier constructs 40 independently sampled paired-Green loops, at lengths `4,8,16,32` over two prime fields. All 40 have an exact full-rank Krylov certificate from `e_0`, proving degree `N` for those samples. This is consistent with Theorem 5; it is not an exhaustive finite-field or Fourier-factorization impossibility result.

### Implication for synthesis

Random dense mixing is not the desired mechanism. A successful family must satisfy exceptional algebraic relations together with the Fourier certificate. This is the central missing constructive step.

Also, similarity does not change a minimal polynomial: replacing an existing degree-`N` q-Weyl nilpotent by `C^(-1) K C` cannot reduce its degree. The proposed loop has to be a genuinely different operator, not a changed basis for the old one.

## 12. Obstruction III: bounded sparse feedback cannot be the escape

Suppose `K` has at most `s` nonzeros per row and `B` has at most `b` nonzeros per row. Since row support under multiplication is submultiplicative,

\[
\operatorname{rowsupp}\left(p(K)B\right)
\le b\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}s^j.
\]

An exact Fourier matrix has `N` nonzeros in every row, so necessarily

\[
\boxed{b\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}s^j\ge N.}\tag{12.1}
\]

For fixed `b` and fixed `s>=2`, this forces `d=Omega(log N)`. For `s=1` it forces `bd>=N`. The proposed maps use dense-but-cheap Green actions rather than identifying fast action with bounded matrix support.

This support argument does not apply merely because a dense Green map is specified by a sparse denominator. Its forward matrix, not its denominator, is the operator whose powers occur in this calculation.

### What the positive controls do not prove

To validate the algebra independently, the first group deliberately uses a dense Fourier oracle. Given a chosen nilpotent `K` and target `T_0=F_N`, it sets

\[
B=(I-K)T_0,\qquad A=B^{-1}K.
\]

This makes `BA=K` and the feedback transfer equal the target. It checks all certificate signs, multiplication orders, polynomial coefficients, and output columns.

**Those `A,B` are oracle-derived dense matrices. They are not presented as paired-Green maps or cheap Fourier factorizations.** Their construction already uses the desired Fourier matrix and therefore cannot establish the complexity conjecture.

Similarly, the Horner test on random paired maps verifies only `p(BA)B` as a polynomial operator. It does not assert that an arbitrary short polynomial is an inverse or that its output is Fourier. The random degree-`N` results explicitly demonstrate why such an assertion would be false.

The checker itself uses direct Fourier matrices and dense elimination. Its running time is not the claimed transform cost. All new tests were run; archived source-reported counts were not copied into these new results as independent checks.

## 14. The proposed research conjecture

### ACFF conjecture

There is a deterministic uniform family of maps of the form (7.1), with four independently ordered weighted Green plans, such that for every `N>=2`:

\[
(I-BA)DB=BS(I-AB),\qquad (I-BA)\mathbf1=Be_0,
\]

and a generated polynomial `mu_N` satisfies

\[
\mu_N(BA)=0,\qquad \mu_N(1)\ne0,\qquad
\deg\mu_N=O(\log\log(N+4)),
\]

with all generation, root preparation, addressing and arithmetic meeting Theorem 3.

**If this conjecture is proved, Theorem 3 gives an exact `O(N log log N)` full Fourier transform. It is not proved here.** The paired-Green family is a concrete proposal; it may be false or require a richer bounded-cost map class. The execution theorem would still apply to any replacement class satisfying its hypotheses.

A bounded-degree version, especially `Q1`, would imply `O(N)`. A construction only for all dyadic lengths would establish only that length-family result until an arbitrary-length reduction with legitimate roots and costs is proved. Finite tested dyadic lengths do not supply that growing family.

### Precise acceptance conditions

A successful next result must provide executable or symbolic coefficient and cycle formulas indexed by `N`; nonzero Green denominators; an exact operator annihilator, not a scalar-projection fit; the phase and seed identities; and a full cold cost ledger. Finding parameters at a few sizes or solving a dense system separately at each length is not enough.

The synthesis equations are concrete:

```
unknowns: four cycle orders, four sets of Green weights/endpoints, mu coefficients
constraints:
    all Green closure denominators != 0
    mu(1) != 0
    mu(BA) = 0
    (I-BA) D B - B S (I-AB) = 0
    (I-BA) ones - B e0 = 0
cost obligations:
    uniform parameter generation + setup + evaluation = O(N * degree(mu))
```

Clearing denominators for algebraic elimination must retain the nonzero guards. A zero-denominator solution of a cleared polynomial system is not a valid feedback map. The certificate avoids an unnecessary inverse of `B`, but does not remove the genuine Green and feedback invertibility conditions.

## 15. Novelty and external comparison

The ingredients—Green resolvents, finite polynomial inverses, Krylov spaces, Fourier shift/modulation identities and matrix feedback—are established mathematics. The Fourier-specific cubic reduction and the paired-Green closure target are deductions developed for this proposal. This package does not assert that no equivalent formulation exists in the literature. A limited prior-art check cannot establish that claim.

The official OpenAI Math result #130 documentation, checked on 10 October 2026, reports a separate uniform exact-complex result with work `O(N (log N)^(1-10^-13))`, supplied chosen roots and unrestricted coefficients. This proposal does not import that algorithm. It must be judged by its own missing uniform construction, not by an assertion that all sub-`N log N` Fourier results are absent.

Ailon's well-conditioned-model lower bound has explicit circuit and conditioning hypotheses. Nothing here establishes controlled intermediate conditioning, benign coefficient sizes, or stable evaluation near a small Green or annihilator denominator. A fast exact scalar circuit can fail to yield a useful floating-point or bit-cost algorithm. Those issues require separate proofs.

## 16. Bottom line

What is proved here is an exact Fourier certification theorem, a finite feedback-closure evaluator with a charged cost, a pure-Green feedback exclusion, and a generic-degree obstruction for the proposed larger family. What is proposed is a uniform Fourier-compatible loop of sublogarithmic algebraic degree.

The missing step is no longer described vaguely as “reuse more of the graph.” It is the simultaneous construction of

\[
\boxed{\text{cheap }A,B\quad+\quad\text{exact cubic Fourier certificate}\quad+\quad
\deg\mu_{BA}=o(\log N),}
\]

with uniform preparation and all outputs charged. No example in this package yet satisfies those conditions as a growing family.
