# Targeted priority decision: two newly audited theorem packages

Date: 2026-10-10. Scope: mathematical priority and publication positioning, not a new correctness audit.

## Decision

**A, odd-cycle scalar-resolvent chirps:** the best potential mathematical contribution is the half-degree zero classification: the exact resonance gcd for all odd orders and ordinary squarefreeness at prime orders. This is a credible narrowly scoped theorem-note candidate **conditional on further priority checks**. Its moment/Padé mechanism and identification of the Rogers–Szegő annihilator are established mathematics, not new discoveries. The all-composite squarefreeness question remains unresolved by this project; it has not been established to be an open problem in the literature.

**B, arbitrary-jet young feedback:** a valid, sharp component obstruction and useful supporting theorem. Its lower-bound method is classical bounded-coefficient volume machinery. The identifiable project-specific content is the routed-output identity, forbidden-marker selection of orthogonal packets, and consequent direct-sum exposure. On current evidence it is substantially weaker as a standalone publication lead than A. It does not establish a new general Fourier lower-bound method.

Neither package can responsibly be called “genuinely novel and publication-worthy” without qualification. Correctness is independently accepted; priority and significance are separate judgments. These results do not presently displace the strongest candidates in the earlier publication assessment.

## A: the exact overlap with classical Padé theory

Let N=2m+1, h=(N+1)/2, q of order N, and μ_k=f(k+1). Then

    μ_k = q^h (q^(3h))^k q^(k(k−1)/2).

Lubinsky's accessible 2002 author paper, §2 Lemma 2.1, gives the partial-theta Padé denominator Q_(a,m)(z)=H_m(−q^a z;q), for a≥m−1 and q of order greater than m, and attributes the identity to Wynn. Apply type [m−1/m] to the scaled moment series above. Since 3h+m−1=2N, its denominator is H_m(−z;q). Reversing it gives exactly the project's T_m(z)=(−1)^m H_m(−z;q). This is an exact formula-level overlap, not merely similarity of terminology. [Lubinsky, §2](https://lubinsky.math.gatech.edu/Research%20papers/DortmundProcCuriousqSeries.pdf)

The residue construction, shifted-Hankel minimum, and free zeroth-moment correction are applications of formal orthogonality, Gaussian quadrature, rational realization, and a rank-one determinant update. The added restrictions λ≠0 and λ^N≠1, and prohibition of confluent resolvents, are meaningful model-specific guards; general quadrature results permit multiplicities and do not automatically meet those guards. [Pozza–Pranić, §§4–6](https://arxiv.org/html/1903.11395)

What remains potentially distinctive is therefore precise:

- gcd(T_m,z^N−1)=1 or z−1 according to N modulo 4, with the latter root simple
- the ordinary-discriminant degeneration and cyclotomic divisibility argument proving squarefreeness for prime N, more generally degree n<p at order p^a
- the resulting legal simple-resolvent minimum, with the forced diagonal and explicitly charged construction

Spiridonov–Zhedanov's inspected degree-N terminal-polynomial formulas are not half-degree zero theorems. Their introduction explicitly separates complex orthogonality from the positive-measure simple-zero theorem. Therefore these inspected formulas do not dispose of the proposed delta. [Spiridonov–Zhedanov, §§1–2, 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/q-alg/9605034)

Ismail–Witte's Rogers–Szegő product evaluates a q-difference discriminant, not the ordinary derivative discriminant. It cannot substitute for the required squarefreeness proof. [Ismail–Witte, equations (1.7)–(1.9), (5.16)–(5.18)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0012259)

Two particularly pertinent original papers remain uninspected in full: Lubinsky–Saff (1987) and Zhedanov (1997). The former's author bibliography confirms a full article about unit-modulus-q Rogers–Szegő zero distributions; the latter is established root-of-unity/Gauss-sum orthogonality literature. Failed public retrieval is a priority blocker, not evidence of novelty. Lubinsky's later accessible Theorem 2.2 excludes roots of unity, so that theorem alone is not the target guard.

## B: where the actual mathematical content sits

The exact row formula has a useful classical interpretation, derived here directly from the audited formula. Put s=b+t, M=2^s, R=r/M, and ignore the harmless row phase. For

    β_j = 1_(j≡q mod R) η^(jf),  f=rev_t(k),

its unitary Fourier image is

    β̂_ℓ = (M/√r) η^(q(f−ℓ)) 1_(ℓ≡f mod M).

Thus, at fixed (t,k), the q-indexed normalized rows form an orthonormal basis of one Fourier frequency cylinder. The forbidden-marker proof says cylinders belonging to different (t,k) are disjoint. This is a concrete instance of character orthogonality and tree-selected packet subspaces. This interpretation is an assessment inference from the supplied formulas, not a claim that an inspected source states the same marker theorem.

Tree-structured orthogonal packet libraries and admissible selections were already central to Coifman–Wickerhauser. Their construction is relevant context, but no exact equivalence of the project's feedback routing to their published theorem was established here. [Coifman–Wickerhauser, §§II–III](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/sites.gatech.edu/dist/2/436/files/2011/04/coifman92en.pdf?bid=436)

Once this Gram matrix and J independently exposed blocks are known, Cauchy–Binet and a determinant potential give the audited lower bound. Morgenstern already treats additions with bounded complex coefficients; Raz and Bürgisser–Lotz treat rectangular volume and singular values. Consequently the new application must not be sold as a new volume technique or as a direct-sum theorem for arbitrary circuits. [Morgenstern (1973)](https://doi.org/10.1145/321752.321761), [Bürgisser–Lotz, §3](https://arxiv.org/html/cs/0301016)

The accepted arbitrary-jet theorem genuinely improves this project's prior executor-specific visit count: it rules out all linear bounded-coefficient circuits that expose the specified outputs. Its Jr independent input coordinates are essential. Substituting jets constrained by N source coordinates does not carry the lower bound through automatically. Neither finite-field arithmetic nor unrestricted scalar/nonlinear models is covered.

## Publication and stopping decision

1. Retain A as a conditional short-note candidate centered on zeros of half-degree Rogers–Szegő polynomials at odd roots of unity. Lead with the zero theorem and state the resolvent application afterward. Do not claim new Gauss-sum orthogonality, new Padé theory, or new polynomial families.
2. Obtain legitimate full texts of Lubinsky–Saff (1987), Zhedanov (1997), and, if needed, Zhedanov (1999); compare exact degree ranges, ordinary versus q-discriminants, and resonance/zero statements. This is the smallest remaining priority check capable of changing the assessment materially.
3. A uniform composite proof would strengthen A substantially, but only after separate correctness and priority review. The new composite-research sector counterexample rules out a tempting generic simplicity inference; the 62 characteristic-2 tests are finite route-finding evidence and do not extend the accepted through-1001 range.
4. Retain B as a proposition in a scoped marker-feedback or obstruction paper. A standalone case would need broader reusable content, such as a genuinely stronger theorem for a natural family of marker languages/interfaces, or an application whose significance survives the arbitrary-jet qualification.

No public posting, outreach, predecessor-code execution, or mathematical claim of global originality was undertaken. The accompanying table records inspection depth and the exact limits of each comparison.
