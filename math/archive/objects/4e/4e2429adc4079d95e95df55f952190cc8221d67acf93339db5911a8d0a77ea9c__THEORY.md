# Recursive Fourier-Mass Contraction
## A Weyl-certified Green-network research theory

**Date:** 10 October 2026  
**Status:** Conditional complexity theorem, a concrete unproved factorization conjecture, an additional proved architecture gate, and executable exact finite checks. **No new universal fast Fourier algorithm has been constructed.**  
**Target:** All explicit DFT coefficients of every fresh arbitrary vector, with sequential arithmetic, coefficient preparation, indexing, storage operations and output materialization charged.  
**Provenance:** Synthesis of the FFT project's Green, graph-union, phase-bank, tensor, inverse-sparsity and Weyl-certificate branches. This is not a reimplementation of OpenAI Math #130, CrocSwap, or integer-mult-kappa. Publication-level novelty is not established.

## Abstract

The proposed search objective is not a constant number of named stages or a low-rank middle tensor. It is the **total length of all recursive Fourier calls after a complete, exact, charged operator rewrite**. Ordinary balanced mixed-radix decomposition uses two banks of smaller Fourier transforms, with combined length 2N. We prove that uniformly reducing this recursive mass to rho*N while halving the binary logarithm of each child length gives O(N*(log N)^(log_2 rho)) work for 1<rho<2, O(N log log N) at rho=1, and O(N) for rho<1, assuming O(N) nonrecursive overhead.

A concrete synthesis conjecture places a partially active Fourier bank between four constant-cost layers, each a sum of two weighted cyclic Green resolvents. This is a specific family of matrix identities to solve, not an existing factorization. We also extend the project's inverse-sparsity barrier to this banked architecture: constant-depth pure Green layers cannot implement the desired rewrite, and adding only bounded-rank inverse defects does not repair the problem. The proposed paired-Green layers are selected because their inverses are not automatically sparse. Neither their Fourier compatibility nor the required escape from a sparse-plus-bounded-rank inverse description has been established.

The accompanying verifier checks weighted cyclic solvers, exact Fourier/Weyl identities, ordinary two-bank controls, deliberately invalid bank deletion, inverse-density examples and the recurrence calculations. It does not supply the conjectured factors.

## 1. Computational contract

First address every dyadic length N=2^k, not merely the subsequence 2^(2^h). The input is an arbitrary vector x in K^N. K may be C in the exact arithmetic model, or a field with characteristic not dividing N and a supplied primitive Nth root omega. Define

    F_N[k,j] = omega^(k*j).

Choosing omega=exp(-2*pi*i/N) gives the usual negative-exponent convention. The inverse is (1/N) times the transform with omega^-1.

A scalar addition, subtraction, multiplication, or inversion of a known nonzero input-independent scalar costs one field operation. A scalar occupies one field register; this is an arithmetic complexity model, not a bit-complexity theorem. Root powers, coefficient generation, permutations, address calculations, reads and writes must be charged. The primitive root itself is an input parameter. The algorithm may not assume an oracle that supplies its unknown Green coefficients.

All linear operators in the conjecture are input-independent. No reuse of earlier input vectors, special signal promise, dense-output compression, or parallel speedup is allowed. Exact comparisons used by verification code are not being presented as stable floating-point recognition.

**Uniformity is part of the conjecture.** A separately discovered factorization for each N, with uncharged coefficient search, is not enough. A deterministic parameter generator must meet the charged overhead budget for every supported length.

## 2. What the inherited research changes

The source ledger records the inspected packages and limitations of this review. The following findings are incorporated rather than rediscovered.

* The complete phase-bank reconstruction and endpoint-local tensor sparsification expose the usual two Fourier basis conversions. An N-entry middle tensor does not make these conversions free. Hero #17 Phase 6 gives the exact support identity nnz(T')=nnz(A F_Q)*nnz(F_M B^T); equality N requires inverse-Fourier endpoint gauges up to monomial transformations.
* Bounded chirp, Green-mode and shear/orbit families have useful exact fast executors, but their universality is not established. They cannot replace an arbitrary-input theorem.
* Three rank-one quasiseparable stages with two fixed internal bit reversals and natural endpoints are excluded at dyadic N>=64. This is a scoped architecture result, not an unrestricted Fourier lower bound.
* The simultaneous-order Green-bank gate v18 excludes a fixed number of separately summed Hamiltonian Green terms at large prime lengths. A fixed number of terms is therefore not a valid default exact construction.
* Pure Green inverse sparsity and sparse-plus-low-rank inverse defects are already strong obstructions. A new proposal must explain why it is not in those families.
* Sparse-surrogate, phasor-table and analytic-correction approximation results have different accuracy contracts. They are not used to establish exactness here.
* Green Phase 30 and the nonlinear compatibility work show that graph cuts, Jacobian dimension, or selected projective invariants cannot stand in for a full Fourier identity. Zero branches and denominators must be handled explicitly.

These results motivate **a recursively active Fourier core, with noncommuting global corrections whose own work is fully charged**. This differs both from a bounded-mode input representation and from an attempt to express the whole Fourier matrix as a small parallel Green sum.

## 3. Definition: recursive Fourier mass

Suppose an exact procedure for a length-N DFT invokes smaller Fourier transforms on vectors of lengths

    n_1, n_2, ..., n_s.

Its recursive Fourier mass is

    M(N) = sum_i n_i.

This definition counts all calls, including repeated transforms of equal size on different data. A transform hidden in coefficient synthesis, a change of basis, or decoding must also be charged rather than omitted from the accounting.

For standard balanced mixed radix N=m*q, there are q calls of length m and m calls of length q. Hence

    M_standard(N) = q*m + m*q = 2N.

The parameter M(N) is **not** the number of independent scalar inputs. A circuit may retain all N coordinates on identity bypass wires while applying Fourier calls to only some blocks. Such a circuit is not automatically Fourier; the exact operator identity remains the decisive requirement.

## 4. Conditional contraction theorem

### Theorem 1

Suppose a uniform exact DFT procedure exists for every N=2^k, k>=1. Above a fixed base case, suppose:

1. Every recursive call has dyadic length n_i<=2^ceil(k/2).
2. The total recursive mass is sum_i n_i<=rho*N for a fixed nonnegative constant rho.
3. All remaining work is at most C*N, with C independent of k and x. This includes parameter synthesis, root-power preparation, linear maps, indexing and output writes.
4. The procedure terminates through these calls and fixed-size base cases; it does not invoke an additional uncharged Fourier primitive.

Then the worst-case sequential work satisfies

| rho | Consequence |
|---|---|
| 0<=rho<1 | O(N) |
| rho=1 | O(N log log N) |
| 1<rho<2 | O(N (log N)^(log_2 rho)) |
| rho=2 | O(N log N) |

These are conditional upper bounds. The theorem does not assert that any rho<2 procedure exists in the proposed family.

### Proof

Let T(2^k) be the charged work and set

    A_h = max_{1<=k<=2^h} T(2^k)/2^k.

For a new exponent k in the shell 2^(h-1)<k<=2^h, every child exponent is at most 2^(h-1). Consequently,

    T(2^k)/2^k <= rho*A_(h-1) + C,

and, allowing older exponents in the maximum,

    A_h <= max(A_(h-1), rho*A_(h-1)+C).

For rho<1, the interval [0, max(A_0,C/(1-rho))] is invariant. For rho=1, A_h<=A_0+C*h. For rho>1,

    A_h <= rho^h*A_0 + C*(rho^h-1)/(rho-1).

Take h=ceil(log_2 k). Then rho^h=O(k^(log_2 rho)), while h=O(log k). Since k=log_2 N, the stated results follow. The finite base cases do not affect the asymptotic bounds. QED.

With O(N) local live workspace and a depth-first execution that processes child calls sequentially, memory is O(N): the retained parent sizes decrease as N, O(sqrt N), O(N^(1/4)), and so on along an active branch. This is an additional scheduling assumption, not a consequence of a low parameter count alone.

### Interpretation

A 25% reduction in ordinary recursive mass, from 2N to 1.5N at every level, would yield

    O(N (log N)^0.5849625007...).

Compressing the two banks into a single full bank gives rho=1 and therefore O(N log log N). Reducing the surviving recursive bank to a fixed fraction of N gives linear work. The other coordinates may still travel through full-dimensional identity and Green paths.

The recurrence analysis is elementary; the substantive research obligation is to build a uniform exact operator rewrite that satisfies its hypotheses.

## 5. A weaker savings threshold

Even a saving that decreases with scale can beat N log N under the same exactness and overhead assumptions.

### Corollary 2

At exponent-height h=ceil(log_2 k)>=2, assume recursive mass at most

    2*(1-1/h)*N,

and nonrecursive work C*N. Then T(N)=O(N log N/log log N).

### Proof

An upper envelope can be chosen to obey

    a_h = 2*(h-1)/h*a_(h-1)+C.

Multiply by h/2^h:

    h*a_h/2^h = (h-1)*a_(h-1)/2^(h-1) + C*h/2^h.

The sum of h/2^h converges. Thus a_h=O(2^h/h), which gives the result. A child in an earlier exponent shell only lowers its required envelope. QED.

This is a useful experimental target, not the strongest current theoretical benchmark: a fixed positive power saving in log N eventually beats division by log log N. No novelty claim about this recurrence is made.

## 6. Concrete synthesis conjecture: one bank and four paired-Green layers

Set

    N=2^k,
    m=2^floor(k/2),
    q=N/m.

Partition an N-vector into q contiguous blocks of length m. For J contained in {0,...,q-1}, define the full N-by-N block-diagonal operator

    B_(N,J) = direct_sum_(b=0)^(q-1) B_b,
    B_b = F_m  if b is in J,
          I_m  otherwise.

The Fourier root for F_m is omega^(N/m). Computing it and its powers must be charged. Every block remains present. Inactive blocks are **identity bypasses**, not discarded data. B_(N,J) is invertible and has recursive mass m*|J|.

### Conjecture GFC-4

There is a deterministic O(N)-work parameter generator producing four operators H_(1,N),...,H_(4,N), and a set J_N, such that for every dyadic N above fixed base cases,

    F_N = H_(4,N) H_(3,N) B_(N,J_N) H_(2,N) H_(1,N).       (GFC)

Each layer is a sum of two independently ordered weighted Green resolvents:

    H_(ell,N)
      = L_(ell,0) (I-D_(a_(ell,0)) P_(pi_(ell,0)))^-1 R_(ell,0)
      + L_(ell,1) (I-D_(a_(ell,1)) P_(pi_(ell,1)))^-1 R_(ell,1).

Here L and R are nonsingular diagonal or monomial maps. Each pi is a Hamiltonian cycle order; the cycles need not be affine, identical, or bit reversal. All cycle denominators must be nonsingular. Parameters may be nonunitary and depend on N but not on x. None is supplied by a hidden Fourier computation.

The number four is a concrete fixed synthesis budget, not a proved minimal or sufficient number. Replacing four by another absolute constant preserves the conditional asymptotics. Merely increasing it in finite experiments is not a theorem.

**GFC-4 is unproved. No factors satisfying it for a growing family of N have been constructed in this package.**

### Consequences of the conjecture

* If J_N contains every block, then m*|J_N|=N. Theorem 1 gives O(N log log N).
* If |J_N|<=theta*q for a fixed theta<1, Theorem 1 gives O(N). For example, at most half the blocks gives rho<=1/2.

The latter is stronger than one-bank fusion. It requires the Green layers and bypass paths to provide the missing Fourier action exactly. Simply replacing Fourier blocks with identities fails, as the negative controls demonstrate.

### Why this family is at least different from excluded templates

It is not a fixed parallel sum of Hamiltonian resolvents: it contains products of noncommuting sums and a recursively computed Fourier bank. It is not a product of pure sparse-inverse Green factors. It does not claim that a sparse tensor center makes endpoint transforms free. It does not use a low-rank representation of the input. It is also not the three rank-one QS / double-bit-reversal architecture already excluded by the project.

These distinctions only explain why the cited scoped gates do not immediately settle GFC. They are **not evidence that GFC is true**. Other rank, algebraic, or conditioning obstructions may exclude this family.

## 7. Charged Green execution

Write a cycle order as c_0,...,c_(N-1), with P connecting c_t to c_(t+1 mod N), and let A=I-D_a P. To solve Az=u, define

    p_0=1,
    p_t=product_(s=0)^(t-1) a_(c_s),
    d=1-product_(s=0)^(N-1) a_(c_s).

Assume d is nonzero. Unrolling the recurrence z_(c_t)=u_(c_t)+a_(c_t)z_(c_(t+1)) gives

    z_(c_0) = (sum_(t=0)^(N-1) p_t*u_(c_t))/d.

With z_(c_0) fixed, recover the remaining coordinates by walking backward around the cycle. This algorithm remains valid when individual a_i vanish: it never divides by a weight.

For G=L A^-1 R with diagonal endpoints, the supplied implementation charges:

| Work | Multiplications | Additions/subtractions | Inversions |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Prefix and denominator setup | N | 1 | 1 |
| One arbitrary-vector application | 4N | 2N-1 | 0 |
| Setup plus first application | 5N | 2N | 1 |

Memory and indexing take O(N) ordinary steps. These are operation counts, not measured comparisons against a tuned FFT library. Permutation validation and coefficient-array storage are also at most linear when supplied as a valid generated listing, or can be validated by a linear visited array. The Python test harness uses extra checks not included in the kernel ledger.

Four paired layers require eight such solves and four output-vector additions. Thus, once a legitimate O(N)-work parameter generator exists, these layers genuinely have O(N) charged nonrecursive overhead. The unknown synthesis cost cannot be inferred from the cheap application cost.

## 8. An additional proved gate: a Fourier bank does not cure sparse inverses

This is a bank-aware extension of the project's Fourier-versus-row-sparse and inverse-defect arguments.

### Lemma 3: Fourier versus row-sparse matrices

Let F be F_N, F_N^-1, or any invertible-monomial left/right rescaling or permutation of either. If S has at most d nonzero entries in each row, then

    rank(F-S) >= floor(N/(d+1)).                            (1)

Proof: let r=floor(N/(d+1)). After undoing endpoint monomial maps, select r consecutive Fourier frequencies. On these rows, the union of columns touched by S has at most rd elements. There remain at least N-rd>=r columns. On any selected r of these columns, F-S equals a nonsingular Vandermonde minor of F. Hence its rank is at least r. The r=0 case is vacuous. Monomial maps preserve row support counts and rank. QED.

### Proposition 4: bank-aware inverse-support obstruction

Suppose an exact factorization of F_N uses one B_(N,J) bank and t invertible surrounding factors H_1,...,H_t, in any positions before or after the bank. If H_i^-1 has at most s_i nonzeros per row, then

    m * product_i s_i >= N.                                (2)

Proof: B_(N,J)^-1 is block diagonal and has at most m nonzeros per row. The inverse of the full factorization is a product with row support at most m*product_i s_i. But F_N^-1 has exactly N nonzeros per row. QED.

In particular, for t pure first-order Green factors, s_i<=2 and therefore

    2^t >= N/m = q.

At balanced dimensions, t must grow at least as (1/2)log_2 N up to rounding. A constant number of such layers cannot replace one Fourier bank.

### Proposition 5: bank-aware inverse-defect obstruction

More generally, suppose

    H_i^-1 = A_i + E_i,
    row_nnz(A_i) <= s_i,
    rank(E_i) <= r_i.

Then any such exact factorization requires

    sum_i r_i >= floor(N/(1 + m*product_i s_i)).             (3)

Proof: retain B^-1 and replace each H_i^-1 by A_i. The resulting baseline has row support at most m*product_i s_i. Telescope the difference from the true inverse product. Each summand contains a single E_i with arbitrary matrix factors on either side and therefore has rank at most r_i. Apply (1) and rank subadditivity. QED.

For balanced m comparable to sqrt(N), fixed t and fixed s_i, the right side is Omega(sqrt(N)). A bounded total inverse-defect rank is insufficient.

### Why paired Green layers were chosen

For invertible A and B,

    A^-1+B^-1 = A^-1 (A+B) B^-1.

When the sum is invertible,

    (A^-1+B^-1)^-1 = B (A+B)^-1 A.

The inverse may be globally dense, even though the forward map is the sum of two linear-time cycle solves. The verification package exhibits exact finite examples with every inverse entry nonzero at N=8,16,32. These examples are not Fourier factors and do not prove GFC. They only verify that a dense inverse is compatible with a cheap forward evaluator. Density alone does not establish distance from sparse-plus-low-rank matrices: for example, a dense matrix can still be diagonal plus rank one. Escaping the stronger inverse-defect gate is an additional requirement on any successful parameters.

## 9. Exact Fourier certification

Let S e_j=e_(j+1 mod N), and let D=diag(1,omega,...,omega^(N-1)). A candidate C is exactly F_N if

    C S = D C,
    C e_0 = (1,...,1)^T.                                   (4)

Indeed,

    C e_j = C S^j e_0 = D^j C e_0 = D^j 1,

whose kth entry is omega^(kj). This proves every matrix entry at once.

The additional Weyl identity

    C D = S^-1 C                                            (5)

is redundant with (4), but useful as an independent implementation check. Testing only one commutation relation is not enough: row-scaled Fourier matrices pass C S=D C. Testing both without the seed is not enough either: a nonunit scalar multiple of F_N passes both.

For a fixed N, building every column on basis vectors and checking these equations gives an exact finite instance certificate. It may cost O(N^2) or more and is not included for free in an asserted O(N) executor. An all-N fast algorithm needs a uniform symbolic identity or a charged efficient proof-producing construction.

Finite-field equality is exact in that field. It does not, by itself, prove an analogous identity over C. A complex claim needs a cyclotomic-field identity, a justified coefficient reconstruction, or an independent symbolic proof. Nonzero minors can sometimes be certified by reduction, but zero residuals in one characteristic are not universal identities.

The Green Phase 30 lesson is also retained: selected anchored projective equations can accept zero-anchor artifacts. A synthesis search must include the full required minors or Weyl residuals, nonzero denominator guards, and every degenerate coefficient branch not legitimately excluded.

## 10. Relation to Graph + Union + Sparse Table + quantum inspiration

**Graph:** the full scalar dependency network, including Green recurrences, identity bypasses, Fourier calls, and coefficient preparation. Its total work is the quantity being improved.

**Union:** exact algebraic fusion that replaces two banks by a smaller total recursive mass. Reusing an expression is valuable only when it reduces the charged scalar work while preserving the complete linear map. This is not union-find by itself, nor approximate value-collision hashing.

**Sparse table:** an O(N)-sized, charged table of powers and index/phase descriptors. There is no table over all possible input vectors and no exponentially large precomputation.

**Quantum inspiration:** Fourier shift/modulation covariance supplies exact Weyl certificates. The algorithm is classical and outputs all N coefficients. Quantum gate counts or access to a state are not used as classical output costs.

## 11. All lengths: what is and is not automatic

A construction only at N=2^(2^h) is inadequate: consecutive available sizes have quadratic gaps. Theorem 1 and GFC demand every dyadic exponent, preventing that padding loophole.

After a dyadic construction, arbitrary lengths can be reduced to a power-of-two convolution through the chirp identity

    omega^(j*k) = zeta^(j^2+k^2-(j-k)^2),  zeta^2=omega,

with zeta a suitable primitive 2N-th root. Choose a power of two M>=2N-1 with M<4N. Padding the j-k kernel in the standard cyclic embedding permits evaluation using two forward length-M transforms, one inverse length-M transform, and O(N) chirp, product and output work. The input-independent kernel transform is charged even on the first input.

Thus, **when the needed roots are available under the declared model and their preparation costs are included**, the reduction gives

    T_all(N) <= 3*T_dyadic(M) + O(N),

preserving the dyadic asymptotic bound. Chirp powers can be generated by a constant-work ratio recurrence per coordinate rather than separate exponentiation.

However, a field containing only an Nth root need not contain an Mth root or a suitable square root of omega. We do not obtain them from a supplied Nth root by declaring root extraction free. An all-length theorem in a strict supplied-root field model requires a separate root-access/extension construction. Consequently, the unconditional statements in this note are the conditional dyadic theorem and the architecture obstructions, not an all-field, all-length speedup.

Run `python verify.py` using Python's standard library. The seed is fixed and all matrix equalities are modulo 65537. Results are saved in `RESULTS.json`.

| Check | Actual coverage |

| Ordinary two-bank transform versus independent direct DFT | Every basis vector at N=4,8,16,32,64; 124 columns, 5,456 entries |
| Invalid Fourier certificates rejected | 15 cases: bank deletion, row scaling, scalar scaling |
| Cheap paired-Green maps with fully dense inverses | 3 exact examples, N=8,16,32 |

| Constant-mass and harmonic recurrence arithmetic | Exact rational calculations; all assertions pass |

All six groups pass. The direct DFT, dense matrix construction and Gaussian elimination in the verifier deliberately have higher costs; they are validation tools, not a fast executor.

**Not tested or proved:** the existence of a contracting GFC identity, a uniform Green-parameter generator, floating-point stability, high-precision bit complexity, or a practical speedup. No matrix in the package is labeled a contracting Fourier factorization.

## 13. Current benchmark and scientific scope

The official OpenAI Math #130 documentation, checked on 10 October 2026, reports a separate uniform exact all-length result with work O(N*(log N)^(1-10^(-13))), including scalar preparation and root-order selection, with a chosen root supplied and unrestricted exact complex coefficients. Its existence means that simply calling sub-N-log-N arithmetic impossible or globally unachieved would be incorrect. We do not import that construction into this theory.

A proved rho=1 or rho=1.5 instance of the theorem would be a much stronger asymptotic logarithmic bound on dyadic lengths in its declared model. The rho_h=2*(1-1/h) corollary, in contrast, is eventually weaker than any fixed log-power saving. These comparisons are conditional on matching models; neither is an achieved new upper bound here.

Bounded-coefficient and well-conditioned Fourier lower bounds have important hypotheses. Ailon's well-conditioned gate model gives Omega(R^-1*N log N) under its restrictions. Our proposed unrestricted-coefficient exact field model is not a guarantee of numerical stability, small intermediate values, or bounded precision. Stability would need an additional theorem even after finding exact factors.

## 14. What remains to turn this into a breakthrough

The missing construction is explicit:

    F_N = H_4 H_3 (direct_sum of F_m / identity blocks) H_2 H_1,

with a total active Fourier length at most N, for every dyadic N, and a uniformly charged O(N) generator for the Green parameters. This would give O(N log log N). A fixed smaller active fraction would give O(N).

The next successful result must therefore be a uniform phase-compatible coefficient identity, not merely a network with sufficient graph rank. Search can be rejected early by the inherited cut and defect gates; a surviving candidate must then pass all exact phase equations with its denominators certified. A small-size identity alone is only a finite construction; it needs a size-lifting rule whose work preserves the mass inequality.

This note supplies a precise target, correct cost thresholds, an additional bank-aware obstruction, and tests that detect several common false successes. It does not replace the missing operator identity with optimism, and it does not abandon that identity merely because its proof is difficult.
