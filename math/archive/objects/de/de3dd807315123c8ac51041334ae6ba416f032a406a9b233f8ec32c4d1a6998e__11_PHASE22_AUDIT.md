# Independent Phase22 audit: Gram gates and the real Fourier residual

9 October 2026. This audit covers the new `fft_green_phase22_gram_gate` checkpoint only. The embedded Phase21 archive was hashed but not re-audited. No uploaded program, test, binary, bytecode, or lineage runner was executed or imported. The accompanying verification programs were independently authored.

## Executive result

The main mathematical statements are correct in their stated restricted settings:

- the connected-Gram obstruction for one complex diagonal between real invertible mixers;
- the necessary bilinear Gram congruence, its permutation-invariant fingerprints, and its conditional exact mask recovery;
- the explicit real parity factorization, orthogonality, and restricted unitary-gate entropy bound;
- the sparse covariance identity and uniqueness from a complete seed column.

All **24 supplied fixed negative pairs** and **12 supplied positive recovery controls** were independently reconstructed, including every reported zero-diagonal count. Exact modular parity, orthogonality, and every covariance matrix entry were checked at `N=4,8,16,32,64,128`. Separate floating-point controls cover 16 even lengths, including non-powers of two.

There is one concrete implementation-cost correction: `apply_R` recomputes individual root powers on every call. Its advertised linear additional arithmetic is not justified by that implementation; ordinary repeated-squaring arithmetic is `O(N log N)`. An advancing-power recurrence gives genuinely linear preparation and application. A small independently authored, tested reference is included.

The randomized oracle claim also needs an explicit contract: the candidate must be a **fixed linear map over the working field**, queried on correctly sized vectors with fresh independent uniform challenges. The archive already warns that fixed-seed trials are regression tests. This audit supplies exact false-accept controls for a known fixed seed and for a nonlinear oracle.

An immediate useful sharpening is available: a fixed real invertible left mixer admits this one-mask Fourier architecture **if and only if every one of its columns is a reflection eigenvector**. Thus disconnected Gram support is necessary but is not itself sufficient.

**No new exact arbitrary-input, all-output `o(N log N)` Fourier evaluator is established.** No unrestricted Fourier lower bound, practical FFT speedup, or integer-multiplication speedup follows.

## 1. Real one-mask obstruction: accepted and sharpened

Let `F[j,k]=exp(-2 pi i jk/N)/sqrt(N)`, `Je_k=e_(-k mod N)`, and `N>=4` be even. Then `conj(F)=JF`, `F^2=J`, and `F` is unitary and symmetric.

### 1.1 The source proof is correct

The real and imaginary parts have ranks `N/2+1` and `N/2-1`. Indeed,

`Re(F)=F(I+J)/2`, and `Im(F)=F(I-J)/(2i)`.

The two eigenspaces of J have precisely these dimensions. If `F=A Q P B` with A and B real invertible, taking real and imaginary parts shows that Q has exactly `N/2+1` nonzero real diagonal entries and `N/2-1` nonzero purely imaginary diagonal entries. No entry can have both parts nonzero, since the two support counts sum to N and Q is invertible.

Unitarity gives `Q* (A^T A) Q = P(BB^T)^-1 P^T`. This is real. Across the real/imaginary partition, its entries can be real only if the corresponding entries of `A^T A` vanish. Both parts of the partition are nonempty. The connected-Gram veto therefore holds.

For a single full cycle, `(I-rho P_cycle)^-1` has strictly positive entries when `0<rho<1`: its finite geometric formula has positive coefficients and denominator `1-rho^N>0`. Independent coordinate reorderings preserve positivity, as do sums with positive coefficients. A strictly positive matrix has a strictly positive column Gram matrix. The corollary is consequently valid for those positive sums. Arbitrary signed sums, complex gauges, path inverses with structural zeros, and additional complex stages are not automatically covered by this positivity argument. Singular proposed outer factors cannot realize invertible F anyway.

### 1.2 Exact classification for a fixed real left mixer

**Proposition.** For a real invertible A, there exist a complex invertible diagonal Q, a coordinate permutation P, and a real invertible B satisfying `F=A Q P B` if and only if

`J A = A E`

for a diagonal matrix E with entries in `{+1,-1}`. Equivalently, each column of A is either reflection-even or reflection-odd. The multiplicities are necessarily `N/2+1` and `N/2-1`.

**Necessity.** Conjugating the factorization and cancelling the invertible Fourier matrix gives

`J = A (conj(Q) Q^-1) A^-1`.

Thus `E=conj(Q)Q^-1=A^-1 J A` is diagonal. Since `J^2=I`, every diagonal entry of E is `+1` or `-1`. This yields `JA=AE`, with the indicated multiplicities by similarity. It also forces each entry of Q onto the real or imaginary axis.

**Sufficiency.** Set `q_i=1` on the +1 columns and `q_i=-i` on the -1 columns. Put `M=A^-1 F`. Then `conj(M)=E M`, so its +1 rows are real and its -1 rows purely imaginary. Consequently `Q^-1 M` is real. For any chosen permutation P, set `B=P^-1 Q^-1 M`; B is real invertible and the factorization follows.

This proposition is an elementary sharpening for this exact architecture, not a novelty claim or an algorithmic lower bound. It does not survive unrestricted extra complex factors. It explains why disconnected Gram support alone is weak: `A=I_N` has no off-diagonal Gram edges, but the column `e_1` is not an eigenvector of J for `N>=4`, so it still cannot be the left mixer in this architecture. The supplied H does satisfy the exact classification.

## 2. Bilinear Gram gate: accepted with its nondegeneracy conditions

Work over a field of characteristic different from 2, with a primitive Nth root and an invertible square root of N. The root assumption already forces the characteristic not to divide N. Normalization then gives `F^T F=J`. For invertible A and B,

`F=A Q P B  =>  C_G=P^T Q K Q P`,

where `K=A^T A` and `C_G=B^-T J B^-1`. Here `C_G` denotes the Gram target; it is unrelated to the real Fourier residual C in later sections.

The derivation uses transpose, **not conjugate transpose**. It is valid for the finite-field setting and for complex bilinear forms. It is not a positive-definite or Hermitian argument.

### Fingerprints and permutation recovery

When the diagonals of both forms are nonzero, `h_ij=K_ij^2/(K_ii K_jj)` is invariant under invertible diagonal congruence. A permutation reorders both its indices. Hence the multiset of row multisets must agree. Sorting finite-field representatives is just a canonical encoding of those multisets.

Unequal zero-diagonal counts are a valid veto over the working field. Equal positive counts of zero diagonal entries must remain inconclusive for this particular ratio routine. If all row fingerprints are distinct and their multisets match, the correspondence determines the only possible permutation. Checking the complete reordered invariant matrix, as the source does, is an additional valid necessary check.

Equal fingerprints are not a sufficient congruence test: squaring discards cycle-sign consistency. The independent checker includes nonsingular 3-by-3 forms differing in the sign of just one off-diagonal pair. They have matching unique fingerprints but admit no recovered diagonal congruence.

### Mask recovery

Assume additionally every entry of K is nonzero, and retain nonzero diagonals for the target. After applying the candidate permutation, define `lambda_ij=(Ctilde_G)_ij/K_ij`. The complete congruence holds exactly when the symmetric ratio matrix is of the form `q q^T`, with all q entries nonzero. Equivalently, the anchored rank-one identities hold and nonzero `lambda_00` has a square root in the working field. Set `q_0` to either root and `q_i=lambda_i0/q_0`; then verify the full equation.

Under these hypotheses, q is unique up to a global sign. Complete nonzero support matters: for sparse/disconnected supports, independent component signs or other degeneracies can survive. The source conservatively returns no reconstruction when K has a zero entry. An absent square root rejects reconstruction over the specified field, not necessarily over a field extension.

A Fourier-entry calibration distinguishes the two signs only for a candidate already known to be `F` or `-F`. It does **not** turn a Gram-congruent matrix into Fourier. The source explicitly supplies the correct warning and counterexample.

### Exact reconstructed controls

The independent code generates the published random parameter streams but forms each Green inverse using finite path products, not the uploaded recurrence. It verifies every entry of `(I-S)G=I`. It then performs independently implemented exact matrix arithmetic and checks invertibility of both sampled outer factors.

- All 24 fixed pairs at `N=8,16,32,64` are excluded. Three have unequal zero-diagonal counts; the other 21 fail the row-fingerprint multiset.
- All 12 deliberate positive controls recover the prescribed permutation and the mask up to its unavoidable sign. Every reconstructed Fourier entry is checked.
- All 36 records and their zero-diagonal counts match the uploaded `audit.json` exactly.
- The `U=S F` sign-twist control preserves `U^T U=J`, the calibrated entry, and the first Weyl identity, but fails the second at all four tested sizes.

The 24 vetoes concern fixed finite-field parameters; they are not an all-parameters Green impossibility theorem. A characteristic-zero transfer would require a specified algebraic lift and suitable nonvanishing denominators. It should not be inferred merely from a modular rejection. The dense positive-control B factors supply no efficient evaluator.

## 3. Parity factorization and entropy: accepted

For `h=N/2`, let H have columns `e_0,e_h`, then the normalized symmetric reflection pairs, then the normalized antisymmetric pairs. Pairwise sums and differences prove that H is real orthogonal and both H and its transpose apply with linear arithmetic.

The symmetric row combinations of F are real cosine rows. The antisymmetric combinations are `-i` times real sine rows. Hence, with `Q=diag(1^(h+1),(-i)^(h-1))`,

`F=H Q C`,

where C has exactly the real cosine/sine entries stated in the archive. Since H, Q, and F are unitary and C is real, C is real orthogonal. This proof works for every even `N>=4`, not just the dyadic sizes supported by the finite-field implementation.

All entries satisfy `|C_jk|^2<=2/N`. A probability distribution with maximum mass at most `2/N` has entropy at least `log_2(N/2)`. Therefore the source lower bound `Phi(C)>=N(log_2 N-1)` is correct. The first two rows are uniform, so one can harmlessly sharpen this to `Phi(C)>=N(log_2 N-1)+2`.

The circuit statement assumes a product of two-coordinate unitary gates starting from the identity matrix. For two affected rows, each column's total probability mass is unchanged. The entropy contributed by dividing a mass s between two entries changes by at most s; summing over columns gives at most 2 bits because the two rows each have norm one. Thus the source gate-count bound follows from the zero entropy of the identity. This is an orthogonal/unitary gate-model lower bound, not a bound for arbitrary nonunitary Green computation, unbounded coefficients, or general shared arithmetic graphs.

The real/complex proof and entropy argument are distinct from the modular controls. In `F_998244353`, an element representing i is already in the prime field; labels such as “real C” describe algebraic analogues and do not provide an ordering, positivity, absolute value, or probability entropy in that field.

Exact modular factorization and orthogonality pass at all six supplied sizes through 128. The real numerical controls include `N=6,10,12,14,18,20,24,30,42` as well as dyadic lengths, with errors recorded separately in `CHECK_RESULTS.json`. Floating checks support implementations only; they do not prove the identities.

## 4. Sparse covariance and oracle certification

Advancing the input column index by one adds `theta_j` to the cosine/sine argument. The addition formulas therefore give the stated rotation blocks and

`C Z = R C`.

The signs are correct for the source's negative-exponent Fourier convention and positive sine rows: each block is `[[cos,-sin],[sin,cos]]`. The endpoints act by +1 and -1. There are only O(N) nonzero entries, though paired coordinates are not contiguous in the chosen ordering.

For a **matrix** X, the uniqueness argument is exact and needs no orthogonality assumption: `X e_k=X Z^k e_0=R^k v`. Thus `XZ=RX` plus `Xe_0=v` fixes every column. Generating those columns separately still requires quadratic work; this is a correctness constraint, not a fast all-output evaluator.

### Precise randomized guarantee

For a fixed linear candidate M over `F_p`, write `D=MZ-RM`. If the seed test passes but `M!=C`, uniqueness implies `D!=0`. For one uniformly random independent vector v,

`Pr[Dv=0]=p^(-rank(D))<=1/p`.

For t independent trials the false-accept probability is `p^(-t rank(D))<=p^-t`. The exact seed call plus two oracle executions per challenge accounts for `1+2t` calls. Extra work is `O(tN)` when R coefficients are prepared correctly; calling it O(N) presumes fixed t. A candidate must be fixed independently of those challenges. The API should document linearity, determinism, field and vector dimensions, and should reject malformed output lengths rather than relying on truncating `zip` comparisons.

### Two explicit limitations

1. **Known fixed seed.** At `N=8`, with the published default seed 22 and two trials, the checker constructs a nonzero row b annihilating `e_0`, both challenge vectors, and their shifts. Adding the rank-one perturbation `e_0 b^T` to C changes the matrix but leaves every tested oracle answer unchanged. This wrong linear candidate passes the fixed-seed sample and has a nonzero covariance defect. This confirms the archive's existing regression-only warning; it does not contradict the fresh-randomness guarantee.
2. **Nonlinear oracle.** `T(x)=(sum_i x_i) Cx` satisfies `T(Zx)=R T(x)` for every input and has `T(e_0)=v`, yet is not the linear transform C. The matrix uniqueness theorem is correct, but it cannot certify an arbitrary nonlinear black box without a linearity premise.

## 5. Implementation and documentation repairs

### A. Fix or qualify `apply_R`'s cost

At `phase22_parity.py:95-97`, each j separately computes powers of omega and its inverse. `apply_R` calls this builder again at lines 100-109 on every application. For varying N, ordinary modular exponentiation performs logarithmic work per exponent, so this path is `O(N log N)` field multiplication work, with additional inversion/setup costs. Treating a fixed finite modulus and bounded supported N as constants does not establish a meaningful uniform asymptotic claim.

Prepare the coefficients once by advancing `u_(j+1)=omega u_j` and `v_(j+1)=omega^-1 v_j`, then form cosine/sine coefficients with fixed scalars. This takes O(N) field operations after root/field setup and O(N) storage. Each application then takes O(N). The included `linear_rotation_reference.py` implements this repair independently and is checked against every column of the exact C matrices through 128. No original source or master project file was modified.

### B. State the oracle contract and probability bound

Use the hypotheses and bound in Section 4. Keep deterministic seeded tests clearly labelled as regression controls. Validate lengths and allowed domains if the routine becomes a general external checker. The current source does not promise deterministic equality certification from sampled success, so this is a contract clarification rather than a refutation of its matrix theorem.

### C. Correct the dense diagnostic call-count wording

`audit.py:36` and the copied `audit.json` say reconstruction costs “O(N^2) oracle operations.” Reconstructing a matrix takes N basis-vector oracle calls; for a linear-work oracle this is O(N^2) scalar arithmetic. Dense matrix products/inverses then incur their separate cubic cost in this implementation. The distinction matters when counting a candidate's fresh-input work.
