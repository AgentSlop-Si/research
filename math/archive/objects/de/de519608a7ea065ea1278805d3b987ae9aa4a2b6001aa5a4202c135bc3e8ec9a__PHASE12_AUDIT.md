## 2. Accepted theorem and exact hypotheses

Let F be the unnormalized complex Fourier matrix. Suppose

    F = H_L ... H_1,    H_i = G_i + R_i,

with each H_i and G_i square and invertible, rank(R_i) ≤ r_i, and maximum column support of G_i⁻¹ at most s_i. Put r=Σr_i, s=Πs_i. Then

    s(r+1)² ≥ N,

and for prime length N=p,

    (s+1)(r+1) ≥ p+1.

The mechanism survives noncommuting factors, arbitrary reorderings of each allowed cycle, and independently supplied nonzero monomial gauges. It is column support, not total nonzeros or rank, that is multiplied.

### Independent proof

For each stage,

    H_i⁻¹ − G_i⁻¹ = −H_i⁻¹ R_i G_i⁻¹.

Its rank is exactly rank(R_i), because both outside factors are invertible. Telescoping the ordered inverse products gives rank(F⁻¹−B) ≤ r for B=G_1⁻¹...G_L⁻¹. A column of a product AB lies in the union of at most colsp(B) columns of A, hence colsp(B) ≤ s.

If r<N, restrict the rank-r difference to any r+1 columns and take a nonzero nullvector x there. Write k=|supp(x)|≤r+1. Since F⁻¹x=Bx, its support is at most sk. The complex Fourier support-product inequality gives N≤k|supp(F⁻¹x)|≤sk². At prime length the additive inequality gives p+1≤k+|supp(F⁻¹x)|≤(s+1)k. If r≥N, both advertised inequalities are automatic; the r+1-column choice is unnecessary. This fills that minor proof boundary explicitly.

The support-product inequality itself follows from the unitary normalization U=F/√N: every output has magnitude at most √(k/N)||x||₂, and Parseval applied to its nonzero support completes the proof. At prime length, the full-spark Fourier-minor theorem yields the additive inequality. The primary proof and theorem are in [Tao, Theorem 1.1 and Lemma 1.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0308286). The theorem and relevant proof were inspected, not inferred from finite tests.

### Consequences and limits

A one-pole shift inverse has a binomial inverse and s_i≤2, even for a long stride or a monomially routed/gauged cycle. Thus s≤2^L. For fixed correction rank per layer r_i≤r₀, the all-length gate forces L≥log₂N−2log₂log₂N−O(1); the prime bound similarly gives log₂N−log₂log₂N−O(1). These only become Ω(N log N) work bounds under the expressly stipulated sequential executor that actually materializes and charges Θ(N) work at every layer.

Rank-r corrections need not cost rN. Dense matrices, full rank, and full commutator rank do not establish computational hardness. Branch sums, general shared evaluators, and high-support inverses are outside the theorem. Conversely, merely making an N×N factor singular cannot escape an exact square-product representation of F: an invertible square product forces every square factor invertible. A genuine noninvertible escape must change the network representation, for example by using rectangular or branching operations.

The theorem assumes that the low-rank and sparse-inverse representations actually exist. It gives no free algorithm for discovering or certifying those representations. Gauges and their inverses require explicit nonzero entries and paid access; dense matrix synthesis or random parameter search is not part of a fast input evaluator.

## 3. Fields, roots, and uncertainty controls

The universal inequalities in the source are explicitly over C. The additive prime-length statement cannot be transferred automatically to arbitrary positive-characteristic splitting fields.

Our fresh exhaustive checks verify all 19, 251, and 3,431 minors for lengths 3, 5, and 7 over GF(7), GF(11), and GF(29), respectively. These field-specific checks support the source's stated finite controls. We also independently enumerate its 25,006 nonzero one- and two-sparse vectors for lengths 5, 7, and 8.

A negative field control makes the boundary tangible. In GF(8)=GF(2)[u]/(u³+u+1), u is a primitive seventh root. The length-7 signal with polynomial 1+z+z³ has input support 3 and Fourier support 4. Their sum is 7, below the complex prime bound 8. This is a genuine splitting-field counterexample to dropping the characteristic/full-spark restriction. At composite length 8, the signal e₀+e₄ over GF(17) has Fourier support 4: the product bound is sharp, while a prime-style additive bound would be false.

The finite-field APIs assume a prime modulus; they do not establish primality. Roots of unity, prime-modulus validity, characteristic not dividing N, and actual root order must be supplied or checked with charged setup. `primitive_root_of_order` searches candidate bases and trial-factors N; no uniform cheap candidate-search guarantee is supplied. This is harmless for bounded test fixture generation, but cannot be hidden in a claimed cold linear algorithm. Exact modular arithmetic also does not establish bit complexity, complex root acquisition, or numerical stability.

## 4. Noncommuting Green pair and chirp inverse

For N>1, primitive ω, S e_j=e_(j+1), and T e_j=ω^j e_j, TS=ωST. If αβ≠0 and A=I−αS and B=I−βT are invertible, then

    [A⁻¹,B⁻¹] = A⁻¹ B⁻¹ [A,B] B⁻¹ A⁻¹,
    [A,B] = αβ(1−ω)ST.

The commutator therefore has full rank N. Meanwhile (A⁻¹B⁻¹)⁻¹=BA has column support at most 2, because B is diagonal. The sparse/low-rank gate applies to any added correction producing F. The source's full-rank statement needs the otherwise implicit guard **N>1**: at N=1, ω=1 and the commutator is zero. Zero α or β likewise invalidates its full-rank conclusion. These are hypothesis boundaries, not Fourier counterexamples.

The claimed odd-length chirp identity is correct. With h=2⁻¹ modulo N, D_jj=ω^(hj²), C_ij=ω^(−h(i−j)²), one has F=DCD. An especially direct proof that C⁻¹ is entrywise nonzero is

    C⁻¹ = D F⁻¹ D,

since every entry of F⁻¹ is a nonzero root divided by N. This avoids any need to charge a Gauss-sum evaluation or prove its sign. It also works over any valid splitting field with N invertible. A product of L cyclic binomial inverses has inverse column support at most 2^L, so representing this C in that class requires 2^L≥N. This remains a factorization restriction, not a general convolution cost lower bound.

Ten independent full-rank commutator checks cover N=2 through 16, including odd and composite lengths. Fresh odd-chirp matrix/inverse checks include composite N=15. These checks support the algebra; they do not supply a numerical conditioning claim.

## 5. Full-input Green-orbit fusion

Assume positive K|N, primitive ω_N, ζ=ω^(N/K), and U=diag(ζ^j). If α^N≠1, then

    (1/K) Σ_(j=0)^(K−1) U^j (I−αS)⁻¹ U^(−j)
      = (I−α^K S^K)⁻¹.

Expand the left inverses as finite geometric sums. The root average kills all exponents not divisible by K. The surviving terms are exactly the inverse on K disjoint stride-K cycles of length N/K. All denominators equal 1−α^N. The identity includes K=1, K=N, and α=0; the source executor also happens to support the K=N stride despite its narrower stride docstring. The characteristic must not divide K, automatically true in the intended primitive-N-root field.

This is a universal-input identity for a different operator, with a real reduction from K literal full Green passes to one full stride solve. Computing α^K and the shared denominator takes O(log N) scalar multiplications by binary powering, one field inversion, and O(N) recurrence arithmetic/output work. In a model with expensive modular inversion, its implementation cost is charged separately. No N-entry Fourier spectrum is returned.

For a general stride t, let d=gcd(N,t), m=N/d. Invertibility is exactly α^m≠1, not merely α^N≠1. Our controls use every stride in a family of lengths, accepting valid noncoprime cases and rejecting all 82 selected resonances. We verify 328 entire operators by every standard basis vector against independently inverted matrices. The 111 orbit matrix identities additionally compare conjugation by U directly with the scaled-pole Green matrices, preventing an unnoticed conjugation-sign error.

At N=65,536 over GF(786433), with ω=108788, α=2, K=16, fresh deterministic dense input passes every equality between the 16-path average and the fused stride solve. This is an independently authored experiment, not a rerun of `large_green_audit.py`.

## 6. Actual implementation cost correction

The source's shift and stride recurrences are linear after shared denominator preparation. `batch_inverse` is a valid one-inversion/O(N)-multiplication routine with explicit nonzero-input checks. Its dense matrix routines and random low-rank construction are audit tools, not putative production FFTs.

However, the modulation evaluator constructs

    values[j] = 1 − beta * pow(omega,j,q)

for every j independently. Modular exponentiation is not a single field operation. Under a standard binary ladder initialized at its leading bit, the count for all j<N=2^n is

    Σ_(j=1)^(N−1) (bit_length(j)−1 + popcount(j)−1)
      = (3n/2−3)N + 3.

At N=65,536 this is 1,376,259 root-power multiplications. Different powering implementations change constants; they do not justify calling a nontrivial variable-exponent primitive free. The source additionally repeats root finding inside `modulation_green_apply`. Its large test's numerical success and timing do not establish a fully charged linear execution schedule.

**Proposed repair:** accept a validated primitive root as an explicit setup input; build all powers by ω^(j+1)=ω·ω^j; form the denominators once; batch-invert them; multiply the fresh input. A straightforward implementation then costs (N−1)+N+N+2N+N = **6N−1 field multiplications**, N subtractions, one field inversion, and O(N) storage, before any root-order validation or inversion implementation cost. The fresh checker executes this repaired schedule and verifies all 131,072 residual equations for the two-stage Green pair at N=65,536. Four independently calculated DFT bins disagree, correctly rejecting Fourier equivalence.

The source has no operation-counter ledger implementing its claimed full charge accounting. In addition to scalar arithmetic, a real model must charge root search/order tests, indexing, input/output, list allocation, and whatever field inversion/bit precision model is chosen. No source timing or `AUDIT_RESULTS.json` runtime is adopted as a speed benchmark here.
