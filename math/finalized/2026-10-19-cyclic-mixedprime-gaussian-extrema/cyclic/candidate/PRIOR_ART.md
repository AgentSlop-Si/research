## Primary sources inspected

1. Gianna M. Del Corso, Federico Poloni, Leonardo Robol, Raf Vandebril, “When is a matrix unitary or Hermitian plus low rank?”, arXiv:1811.05854, version 3. Author-deposited full text: https://arxiv.org/html/1811.05854 . It characterizes Hermitian/unitary-plus-low-rank matrices and distances in spectral and Frobenius norms. Those approximation classes differ from an unrestricted real core with entrywise imaginary part, complex correction rank, and Fourier row/column phases. Its results are related background; they are not cited as proving this Fourier minimax family.

2. Di Zhao, Axel Ringh, Li Qiu, Sei Zhen Khong, “Low Phase-Rank Approximation”, arXiv:2011.14811 and Linear Algebra and its Applications 639 (2022), 177–204. Author-deposited abstract: https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.14811 ; publisher abstract: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0024379522000040 . Its phase-rank counts canonical sectorial phases, with geometric-mean or geodesic objectives. This is a different notion from the complex correction rank used here. Only the abstract and publisher description were reviewed; no claim is made about every theorem in the paper.

3. Wojciech Tadej, “Defect and equivalence of unitary matrices. The Fourier case”, arXiv:1310.8119v2. Author-deposited abstract: https://arxiv.org/abs/1310.8119 . Its defect is a first-order deformation-space dimension preserving unitarity and entry moduli, and it studies Fourier enphasing/permutation equivalence. This differs from the present external operator-norm real-core approximation objective. Only the abstract was reviewed, so this is a terminology/scope distinction, not a comprehensive comparison of its 92 pages.

4. G. H. Golub, Alan Hoffman, G. W. Stewart, “A generalization of the Eckart-Young-Mirsky matrix approximation theorem”, Linear Algebra and its Applications 88–89 (1987), 317–327. Publisher page: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0024379587901145 . The paper treats lower-rank approximation with selected columns fixed. It locates the standard approximation background; the present fixed-phase operator-norm identity is proved directly and does not rely on its constrained result.

The DOI landing page for Eckart and Young's 1936 original, https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02288367 , was unavailable through the browsing tool. It was not treated as inspected primary text. Unrelated search hits, including an accidentally opened arXiv identifier 2201.02000, were excluded from the mathematical evidence.
