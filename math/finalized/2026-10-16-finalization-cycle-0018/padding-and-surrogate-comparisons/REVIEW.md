## Primary-source and mathematical checks

I independently inspected [NSW v1](https://arxiv.org/html/1909.11123v1), Theorem 1.1, §1.5, Appendix A, Algorithm 3 and Theorem A.16. Its algorithm uses supplied spectral-tail noise information and a polynomial SNR bound. The final report correctly discloses that A.16 prints a different noise-scale normalization from the algorithm. The reported `O(N log^3 N log k)` ideal-arithmetic time is not promoted to a deterministic capped random-bit executor. Its polynomial confidence is not equated to every requested failure budget. The comparison is expressly conditional, rather than a repair or complete reproof of the paper.

Independent checks of the report's deductions:

1. For ALG-024, the rough-number argument keeps all prime divisors of Q at least M. Thus the product bound on the totient gives `phi(Q)/Q >= 1-log(Q)/(M log(M))`. The consecutive candidate search is the paid procedure; an inclusion-exclusion table is not built. The covariance result does not itself compute a transform.
2. For fixed `0<alpha<1/2` and sufficiently large N, `k=ceil(N^(2alpha))` lies in the stated non-dense range. Since the best spectral tail has norm at most `||x||_2`, division by `sqrt(k)` is at most `N^-alpha ||x||_2`. Substitution into the cited costs gives the displayed sample order and `O(N log^4 N)` bound. These are different complete contracts, so comparing upper bounds proves no universal speed ordering.
3. An all-bin error bound `C N^-alpha ||x||_2` implies only the displayed relative-l2 upper bound with an additional `sqrt(N)` factor. The impulse/zero-output example correctly demonstrates that the weak guarantee can allow relative-l2 error one; it does not claim an algorithm actually returns zero.
4. For ALG-027, `tau=||x||_1/(sqrt(N)||x||_2)` belongs to `[N^-1/2,1]` for nonzero x. The sufficient total-energy calibration is `k>=1/(epsilon^2 tau^2)`. Dropping tau would silently weaken the target. When the coarse requirement reaches N, the argument supplies no transfer and asserts no impossibility.
5. Input-dependent sampling probabilities are chosen before fresh draws for each fixed input. They do not define one fixed map that succeeds simultaneously for all inputs. Exact heads, pre-RNG bypasses, coalesced draw costs, root preparation and all N outputs remain charged. Numerical minimization of the implemented objective is kept separate from a certified probability or floating-point theorem.
6. Time-domain surrogate support and frequency-domain recovery support are distinct. The existing Parseval and [Alon](https://web.math.princeton.edu/~nalon/PDFS/identity1.pdf) restrictions therefore cannot become lower bounds on arbitrary sparse-recovery runtime. I checked the stated realification dimension, rank factor and epsilon range. [Kapralov's source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1604.00845) preserves its different tail-norm and access assumptions. [FFTW's discussion](https://fftw.org/pruned.html) supports the classical arithmetic/performance distinction, not production acceptance of this implementation.
