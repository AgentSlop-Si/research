## 1. Scope and retained dependencies

The objective is precisely the sum of r(a+b) over nonempty Cartesian tiles partitioning all N² entries, where r is actual complex rank. Row and column subsets may be arbitrary. Entry-disjoint coverage, rather than overlap, sharing, or cancellation, is essential to the final summation.

The retained accepted rank-two review provides the sharp rank-two density E₂(N)/N, where E₂(N)=min_(d|N,d<N)(2d+N/d). The retained rank-one classification and stable-rank inequality are used only as stated. The prime-cube accepted result contributes its universal prime-power-minor lemma and the previously settled length 1331; its residue-occupancy theorem is not silently extended to pq². The dominant-prime result contributes only to the domain census.

For completeness, stable rank is immediate from the unnormalized Fourier norm √N: the Frobenius norm squared of a rectangle is ab, its operator norm is at most √N, and thus ab≤Nr. The rank-one structure follows from N dividing every product of a row difference and a column difference. If H is generated by the row differences, the rows lie in one H-coset and the columns in one H-perpendicular coset. Their containing sizes multiply to N. Equality in the reciprocal size bound forces full cosets and a minimizing divisor pair. Nothing in the new proof changes these dependencies.

## 2. Prime-power minors and short-support zeros

The universal-minor lemma is correct in the stated range k≤ℓ. Choose nonnegative representatives for the k row exponents, reorder them if necessary, and divide the alternant by the ordinary Vandermonde. The quotient is an integer polynomial. Its value at all ones is the displayed quotient of difference products, up to sign. Every factor in the numerator and denominator is an ℓ-adic unit, so this integer is nonzero modulo ℓ.

Reduction modulo 1−ζ_(ℓ^m) sends ζ to one and has residue field F_ℓ. Therefore the quotient evaluated at the selected roots is nonzero. Distinct columns give a nonzero Vandermonde factor. Transposition permits using either side as the residue-distinct side. At m=1 this proves every square prime Fourier minor needed below.

The short-support proof at pq uses a valid CRT decomposition of the characters. CRT units merely permute frequencies. For a combination with k′≤k≤p nonzero terms, group by the q-frequency v, with t groups of sizes k_v. Each g_v is nonzero and has at most k_v−1 zeros over the p-point coordinate. Thus the number z₀ of points where all groups vanish satisfies

    z₀≤min_v(k_v−1)≤k′−t.

At any other p-coordinate, at most t q-characters are active, and prime nonvanishing minors limit their zeros to t−1. Consequently

    Z≤p(t−1)+(q−t+1)z₀
     ≤p(t−1)+q(k′−t)
     ≤q(k′−1)≤q(k−1).

All signs are correct: t≤k′≤p<q, the coefficient of z₀ is positive, and k′−t is nonnegative. A dependence among k selected columns is a nonzero combination with at most k terms, so more than (k−1)q selected rows rules it out. Zero coefficients in a dependence cause no problem because the proof explicitly permits k′<k.

This is a complete proof of exactly the short-support statement used in the theorem. Acceptance does not depend on an imported general uncertainty theorem or any finite enumeration.

## 3. Direct-sum separation

For r=y+qk and c=v+pq w, the dependence on the lift w is exactly ζ_q^(yw), independently of k. A fiber with s selected lifts therefore supplies an s-by-s prime Fourier mixing matrix on the s occurring row residues y. That matrix is invertible.

Inverting it produces, inside the original column span, the representative column restricted to each individual row class and extended by zero on the other classes. The representative v need not itself be a selected column; its restricted vector is still obtained by this inversion. Repeating this for each rich fiber yields the supported block spaces asserted in the report.

Within row class y, after removing the nonzero scalar ζ_N^(yv) from each representative column, the resulting entries are (ζ_N^q)^(kv), namely F_(pq)[A_y,T]. The removed scalar may depend on y and v, which is harmless because each block rank is taken separately. Supports of different y-blocks are disjoint, so the dimensions add. Hence

    rank F_N[R,C] ≥ Σ_y rank F_(pq)[A_y,T].

This is an internal linear-algebra argument on one rectangle, not an operation that shares work across different tiles.

## 4. All residue cases of the dichotomy

Assume a>4q and b>B, with B=max(6p,3p+2q−2,3q). No ordering a≤b is required.

First, each side occupies at most three residues modulo q. If the columns occupied four residues, a>4q>3p would put four rows in one residue class modulo p. After removing row/column phases, these rows and the four columns yield a q²-order Fourier minor. The row indices are distinct modulo q² and the column indices are distinct modulo q, so the transposed universal-minor lemma applies with k=4. This contradicts rank≤3. Transposition proves the row-residue bound because b>B≥6p>3p. In particular, no mixed-prime full-spark assertion is used here.

The row-residue count s is therefore one, two, or three.

### One row residue

The restriction becomes an order-pq Fourier matrix with possible repeated columns. Each column fiber modulo pq has at most q elements. Since b>3q, there are at least four distinct fibers. Since the row set has a>4q>3q elements, the k=4 short-support result gives rank at least four. Its hypothesis k≤p holds because p≥5.

### Two row residues

There are at most 3p column fibers modulo pq: at most three residues modulo q, each admitting p refinements modulo pq. If at most two fibers have two or more columns, the total number of columns is at most

    2q+(3p−2)=3p+2q−2.

This remains a valid upper bound if fewer than 3p fibers are occupied. Hence b>B forces three rich fibers. One row class has more than 2q elements, so the three representative columns on that class have rank three by the k=3 short-support result. The other nonempty class has rank at least one because all Fourier entries are nonzero. Direct-sum separation makes the total rank at least four.

### Three row residues

If all column fibers had size at most two, b≤6p. Thus a fiber contains three columns. Their prime mixing matrix has rank three on the three nonempty row classes, so they form a basis of the entire column space under the rank-at-most-three assumption.

For any two rows in one q-residue class, their entries on these three basis columns are proportional by the same nonzero row factor. Every other column is a linear combination of those basis columns, so the same row proportionality holds on the whole selected column set. This implication is justified by a spanning basis; it is not the false general claim that every rank-three unit-modulus matrix has three row directions.

Fix c₀ in C, let H be generated by c−c₀, and let K=H-perpendicular, of order d. Fourier rows restricted to C are projectively equivalent exactly when their difference belongs to K. Therefore the at-most-three projective row classes lie in at most three K-cosets, while C lies in one H-coset:

    a≤3d,    b≤N/d,    d|pq².

Now a>4q implies d>4q/3>q. Since p<q, the only divisors of pq² above q are pq, q², and pq². In every case N/d≤q, contradicting b>B≥3q. This completes the third case.

Thus a>4q implies b≤B. Applying the same proven assertion after transposition yields the stated min/max dichotomy. When B<4q, the second branch cannot occur with both sides above 4q, exactly as the report says.

## 5. Family constants, equality, and penalty

For N=11q² and prime q≥13, the divisor pair closest to √N is uniquely {q,11q}, so D_N=12q. For rank three, order the sides a≤b. Stable rank gives ab≤3N.

If a≤4q, then

    3/a+3/b ≥ 3/a+a/N ≥ 3/(4q)+4q/N = 49/(44q).

The second inequality follows from 16q²<33q²=3N. Equivalently, the report's exact factorization has two nonnegative factors. It is applied only on the required interval.

In the other dichotomy branch, both sides are at most B=max(66,31+2q,3q), so the density is at least 6/B. Checking each linear term shows 49B≤264q for q≥13. The worst endpoint for each nontrivial inequality is q=13, where both hold. Therefore 6/B≥49/(44q).

For rank two, the retained proper-divisor formula gives E₂=13q. The divisor q attains it, and the other proper divisors give larger values for q≥13. For rank r≥4, stable rank and AM–GM give density at least 4/(√11 q). Both bounds strictly exceed 49/(44q); the irrational comparison is valid after squaring positive quantities and reduces to 30976>26411.

Rank-one density is at least D_N/N=12/(11q)=48/(44q). Thus every higher-rank tile has excess density at least 1/(44q). Summing over positive tile areas, whose sum is N², gives exactly the accepted penalty bound.

The standard full subgroup/annihilator coset partition attains N D_N=132q³. Strict higher-rank slack excludes all rank-at-least-two tiles from every optimum. Equality in the rank-one bound forces full cosets and the unique unordered divisor pair {q,11q}. Each such tile has area N, so exactly N tiles are necessary. The characterization is tilewise and does not force a common orientation across the whole tiling.

At q=13, N=1859, D=156, and N D=290004. The rank-three bound is 49/572, the rank-one baseline is 48/572, and their difference is 1/572. These constants are checked exactly.
