PRIMARY-SOURCE DIGEST
Kaltofen–Yang, “On Exact and Approximate Interpolation of Sparse Rational Functions,” ISSAC 2007, pp. 203–210: https://kaltofen.math.ncsu.edu/bibliography/07/KaYa07.pdf

Lemma 2.1 (p.204) is deterministic. For coprime F,G over K, degree bounds dbar,ebar and deg(H)<m=dbar+ebar+1, assume F=GH modulo product_i(X−i_i). Repeated i_i are allowed in this congruence statement. Extended Euclid gives the bounded numerator/denominator pair, recovering F,G up to common scaling when their bounds hold.

Lemma 2.2 (pp.204–205) assumes coprime multivariate f,g, degrees d,e, shifts B preserving the denominator leading coefficient, and J distinct exponent vectors in {1,...,t}^n, first vector all ones. Uniform independent coordinates from finite S give simultaneous coprime line images with probability at least 1−2((J−1)t+1)de/|S|.

The known-degree evaluation algorithm (pp.205–206) uses d+e+1 distinct finite black-box values, at most d+2e+1 trials under degree preservation, interpolation and Euclid. Unknown degrees add randomized validation. Section 3 (pp.206–207) combines randomized shifts/anchors, simultaneous sparse polynomial interpolation and early termination; once one polynomial is complete, one rational evaluation can supply the other. This is chosen-point oracle recovery, not a fixed-data acceptance theorem.

ALG-009 INPUT, FORMULA, GUARDS AND OUTPUT
Let R_k=sum_j r_j omega^(kj), S r_j=r_(j−1 mod N), and h=(I−alpha S^delta)r. The conditional family has support(h)<=2, unknown nonzero alpha and 1<=delta<N. The owner implementation requires N>=8, prime p>55 and a supplied primitive Nth root omega. General exact-field reasoning and literal backend admissibility must not be conflated.

Eight fixed consecutive Fourier probes R_0,...,R_7 are computed from the raw vector. Put w=omega^delta and Y_k=R_k(1−alpha w^k). The two 3x3 Hankel determinants P_0,P_1 are cubic in alpha with w-degrees at most 6 and 9. Their padded six-by-six Sylvester resultant T(w) has degree at most 3*6+3*9=45. Degree drops may introduce spurious candidate roots. Therefore T nonzero, a degree-one specialized gcd, later Hankel filters, and full raw defect certification have distinct roles. A zero resultant or nonlinear gcd is gate decline; it is not evidence that no two-defect description exists.

The full defect certificate computes every coordinate of h for any accepted candidate and checks support<=2. An eight-probe fit alone does not establish membership for arbitrary raw input. The finite-subgroup uniqueness lemma concerns TWO EXISTING sparse-rational descriptions, nonzero sampled denominators and N>=2Ks; K=s=2 gives eight samples. It proves equality only at subgroup points where both fractions are defined, not formal rational-function equality or a unique physical factorization. It cannot be applied across sampled poles.

EXACT INTERFACE COMPARISON: OUR DERIVATION
Write R(z)=sum_(j=0)^(N−1) r_j z^j, H(z)=sum_j h_j z^j and Q(z)=1−alpha z^delta. Cyclic shifting gives
    Q(z)R(z) = H(z) modulo z^N−1.
Thus R(omega^k)=H(omega^k)/Q(omega^k) only when Q(omega^k) is nonzero. The raw polynomial R(z) can be evaluated elsewhere, but those values need not equal H(z)/Q(z). This precise missing oracle is the main reason the named prior theorem does not immediately discharge the owner claim. Merely renaming Fourier samples as rational-function evaluations does not construct that oracle. Reconstructing all of R instead generally changes the target to a degree-(N−1) polynomial.

Sparsity and degree are separate. H can have two terms while deg(H)=N−1; Q has two terms while deg(Q)=delta. Substituting term counts for d,e is invalid. A promised sparse-rational description and recoverable degrees also do not certify that an arbitrary input vector has that description. The missing reduction would have to show where every requested value comes from, how failures are detected, how original raw values are preserved and what all those steps cost.

POLES ARE PART OF THE RAW OUTPUT
Let g=gcd(N,delta). The map k -> omega^(k delta) has N/g distinct values, each repeated g times. Therefore the number of zero denominators is g if alpha^(N/g)=1 and zero otherwise. At each zero, the cyclic relation says H(omega^k)=0 and places no restriction on R_k. The kernel of I−alpha S^delta supplies exactly those free Fourier coordinates. Reducing common factors of H and Q can erase that information; it cannot recover the original raw vector's missing transform coordinates. This is a mathematical interface obstruction, independent of any code bug.

The accepted branch handles at most one resonant frequency by a separately charged full direct Fourier sum. Genuine noncoprime resonance requires g repaired coordinates and is outside that bounded branch. Any extension using direct sums costs O(gN), rather than silently inheriting O(N) when g varies. Freshly authored independent interface controls accompany the review; they distinguish a previously accepted nondegenerate witness from a separately labeled general two-defect pole example. They are explanatory finite checks, not a new correctness theorem or priority result.
