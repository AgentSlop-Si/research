## Section 7: necessary full-bank block-affine routing

Let B=I_q tensor F_m, where F_m is the positive unnormalized Fourier matrix over a field with a supplied primitive mth root, and char(K) does not divide m. Suppose T and A=B T B^-1 are monomial.

Write T_bc for an m-by-m block. It is partial monomial. If it is nonzero, every row of F_m T_bc is nonzero, because each occupied column has exactly one nonzero contribution. Right multiplication by invertible F_m^-1 preserves each nonzero row. Likewise, every column of T_bc F_m^-1 is nonzero, and left multiplication by F_m preserves it. Thus A_bc has no zero row or column. Since it lies inside a globally monomial A, it has exactly one nonzero per row and column and rank m. Consequently T_bc also has rank m and, being partial monomial, is a full monomial block. Global monomiality permits exactly one such block in each block row/column.

For a full block U, let U e_j=c_j e_pi(j) and F_m U F_m^-1 e_k=d_k e_tau(k). The relation F_m U=V F_m gives, at row tau(k),

c_j zeta^(tau(k) pi(j)) = d_k zeta^(k j).

Divide by the j=0 and k=0 instances. Since zeta is primitive,

(tau(k)-tau(0))(pi(j)-pi(0)) = k j mod m.

For m>1, k=j=1 makes the two differences units. Taking k=1 proves pi(j)=a j+b mod m with a a unit. The k=0 equation gives c_j/c_0=zeta^(-tau(0) a j), a character. The m=1 case is trivial and does not use an index 1. This proves exactly the necessary Section 7 claim, including composite m. It does not imply that arbitrary proposed exteriors realize these actions or are inexpensive.

## Section 8: mixed-radix identity and corrected interface orientation

For N=a m b, use the original source equations (5)–(6), with j=r0+a r1+a m r2 and k=t2+b t1+b m t0. The sole path through the fixed bank for each j,k has phase exponent

r0 k + a r1 t2 + a b r1 t1 + a m r2 t2.

Subtract this from j k. The remainder is a multiple of N, proving F_N=P(I_ab tensor F_m)Q for every a,m,b under the root/characteristic guard. P has exactly a nonzeros per row/column; Q has exactly b. Thus the support product a m b=N is sharp. This is a classical mixed-radix Fourier identity, not a growing-radix saving.

### Required inherited orientation clarification

The source sentence about both Weyl actions must be read as actions of **Q and P^T**, exactly as Section 6 and the recovered prior acceptance M8.2 already required. Claiming that P itself conjugates both standard S,D to monomials is false in general.

For a generic high-digit partial Fourier map H_c, put N=Lc and j=s+Lr. Set H_c[(s,t),j]=zeta_c^(r t). Conjugating the clock moves t to t-1, with weight omega^s. Conjugating the shift increments s, with t unchanged and wrap weight zeta_c^t. Both are monomial. Q is an output-monomial multiple of H_b with L=am; P^T is an output-monomial multiple of H_a with L=bm, where s=t2+b t1. Output monomial conjugation preserves monomiality. Hence all four *actual bank actions* are monomial, as required. This establishes the intended result without the false P-orientation interpretation.

A fresh exact counterexample has a=m=b=2, N=8, K=GF(17), omega=2. The first row of P S P^-1 is [0,0,0,8,0,0,0,10], with two nonzeros. The same case's Q and P^T shift/clock actions are all monomial. Four additional exact cases verify the same distinction. See `checks/alg045/check_orientation.py` and `checks/alg045/ORIENTATION_CHECKS.json`. ### Paired-Green realization and boundary

For dyadic a,b in {1,2,4}, radix-2 butterflies realize F_a,F_b using at most two layers per exterior. A butterfly is a sum of a nonsingular diagonal monomial and a nonsingular off-diagonal monomial. Endpoint monomials are absorbed into the adjacent layer. Identity padding can use I=(1/2)I+(1/2)I. Thus the **exactly two paired-layer** claim is accepted only with char(K) != 2; this is automatic for dyadic N>=2 under the root guard. The scalar N=1,F2 case is excluded from this padding claim: two nonzero scalar summands add to zero there. The general Fourier identity/normal form itself still includes N=1.

The legal fixed-a,b family has q=ab<=16 and arbitrary dyadic m with supplied roots. Its cost is (N/a)T(a)+(N/m)T(m)+(N/b)T(b)+O(N), including routes/twiddles, root powers and preparation. In the square-bank case m=q, four zero-mask paired layers permit at most 16 exterior support branches per row, so q<=16; N=256, a=b=4, m=16 attains this support endpoint. No statement about nonzero Green layers, general Green exteriors or unrestricted Fourier complexity follows.
