## Target and exact test

Let N=4L≥16, let G be any finite abelian 2-group of order N, and let I be any 2L+1-element subset of its character group. Over a characteristic-zero splitting field, C_I is the span of the character columns indexed by I, of length N and dimension k=2L+1. The requested conclusion is d_(L+1)(C_I)≥2L+2.

For every J⊂G of size 2L−1, the evaluation map on J has kernel dimension k−rank(U_G[J,I]). Thus an (L+1)-dimensional subspace supported on at most 2L+1 coordinates exists exactly when some such rectangle has rank at most L. Consequently the weight conclusion is exactly equivalent to rank(U_G[J,I])≥L+1 for every J of that size. Scalar normalization of the character table does not affect this statement.

## Finite-field bridge: what is valid and what still must be proved

The following is a direct algebraic observation for this comparison, not a theorem attributed to either inaccessible paper.

Let e=exp(G), choose a primitive e-th root ζ, and use the unnormalized character table over R=Z[ζ]. Choose any maximal ideal above an odd prime p and pass to its finite residue field K. Because p∤e and p∤N, the reduced e-th roots are distinct and the full character table is invertible by character orthogonality. Thus the reduced C_I has the same dimension k=2L+1. Equivalently one may choose a finite splitting field F_q with e | q−1 and odd characteristic.

Every nonzero minor after reduction was already nonzero in characteristic zero. Hence, for each fixed rectangle, rank_0≥rank_K. A parameter-complete lower bound rank_K≥L+1 for all target rectangles would therefore prove the characteristic-zero claim. The weight formulation follows from the kernel/rank equivalence above; in particular d_r(C_I in characteristic zero)≥d_r(reduced C_I).

This does not supply the missing finite-field lower bound. It also does not justify using binary Fourier ranks: in characteristic 2 all 2-power roots reduce to 1, the character table collapses, and the dimension-preservation premise fails. For a nonsplitting base field, arbitrary character subsets additionally require a descent/Frobenius-invariance check; choosing a splitting field avoids that issue but must still respect the theorem's allowed field parameters. The general conclusion “finite field implies characteristic zero” without these checks is inadequate.
