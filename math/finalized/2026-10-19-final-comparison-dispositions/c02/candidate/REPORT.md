## 3. Fresh Cartesian-source boundary

Primary source: Beelen–Datta, [arXiv:1706.02114v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1706.02114v2), 1 August 2017. Fresh text inspection covered Section 2 equations (1)–(5), printed pp.2–4, and Theorem 4.6, printed p.8; Theorem 5.4 on p.9 was checked for its finite-field section restriction. PDF screenshots failed with a cache-miss response; no visual-page verification is claimed.

Theorem 4.6 allows arbitrary fields but optimizes over the full total-degree-cutoff polynomial space. Theorem 5.4 states the coding reformulation over finite fields. Section 2 supplies a leading-monomial shadow bound, without asserting it is sharp for arbitrary monomial spans. The character model does map to evaluation of arbitrary selected monomials on products of roots of unity. It does not automatically map to the full degree-cutoff space.

**Symbolic comparison control, not a finite-N experiment or a counterexample to C02.** Take the cyclic subgroup case N=4L, with L a power of two and L>=4, and choose the permitted exponents

    I = {2L−1, 2L, ..., 4L−1},  |I|=2L+1;  r=L+1.

In the natural polynomial representatives, r independent polynomials can have leading exponents S={3L−1,...,4L−1}. Their upward shadow inside {0,...,4L−1} has size L+1. Thus the bare shadow minimization for arbitrary selected exponents supplies only L+1 here, not the required 2L+2. Embedding the entire selected span in the full degree <=4L−1 space likewise supplies only r=L+1.

This weakness is representational, not a mathematical obstruction: multiplying evaluations by the nowhere-zero monomial x^(−(2L−1)) identifies this particular selected space with degree <=2L. For r independent polynomials there, an echelon basis contains a nonzero polynomial of degree at most 2L−(r−1)=L. Their common zeros number at most L, and the product of any L chosen root factors times the polynomials of degree <=L attains L common zeros. Its actual r-th support weight is therefore 4L−L=3L, which exceeds 2L+2. Consequently, treating the raw arbitrary-monomial shadow lower bound as an equality formula would be false.

This calculation only rules out that unqualified substitution. It does not rule out normalization, multiple term orders, stronger ideals, cyclic shifts, group automorphisms, or a more sophisticated footprint implication. A uniform bridge covering every character set and every product decomposition remains unprovided. No universal lower proof or old proof is rerun here.
