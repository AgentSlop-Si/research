                                Rogers–Szegő discriminants:
                              removing the cyclotomic guards
                        Extension of the existing local-discriminant research

                                                 17 October 2026


                                                        Abstract
         The previous local ordinary-discriminant theorem required residual squarefreeness, separation, and
     simplicity of an auxiliary Hermite polynomial. We remove the auxiliary condition for every odd root
     order and every odd residual degree. We also remove the static conditions for all residual degrees at
     every odd prime order. Consequently the exact prime cyclotomic order of the Rogers–Szegő ordinary
     discriminant is unconditional, in all degrees. The new arithmetic step proves that every root of an
     adjacent parity-Hermite Wronskian satisfies v2 (y − 1) = 1/2, excluding collisions over odd cyclotomic
     fields. The proof extends the earlier theorem; its local expansion and leading coefficient formulas are
     retained as established inputs. This is mathematical progress in the algebraic branch. It supplies no
     improved FFT complexity bound. Historical priority beyond the comparisons recorded here remains
     unresolved.

7    Verification, source comparison, and scope
The general statements above rest on the proofs. Finite calculations were used to identify the arithmetic
mechanism and to check normalizations. They are supplied as short, standard-library Python programs,

with exact integer, rational, or finite-field arithmetic. The partial-sum controls test the cyclotomic
identities independently of the complex sector proof. The static controls reconstruct small ordinary
discriminants and the integral node quotients in (17)–(19), and include the excluded boundary b = p.
The parity-Wronskian argument has been checked by a second derivation: an integral recurrence proves
Wm R (1 + 2u) ≡ 2m−1 (mod 2m ), which independently excludes unramified roots. The stronger proof in

Theorem 3 determines their exact valuation.
One independent control reconstructs the actual H12 and its first q-derivative in Q(ω), where ω has
order 7. Using the reciprocal factorization H12 (z; q) = z 6 R(z + z −1 ; q) and D12 = R(2)R(−2) Disc(R)2 ,
it computes the entire local coefficient and confirms exactly

beyond the previous b = 1, 3 families. The accompanying composition review checks all boundary cases
and the precise prime-power restriction.
The comparison made in this work is bounded and explicit:
• The classical Gaussian-binomial recurrence, the Hermite recurrence and discriminant, and the
  Hermite–Laguerre quadratic relations are established inputs. They are not originality claims.
                                                                        (α)    (α)
• Sawa and Uchida’s published Theorem 3.6 gives discriminants of Lm + cLm−1 . Our parity family
  fits it with A + 1 = 2m + s, α = s − 12 and c = A(τ − 1)/(2m). Thus the general quasi-Laguerre
  discriminant formula is not new. The additional assertion proved here is the exclusion of its nonzero
  collision parameters over unramified 2-adic fields, and its use for the RS parameters.
• Grosu and Grosu’s Theorem 7 gives Newton-slope bounds for Wronskian Hermite polynomials at
  primes p > max{2, 2m − 1}, with an additional Vandermonde condition. Their Theorem 8 uses odd
  moduli. Those stated results do not directly cover the shifted valuation at p = 2 in (8).
• Bonneux, Dunning and Stevens give broad coefficient formulas and Hermite–Laguerre Wronskian
  correspondences. The formulas are relevant prior work; the present short proof does not establish
  that no equivalent valuation consequence exists elsewhere.
• Lubinsky and Saff’s 1987 paper is relevant to static Rogers–Szegő zeros and root-of-unity special-
  izations. The publisher’s abstract and the author’s publication record were checked. The complete
  relevant body was unavailable in this session, so no assertion of nonoverlap with every theorem in
  that paper is made.
The exact project increment is therefore secure relative to the version-3 theorem and the accepted
two-residual review: their unresolved hypotheses have been removed in the ranges stated above. The
stronger arithmetic lemma is a candidate for publication-level novelty, subject to a complete literature
comparison.
For a general odd composite d, (15) holds in the full odd residual range, but the two static conditions
remain open here outside Theorem 6. Nothing in this note treats even d, gives a positive-characteristic
version of the main theorem, or supplies a uniform numerical conditioning estimate as n grows. In
particular, the original arbitrary-input FFT target and its exponent κ remain unchanged.
