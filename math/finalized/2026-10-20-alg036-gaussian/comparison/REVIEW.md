## Exact optimizer proposition and normalization

For proper-complex Z with covariance I−uu*, q_j=|u_j|² is the normal-weight vector, not leverage. The accepted result maximizes F_a(q)=Pr(max_j|Z_j|≤sqrt(a)) over q for every finite a>0. The only maximizing types are (0,1/2,1/2) below the unique crossing, (1/3,1/3,1/3) above it, and both at the crossing, modulo phases and permutations. The accepted proof and separate mathematical review supply this status; this review does not reprove it.

I independently checked all three comparison bridges:

1. The complex two-plane has real dimension four, so scaling z=sqrt(a)w gives F_a=a²π^-2 times the Gaussian-weighted unit-polydisc section integral. Its small-a limit is unweighted volume only. It does not preserve optimizer ordering at all finite a.
2. Standard realification gives three circular-cylinder constraints in R⁶ on a J-invariant four-plane, not real cube constraints on an arbitrary real hyperplane.
3. If h_a(z)=e^-|z|² 1_{|z|≤sqrt(a)}/[π(1−e^-a)] and g is the planar density of Σ conjugate(u_j)Y_j for independent h_a variables, coarea gives F_a=π(1−e^-a)³g(0).

Independent controls for bridge 3 are the coordinate normal, yielding (1−e^-a)², and the equal two-coordinate normal, yielding (1−e^-a)(1−e^-2a). The latter matches B(a); there is no missing factor two.

## Original contract and sources

The supplement correctly retains the proper-complex Gaussian input, fixed unitary, independent seed, measurable transcript-only linear information and decoder, and deterministic almost-sure terminal complex rank deficit D≥d. The norm implications are correct: the all-bin C N^-alpha||x||₂ event gives relative ℓ₂ error at most C N^(1/2−alpha). A relative-ℓ₂ upper certificate alone gives the required ℓ∞ event only when it is at most C N^-alpha. Failure of a sufficient certificate does not disprove an algorithm's actual ℓ∞ success.

The inclusion uses a norm cutoff, not Gaussian posterior conditioning on the norm event. For D≥d, monotonicity of D log(1−e^(-Nr²/D)) justifies replacement by d.
