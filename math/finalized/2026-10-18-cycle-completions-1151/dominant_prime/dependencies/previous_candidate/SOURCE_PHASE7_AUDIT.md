## 1. Rank-one classification and exact optimum

For `F[k,j]=omega^(-kj)`, the multiplicative four-entry ratio is `omega^(-(k-k')(j-j'))`. Primitive order N makes it equal to one exactly when `N` divides the product of the differences. This is equivalent to vanishing of every 2-by-2 minor of a nonempty rectangle, hence to rank one because Fourier entries are nonzero.

Let H be the subgroup generated by the row differences and let `d=|H|`. The rows lie in one H-coset. Every column difference annihilates H, so the columns lie in one coset of its annihilator, which has size `N/d`. Thus a rank-one rectangle with side lengths a,b satisfies `a<=d`, `b<=N/d`, and `ab<=N`. The same character-pairing argument applies to finite abelian character tables over compatible splitting fields.

For each tile,

\[
a+b=ab(1/a+1/b)\ge\frac{ab}{N}\min_{d\mid N}(d+N/d).
\]

Disjoint coverage makes the tile areas sum to `N²`. This proves the lower bound `N min_(d|N)(d+N/d)`. For any divisor d, taking every product of a row H-coset and a column annihilator coset gives exactly N disjoint rank-one tiles, each of shape `d` by `N/d`, and attains `N(d+N/d)`. The minimum is therefore exact.

The perfect-square value is `2N^(3/2)` and the prime value is `N(N+1)`. The proof optimizes the defined independent endpoint-incidence volume; it does not count minimal scalar gates in a circuit permitted to reuse intermediate sums across tiles.

## 2. Higher-rank tiles

Over the complex numbers, a Fourier submatrix with a rows, b columns and rank r has squared Frobenius norm `ab` and spectral norm at most `sqrt(N)`. Therefore `r>=ab/N`, and nonemptiness also gives `r>=1`. Hence

\[
r(a+b)\ge\max(1,ab/N)\,2\sqrt{ab}\ge 2ab/\sqrt N.
\]

Summing gives `2N^(3/2)` for every disjoint tiling in the declared dense rank-factor endpoint model. At perfect squares, the balanced rank-one tiling attains this lower bound.

For prime N in characteristic zero, the Fourier full-spark theorem gives `r=min(a,b)`. If `a<=b`, then `r(a+b)/(ab)=1+a/b>=1+1/N`, giving `N(N+1)` overall. The cited [Garcia-Karaali-Katz paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.07648) explicitly recalls the prime Fourier nonvanishing-minors theorem. This step is not a theorem for arbitrary finite fields. The stable-rank argument also depends on the complex norm and is not imported into modular rank tests.

The source correctly leaves open whether rank-r tiles attain the rank-one incidence optimum for every nonsquare composite length. Its finite-field MILP experiments at N2 through N6 are only small experiments; they do not settle that question.

## 3. Shared evaluation and literal cost accounting

Writing `N=dq`, rows `k=r+qv`, and inputs `j=c+dw`, the kernel factors into `omega^(-rc) omega^(-rdw) omega^(-qvc)`. Therefore one can first compute the q-point Fourier transforms along each fixed c, multiply the N twiddles, and then compute d-point Fourier transforms along each fixed r. For power-of-two factors the combined butterfly count is exactly `(N/2)log2 N`. Counting every butterfly multiplication, even a trivial one, gives this many multiplications plus N intermediate twiddle products, twice this many additions, and N-1 products for a supplied-root table. The source code implements this classical decomposition with no claim of a new asymptotic improvement.

For independent tile contractions, the declared count `N(d+q)` products and `N(d+q-1)` additions is an attainable circuit count: initialize each q-term input sum with its first product, then perform q-1 additions; use d accumulated fanout additions for each tile. Our independent implementation realizes and checks exactly that count.

The literal source `one_pass_tiled_dft`, however, initializes `acc=0` and executes `acc=(acc+v)%p` even on the first term while incrementing `ledger.adds` only when `j>0`. Thus its literal Python loop performs N more modular additions than recorded. Replacing the first iteration by `acc=v` realizes the ledger. This does not change the incidence optimum, asymptotics, or shared-FFT comparison; it qualifies the source wording that every executor addition is counted.

Root generation uses a supplied primitive root. Prime/field discovery in test fixtures, integer index arithmetic, container allocation, and memory traffic beyond the explicit output-write counter are outside the scalar arithmetic ledger. No word-RAM or bit-complexity conclusion follows automatically.

## 4. MILP and novelty boundaries

The archived rank-one audit enumerates small rectangles with an integer divisibility criterion, builds a binary exact-cover formulation, and calls SciPy's MILP solver with zero requested relative gap. It checks an optimal status, a small reported gap, the rounded objective, and the theorem's predicted objective. This is useful numerical solver corroboration, but the archive contains no independently checked rational dual certificate or proof tree. The exact all-N optimum rests on the subgroup proof above, not on interpreting floating optimization status as a formal exact certificate.

The source's warning about prior rank-one Fourier-block work is well grounded. Delvaux and Van Barel's [primary 2007 paper](https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=8e34019b75494fa56d9b8791527fcaed304a7ff9&repid=rep1&type=pdf) already describes subgroup-derived tilings by rank-one blocks. Its bibliographic details are corroborated by the [authors' institution](https://www.cs.kuleuven.be/publicaties/lirias/TEST2/cwpub.php?year=2007). This review does not establish a publication-priority claim for the exact endpoint-incidence formula.

The source's stronger all-input comparison was checked against the current [official OpenAI Math #130 scope](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/lean/docs/130.md). It states a uniform exact-complex-arithmetic power-saving bound including scalar preparation, with a chosen root supplied and unrestricted coefficients. It is not a bounded-coefficient or bit-cost benchmark, and the separate subsequential statement should not be substituted for the uniform one.
