## Long-zero proof and support bound

For nonzero raw support n_1,...,n_s, let z_r=exp(-2 pi i n_r/M). A run of s Fourier zeros starting at j0 gives the square matrix z_r^q, q=0,...,s-1, with unknowns x_(n_r) z_r^j0. Its determinant is the product of distinct nonzero root differences. Integer indices can be continued across the cyclic wrap because the Fourier sequence is M-periodic. Thus all the unknown coefficients would vanish, contradicting nonzero support.

The complement of a cyclic interval of N consecutive residues has M-N consecutive residues. Consequently a nonzero vector whose transform is supported in the centered interval cannot have s<=M-N. The resulting integer bound s>=M-N+1 is correct, including its strict +1. The least-power-of-two condition on 4N gives M/2<4N<=M, equivalently M/8<N<=M/4. Substituting M=2^28 yields the claimed 201,326,593 minimum over the whole compatible outer-length range.

The polynomial proof is an independent valid formulation: after factoring a nonzero phase, inverse-transform samples are values of a nonzero polynomial of degree at most N-1 at M distinct roots of unity. It has at most N-1 roots. Neither argument needs dyadic coefficients, conditioning estimates, a floating tolerance, or an uncertainty principle for arbitrary support shapes. Consecutiveness is essential: the independent suite includes a two-entry vector at M=8 whose transform vanishes at four nonconsecutive positions.

Only zero can lie in the overlap at the present sparse caps. The supplier handles zero separately, outside its nonzero normalized recovery contract; this review does not turn zero into a normalized nonzero source.
