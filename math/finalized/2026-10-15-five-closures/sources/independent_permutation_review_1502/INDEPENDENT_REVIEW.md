## Primary-source implication

Zaslavsky permits a gain group inside the multiplicative group of any skew field. His canonical column for an oriented edge v to w with gain g is -e_v + g e_w. Theorem 2.1(a) identifies its dependence matroid with the bias matroid; the latter's rank is n minus the number of balanced components. Loops and parallel edges are allowed.

Take edges pi(j) to j with gain omega^j. The columns are exactly (D-P)e_j. Each component is a permutation cycle; its gain product is omega raised to its index sum. Hence the number of balanced components is z(P), yielding rank(D-P)=N-z(P). A fixed point contributes (omega^j-1)e_j, including the zero column at j=0. A transposition gives two parallel edges and is covered as well. No characteristic-zero or cycle-length-coprimality restriction is needed.

## Independent mathematical checks

The cyclic subgroup generated by omega satisfies the primary theorem's multiplicative-subgroup hypothesis. Its existence in characteristic p implies p does not divide N, but p may divide an individual permutation-cycle length. This is harmless: along each cycle the kernel equations use only nonzero scalar weights, and closing the cycle gives one scalar consistency condition. They give exactly one free parameter in a zero-sum cycle and none otherwise, without any diagonalizability assumption.

The N powers of an exact-order-N root are distinct. Their Vandermonde Fourier matrix is invertible, so ZF-FP=F(D-P) has the same rank. Opposite cyclic-shift convention negates all exponents and leaves zero sums unchanged.

For the optimum, only the residue 0 can be a zero-sum singleton. All other balanced cycles need at least two vertices. Thus z(P) <= ceil(N/2); negation attains this bound, so the minimum rank is floor(N/2).

Odd N=2k+1: k+1 balanced cycles force the 0 singleton and k pairs consuming the other 2k vertices. Each pair is {j,-j}, uniquely fixing the permutation.

Even N=2k: the sum of all residues is k modulo 2k, so some cycle is unbalanced. If an optimizer had no 0 singleton, k balanced cycles would use all 2k vertices, a contradiction. With 0 fixed, the remaining k-1 balanced cycles require at least 2k-2 vertices and an unbalanced cycle requires at least one. Equality forces balanced pairs and one nonbalanced singleton. The pairs are {j,-j}; the unpaired nonzero self-negative residue is k. Thus negation is again unique. The case N=1 is immediate; the even argument includes N=2.

The even-N argument repairs the original source's abbreviated equality assertion and is already present in the accepted live theorem. Nothing in this review widens or narrows its accepted mathematical scope.
