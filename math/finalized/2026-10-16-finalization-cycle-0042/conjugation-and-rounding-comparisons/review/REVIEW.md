## Conjugated feedback checks

For invertible A,C, an involution J, and nonzero lambda with lambda squared unequal to one, let K=BA=lambda CJC inverse. Its minimal polynomial divides z squared minus lambda squared. In characteristic unequal to two, the two roots are distinct, so the spectral projectors are (I plus or minus CJC inverse)/2. The scalar function f(z)=z/(1-z) agrees at these roots with p(z)=(z+lambda squared)/(1-lambda squared). Therefore the owner identity is the two-point matrix-function interpolation formula, with transfer f(K)A inverse. The order of the factors matters. Scalar J is a degree-one special case.

The exact classical mapping is supported by [Higham's 2006 author manuscript](https://eprints.maths.manchester.ac.uk/310/1/fm_final.pdf), Section 1, interpolation definition (4) and Facts 1, 2, 5 and 6. This is an algebraic specialization, not an independent Fourier synthesis algorithm. The complex-matrix source must not be credited as a finite-field numerical implementation theorem.

[Davies and Higham](https://eprints.maths.manchester.ac.uk/156/1/paper.pdf), equations (1.1)–(1.4), establish the similarity/Schur framework and block recurrence. Algorithm 5.1 explicitly assumes analyticity on a convex set whose interior contains the spectrum. For the owner's nonscalar lambda=2 example, the convex hull of {-2,2} contains the pole 1 of f, so that particular stated contract does not apply directly to f. This does not invalidate f(K), ordinary rational evaluation, or adapted Schur methods. The entire polynomial p is available only because the annihilator is supplied. The paper's numerical and dense-matrix work qualifications cannot be converted into the owner's linear-work construction or precision certificate.

The A/B-only evaluator uses two B actions, one A action and linear vector work once the plans and closure proof are supplied. Evaluating the equivalent product instead requires A inverse, C inverse, the affine function of J, and C. Cheap forward A alone does not certify its inverse. If J is not a supplied diagonal sign matrix, an abstract diagonalization is not a free basis change. Setup, denominators, intermediate conditioning, roots and finite-word error remain paid obligations.

Forcing A=F inverse C Delta C inverse imports the target Fourier inverse unless a separately paid construction removes it. The accepted noncommuting control remains only a control: its cut rank at most five excludes the Fourier matrix for even N at least 12, while N=10 is inconclusive. ## Phase frame checks

ALG-035 has Q=KM, supplied roots and unit phases. The complete normalized summary-to-output map is unitary; a raw input block contributes singular values 1/sqrt(K), so those coordinates cannot be interchanged in a rank argument. Uniform size-s phase subsets yield exact relative mean-square error (M-s)/s and fixed-subset operator error max(1,M/s-1) for s<M, zero for s=M. These facts do not state a general scalar-operation lower bound.

The [NSW v1 paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1909.11123v1) is a conditional sparse-frequency recovery comparator. Its per-fixed-input success statement, supplied spectral-tail scale, polynomial signal-to-noise bound, time-coordinate access and ideal arithmetic assumptions remain essential. Algorithm 3 and Lemma A.15 use tail divided by sqrt(k); Theorem A.16 prints tail divided by k. Both PDF and HTML show the discrepancy. The comparison may disclose and condition on the algorithm convention; it must not silently repair the formal statement. The zero-tail case and k=Q are not supplied exact dense fallbacks. Its random repeat-until shift loop and uncharged finite-precision issues also remain distinct from a capped executor.

Independently, write X=U_Q x and E_q for its best q-term spectral tail. Since E_q is at most the input norm, q=ceil(Q^(2 alpha)) gives the weak bound E_q/sqrt(q) at most Q^(-alpha) times the input norm, for fixed 0<alpha<1/2 and sufficiently large Q with 2<=q<Q. This is an illustrative implication on the comparator's admitted domain, not a replacement target for exact full-frame synthesis. The corresponding all-bin bound allows relative l2 error up to Q^(1/2-alpha); an impulse and the zero output show why it need not force useful relative accuracy.

For a flat-magnitude Q-bin spectrum, the direct l-infinity-to-l2 upper bound becomes sqrt((Q-q)/q), so obtaining tolerance epsilon through that particular sufficient conversion requires q at least Q/(1+epsilon squared). This is not a necessary condition on the algorithm's actual error and is not a runtime lower bound. For the owner's different uniform phase-subset estimator, its exact RMS relative error is at most epsilon precisely when s is at least M/(1+epsilon squared), for nonzero input. That is an expectation identity, not a high-probability claim. The similar formulas do not identify their different resources or measurement interfaces.

The frequency support q is not the number s of phase rows. A data-dependent sparse decoder is not a fixed rank-deficient approximation to the unitary synthesis operator. Nor are arbitrary time-coordinate samples the same interface as a fixed number of phase measurements in each block. Therefore neither the owner's local rank statement nor its quantitative Gaussian information-loss bound is an unrestricted lower bound on the comparator.
