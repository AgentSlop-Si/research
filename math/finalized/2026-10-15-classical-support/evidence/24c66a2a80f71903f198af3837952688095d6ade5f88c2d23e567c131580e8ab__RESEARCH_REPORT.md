# Galois-orbit primitivity: exact equivalence and sharp obstructions

## Verdict

The uniform assertion that the content c_d has no prime divisor outside d, for every d≡1 (mod 4), is **neither proved nor refuted here**.

This pass establishes three precise results instead:

1. For every nonzero element M of Z[ζ_d], the ideal generated by its Galois conjugates is exactly content(M)·Z[ζ_d,1/d] after inverting d. There is an explicit integral trace certificate with right side d·content(M). Thus the previously proposed Galois-orbit Bézout certificate for M_d is **equivalent** to the missing primitivity assertion; abstract Galois descent does not by itself make that assertion easier.
2. The actual d=17, p=2 determinant gives a sharp obstruction to using only powers-of-two conjugates, even together with complex conjugation. Its bad residue factor persists under that entire subgroup. A conjugate outside the subgroup is necessary and sufficient to remove that particular obstruction.
3. For every d=4r+1≥25 and every odd p∤d, a monic contracted polynomial can preserve all four exact special values at 2, −2, η_1, η_(2r), while its determinant vanishes modulo p at every primitive-root residue factor. This is a **synthetic countercontrol**, not the actual Gaussian polynomial. It proves that reciprocity and these special-value identities alone are insufficient: a successful proof must exploit further Gaussian-coefficient structure.

Fresh controls reconstruct the actual d=17 arrays, verify its binary-field obstruction using an independent implementation, produce an exact full-orbit certificate, and instantiate the synthetic perturbation at d=25, p=3. No new full-content base census is performed.

## 1. Definitions and accepted input

Let

    H_n(z;q)=Σ_(k=0)^n [n choose k]_q z^k,
    D_n(q)=Disc_z H_n(z;q).

The discriminant is the ordinary derivative discriminant. For odd d, put b=(d−1)/2 and

    R_d(q)=Res_z(z^d+1,H_b(z;q)),
    B_d=rem_(Φ_d)(D_b R_d),
    c_d=gcd of the coefficients of B_d, taken positive.

Use c_d=0 if B_d=0. The d=1 convention is c_1=1.

For d=4r+1≥5, reciprocity defines the monic degree-r polynomial P in O[u], O=Z[ζ_d], by

    H_(2r)(z;ζ_d)=z^r P(z+z^−1).

Write η_j=−ζ_d^j−ζ_d^(−j), 1≤j≤2r, and

    C_d^*(u)=∏_(j=2)^(2r−1)(u−η_j),
    M_d=Res_u(P,(u−2)P'(u)C_d^*(u)).

The product defining C_d^* is empty for r=1. The accepted report and audit establish, for each prime p∤d,

    p|c_d  ⇔  M_d∈pO.                              (1)

They also establish that P(−2), P(η_1), and P(η_(2r)) are units in O[1/d], and that

    D_(2r)R_d=(-1)^r H_(2r)(1)
               [H_(2r)(−1) Disc(P) Res(C_d,P)]².

These are inputs to this report. In particular, “nonzero in at least one residue field” must not be replaced by “a unit in every residue field.”

## 2. Exact orbit-ideal theorem

The theorem below applies to every cyclotomic integer; no Gaussian identity is needed.

### Theorem 1

Let d≥2, ζ=ζ_d, O=Z[ζ], G=(Z/dZ)^×, and let

    M=Σ_(j=0)^(m−1) m_j ζ^j ≠0,   m=φ(d),
    c=gcd(m_0,...,m_(m−1))>0,
    I(M)=Σ_(a∈G) O·σ_a(M).

Then

    d c O ⊆ I(M) ⊆ c O,                            (2)
    I(M) O[1/d] = c O[1/d].                         (3)

Moreover one can explicitly construct W∈O such that

    Tr_(Q(ζ)/Q)(W M)
      =Σ_(a∈G) σ_a(W) σ_a(M)=d c.                  (4)

### Proof

Write f(X)=Φ_d(X)=Σ_(k=0)^m f_k X^k and Q(X)=(X^d−1)/f(X), both integral. Put

    S_j(ζ)=Σ_(k=j+1)^m f_k ζ^(k−1−j).

The power-basis trace dual is β_j=S_j(ζ)/f'(ζ). To see the coefficient extraction directly, interpolate X^i at the m conjugates ζ^a, for 0≤i<m, using f(X)/((X−ζ^a)f'(ζ^a)), then compare the coefficient of X^j. It gives

    Tr(β_j ζ^i)=δ_ij,
    Tr(β_j M)=m_j.

This is the classical trace-dual formula, also stated in Keith Conrad, *The Different Ideal*, Theorem 3.7, pp. 6–7: https://kconrad.math.uconn.edu/blurbs/gradnumthy/different.pdf .

Differentiating X^d−1=f(X)Q(X) and evaluating at ζ gives

    f'(ζ)Q(ζ)=d ζ^(d−1).

Consequently the following numerator is integral:

    δ_j=d β_j=ζ Q(ζ)S_j(ζ) ∈ O,                   (5)

because ζ^(1−d)=ζ. Thus

    d m_j=Σ_(a∈G) σ_a(δ_j)σ_a(M) ∈ I(M).           (6)

Choose ordinary integer Bézout coefficients b_j with Σ b_j m_j=c, and set W=Σ b_jδ_j. Equation (6) gives (4), and hence the left inclusion of (2). The right inclusion holds because M/c∈O and every conjugate of M is divisible by c. Localizing (2) at d yields (3). ∎

### Corollary: the proposed certificate is exactly equivalent

For nonzero M the following conditions are equivalent:

- No prime outside d divides its canonical coefficient content.
- I(M)O[1/d]=O[1/d].
- There are A_a∈O and L≥0 such that Σ A_a σ_a(M)=d^L.

For the forward implication, choose k with c|d^k. Multiply (4) by d^k/c to obtain the third condition with L=k+1 and A_a=(d^k/c)σ_a(W). Conversely, if p∤d divides c, every term on the left of such an identity lies in pO, while d^L does not.

If M=0, no such identity exists and its reduction vanishes at every unramified prime. By (1), applying this corollary to M_d gives precisely the desired unramified primitivity statement, not a stronger independent criterion.

The useful additional output is the explicit denominator d and the integral sandwich (2). The construction of W does not solve the uniform problem: the integer Bézout target is the unknown content c, and replacing d c by a pure power of d presupposes the missing support assertion.

## 3. The local Galois-support obstruction

Fix p∤d. Let H=<p>⊂G and f=ord_d(p). The residue algebra O/pO is a product of fields F_(p^f), indexed by G/H. Choose an indexing and let B⊂G/H be the components at which M vanishes.

For a set S⊂G, the ideal generated by {σ_a(M):a∈S} is the whole residue algebra if and only if

    ∩_(a∈S) aB = ∅,                               (7)

up to the harmless choice of inverse in the indexing convention. Indeed an ideal in a product of fields is the whole product exactly when each component has at least one nonzero generator.

Two immediate consequences are important.

- If S=G, condition (7) holds exactly when B is not all of G/H. This recovers the existential good-factor condition.
- If every a∈S lies in H, then aB=B. Frobenius powers cannot remove even one bad component. In elementary terms, for an integer-coefficient polynomial representing M, M(ω^(p^j))=M(ω)^(p^j), so their zero sets agree.

More generally, for a subgroup K⊂G, all conjugates in K fail precisely when B contains an entire K-orbit on G/H. A good conjugate set must actually move the bad components; being large or containing complex conjugation is not sufficient.

This statement is structural and uniform. It does not assert that B is proper for the particular M_d.

## 4. An actual sharp obstruction at d=17, p=2

Fresh exact construction gives the same complete D_8, R_17, Disc(P), and M_17 arrays as the accepted audit. The reduction of M_17 is

    M_17 ≡ q^15+q^14+q^10+q^5+q+1  (mod 2).

Over F_2,

    Φ_17=g_good g_bad,

    g_good=q^8+q^5+q^4+q^3+1,
    g_bad =q^8+q^7+q^6+q^4+q^2+q+1.

Both factors are irreducible. The fresh calculations establish

    gcd(Φ_17,D_8 mod 2)=1,
    gcd(Φ_17,R_17 mod 2)=g_bad,
    gcd(Φ_17,M_17 mod 2)=g_bad.                     (8)

Thus this bad factor comes from the residual cluster obstruction. It cannot be dismissed by an ordinary-discriminant argument or by either of the two removed cluster units.

An independently authored binary-field implementation uses the multiplicative Gaussian formula, rather than q-Pascal or the exact-ring determinant implementation. In F_2[q]/(g_bad), it finds

    gcd_z(H_8,H_8')=1,
    gcd_z(H_8,z^17+1)=z²+(q^6+q^5+q^4+q+1)z+1.

The common cluster roots are precisely q^6 and q^11. In the g_good field both gcds are 1. The field encoding in the machine-readable control represents q^6+q^5+q^4+q+1 by the integer bit mask 115. Irreducibility is checked by the degree-eight Rabin criterion, not merely assumed.

Here

    H=<2>={1,2,4,8,9,13,15,16}.

The subgroup has index two and contains −1=16. For every a∈H,

    gcd(Φ_17,M_17(q),M_17(q^a)) mod 2 = g_bad;

for every a∉H the gcd is 1. Thus two suitably chosen conjugates are necessary and sufficient at this local obstruction, but arbitrarily many powers-of-two conjugates, even with complex conjugation, cannot work.

This is **not** an extraneous-prime counterexample: the good factor remains, and 2∤c_17.

As a separate exact check,

    Norm(R_17)=2^16 · 17^68 · 101^4.

The nonzero 2-adic norm valuation coexists with no rational factor 2 in the content. A norm adds local valuations and cannot replace the minimum/all-components criterion.

### Full-orbit certificate for the established base

The coefficient content of the reconstructed M_17 is 17². Theorem 1 constructs W∈Z[ζ_17] and the program verifies, as an exact sixteen-coefficient identity,

    Σ_(a=1)^16 σ_a(W)σ_a(M_17)=17³.                (9)

All coefficients of W and the integer Bézout combination producing it are supplied in FRESH_17_CONTROL.json. This certifies a known finite base and demonstrates the general extraction formula. It is not used as evidence for an infinite theorem.

## 5. Why the known special values cannot finish the proof

### Theorem 2: exact-value-preserving synthetic obstruction

Let d=4r+1≥25, O=Z[ζ_d], and p be any odd prime not dividing d. For the actual monic contracted P of degree r, there exists a different monic polynomial P̃∈O[u], also of degree r, such that

    P̃(t)=P(t),  t∈{2,−2,η_1,η_(2r)},              (10)

as exact equalities in O, while u² divides P̃ in (O/pO)[u]. In particular

    Res(P̃,(u−2)P̃'C_d^*) ∈ pO.                    (11)

### Proof

Set

    F(u)=(u²−4)(u−η_1)(u−η_(2r)).

The constant F(0)=−4η_1η_(2r) is a unit modulo p. Indeed η_j=0 would force ζ_d^(2j)=−1 in a residue field; this is impossible for an odd-order root of unity in odd characteristic. Therefore in A=O/pO choose

    a=−P(0)/F(0),
    b=−[P'(0)+F'(0)a]/F(0).

Lift a,b to A_0,B_0∈O, choosing the lifts not both zero (adding p to one lift if necessary), and define

    P̃=P+F(u)(A_0+B_0u).

The perturbation has degree at most five, strictly below r because r≥6, so monicity and degree are unchanged. Its four exact zeros give (10). The definitions of a,b force P̃(0)=P̃'(0)=0 in A. Thus u² divides P̃ there, so P̃ and P̃' have a common root in every residue field, proving (11). ∎

The associated reciprocal polynomial z^rP̃(z+z^−1) retains the four corresponding exact endpoint and Gaussian special values. It also retains all formal reciprocal discriminant/resultant factorizations, which hold for any monic P.

This is a rigorous insufficiency result about the proposed structural inputs. It does **not** preserve the full q-Pascal or three-term Gaussian recurrence, and it is not a counterexample to primitivity of the actual M_d. A future proof must exclude such perturbations using additional information specific to the Gaussian coefficients, or establish a genuinely new uniform orbit-support bound.

The fresh d=25, p=3 control explicitly constructs A_0,B_0 and P̃, verifies all four equalities coefficient by coefficient over O, and checks that its constant and linear coefficients vanish modulo 3. It deliberately does not label this modified polynomial as H_12.

## 8. Stopping boundary

The remaining mathematical claim is still:

    For every d=4r+1≥5 and p∤d, the actual M_d is
    nonzero in at least one factor of O_d/pO_d.

This report does not infer it from finite samples. It also does not infer it from an orbit-ideal reformulation that is equivalent to the claim, from norms, from powers-of-Frobenius conjugates, or from the three known Gaussian unit values.

The concrete progress is an exact orbit certificate theorem with explicit coefficients and denominator, a fully classified actual subgroup obstruction at (17,2), and a uniform synthetic obstruction to the special-values-only strategy.
