# Authorship attribution and rights

## Project code and results

verifier.py is an adapted version of the independently generated reviewer
checker accompanying the accepted Q11 matching Green report of 12 October
2026. The original checker was created for this research project; it did not
import author or historical research programs. This derivative preserves
its mathematical checks and deterministic finite-field controls. The
additional unit tests, CLI, manifest validation, and literal four-table
preparation instrumentation were generated for this repository supplement.
The expected-results file is generated by this derivative and was compared
against the accepted numerical certificate. This is a reproducibility
derivative of the prior review, not a second independent acceptance review.

Source hashes and material changes are listed in provenance.json. No
third-party implementation, package, or paper is included. Python's standard
library is used through the user's installed Python interpreter and is not
bundled. No existing source copyright or license notice was removed.

## Mathematical ingredient credits

The construction explicitly assembles classical low-rank Fourier blocking,
canonical matching rounds, weighted-cycle inverses, and scalar scans. The
complete model-and-resource contract's historical priority remains
unresolved. These references are credits, not redistributed papers or claims
that their authors state the entire Q11 theorem.

- Delvaux and Van Barel, *Rank-deficient submatrices of Fourier matrices*,
  Linear Algebra and its Applications 429 (2008), 1587-1605, Eq. (15): exact
  composite Fourier rank-one blocks. The source uses normalized Fourier
  matrices. Q11 refines its column partition with n=5q and m=q.
  https://doi.org/10.1016/j.laa.2008.04.043
- Lambrechts, Ficker, Goossens and Spieksma, *Round-robin tournaments generated
  by the Circle Method have maximum carry-over*, Mathematical Programming
  172 (2018), 277-302, Section 2.3: an accessible primary account of the
  canonical matching schedule. This does not attribute earliest invention of
  the Circle Method to these authors.
  https://fspieksma.win.tue.nl/papers/MathPro2018paper.pdf
- Huhtanen and Peramaki, *Factoring Matrices into the Product of Circulant and
  Diagonal Matrices*, Journal of Fourier Analysis and Applications 21 (2015),
  1018-1033, Theorem 2.9: the guarded weighted cyclic inverse, including zero
  weights. Their principal factorization is multiplicative; Theorem 2.10's
  nonzero-weight normalization is not used for Q11's zero-edge branches.
  https://math.aalto.fi/~mhuhtane/manu.pdf
- Chandrasekaran, Epperly and Govindarajan, *Graph-Induced Rank Structures and
  their Representations*, version 3, Section 2.2, Eqs. (2.6)-(2.10): scalar
  state-space recurrences and a related shifted block-resolvent sum. The
  source's shifts, block dimensions, and free diagonal are not automatically
  legal scalar Q11 branch counts.
  https://arxiv.org/html/1911.05858v3
