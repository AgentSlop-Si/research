## 2. Claim-by-claim comparison

The target uses H_n(z;q)=sum_k [n choose k]_q z^k and the ordinary derivative discriminant. Fix odd d, primitive complex d-th root omega, n=Ad+b, and q(epsilon)=omega(1+epsilon^2)^(1/d). The novelty-comparison target fixes n and moves q away from a prescribed nontrivial root, d>=3. The d=1 endpoint is substantially classical.

For the separated case, put F=z^d+1, G=H_b(z;omega), Delta=Disc G, R=Res(F,G), P_A=product_(j=1)^A j^j. The exact comparison target is

C_sep=(-1)^(A(d-1)/2) d^(dA(A+1)/2) P_A^d Delta R^(2A),

as the coefficient of (q^d-1)^K. At one simple overlap the target coefficient is

C_ov=(-1)^(A(d-1)/2) d^(dA(A+1)/2+A) P_A^(d-1) Disc J Disc U Res(F,U)^(2A) U(-1)^2,

as the coefficient of (q^d-1)^(K+A), under the manuscript's nonvanishing guards. The separated coefficient vanishes at overlap and does not provide the exact higher order there. A coefficient in q-omega has the additional factor (d omega^(d-1))^K or (d omega^(d-1))^(K+A); an integral quotient by Phi_d uses Q_d(omega) to that exponent, Q_d=(q^d-1)/Phi_d. A historical comparison that loses these distinctions is not exact.

Two already-saved P2109 controls illustrate the operator issue without supplying a new proof: H_2 has ordinary discriminant (q-1)(q+3), versus q-discriminant (q-1)(q+1)^2; at a primitive cubic root, H_3=z^3+1 has ordinary discriminant -27 while its q-discriminant vanishes. These controls were not rerun here.
