Small order Fourier synthesis by signed Weyl resolvent banks

10 October 2026

Abstract
We study additive representations of the negative Fourier matrix by phase-weighted averages
of cyclic resolvents built from the four signed pairs of one fixed Weyl system. The counted object
is a whole bank with one parameter pair and arbitrary phase weights. We give exact small-order
lower and upper arguments, with all inverse guards and normalization conventions explicit. The
accepted lower and upper results establish minima four, five, and eight at orders three, five, and
seven, respectively, over the complex numbers. The order-seven construction lies in the splitting
field of an explicit cubic over the seventh cyclotomic field. The method combines classical
Gaussian-binomial expansion, finite-character projection, and moment recurrences with support
restrictions and exact coefficient elimination. The certificates use characteristic-zero algebra
or justified good reduction, not extrapolation from finite-field searches. The results concern
representation count; they do not establish an arithmetic speedup for the Fourier transform.

Every multiplicity and degenerate case

Transposition sends (ε, δ) to (−ε, δ) and reindexes j to −j, preserving parameters, guards, and
the symmetric target F7 . It converts the positive-delta split (1, 2) to the excluded split (2, 1). If
P ei = e−i , conjugation by P sends signs to (−ε, −δ), preserves the phase convention, and satisfies
P F7 P = F7 . It transfers the obstruction to either split with negative delta.
Every orientation is necessary by Lemma 3.1. A side with only two banks therefore has one
of each epsilon sign and can be padded to the excluded three-bank case by a legal zero bank:
α = β = 1, wj = 0, ∆ = −1. A side with fewer than two banks already lacks an orientation.
Consequently each side requires at least four banks, proving the lower bound eight.
This exhaustion permits arbitrary opposite-side parameters, zero or repeated forward nodes,
zero forward amplitudes, and D = 0. The singleton y = 0 is excluded by its nonzero endpoint;
the sole division by y 2 is therefore justified. No node-difference, discriminant, or unproved rank
assumption enters the lower bound.

The human-readable proof above specifies every coefficient needed for its short identities and every
amplitude needed for the constructions. A companion SOURCE_REGISTER.json gives full relative

are identifiers of bytes, not proofs of the mathematics.
Exact data conventions. Cyclotomic elements use ascending coefficients of 1, q, . . . , q φ(N )−1 ,
with rational entries, reduced modulo ΦN . Polynomial lists in an auxiliary variable are also ascending. In the order-seven lower certificate, each auxiliary coefficient is a six-entry cyclotomic
vector; its field named F is the polynomial called E in (21). The short modular arrays in this
manuscript are ordinary residues in {0, . . . , 28}. The order-seven upper data represent triple amplitudes by degree-at-most-two polynomials in the cubic algebra, to be evaluated at each distinct
root. They do not choose approximate embeddings.
Order three. The accepted signed-magnetic packet contains the general model, support proof,
construction, and an independent control that verifies all twelve literal branch matrices over several
fields, including characteristic two. The all-field claim comes from the proof, not extrapolation from
those examples.
Order five lower bound. The exact checker reconstructs the Gaussian numerator identity in
symbolic parameters, phase orthogonality, all 25 trace coefficients, forced amplitudes, row/axis
equations, type splitting, and the four nonzero elimination residues. Independent review accepts
the characteristic-zero exclusion and all degeneracy coverage.
Order five upper bound. The literal file WITNESS.json specifies all five parameter pairs, guards, normalized amplitudes, and expanded raw weights. Its independent literal control inverts the
original branches by exact elimination, verifies both inverse products and every original Fourier
entry, and checks all primitive-root conjugates. A separate control verifies the nonsingular parameterization and all determinant identities.
Order seven lower bound. The accepted review’s EXACT_ROW_CERTIFICATE.json records

S.json records direct model and symmetry checks. The frozen author data supply the modular

Relation to prior work

The underlying tools are classical. Gaussian-binomial expansion and the root-of-unity power identity precede this synthesis [1, 2]. The cyclic magnetic system of Ouvry, Wagner, and Wu [4,
Section 2.2, equations 7–8] contains, after setting their reverse-shift and reverse-modulation variables to zero, the same branch matrix and denominator 1 − αN − β N . This is a specialization of
their system; it does not attribute the present additive bank-minimization problem to them. Finite
Weyl/character algebra [3] and Prony recurrences [5, 6] explain the projection and rank steps.
The grammar-specific content is the isolation of coefficients by the four signed triangles, the axis
coupling that excludes all order-five four-bank candidates, the two-row polynomial incompatibility
at order seven with full degenerate-case coverage, and the explicit small-order legal realizations.
The existing bounded prior-art comparison inspected the cited primary passages and found no
equivalent exact classification in those passages. That bounded result is not evidence of worldwide
priority. No new literature search was undertaken for this manuscript, and no priority claim is
made.
