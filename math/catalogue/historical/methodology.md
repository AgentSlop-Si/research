# Method for the cross-corpus research ranking

Date: 10 October 2026. This is an evidence-based ranking of **best-supported research candidates**, not a certificate that any result is new worldwide, a general FFT breakthrough, or publishable in a particular journal.

## The question being ranked

The primary ranking asks: which established result in this corpus has the strongest combination of a substantive, precisely identifiable contribution and enough evidence to justify serious manuscript development?

It does not rank historical excitement, the number of files or checks, the amount of assistant effort, or the size of a displayed asymptotic lower bound divorced from its model. A fixed five-dimensional theorem, an all-size sharp extremal theorem, and an exact-field algorithm are different kinds of contributions. Their rank must explain those differences rather than hide them in a made-up numerical score.

## Eligibility gates

1. **Identify the exact claim.** Record quantifiers, size family, coefficient field, structural grammar, allowed permutations/masks/zero weights, input promise, approximation norm, randomness, and cost model wherever material. Separate a theorem from a proposal, conditional implication, certificate, implementation claim, or finite experiment.
2. **Locate mathematical evidence.** Prefer the actual theorem/proof and its independent audit to a status summary. “Accepted” means accepted by an internal mathematical review. It does not mean journal peer review. This pass reads preserved mathematics as inert text and does not replay the earlier computations.
3. **Keep the surviving statement.** A narrowed theorem can be eligible even if its initial motivation failed. Conversely, a conditional fast-transform implication is not eligible as an achieved fast algorithm when its uniform representation premise remains unproved.
4. **Name the increment over prior work.** A new formula, exact minimum, application of known tools, strengthened scope, or fully charged algorithm is a candidate increment. Familiar ingredients must be attributed. An unsuccessful search alone is no increment.
5. **Separate duplication from rejection.** Later versions, an audit, a fixed-size base certificate, an infinite lift, and a corollary can be rows in the catalogue while belonging to one manuscript family. Their count must not inflate rank. A supporting result is not a failed result.

An explicit computational model is not an unproved premise. In particular, a proved exact-field or supplied-constant theorem can qualify, while a theorem saying that an unconstructed factorization would imply a fast transform does not establish that transform. Similarly, nonvanishing/coprimality guards in a proved arithmetic theorem are different from an unproved claim that the guards always hold.

## Common comparison axes

### A. Mathematical support

- Direct theorem/proof plus an accepted independent audit, with key hypotheses checked
- Accepted audit of a supplied exact certificate, including its mathematical lifting argument
- Source theorem only, or a mathematical argument with an unresolved proof obligation
- Finite test, status-only report, conditional plan, or refuted overclaim

The first two can both be strong. A certificate-intensive proof is not weaker merely because it uses computation. But recorded audit controls are historical evidence; they must not be advertised as fresh runs by this reviewer. Independent checks written by one assistant are not independent human peer review.

### B. Substantive mathematical advance

Prefer a matched sharp theorem, new uniform coefficient/structure theorem with meaningful consequences, or a genuine algorithmic improvement in a completely stated model. Give less weight to an elementary reformulation, direct specialization of a known general result, isolated computation without a theorem, or an improvement only to an internal prototype.

Short proofs can be important. Long proofs, many guards and elaborate certificates do not themselves establish importance.

### C. Generality and naturalness

Distinguish growing dimension from infinitely many parameters in fixed dimension; all inputs from a promised structured class; all lengths from dyadic lengths; arbitrary circuits from a narrow branch grammar; exact algebraic rank from numerical rank; and one fixed input with fresh randomness from one random sketch that works for every input.

A narrow result may still rank well when it settles a clear exact question. The restrictions must remain visible in its title and rationale.

### D. Actual prior-art difference

Use these qualitative dispositions, not confidence percentages:

- **Plausible substantive candidate; priority unresolved:** the exact increment is clear, and inspected primary sources do not directly supply it, but this is a bounded comparison.
- **Specialized synthesis/application candidate:** classical machinery does most of the work; the precise combination or application may still be new.
- **Known core with a narrower possible extension:** preserve the result and identify the unassessed extension; do not sell the known core as a discovery.
- **Supporting/certificate/implementation result:** useful, but presently insufficient as a standalone research claim.
- **Open or conditional:** retain as research direction, outside the achieved-result ranking.
- **Unsupported or contradicted overclaim:** retain the correction and supporting counterevidence, not the overclaim as a candidate.

Mark each primary comparison as full relevant theorem text, relevant sections, abstract/metadata only, or inaccessible. Record unresolved close comparators explicitly. “No identical statement located” is not “proved novel.”

### E. Research usefulness and exposition readiness

Ask what question the theorem settles and who could use it independently of the original FFT search. A coherent theorem with applications can outrank a more exotic but isolated construction. Distinguish the best substantive research package from the cleanest near-term short draft: these need not be the same item.

## Ordering rule

First exclude unresolved achieved-result claims. Among internally supported candidates, favor a clear substantive increment and a natural scalable question. Use generality, sharpness and independent usefulness to break ties. Then account for concrete prior-art overlap and the severity of remaining priority checks. The resulting order is an editorial judgment with explicit reasons, not an objective numerical measurement.

The sheet should expose separate mathematical-status, novelty-status, scope and priority fields. A lower rank must not silently change an accepted theorem to “false,” “known,” or “rejected.” Where two candidates trade different strengths, record the tradeoff and the alternate use-case ordering rather than inventing certainty.

## Mandatory distinctions for this corpus

- Exact-three Green length is established for the specified **five-by-five** blocks over a quadratic extension. The growing ambient family inherits only a lower bound of three. Two summands are nontrivial resolvents and the third is a diagonal correction. No full-family linear-time transform follows.
- The dyadic endpoint result is the sharp **single-matrix cut-rank minimax** N/4+1. Bit reversal is classical. The universal lower bound and matched optimum are the candidate increment. Arbitrary interstage permutations defeat an uncharged product-rank deduction. There is no arithmetic FFT lower bound.
- The Rogers–Szegő candidate concerns the **ordinary derivative discriminant**, fixed-root local splitting, exact leading coefficients and arithmetic consequences. Existing q-discriminants are a different object. Quasi-Hermite/quasi-Laguerre discriminants, q-Lucas factorization and cyclotomic arithmetic are antecedents, not separate new discoveries here. Uniform exclusion of all extraneous content primes remains open.
- Separator A supplies an additive representation obstruction for independently routed bounded-separator inverses. The inherited ordered-separation theorem already handled independent routing, logarithmic cycle-branch counts and an error mechanism. A's tree witness closes an internal non-containment question; classical forest-width theory and the 2025 TQS binary-tree precedent limit the external increment. Uniform conditioning does not prove fixed-tolerance numerical-rank hardness.
- Weyl rank and Hankel rank lower-bound branch counts only in their exact fixed grammars. The corpus itself supplies full-rank Weyl coefficient arrays with linear-time application, refuting a rank-to-general-runtime inference.
- The growing-fiber algorithm discovers and fully certifies the promised intermediate sparsity and writes all outputs; this is stronger than being handed the sparse decomposition. It remains an exact-field, supplied-root, structured-input theorem. The largest recorded operation profile is about 2.4% above its baseline, so asymptotics are not evidence of an observed speed win.
- The all-length approximation theorem has fixed 0<alpha<1/2, a weak absolute l-infinity/l2 error scale, per-fixed-input fresh randomness, exact supplied constants and a theoretical sampler repair. Alpha=1/2, finite precision and practical speed are not proved. Sparse-Fourier comparators must match the norm, probability, length family, signal-to-noise assumptions and explicit output costs before declaring equivalence or separation.
- Fresh comparison materially narrows that approximation's novelty: Hassanieh–Indyk–Katabi–Price, Theorem 4.4 and Lemma 4.3, already yield the same weak accuracy target on dyadic lengths in sublinear sparse-recovery work plus linear explicit-output work, by choosing k of order N^(2alpha) and polynomially small filter error. This is a derived comparison, not their printed headline. It does not yet subsume the all-integer-length construction. [Primary paper](https://people.csail.mit.edu/haitham/Papers/sFFT.pdf)
- The current-Library norm-certified tabulation theorem is a separate, stronger-accuracy candidate: deterministic relative-l2 approximation for every O(log N)-bit, polynomial-dynamic-range input at dyadic lengths, in O_epsilon(N log N/log log N) word operations including cold tables. Its full proof/model was independently inspected in this pass. It is finite-word and approximate, with nonlinear quantization and lookup; its supplied software was not rerun or production-benchmarked. The general precision-schedule corollary needs its exponent supplied or efficiently computable with cost charged. Its novelty comparison remains newer and less mature than several older candidates. Alman's finite-field lookup-transform result is a mechanism precedent, not automatically the same theorem.

## Coverage statement

The restored corpus is much larger than either publication assessment. The 02:08 assessment contains thirteen selected cards. The 05:38 A/B report is a targeted comparison and the 05:58 appendix updates A; neither supplies an all-corpus elimination ledger. A claim that these are the only real candidates is unsupported.

The census distinguishes file indexing, unique-byte counts, unique narrative documents, claim-family identification, actual proof/audit reading and primary-literature comparison. None implies the next. A global catalogue may cover all **identified claim families** without every proof being independently re-proved or every novelty question being cleared. Its sheet and summary must state that limitation prominently.

These are 178 evidence rows, not 178 independent discoveries.
