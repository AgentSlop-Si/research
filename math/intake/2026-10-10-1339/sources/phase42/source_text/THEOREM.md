# Phase 42 — Fourier signature collisions, exact three-row tangent ranks, and product escape

**Date:** 10 October 2026  
**Status:** self-contained restricted exact theorems and exact finite certificates. **No sub-`N log N` FFT and no independently established publication priority.**

## 1. Model and notation

Let `F_N = (omega^(k j))_{k,j=0}^{N-1}`, where `omega` is a primitive complex `N`th root. Normalize it by `1/sqrt(N)` if desired; rank arguments are unaffected.

A weighted Hamiltonian cycle `W` is an `N x N` monomial matrix with exactly one nonzero per row and column and underlying permutation one directed `N`-cycle. Its weights are **arbitrary nonzero complex numbers**, not necessarily unit phases. Write its holonomy `H` for the product of its `N` weights, and suppose `H != 1`. The cyclic Green operator is

`G = (I-W)^(-1) = (I+W+...+W^(N-1))/(1-H)`.

We study sums of `q` such Green operators with any per-term invertible diagonal input/output scalings; the uniform theorem even allows different input and output coordinate permutations **inside each summand**. The fixed `N=128` certificate is for exactly the three recorded cycle orders, with per-term diagonal gauges and arbitrary *external* permutations/scalings. Extra independent per-term permutations alter those three orders and are **not** covered by the fixed certificate.

## 2. The Green projective-sector lemma

**Lemma 42.1.** Fix `r` distinct output rows `R`. The columns of the restricted matrix `G[R,:]` lie in at most `r` distinct one-dimensional subspaces, with a combinatorial class label determined entirely by the cyclic order of `R` and the input vertex.

**Proof.** Write the vertices in directed cycle order as `c_0,...,c_(N-1)`. Choose potentials `p_i` equal to the product of edge weights along the path from `c_0` to `c_i`, with `p_0=1`. If `i,j` denote cycle positions, the unique-path Green formula is

`G[c_i,c_j] = (p_i/p_j) H^[i<j] / (1-H)`.

Fix reference row `c_a`. Dividing row `i` by row `a` gives

`G[c_i,c_j] / G[c_a,c_j] = (p_i/p_a) H^([i<j]-[a<j])`.

As the input vertex moves cyclically, the vector of indicator differences changes only when it crosses one of the `r` selected output positions. Thus there are at most `r` projective row signatures. Output diagonal gauges alter each signature but not its classes; input diagonal gauges alter only the scalar multiplying a column. Arbitrary input/output permutations similarly relabel rows, columns, and cyclic positions. QED.

## 3. A uniform all-length additive obstruction

**Theorem 42.2 (all weighted cycles).** Let

`M_N = sum_(s=1)^q L_s P_s (I-W_s)^(-1) Q_s R_s`,

where each `W_s` is an admissible weighted Hamiltonian cycle, `P_s,Q_s` are arbitrary permutations and `L_s,R_s` arbitrary nonzero diagonal matrices. If `M_N` is equivalent to `F_N` under an additional pair of outer monomial matrices, necessarily

`N <= q (q+1)^q`.

**Proof.** Under the purported equivalence select the `q+1` physical output rows corresponding to consecutive Fourier frequency rows `0,1,...,q`. For each summand, Lemma 42.1 partitions the input columns into at most `q+1` projective-signature classes. The joint signature has at most `(q+1)^q` classes. For any one class, the `q+1`-row columns of `M_N` belong to the span of `q` fixed vectors, one contributed by each Green summand. Their rank is at most `q`. If one class contained `q+1` or more input columns, this would contradict the Fourier restriction to these rows: after invertible row/column scalings and permutations it is a Vandermonde matrix in `q+1` distinct `N`th roots, whose determinant is `prod_(a<b)(z_b-z_a) != 0`. Therefore each class holds at most `q` columns, and `N <= q (q+1)^q`. QED.

For `q=2`, `N<=18`; for `q=3`, `N<=192`; for `q=4`, `N<=2500`. In particular, **three additive Hamiltonian Green inverses cannot represent a general DFT at any size `N>=193`**, regardless of complex amplitudes, conditioning, or the independent coordinate gauges just listed. This lower bound on the *number of terms*, `q=Omega(log N/log log N)`, is **not a general Fourier arithmetic-complexity lower bound**. Related rank-separator arguments in earlier project phases can give different or stronger restrictions under different circuit assumptions.

## 5. A three-row phase-geometry refinement

Fix three output rows to correspond to Fourier rows `0,1,2`, and continue to use **unit-phase** Green weights. Each cycle has at most three projective signatures, so at most 27 joint signature groups; the project's seeded graphs have 9–11 realized groups at `N=16,32,64,128`.

Within one joint signature, write the three output coordinates of a column as `y = V t`, where `V` is the `3 x 3` matrix of three fixed projective signature vectors and `|t_s|` is constant as the input column varies (though the constant may differ with `s`). Under a Fourier equivalence, the same columns equal `c(z) D (1,z,z^2)^T` with `|z|=1`, `D` invertible diagonal, and distinct roots `z`.

If `V` is nonsingular, then `t=c(z)V^(-1)D(1,z,z^2)^T`. Equality of the fixed squared magnitudes of two components of `t` gives a Laurent polynomial in `z` of degrees between -2 and 2. Unless this polynomial vanishes identically, multiplying by `z^2` gives a **nonzero ordinary polynomial of degree at most 4**. Hence **at most four distinct Fourier frequencies per nonexceptional signature**. If `V` is singular over the complex numbers, its projective span meets the conic `(1,z,z^2)` in at most two points. This provides a separate conditional three-row obstruction.

A simple necessary condition for an *exceptional* invertible signature is that the complex numbers

`conjugate(adj(V)[s,0]) * adj(V)[s,2]` (s=0,1,2)

have identical arguments after division by **positive real** squared summand magnitudes. Thus, if any pair has a nonreal ratio, **no choice of external row scalings or nonzero complex scalar summand weights** makes the Laurent constraint identically zero. `phase42_signature_gate.py` uses nonzero imaginary cross-products to certify this algebraically at two split primes, grouping columns by graph-order sectors rather than by potentially colliding modular values. At the seeded `N=64,128` configurations, 11 signature classes produce capacity `4*11=44 < N`, excluding even arbitrary external column permutations/scalings for the **specific selected triple of output rows**. Unlike §4, this test alone does not certify every possible outer row permutation.

## 6. Exact three-row Jacobian comparison

For the sum `S=G_1+G_2+G_3`, with unit-modulus weights parametrized by `2N+1` independent real cycle holonomies and fixed summand coefficients, define the anchored ratios

`Q_(r,j) = S_(r,j)*S_(0,0)/(S_(r,0)*S_(0,j))`, `r=1,2`, `j=1,...,N-1`.

The first two rows offer `2N-2` real conditions, and the first three rows offer `4N-4` real conditions. Phase 41 established full rank `2N-2` for the first pair. This phase computes all three-row derivatives exactly at *the same* rational-Gaussian unit-circle parameter point:

| N | Real phase variables | Two-row rank | Three-row rank | Increment |

| 8 | 17 | 14 | 17 | 3 |
| 16 | 33 | 30 | 33 | 3 |

Both split-prime embeddings certify identical ranks and nonzero full-column square minors. The corresponding rational characteristic-zero Jacobian minors are nonzero. By the implicit-function theorem, **near these certified regular points**, keeping the first two rows' projective ratios fixed leaves a smooth *three-real-dimensional* family of phase perturbations, and the full three-row image has local dimension `2N+1`.

This is a **local parameter-dimension obstruction**, not an all-couplings proof excluding the particular Fourier three-row target. If independent complex summand coefficients are also tuned, more real parameters become available.

## 7. Noncommuting products escape: constructive local three-row control

For two stages, take `M=(G_4+G_5+G_6)(G_1+G_2+G_3)` with independently ordered cycles in each stage and unit-modulus complex edge weights. Each stage has `2N+1` independent phase holonomies in the recorded graph normal form. There are `4N+2` real edge-phase parameters.

Explicit determinant witnesses exist in `phase42_product_jacobian_audit.json`. Independent complex numerical finite differences at `N=8,16` validate the product derivative formula to relative error below `7e-10` on the tested directions. A separate finite-field control shows the product turns an otherwise forced rank-3 Fourier-minor witness into rank **4**, while **both sampled full Weyl tests still fail**. Therefore this architecture escapes the additive obstruction and locally controls *three-row projective ratios*, but **no particular Fourier target has been proved reachable, no exact full DFT factorization is known, and no sub-`N log N` arithmetic or bit bound has been obtained**.

## 8. Complexity and limitations

- One weighted Hamiltonian Green solve costs `O(N)` finite-field/complex arithmetic with its weights and inverse holonomy already available. A fixed two-stage product of three-term sums costs `O(N)` **online arithmetic** given all six weight lists.
- Determining exact weights, testing local Jacobians, storing a candidate circuit, and controlling complex numerical precision have costs not included in that online bound.
- The `N=128` exhaustive combinatorial audit is *offline verification*, not a faster transform.
- The additive theorem does not cover products of Green sums, nonlinear data-dependent workflows, fully general Green matrices, or circuit classes beyond the stated Hamiltonian-resolvent model.
- Neither local submersion nor small residual alone proves that a prescribed Fourier target lies in the image.
- The analysis builds on standard Vandermonde determinants, cyclic permutation resolvents, projective invariants and Jacobian rank tests. **Research novelty and publication priority have not been independently established.**
