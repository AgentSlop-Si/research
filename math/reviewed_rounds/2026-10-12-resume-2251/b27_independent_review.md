The original parameter is retained:

The Hermite convention is He_0=1, He_1=x, He_(n+1)=x He_n-n He_(n-1).

## Why the finite-A specialization proves characteristic-zero nonvanishing

Let K=Q(zeta_57) and write each original discriminant as a rational function of zeta_57 with denominators generated by the original W denominator and g. The map Z[zeta_57] to F_229 sending zeta_57 to 3 is well-defined: 3 is primitive of order 57 and 229 does not divide 57. All denominators used are nonzero at that map, so it extends to the needed localization.

If a characteristic-zero discriminant were zero, its cleared numerator would be zero in Z[zeta_57] and would reduce to zero under this map. The computed gcd=1 of the reduced polynomial and derivative gives a nonzero reduced discriminant, a contradiction. Thus every required finite-A characteristic-zero discriminant is nonzero. No assumption that a general cyclotomic quotient is a field is needed.

For each fixed A the discriminant lies in Q[tau]. Therefore vanishing at one primitive complex embedding would imply vanishing at all conjugates. Nonvanishing proved at the certified conjugate, or in K by the specialization argument, proves it at every primitive embedding. Any characteristic-zero ambient field containing q has an injective copy of K; an embedding of the entire field into C is unnecessary.

## Audit of the uniform A bridge

The accepted annulus argument has been checked at the level of its algebra and hypotheses.

Nonreal tau excludes the repeated-zero cases: tau=0 for odd A, tau=-1/A for even A. At a nonzero repeated root, consecutive Hermite polynomials cannot both vanish, by descent to He_0=1. Differentiation gives
J'=(1+A tau)He_A+A x(1-tau)He_(A-1),
so elimination is legitimate and gives
y=x²=A tau+(A+1)+(A+1)/(tau-1).

For A>=3 the parity Jacobi representation has real nonzero tridiagonal off-diagonal entries and last diagonal perturbation A(tau-1). A repeated eigenvalue has a one-dimensional eigenspace and a Jordan chain. Complex symmetry gives u^T u=0, while the nonzero last coordinate and the triangle inequality give 0<t=|u_last|²/||u||²<=1/2. The distinct Hermitian identity gives Im y=A Im tau t. Taking imaginary parts of the eliminated formula therefore yields
A t=A-(A+1)/rho, rho=|tau-1|²,
and hence 1+1/A<rho<=2+2/A.

No diagonalizability assumption or finite-A extrapolation occurs. For A>=8, 2+2/A<=9/4, so the strict d57 certificate excludes every such A. For A=1,2, the discriminants are 4tau and 4(1+2tau)^3, nonzero by nonreality. The independent five finite-A tests fill exactly A=3..7. Thus d57 is complete for all A. For the other 76 middle orders, rho>8/3 excludes every A>=3 directly.

## Inherited ranges and endpoints checked at b27

Write b=2m+1 with m=13, d=2h+1 and v=pi/d. Define
s_j=product_(r=1)^j(1-q^(2r-1))/(1-q^(2r)), S_j=sum_(r=0)^j s_r, E=product_(r=1)^h(1-q^(2r)).
The original derivative recurrence and original W cancellation give g=B_m S_m and tau=S_h/S_m=d/(E S_m); this is a proved identity, not a new normalization. All denominator product lengths are below d and q² has order d.

In the principal embedding, s_j=a_j exp(-ijv) with a_j>0 for j<=h and hv<pi/2. Hence S_m is nonzero and the imaginary part of (S_h-S_m)conjugate(S_m) is a strictly negative sum. Thus tau is nonreal. Cyclotomic automorphisms commute with complex conjugation, which transports nonreality but not metric bounds.

Lower range: reflection gives s_(h-j)=s_h conjugate(s_j), |s_h|=1, so rho=|S_(h-m-1)|²/|S_m|². The values |S_j|² strictly increase because their increment is a_j²+2 sum_(r<j)a_j a_r cos((j-r)v)>0. Therefore rho<=1 when d<=2b+1=55, including equality at d55. Together with d>b, this covers exactly odd d29..55. These hypotheses remain valid at m13.

Tail: the inspected accepted review states the general threshold d>=15(m+1). The following direct estimate confirms applicability independently of any finite b25 data. For j<=m, the positive sine-product identity
sin²(2rv)-sin((2r-1)v)sin((2r+1)v)=sin²v
implies a_j²<=sin v/sin((2j+1)v). When d>=15(m+1), u=(2j+1)v<2pi/15. Using pi²<10 and sin u>=u-u³/6 gives sin u>(131/135)u; sin v<v then gives a_j²<135/[131(2j+1)]. The midpoint integral bound for the convex function x^(-1/2) yields
|S_m|²<270(m+1)/131.
Also |E|²=d and E S_h=d give |S_h|²=d. Consequently
|tau|²>131d/[270(m+1)]>=131/18.
The exact positive comparison (101/18)²-4(131/18)=769/324 implies rho>(sqrt(131/18)-1)²>8/3. All sine arguments used have the required positive signs.

At m13 the threshold is d>=210; the first odd order is 211. Thus lower 29..55, new middle 57..209 and tail 211 onward cover every odd d>27 with no missing endpoint.
