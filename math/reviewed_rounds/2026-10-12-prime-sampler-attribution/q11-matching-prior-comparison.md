## Conclusion

The accepted full-target bound remains valid within its reviewed scope. Its principal mechanisms have close, exact precedents: the composite Fourier rank-one grid, canonical round-robin matchings, weighted-cycle inversion with zero weights, and scalar forward/backward state-space scans. Attribution should be stronger than a general reference to butterfly methods.

The construction combines those mechanisms into 20q+1 permitted additive branches, including a paid diagonal correction, for the frozen Q11 family. No inspected primary statement was identified that already supplies this complete model-and-resource contract by a model-preserving specialization. This finite comparison does not establish historical novelty, logical independence from the literature, minimality, or publication significance. The assembly may reasonably be described as an explicit application of classical low-rank blocking, matching and scan techniques. Whether the precise packing statement or an equivalent broader theorem was previously published remains unresolved.

The result is an improvement over the project's dense additive Green baseline. Its Theta(N^(3/2)) direct application is slower than the accepted two-DFT O(N log N) evaluator. No FFT-speed or bit-speed claim follows.

### Canonical matching rounds

Lambrechts, Ficker, Goossens and Spieksma, Round-robin tournaments generated by the Circle Method have maximum carry-over, Mathematical Programming 172 (2018), 277–302, Section 2.3, p.281, specifies the canonical pairing: for even n, the fixed team n plays round r, while remaining teams i,j are paired when i+j=2r modulo n−1. Put n=s+1 and delete the fixed team. This gives exactly Q11's singleton t and pairs {t+d,t−d} on odd s, after relabeling. Thus the matching schedule itself is established, including its exact modular rule. The paper is used as an accessible primary account, not as an assertion that its authors originated the Circle Method. Its carry-over theorem has no bearing on Green branch count. [Author-hosted paper](https://fspieksma.win.tue.nl/papers/MathPro2018paper.pdf).

### Weighted cycle inverses and diagonal scaling

Huhtanen and Perämäki, Factoring Matrices into the Product of Circulant and Diagonal Matrices, JFAA 21 (2015), 1018–1033, Theorem 2.9, author-manuscript p.6, explicitly inverts I+DS for a cyclic shift S and arbitrary diagonal D, subject only to nonsingularity. Substitution D=-diag(w), with orientation adjusted, gives the individual Q11 inverse, including zero weights. Theorem 2.10's conversion to a nonzero constant cyclic weight assumes invertible diagonal data and takes a root; it does not cover Q11's zero-edge nilpotent branches by that argument. The paper's principal 2n−1 result is multiplicative. It is not a theorem about this number of additive diagonal-ended resolvents. The inverse identity itself needs no extra root in L. [Author manuscript, Theorems 2.9–2.10](https://math.aalto.fi/~mhuhtane/manu.pdf).

### Scalar state space and resolvent sums

Chandrasekaran, Epperly and Govindarajan, Graph-Induced Rank Structures and their Representations, arXiv:1911.05858v3, supplies a particularly close comparison. Equations (2.4), (2.6)–(2.8) give forward/backward generators and scan multiplication. Section 3.1 constructs cycle-semiseparable representations by modifying and restoring corner blocks. Thus both the scan executor and a related resolvent-sum expression are established, not merely similar terminology. General state dimensions, block endpoints, external shifts and a free diagonal are not scalar legal-branch counts. Q11 specializes to zero-separated scalar paths, changes the ordering by round, and explicitly encodes the diagonal in one branch. General transition matrices cannot simply be counted as independent scalar modes. [Primary body, Section 2.2 and Eq. (2.10)](https://arxiv.org/html/1911.05858v3).

### Green and quasiseparable vocabulary

This accounts for the structural terminology. It does not identify quasiseparable order with a number of diagonal-ended Hamiltonian-cycle inverse summands. ## Exact algebra connecting the precedents

The calculations in this section are translations and checks of the accepted proof. They are not claims that the cited authors state the complete Q11 theorem.

### The Q11 grid refines the classical grid

Using the primary paper's labels, N=mn, rows i=a+nu and columns j=b+mv satisfy

    ij = ab + nbu + mav + mnuv,
    omega^(ij) = omega^(ab+nbu) omega^(mav).

The source Fourier matrix includes a global 1/sqrt(mn) normalization, which does not change rank. The displayed unnormalized factors follow directly from the exponent identity without a root-extraction step. Every residue-n row class crossed with a residue-m column class has rank one. Take n=5q and m=q. Q11 uses the row classes S_a={a+5qu}. Each column class modulo q splits into five column classes modulo 5q. Restricting the classical rank-one block to any one of these subclasses gives exactly an S_a-by-S_b block. Thus Q11's same partition on both axes is obtained by refinement, rather than requiring a new low-rank Fourier phenomenon.

Equivalently, s=5q has s^2=5N, so (i-a)(j-b) is a multiple of N for i in S_a and j in S_b. This yields the factors omega^(ib) and omega^(a(j-b)). Replacing omega by omega^(-1) and multiplying by alpha_i on the left and alpha_j on the right preserves rank one. These chirp diagonals are nonzero. They are ordinary two-sided diagonal scalings, not diagonal similarity and not an additional transform.

### Masks and zero transitions are substantive model permissions

A nilpotent weighted path on a listed component has

    (I-W)^(-1) = I+W+...+W^(N-1).

When live weights equal one, the corresponding entries are one precisely from an earlier row to a later column in the path. Left and right masks on disjoint halves isolate a rectangular outer product. Zero edges between components permit independent factors on separate paired class blocks in the same branch. The same scalar recurrence produces the singleton triangular block. These facts explain why the state-space comparison is exact at the branch-executor level.

For nonsingular endpoints, a branch H=L(I-W)^(-1)R obeys H^(-1)=R^(-1)(I-W)L^(-1). In the matching construction many endpoints are zero, so that equivalent sparse-inverse description is unavailable for those branches. No comparison requiring invertible endpoint scalings may silently discard the masks. A common permutation used to describe a branch is only a proof-side relabeling: conjugating the entire weighted path back to the original coordinates leaves both endpoints diagonal. It does not authorize independent input/output permutations.

A Hamiltonian structural cycle completed by zero-weight edges is not a genuinely nonzero Hamiltonian weighted cycle. Here every effective graph is a union of directed paths, the holonomy is zero, and all inverse guards are one. The result cannot be transferred to an all-nonzero-edge grammar or to a fixed cycle order on that basis.

### What the packing step actually adds

For any odd number s of coordinate classes, suppose each ordered class block is a sum of r supplied outer products. Add any desired coordinate diagonal. In each canonical matching round, disjoint pairs allow their independent block factors to occupy one forward and one reverse scalar scan per mode. The singleton class is handled by the two triangular scans. Their duplicate coordinate diagonal is corrected once globally, using W=0 with diagonal endpoints. The count is consequently 2rs+1.

Q11 takes r=2 and s=5q. The extra work specific to the accepted construction is the explicit assignment of factors, original-coordinate Hamiltonian descriptors, zero boundaries, singleton bookkeeping and diagonal calibration, followed by a paid compressed implementation. This finite derivation explains the assembly from classical pieces; it does not certify whether the general packing observation has a pre-existing name or theorem elsewhere. A predecessor need not mention Q11 or use this particular proof: any broader theorem with a correct specialization could subsume it.

## Complexity comparison

Low-rank block multiplication alone already explains the N^(3/2) scale: s^2 blocks of size q-by-q and constant rank cost O(s^2 q)=O(Ns). The matching construction makes that block computation fit the additive Green grammar; it is not needed merely to discover a subquadratic matrix-vector algorithm from the supplied rank-one blocks.

The O(N) preparation is stronger than explicitly storing arbitrary factors for all blocks only because the accepted Fourier factors share four length-N tables with formulaic addresses. A generic r-rank block matrix requires its own factors to be supplied or computed; the generic 2rs+1 packing observation does not give every such matrix O(N) preparation. All expanded factor occurrences remain Theta(Ns), and each application's accesses and routing are paid. No primary source's construction cost is imported as a substitute for this proof.

The inherited identity also expresses Bx by two ordinary DFT applications and diagonal operations. Accordingly, the accepted O(N log N) two-DFT comparison remains the relevant faster evaluator. Historical priority for a branch-count upper and superiority as a Fourier algorithm are separate questions. Neither classical butterfly nor displacement literature is needed to manufacture a speed claim that the packet expressly disclaims.
