## Accepted restricted statements

For the prescribed positive-sign, bit-reversed-input DIT partial-FFT base, every centered relative shear fixes coordinate 1. The rationally closed algebra generated by these shears and arbitrary diagonals preserves that singleton. No invertible correction in this algebra can lift r-covariance to any s-covariance with s>r, at any width or depth. The full residue-fiber commutant is larger: ordinary FFT continuation succeeds there, and adding a translation that moves the singleton escapes this particular obstruction.

For N=2^w and 1<=r<s<=w over C or a field containing a primitive N-th root, put h=2^(s-r). Every standard-base lift has the normal form C=A M_s M_r^-1, with A commuting with Z_s. This includes singular solutions; invertibility is exactly invertibility of the independent blocks of A. Every inverse column of an invertible lift has support a positive multiple of h.

Thus a correction that is a product of factors whose inverses have at most K_j nonzeros per column, with invertible monomial routing, must satisfy product K_j>=h. At an actual evolving base G_r M_r, the necessary condition is S product K_j>=h, where S is the maximum column support of G_r^-1. That base qualification is essential. The weighted-cycle decomposition has N/h cycles globally, each of length h.

## What remains open

The conclusions do not cover arbitrary additive sums of inverse-sparse factors or auxiliary-variable, Schur-complement and scattering networks whose effective inverses can be dense. Different covariant bases can already be lifted. Expressing a correction with sparse matrices or Green inverses does not by itself prove a linear-cost solver or preparation procedure.

A proposed escape must specify the enlarged grammar, verify the exact lift along the actual produced ladder, and charge all coefficient generation, routing, fiber work, inverse application and normalization. Uniform O(N) work per doubling would give the conditional O(N log log N) target only under those full hypotheses. Bit complexity, numerical stability, practical speed and historical priority remain separate. Neither review proves a universal Fourier arithmetic lower bound or grants novelty clearance.

The first review also corrects the manuscript’s intermediate covariance equation and clarifies the 2-adic endpoint conventions while preserving the original source. It credits Hull and Dobell for the classical affine-period ingredient. Its 111 fresh exact modular lift-pair controls distinguish the restricted algebra from the full-commutant positive control. The second review proves the normal form and support bounds, with 40 fresh exact cases and byte-identical cold results. The proofs supply their general scope; finite controls validate conventions. Neither independent review executes supplied author or historical code.
