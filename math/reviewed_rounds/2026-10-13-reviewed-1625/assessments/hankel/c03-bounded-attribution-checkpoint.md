## The material mathematical boundary

The retained accepted proof fixes formal degree (m,m) even after actual-degree drops and obtains universal integral identities before specialization. That scope includes singular matrices, zero resultant, characteristic two and zero-divisor rings. A full-degree field theorem or a coprime chart cannot replace those statements.

On the field chart w != 0 and det(A) != 0, the accepted bridge sets
  alpha = (-1)^m w^(m(m-1)) det(A),
  beta = (-1)^m w^(m^2) det(B),
  u = f/alpha, h = g-beta*u.
Then u is monic of degree m and h has degree below m, allowing h=0. With H from the expansion of h/u at infinity and epsilon_m=(-1)^(m(m-1)/2), the existing accepted identity is
  R = epsilon_m alpha^m det(H).
For w != 1 its residual form is
  V(w+w^(-1)) = (-1)^(m(m+1)/2) (det A)^m det(H)/(w-1)^(m^2).
These are retained accepted formulas, not newly certified 1989 quotations. The stated substitution and powers agree with that accepted record.

When w != 0,1 on this field chart and V vanishes, R and det(H) vanish and u,h are noncoprime. Thus imposing coprimality silently removes precisely the determinant-zero cases needed for the recognition statement. A coprime-only 1989 result may serve as a restricted historical component comparison, never as a complete citation for that recognition equivalence.

## Independent primary checkpoint for the noncoprime route

Garcia-Armas, Ghorpade and Ram, arXiv:1011.1760v1, Section 3, separately define Padé pairs and their coprime subset. Lemma 3.2 and Definition 3.3 define the expansion and Hankel matrix for every Padé pair. Proposition 3.4 states the Bezoutian factorization on that larger domain; its coefficient proof introduces no coprimality condition. The immediately preceding discussion expressly notes that some literature uses the narrower hypothesis. Section 2 identifies the relevant Bezoutian convention. This supports the retained all-pair field bridge. It does not by itself claim the universal-ring or padded C03 theorem.

## Ring and endpoint safeguards

Over a general ring, the accepted normalization requires w and det(A) to be units, or an explicitly stated localization; nonzero alone is insufficient. The universal identity is independently established before localization. Full-degree gcd nullity over a field must not be advertised as the arbitrary-ring rank statement or as the padded degree-drop formula. The both-zero polynomial exception remains separate.

The author's m=1, A=B=[1] illustration is consistent with the retained formulas: f=1-a, g=1-aw, H=[1-w], R=w-1 and V=1. At w=1 the raw Hankel determinant is zero but V(2)=1. The specialized ratio is 0/0 and an inverse-Hankel theorem cannot supply the endpoint value. No formula-image check is needed for this elementary consistency observation; it does not re-open C03 correctness.
