## 2. Accepted scope retained without reopening

Let N=2^k, k>=2, and work over a field containing a primitive N-th root, with characteristic not dividing N. Each Q_i is two-sided quasiseparable of order at most r_i in the natural order: both strict off-diagonal blocks at every prefix cut have rank at most r_i. The result does not require a particular generator chart or nonzero transition coefficients.

For odd k=2m+1, the two equal-size groups are distinguished by the middle bit, and d_N=2^(m+1)-1. For even k=2m, the groups are agreement versus disagreement of the two middle bits, and d_N=2^m. Here d_N is the number of membership changes along the natural-index path.

Every interstage permutation must preserve these groups or exchange the two complete groups. Arbitrary permutations within a group are allowed. Bit reversal obeys this condition. All four Fourier blocks indexed by the two row groups and two column groups have rank N/2 at the accepted scope.

The retained conclusion is

    N/2 <= d_N * sum_i r_i.

Thus sum_i r_i is Omega(sqrt(N)); four order-one stages fail for all dyadic N>=128. N=64 merely passes this necessary budget and is not a construction. The finite mixed-shuffle wire certificates use their own explicit scan-network grammar and do not enlarge the all-length theorem.

## 4. Exact interface mapping

This section explains the correspondence to the accepted audit. It is not a newly claimed mathematical contribution.

### Fixed cut versus all path cuts

The accepted audit decomposes a central group into consecutive runs. Each left/right contribution is a submatrix of one natural QS cut block. The total number of contributions is d_N, yielding rank<=d_N r_i in either direction.

Arbitrary within-group permutations preserve this particular cross rank. They need not preserve every natural prefix or the whole path-graph GIRS constant. Consequently, one cannot simply declare every freely routed factor GIRS-r_i on the original path and apply a global product theorem without checking its hypotheses.

At the selected cut, a block multiplication has two summands, so its unexpected-group rank increases by at most the new stage's cross rank. A whole-group swap changes which group is “unexpected.” Tracking that parity is exactly the accepted audit's interface rule. No free partially mixing permutation is justified.

### Compatible projector displacement

Let E be the coordinate projector onto the central group X. For a stage Q, with the basis grouped as X,Y,

    E Q-Q E = [[0,Q_XY],[-Q_YX,0]],
    rank(E Q-Q E)=rank(Q_XY)+rank(Q_YX)<=2 d_N r.

Replacing both projectors by I-E only changes the sign. A preserving permutation P satisfies EP=PE. A complete swap satisfies (I-E)P=PE. Thus one assigns E or I-E at each interface, changing it only when a whole-group swap occurs. Every free permutation then has zero compatible displacement.

For conformable A,B and interface operators L,M,R,

    L(AB)-(AB)R = (LA-AM)B + A(MB-BR).

Iteration charges at most 2 d_N sum_i r_i to the product. The target displacement E_out F-F E_in has rank N: it consists of the two full-rank off-diagonal Fourier blocks when the endpoint projectors agree and of the two full-rank diagonal blocks when they differ. This reproduces N<=2 d_N sum_i r_i, exactly the accepted inequality.

This comparison also isolates the computational mismatch. A projector commutator is not a unique encoding: EI-IE=0 although I is nonzero, and arbitrary block-diagonal matrices lie in its kernel. Rank of this displacement alone cannot reconstruct the matrix or price its matvec. A low-rank generator algorithm requiring an invertible displacement map cannot be imported merely by substituting E.

### Endpoints

The accepted theorem fixes the special central row and column groups, up to the permitted complete swaps and within-group reorderings. Nonzero diagonal endpoint scaling preserves each selected block rank, but this observation is not a license to add arbitrary uncharged endpoint operators to the grammar.

Independent arbitrary endpoint permutations can carry a central half to the even indices. If N is divisible by 4, the even-row/even-column Fourier half-block has rank N/4, not N/2: its entries are (omega^4)^(ab), with each residue modulo N/4 repeated twice. The N/4-square Fourier subblock is nonsingular and the repetitions give the exact rank. This explains why cardinality alone and unrestricted endpoint freedom cannot replace the special central-block premise.

A permutation swapping just one X-index with one Y-index already has cross rank one while nominal QS-stage order is zero. Conversely, a complete group swap has full fixed-E commutator rank but zero transported-projector displacement. These two elementary controls distinguish forbidden mixing from allowed parity transport.
