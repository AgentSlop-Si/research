# Corrections, model boundaries and lineage

The original submitted documents are immutable evidence identified by their hashes in SOURCE_MANIFEST.json. The public notes are edited scientific derivatives with different hashes. Earlier snapshots are not replaced. A corrected or newly accepted conclusion does not retroactively change what an earlier source said.

## Necklace Green transport

- The trace coefficient of a length-k necklace is its least period r_C, not k. Repeated edges retain multiplicity. The polynomial identity also covers zero weights.
- A sparse Gram representation divides by baseline weights and therefore needs all those weights nonzero. The polynomial differentiation route handles zero baselines instead. The low-rank statement is rank(H-Delta)<=L(k), not rank(H)<=L(k).
- For horizon K=n+c, the accepted arithmetic/construction bound is O(N 2^c). The O(log N)-bit address contract is retained for K=O(log N); unrestricted much larger K can need O(K)-bit addresses.
- The determinant prefix via Newton identities additionally needs characteristic zero or greater than K. It is a proof consequence, not a separately implemented fresh test.
- For the canonical carrier tr(B^k), characteristic p dividing k makes every first derivative and the Hessian identically zero. This rejects that carrier in those characteristics; it does not reject the complex conjecture or arbitrary orbit coefficients.
- Full rank of a carrier is not an inexpensive Fourier congruence. The exact seed/intertwining identities and a uniformly generated low-work transport remain absent. Q31's conditional derivative reduction remains accepted while its constructive source remains open.
- The locality bound N<=s d k 2^max(0,k-n-1) constrains bounded row/column marking only. It is not an unrestricted circuit bound.
- The archived independent result had nine groups, including source-integrity hashes. The portable mathematical result has eight groups; it removes only that non-mathematical archive dependency. No control is silently claimed twice.

## Real-collinear unitary sums and phase averaging

- The accepted exclusion covers every integer N>=4, arbitrary finite q and signed real coefficients multiplied by one common complex scalar. It is not limited to even N or positive coefficients.
- Every term is an ordinary nonsingular inverse. A zero coefficient does not legalize a singular inverse. Single Hamiltonian-cycle support provides common diagonal entries; unitarity alone does not.
- The source proof and independent phase-averaging addition have separate identities. The original phase note's historical pending-review label is superseded by final acceptance at source hash 880c45d731f4d5296f7f73ba637838542ada72dd321adb3b2b553458b5723fda. Both the explicit construction and the full independent proof are retained in sanitized scientific form.
- N=2 has a one-term real witness and N=3 a nine-term signed-real witness. N=1 is conditional on admitting a weighted one-vertex loop. These are actual legal Green representations, not merely cross-ratio tests.
- Complex span universality uses ternary diagonal characters and an explicit 3^(N-1)-term construction. It does not claim a minimal term count or an efficient evaluator. Binary phases alias opposite directed entries.
- The common diagonal of F_N diag(omega^(-b^2)) is one. The minus sign matters.
- The submitted Theorem B overlaps the already accepted Schur v28 complete-extension treewidth bound ceil(N/4)-1. It is not another new theorem. The signed magnetic-bank model remains distinct and is not classified by this unitary-cycle result.

## Schur v30 and completion refinements

- Density forces reverse orders for an uncoupled direct sum of two triangular transfers. The reduction invokes the earlier v29 opposite-sweep denominator-support theorem; the inherited Omega(N^(3/2)) support theorem is not a second v30 discovery or an unrestricted solve-cost bound.
- The LIS inequality rank(R)>=floor(LIS/2) is necessary. Two reversed consecutive half-blocks have LIS=2 while a shared half-by-half zero rectangle forces rank at least N/2. Do not label LIS a sufficient or universally sharp characterization.
- A strict-upper minimum-rank completion and an invertible triangular residual are logically distinct. The independently accepted row-tail and block constructions now prove both for every totally nonsingular matrix, over the same field. Schur realization may still use dense routing and denominators.
- The q>=floor(N/2) same-branch bound requires a totally nonsingular target in the actual field and relevant coordinates. It survives a singular triangular branch pivot if the whole bordered system is invertible, by Jacobi complementary minors. An A^(-1) correction formula still cannot be used for singular A.
- The full-rank cross-branch bound q>=N has different hypotheses and does not require total nonsingularity. Count every coupling and routing edge; arbitrary dense endpoint maps can hide the target.
- Complex F4 in simultaneous order (0,2,1,3) admits q=1 via X=(1,-1,-i,0)^T and Y=(0,1,1,1). Its residual diagonal is (1,2,2i,i). This disproves the arbitrary-order composite-Fourier extrapolation of the floor(N/2) theorem.
- The submitted power-of-two same_branch_min_interface table is unsupported by the stated totally-nonsingular theorem. This does not assert that every displayed value is false in every prescribed order. The original table remains traceable through its source results.json hash, but is not redistributed as a validated result.
- Complex prime Fourier is totally nonsingular. Prime length by itself does not imply the same over an arbitrary finite field. Exhaustive finite-field full-spark evidence is (5,11) and (7,29), totaling 3,682 minors. The pairs (9,19), (11,23), (13,53) have explicit singular minors. Individual natural-order constructions at those pairs may still be correct.
- Earlier complete-extension treewidth and fixed-minor-free size bounds, and the planar O(N^2) realization, remain accepted in their original scope. They are cross-referenced, not superseded or duplicated.

Historical priority remains unresolved. Classical orbit enumeration, automatic differentiation, Cayley transforms, finite-character filtering, cross-ratios, partial-matrix rank and complementary-minor identities retain their classical attribution.
