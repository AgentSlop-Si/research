# Primary references and comparison scope

Links checked 10 October 2026. The following are bibliographic references and bounded comparisons, not redistributed publisher content or proof of worldwide novelty. Independent scientific notes state exactly which identities they use.

## Inverse-submatrix and approximation lineage

- Raf Vandebril and Marc Van Barel, A short note on the nullity theorem, Report TW 397 (2004), Section 3 and Corollary 1. The inverse-submatrix rank relation is classical and directly yields the structural part of the inverse-defect certificate. [Institutional report](https://www.cs.kuleuven.be/publicaties/rapporten/tw/TW397.pdf)
- Ruitian Lang, The Inverse of a Nearly Banded Matrix (2008), Theorem 2.1 and equation (3.2). The nullity identity and spectral low-rank approximation facts support the independent reduction; no claim is made that Lang states the exact present inverse-defect application. [Author preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/0812.0172)
- Emmanuel Candès and Justin Romberg, Quantitative Robust Uncertainty Principles and Optimally Sparse Decompositions. Random-support Fourier concentration is related context, not the identical deterministic d-dimensional result. [Author preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0411273)
- Zeev Dvir and Allen Liu, Fourier and Circulant Matrices are Not Rigid. Regular rigidity and Fourier non-rigidity concern different sparsity conditions from the present row-only inverse decomposition. No general FFT lower-bound consequence follows. [Author preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.07334)
- Venkat Chandrasekaran and coauthors, Rank-Sparsity Incoherence for Matrix Decomposition. Relevant background for sparse-plus-low-rank identifiability, not an established priority match for the inverse approximation theorem. [Author preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/0906.2220)

## Recurrences and the general-transform benchmark

- Stavros Garoufalidis and Christoph Koutschan, Twisting q-holonomic sequences by complex roots of unity, ISSAC 2012. The paper proves preservation under root-of-unity twisting and rational substitution. That general result does not by itself establish priority for the exact Phase21 specialization. [Author preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/1201.3353)
- OpenAI Math, Problem 130. The public research note states a uniform exact-complex DFT/convolution bound O(N(log N)^(1−10^−13)) with the supplied-root model and scalar preparation accounted for. It is a separate general-transform benchmark, not a peer-reviewed priority certificate for these source families. [Official research note](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/lean/docs/130.md)

## Multiplicative characters, projected Fourier and Gauss sums

- NIST Digital Library of Mathematical Functions, Section 27.10, equations 27.10.4–5 and 27.10.9–12. These state the Ramanujan-sum and Gauss-character Fourier identities used in the modal decomposition. [Official reference](https://dlmf.nist.gov/27.10)
- László Tóth and Pentti Haukkanen, The discrete Fourier transform of r-even functions, equation (7) and Proposition 2. The gcd-even/Ramanujan transform and Parseval are direct antecedents for the C=1 component and error identity. [Author preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/1009.5281)
- Andrzej K. Brodzik and Richard Tolimieri, Fourier and Zak transforms of multiplicative characters (2021), Sections 2–4. Character DFTs, Gauss sums and CRT combinations supply context; this does not establish priority for the precise dyadic cutoff algorithm. [Author preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.09295)
- Richard Tolimieri, Multiplicative characters and the discrete Fourier transform, Advances in Applied Mathematics 7(3), 344–380 (1986), DOI 10.1016/0196-8858(86)90039-4. The publisher abstract describes decomposition into multiplicative-character subspaces and its connection to multiplicative complexity. Full-text equivalence with Phase33 remains unverified. [Publisher record](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0196885886900394)

The bridge support identity and additive-shell comparator are given with self-contained derivations. Their constituent support counting, geometric sums, singular values and ordinary FFTs are classical. Correctness checks are not historical-priority checks.
