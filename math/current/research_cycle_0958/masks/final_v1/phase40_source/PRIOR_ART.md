# Prior-art distinctions — Phase 40

1. **Determinantal and Macaulay certificates:** Polynomial multiples spanning the homogeneous polynomial space, so that there cannot be a common nonzero projective root, are part of established elimination and algebraic-geometry machinery. For reference, see [Macaulay2 elimination matrix documentation](https://macaulay2.com/doc/Macaulay2/share/doc/Macaulay2/EliminationMatrices/html/_elimination__Matrix_lp__Z__Z_cm__List_cm__Matrix_rp.html). The present 5-cycle 20×20 construction is an instance-specific application; we have not established global priority or publication novelty.
2. **Gain-graph switching and cycle invariants:** The E-N+1 classification of gauge-equivalence classes by spanning-tree chords is established theory, e.g. [On cospectrality of gain graphs, Corollary 3.5](https://doi.org/10.1515/spma-2022-0169). **Do not claim to have discovered gain-graph holonomy.** Its integration with noncommuting Green FFT experiments is our specific application.
3. **FFT lower bounds:** Morgenstern's 1973 JACM result concerns linear circuits with bounded-magnitude constants: [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/321752.321761). Nothing here proves a general Fourier-operation lower bound and the normalization and coefficient model matter.
4. **Fourier cross-ratio / complex Hadamard invariants:** Diagonal/permutation-invariant ratios are classical. This project uses them to form exact no-root tests of narrowly specified Green families.
5. **Jacobian dominance:** A full-rank differential certifying dominance of a rational map is standard algebraic geometry. Nonzero finite-field reductions of exact integer/rational parameter examples certify characteristic-zero nonvanishing, not novelty of the general Jacobian theorem.

**Potential new contribution to audit:** the combined four-middle-generator projective five-cycle certificate and explicit adaptive dual-cycle computation; and the exact local curvature Jacobian demonstration for two weighted Green cycles in this FFT-oriented architecture. They are limited results and must be compared against specialist literature before publication claims.
