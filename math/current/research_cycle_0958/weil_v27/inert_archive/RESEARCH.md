## 1. Starting point

v26 classified direct higher-dimensional Weil operators over finite local rings. It showed that their dense cores are additive-group Fourier transforms and that those cores cannot equal a cyclic Fourier matrix of the same larger dimension when the ambient additive group has insufficient phase exponent.

One loophole remained:

> Perhaps a larger representation contains a specially chosen `N`-dimensional invariant subspace on which the ambient operator restricts to the cyclic `N`-point DFT, while the ambient operator itself has a cheaper global evaluator.

This checkpoint tests that loophole under the natural exact covariance contract: the embedding must preserve the cyclic shift and modulation operators whose interchange characterizes the DFT.

## 2. Primitive Weyl normal form

Let `K` be a field whose characteristic does not divide `N`, and assume `K` contains a primitive `N`th root `zeta`. Let `W` be a finite-dimensional `K`-vector space with invertible operators `X,Z` satisfying

\[
X^N=Z^N=I,
\qquad
ZX=\zeta XZ.
\tag{2.1}
\]

### Theorem 2.1 — dimension divisibility and block normal form

There is an integer `m>=1` and an isomorphism

\[
W\cong K^N\otimes K^m
\]

under which

\[
\boxed{
X=S_N\otimes I_m,
\qquad
Z=D_N\otimes I_m,
}
\tag{2.2}
\]

where

* `S_N e_j=e_(j+1 mod N)` is the cyclic shift;
* `D_N e_j=zeta^j e_j` is the modulation operator.

Consequently,

\[
\boxed{N\mid \dim W.}
\tag{2.3}
\]

#### Proof

Because `Z^N=I` and the characteristic does not divide `N`, `Z` is diagonalizable and every eigenvalue is an `N`th root of unity. If `Zv=lambda v`, then

\[
Z(Xv)=\zeta XZv=\zeta\lambda Xv.
\]

Thus `X` bijects the `lambda`-eigenspace of `Z` with the `zeta lambda`-eigenspace. Since `zeta` is primitive, the orbit has exactly `N` distinct eigenvalues. All corresponding eigenspaces have the same dimension `m`, and these are all possible `N`th-root eigenspaces. Choosing a basis of one eigenspace and transporting it by powers of `X` gives (2.2). QED.

### Corollary 2.2 — full matrix algebra and invariant subspaces

The `N^2` Weyl monomials

\[
S_N^aD_N^b,
\qquad
0\le a,b<N,
\]

form a basis of `M_N(K)`. Therefore the algebra generated by `X,Z` is

\[
M_N(K)\otimes I_m,
\]

its commutant is

\[
I_N\otimes M_m(K),
\]

and every common invariant subspace has the form

\[
K^N\otimes M
\]

for some subspace `M subseteq K^m`.

In particular, every nonzero exact primitive-phase invariant subspace has dimension at least `N`; its dimension is a multiple of `N`.

## 3. Fourier-swap rigidity

Let

\[
F_N[i,j]=\zeta^{ij}.
\]

With the conventions above,

\[
F_NS_NF_N^{-1}=D_N,
\qquad
F_ND_NF_N^{-1}=S_N^{-1}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Suppose an ambient operator `J` implements the same symplectic swap:

\[
JXJ^{-1}=Z,
\qquad
JZJ^{-1}=X^{-1}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

### Theorem 3.1 — every swap is Fourier on the primitive factor

In the normal form (2.2), there is an invertible `m x m` matrix `U` such that

\[
\boxed{
J=F_N\otimes U.
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

#### Proof

Both `J` and `F_N tensor I_m` implement (3.2). Therefore

\[
(F_N^{-1}\otimes I_m)J
\]

commutes with both `X` and `Z`. By Corollary 2.2 it belongs to `I_N tensor M_m(K)`, proving (3.3). QED.

### Corollary 3.2 — covariant embedding/extraction gate

Let

\[
E:K^N\to W,
\qquad
P:W\to K^N
\]

be exact intertwiners:

\[
XE=ES_N,
\quad
ZE=ED_N,
\]

\[
PX=S_NP,
\quad
PZ=D_NP.
\]

Then in the normal form there are `a in K^m` and `ell in (K^m)^*` such that

\[
E(v)=v\otimes a,
\qquad
P(v\otimes w)=\ell(w)v.
\]

Using (3.3),

\[
\boxed{
PJE=\ell(Ua)F_N.
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

Thus every exact Weyl-covariant invariant-subspace embedding/extraction produces only a scalar multiple of the full `N`-point Fourier transform. After normalizing that scalar, the embedded core is exactly `F_N`.

This is the main v27 gate:

> A larger primitive-phase representation does not replace `F_N` with a smaller or lower-state operator. It contains complete `N`-dimensional Fourier blocks, and every exact covariant extraction sees one of those blocks.

## 4. Coordinate embeddings in additive character tables

The preceding theorem assumes operator covariance. There is a separate coordinate-level statement that needs only matrix entries.

Let

\[
R=\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z,
\qquad
V=R^n,
\]

with perfect pairing

\[
\langle x,y\rangle=x^Ty\pmod N.
\]

Let `x_0,...,x_(N-1)` and `y_0,...,y_(N-1)` be ambient row and column labels. Suppose their additive-character submatrix is monomially equivalent to the cyclic Fourier matrix: after ordering the selected coordinates there are nonzero row/column gauges `r_i,c_j` such that

\[
\zeta^{\langle x_i,y_j\rangle}
=r_i c_j\zeta^{ij}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Taking four-entry cross ratios removes the gauges:

\[
\boxed{
\langle x_i-x_0,y_j-y_0\rangle=ij\pmod N.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Put

\[
u=x_1-x_0,
\qquad
v=y_1-y_0.
\]

Then

\[
\langle u,v\rangle=1,
\tag{4.3}
\]

so `u` and `v` form a primitive dual pair.

### Theorem 4.1 — direct-factor plus orthogonal spectators

The ambient module splits as

\[
\boxed{
V=Ru\oplus\ker\langle -,v\rangle.
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

Moreover, there are spectator vectors `w_i,w'_j` such that

\[
x_i=x_0+i u+w_i,
\qquad
\langle w_i,v\rangle=0,
\]

\[
y_j=y_0+j v+w'_j,
\qquad
\langle u,w'_j\rangle=0,
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\langle w_i,w'_j\rangle=0
\quad\text{for every }i,j.
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

#### Proof

Equation (4.3) makes the functional `x -> <x,v>` surjective and split by `t -> tu`, proving (4.4). Define the spectators by subtracting the direct-factor terms. Equations (4.2) with `j=1` and `i=1` give the first two orthogonality conditions. Substitution into (4.2) leaves precisely (4.5). QED.

### Interpretation

An ambient additive Fourier table may contain many visually nontrivial copies of `F_N`, especially over rings with zero divisors. But every such copy obtains its primitive phase from one direct cyclic factor. The remaining coordinates are mutually orthogonal spectators and do not contribute to the Fourier phase.

For `N=p^2`, nonzero spectator coordinates can occur on both sides: `p`-multiples pair to zero modulo `p^2`. The executable package constructs explicit nontrivial examples at `N=9` and `N=25`. They are exact embeddings, but their extra coordinates carry no additional computational information.

## 5. Exact computational consequence

Suppose a proposed larger-space algorithm has:

1. an exact injection preserving cyclic shift/modulation;
2. an ambient exact swap operator;
3. an exact covariant extraction.

Then (3.4) says the induced map is exactly a scalar multiple of `F_N`.

The charged injection and extraction each require at least writing/reading `N` coordinates, so they cost `Omega(N)`. More importantly, the ambient operator restricted to the primitive block is not a shorter transform, a low-order recurrence, or a partial tensor stage—it is the complete `F_N` core.

Therefore the embedding itself supplies no asymptotic improvement:

\[
\boxed{
\text{a fast evaluator of the embedded primitive block is already a fast evaluator of }F_N.
}
\]

Computing the entire ambient transform is normally more expensive. Restricting to the relevant block removes that overhead but leaves the original Fourier problem unchanged.

## 7. Scope and limitations

This checkpoint does **not** establish:

* an exact `o(N log N)` FFT;
* a lower bound for unrestricted arithmetic circuits;
* a lower bound for arbitrary non-covariant embeddings with dense extraction maps;
* a bit-complexity or fixed-precision result;
* a practical speed advantage over FFT libraries;
* a literature-wide novelty claim.

The theorem applies when the embedding preserves the primitive Weyl/Heisenberg structure exactly, or when the embedding is a coordinate submatrix of an additive character table. A construction that abandons those contracts remains logically outside the gate, but must charge its injection, extraction and ambient evaluation explicitly.

## 8. Prior-art boundary

The finite Heisenberg group, the Stone-von Neumann uniqueness phenomenon, and the construction of Weil representations from the Heisenberg representation are classical. The project-specific contribution here is the computational combination:

1. an elementary all-`N` block normal form for exact finite Weyl pairs;
2. the complete swap-solution classification `J=F_N tensor U`;
3. the covariant injection/extraction formula `PJE=scalar*F_N`;
4. the direct-factor/spectator classification of exact coordinate embeddings;
5. exact exhaustive and finite-field certificates tied to the FFT operation ledger.

No claim of literature-wide priority is made.

## 9. Honest conclusion and next gate

We still do **not** have an exact sub-`N log N` cyclic FFT.

What v27 proves is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{primitive cyclic phase depth cannot be hidden inside a smaller invariant block.}
}
\]

Any exact Weyl-covariant ambient representation decomposes into full `N`-dimensional Schrödinger blocks, and every exact symplectic swap acts as `F_N` on each block.

Likewise, every coordinate embedding of `F_N` into a larger additive character table splits off a direct cyclic factor; extra coordinates are phase-orthogonal spectators.

The surviving exact routes are therefore narrower:

1. a **non-covariant** sparse injection/extraction whose total charged work is sub-`N log N` and whose correctness does not secretly call `F_N`;
2. a **non-Weil high-treewidth operator** with a genuine exact non-Krylov fast solver;
3. a new global arithmetic DAG outside character-table and Heisenberg covariance, with a complete operation and precision ledger.
