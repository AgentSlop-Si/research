## 1. Reciprocal orbit sizes in the necklace poset

Let G act regularly on its own elements and by translation on subsets. For a subset S, orbit–stabilizer gives

    |Orb(S)| = m/|Stab(S)|.

Thus for a saturated chain T₁⊂⋯⊂Tₘ=G,

    Z = m Σⱼ₌₁ᵐ 1/|Orb(Tⱼ)| − m.

The theorem is therefore equivalent to

    max_chains Σⱼ₌₁ᵐ 1/|Orb(Tⱼ)| = 1+W(m).

For G=C_m this is a weighted maximal-chain question in the binary necklace poset B_m/C_m. A rank-complete chain of orbits can always be lifted to a nested chain of actual subsets: if one chosen representative is A and its orbit is below the next orbit, translate a witnessing representative of the next orbit to contain A, then continue. The weight is unchanged by this choice.

The symmetric-chain decomposition papers cited in the main report treat decomposition of the entire quotient poset. Their stated results do not establish the displayed extremum for one weighted maximal chain.

## 2. Sum-indecomposable components of powers of one cycle

For π∈S_m, let c(π) be the number of components in its unique decomposition as a direct sum of nonempty sum-indecomposable permutations. Equivalently,

    c(π) = #{j ∈ {1,…,m} : π({1,…,j})={1,…,j}}.

To see the equivalence, all proper invariant initial segments are precisely the cut locations in the finest direct-sum decomposition; the final cut at m accounts for its last component.

Given an ordering x₁,…,xₘ of C_m, let ℓ(xⱼ)=j and let τ=ℓ∘(x↦x+1)∘ℓ⁻¹. Then τ is an m-cycle, and translation by r stabilizes Tⱼ exactly when τ^r stabilizes {1,…,j}. Double counting the pairs (r,j) yields

    Z = Σᵣ₌₁ᵐ⁻¹ c(τ^r).

Conversely every m-cycle is conjugate to the regular translation x↦x+1, so it arises this way. Hence the proven cyclic theorem has the equivalent standalone formulation

    max_{τ an m-cycle} Σᵣ₌₁ᵐ⁻¹ c(τ^r) = m W(m).

For a general finite abelian regular permutation group, replace powers of τ by its nonidentity regular translations and optimize over relabelings.

This is an exact reformulation derived here, not a theorem attributed to the literature search. It may be a more effective formulation for a later specialist priority check.

## 3. Intersections with maximal Young subgroups

For a labeling ℓ:G→{1,…,m}, write Γ=ℓGℓ⁻¹ for the corresponding regular subgroup of S_m. Let Pⱼ=S_{1,…,j}×S_{j+1,…,m}. Then

    |Stab_G(Tⱼ)|=|Γ∩Pⱼ|.

For the proper cuts, the objective becomes

    Z = (m−1) + Σⱼ₌₁ᵐ⁻¹ (|Γ∩Pⱼ|−1).

Thus the theorem also optimizes this sum of intersection orders over conjugates of a fixed regular abelian subgroup. These are setwise stabilizers, unlike the descending pointwise stabilizer chains used in ordinary permutation-group algorithms.

## 4. Closest primary sources inspected and their scope

### Necklace-poset chain decompositions

- Patricia Hersh and Anne Schilling, *Symmetric chain decomposition for cyclic quotients of Boolean algebras and relation to cyclic crystals*, [arXiv:1107.4073](https://arxiv.org/abs/1107.4073), [full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1107.4073). Theorem 2.12 gives a symmetric chain decomposition of B_m/C_m; Theorem 4.4 gives the crystal interpretation. The displayed weighted-chain extremum above is not a stated result. Searching the accessible full text for “stabil” returned no match.
- Dwight Duffus, Jeremy McKibben-Sanders, and Kyle Thayer, *Some Quotients of the Boolean Lattice are Symmetric Chain Orders*, [arXiv:1107.1098](https://arxiv.org/abs/1107.1098), [full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1107.1098). Theorem 1 concerns groups generated by powers of disjoint cycles and symmetric-chain orders. It does not state the reciprocal-orbit-weight maximum; accessible full-text searches for “stabil” and “weight” returned no matches.

### Recursive cosets and group subset periodicity

- Alexander Hulpke and Steve Linton, *Total Ordering on Subgroups and Cosets*, [author-hosted paper](https://www.math.colostate.edu/~hulpke/paper/order.pdf). Its objective is efficient lexicographic ordering of subgroups and cosets in computational group theory, rather than optimizing stabilizers of prefixes in an ordering of group elements. The similar terminology does not identify an equivalent theorem.
- Keresztély Corrádi, *On the direct product of cyclic subsets*, Acta Sci. Math. (Szeged) 60 (1995), 119–129, [journal full text](https://acta.hu/download.phtml?id=2269). Theorem 2 concerns quasi-periodic partial products in a unique-factorization representation of a finite abelian group by cyclic subsets. This is related periodicity and subgroup-chain structure, but its hypothesis is a direct product factorization and its conclusion is the existence of a subgroup factor. It does not state an extremum over arbitrary orderings or a sum of stabilizer orders.

### Powers of permutations

- Amanda Burcroff and Colin Defant, *Pattern-Avoiding Permutation Powers*, [arXiv:1907.09451](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.09451), [full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.09451). The paper treats avoidance by a permutation and its powers, and by all elements of embedded groups. It defines sum indecomposability and notes that direct sums commute with taking powers. Its stated theorems optimize or count pattern-avoiding permutations; they do not state a maximum of the sum of direct-sum component counts over nonidentity powers of an m-cycle.
