Fourier signed bank theorem

Exact assumptions and count

Let N be odd with N ≥ 23, let q be a primitive Nth root of unity in characteristic zero, and set K = Q(q). Put d = ⌊(N + 10)/8⌋ and m = N − d. Then d ≥ 4 and N ≥ 8d − 10. Over a splitting field L of a monic degree m polynomial g ∈ K[t], the negative Fourier matrix is a sum of at most

B(N) = 2N − 2⌊(N + 10)/8⌋ + 2 = 7N/4 + O(1)

individual permitted signed banks. In the orientation order (++ , +− , −+ , −−), the multiplicities are (m, 1, 1, m). The two same-sign groups share the m distinct nonzero roots of g. Both cross-sign singleton nodes are 1, and every β parameter is 1. One may take [L : K] ≤ m!.

The count is for whole banks in the original signed-resolvent grammar. Each bank retains its N raw weights, giving N(2m + 2) raw weights in this construction. A bank is not counted as a scalar arithmetic operation.

The stronger underlying statement

More generally, for odd N, d ≥ 4, N ≥ 8d − 10, and m = N − d, let W be the degree-at-most-m whole-left recurrence kernel defined in the frozen proof. A K-linear subspace U ⊆ W has a monic affine slice on which the following simultaneous conditions form a nonempty K-defined polynomial open set:

g(0) ≠ 0,     g is squarefree,     rank X_N(g) = N.

Here X_N(g) has rows (p_a, 1, p_[−a]_N), with p_j the coefficient vector of tʲ modulo g and 0 ≤ a < N. All residue exponents are nonnegative. The same simultaneous open condition is nonempty on the monic slice of W. The theorem supplies coefficients in K; it does not assert that the roots split in K.

From the polynomial to actual banks

The common recurrence and Vandermonde interpolation cover every positive physical row. Reflection covers the negative rows using the same banks. The full axis rank covers the remaining zero row. The accepted finite-character transform reconstructs the raw weights. Every node α is nonzero, so the resolvent denominator Δ = −αᴺ is nonzero and the finite geometric-series inverse is legal in both orders.

Why the new axis argument works

A controlled zero-node degeneration

The enlarged envelope contains every actual recurrence row, so its annihilator U lies in W without a rank-saturation assumption. Sparse-functional and exact shift arguments prove gcd(U) = t − 1 and gcd(U₀) = t(t − 1), where U₀ imposes g(0) = 0. They also prove independent residual endpoint functionals.

After the fixed divisor is removed, the universal-polynomial argument produces a residual f that is coprime to its fixed-grade reversal H_f = tʳf(1/t), where r = m − 2. The endpoint independence rules out an entire reversal eigenspace. This yields a monic test polynomial g₀ = t(t − 1)f with f(0) ≠ 0.

For any monic g, including one with zero constant term, the quotient-axis row-dependence space consists exactly of polynomials A of degree at most N − 1 satisfying

g ∣ A,     g ∣ J_N A,     A(1) = 0,

J_N A = tᴺA(1/t) + A(0)(1 − tᴺ).

At g₀, the condition g₀ ∣ A forces A(0) = 0. The two divisibilities then imply t(t − 1)fH_f ∣ A. This divisor has degree 2m − 2 > N − 1, so A = 0 and the axis rank is N. Thus one polynomial axis minor is nonzero on the monic slice.

The constant-coefficient guard and discriminant guard are separately nonzero on that same affine slice. Its coordinate ring is an integral domain, and K is infinite. Their product with the axis minor therefore has a K-valued nonzero point. That point has legal nonzero distinct nodes. The zero node was used only to prove a minor polynomial is not identically zero; it is never assigned to a counted bank.

The old shortcut stays rejected

At legal points, reciprocal coprimality alone does not force full axis rank. For example, at N = 5 and m = 3, g = t³ + t² + t + 2 is coprime to its reversal, but A = (t − 1)g = t⁴ + t − 2 is a nonzero axis dependence. The new proof does not use the rejected implication. Its decisive step is the zero-constant test point followed by simultaneous open-set nonvanishing.

Prefix stabilizer optimum

Definitions and proved classes

Let G be a finite group of order n acting on itself by left multiplication. For a labeled ordering x₁, …, xₙ, put T_j = {x₁, …, x_j}, H_j = {g ∈ G : gT_j = T_j}, and Z = Σⱼ(|H_j| − 1). Define M(G) = max Z and W(n) = Σₚ vₚ(n)(1 − 1/p), with W(1) = 0.

For either of the following hypotheses, the exact result is M(G) = nW(n): (i) HK = KH for every pair of subgroups H, K ≤ G; or (ii) G is a finite p-group, with no subgroup-permutability assumption. In case (ii), if n = pᵃ, this equals a(p − 1)pᵃ⁻¹.

Equality and the exact labeled count

An ordering is maximizing exactly when it is built recursively by choosing a maximal subgroup K, placing its right cosets Kx in consecutive blocks, and using a maximizing ordering of K within each block. Every such K is normal of prime index. For nontrivial G the top K is unique, and is generated by the proper-prefix stabilizers. The singleton group is the explicit recursion base.

A(G) = Σ over maximal K < G of [G : K]! · A(K)^[G : K],

A({e}) = 1.

A(G) counts all labeled permutations. In a p-group, optimality is also equivalent to |H_j| = gcd(j, pᵃ) at every prefix. The action convention matters: left multiplication has right-coset orbits, and right translation preserves the actual left stabilizer.

The sharp structural boundary at S₃

Take a = (12), b = (23), with rightmost-first composition. The order e, a, b, ba, ab, aba has stabilizer orders 1, 2, 1, 2, 1, 6 and score 7. Its second and fourth stabilizers generate S₃, so it has no recursive right-coset hierarchy. Exactly 360 labeled orders are optimal: 216 are hierarchical and 144 are not. Thus the unrestricted equality and counting extension is false, while this example retains the numerical value nW(n).

The separate proposition M(G) ≥ nW(n) is proved for every finite solvable group. No matching upper bound for arbitrary finite groups, all solvable groups, or mixed-order nilpotent groups is accepted here. Ten bounded exact group controls do not extend the theorem beyond its hypotheses.

Proof references: NONABELIAN_PREFIX_BOUNDARY.md §§1–5; nonabelian_review/INDEPENDENT_REVIEW.md §§1–6. The review checked 209,696 identity-first DP states, 81,360 full labeled permutations, and 4,480 subset/translation pairs.

Guarded necessary circuit reduction

Exact domain and current endpoint

Work over Q(ζ), with ζ a primitive eleventh root and coefficient relation Φ₁₁(ζ) = 1 + ζ + ⋯ + ζ¹⁰ = 0. The object is the accepted guarded necessary circuit for the fixed three-layer, 99-edge Fourier support problem. The predecessor is the reviewed 12-free-coordinate, 55-residual circuit with 14 retained nonvanishing guards.

Projection and triangular rational reconstruction give a bijection with a new circuit having the following nine free coordinates:

x₀, x₁₁, x₂₅, x₃₄, x₄₁, x₄₉, x₆₅, x₈₃, x₉₂.

It has 52 residual equation constraints and 17 guards: the same 14 original guards and exactly 3 derived denominator guards. These are counts of stored constraints, not claims of algebraic independence or dimension.

Why no guarded component is lost

The three new source identities, checked coefficient by coefficient before rational substitution, are

E₅₈ = x₂₂D₂₂ − ζ,     D₂₂ = x₃₄ + x₄₉,

E₃₆ = x₈₈D₈₈ + ζ¹⁰,     E₄ = x₅₂D₅₂ + ζ⁴x₈₃.

The first two numerators are nonzero field constants. The third is nonzero because the original x₈₃ guard remains. On the predecessor solution set the equations therefore force D₂₂, D₈₈, and D₅₂ to be nonzero. In particular x₂₂ ≠ 0 is a valid consequence. This is an equation-forced denominator argument, not a generic-nonvanishing assumption.

Reconstruct x₂₂ and x₈₈ first, then x₅₂; continue with the unchanged x₃₉, x₄₄, x₅₅ solves and accepted x₉₁ restoration. D₅₂ uses x₈₈ only after it has been reconstructed. The 52 retained residual references are exactly the other constraints of the accepted 55-residual system. The review also accounts for the full parent equation dictionary and all inherited denominator guard identities.
