## 1. Adversarial proof audit

### Accumulated stabilizers

The containment is valid. At insertion j, the previous coset x_1+K_(j-1) remains inside the new prefix. Applying H_j to that entire contained coset yields x_1+(K_(j-1)+H_j), still inside the prefix. In an abelian group this sum is a subgroup, so the induction proves the asserted containment. Applied at m-1, it forces the generated subgroup to be proper.

This is genuinely a containment argument. It does not incorrectly promote earlier stabilizers to stabilizers of later prefixes, and it does not need the individual H_j to be nested. Such an incorrect promotion would invalidate the result, but the source explicitly avoids it.

### Maximal-subgroup reduction

A maximal proper subgroup containing K_(m-1) exists in the finite group. Its quotient is simple and abelian, hence cyclic of prime order q. If |K|=k, each nonfinal global stabilizer acts inside every K-coset and in particular inside the coset receiving the current insertion. Thus H_j is a subgroup of that insertion's local stabilizer L_j.

Summing local insertion weights counts every nonempty induced prefix exactly once, including each local full coset. At the final insertion the exact correction is (m-1)-(k-1)=m-k. There is no omitted final-prefix term, no double counting, and no assumption that global insertions already form blocks.

The induction step is exact because W(m)=W(k)+(1-1/q). In particular, the prime index chosen for the upper bound need not be predetermined or unique for a nonmaximizing ordering.

### Attainment

For a consecutive-coset ordering, translation permutes K-cosets. If the prefix ends within a block, the occupied cardinality of the unique partially occupied coset is strictly between 0 and k; all other cosets have cardinality 0 or k. A stabilizing translation must fix that coset and therefore lie in K. Inside K, precisely the local stabilizer remains.

At a proper block boundary, a nonempty proper subset of a prime-order quotient has trivial translation stabilizer. Therefore the global stabilizer equals K. The full-prefix correction remains m-k. This verifies equality in every step of the bound.

### All equality cases

At an optimum, the sum of the nonnegative inclusion deficits |L_j|-|H_j| is zero, and every internal objective is maximal. Finite subgroup inclusion plus equal cardinality gives H_j=L_j individually.

At the first completion of a coset, the active local stabilizer is all of K. Consequently the global prefix is K-invariant, so every other coset must be either full or empty. No other coset was already full, and only one coset can first become full in one insertion. Thus no other coset was touched. Repeating this reasoning at subsequent completion times forces consecutive whole-coset blocks. The final block then follows automatically. This repetition is sound without assuming that previous local hierarchies agree.

### Unique top subgroup and counting

All proper-prefix stabilizers lie in the chosen top K, while the first full block has stabilizer K. Hence the subgroup generated by proper-prefix stabilizers equals K. This recovers K from the ordering itself and rules out overcounting across different top choices.

The edge case k=1 is valid: it occurs for a prime-order G, every proper nonempty prefix has trivial stabilizer, and the unique top subgroup is {0}. The singleton group is correctly treated as a separate base case.

For each top K, the q distinct cosets have q! orders. Fixing any one representative of each coset gives a bijection between its internally maximizing orders and the A(K) maximizing orders of K. The representative is an identification, not another counted choice. Independent internal choices therefore give q! A(K)^q, with no extra translation factor. Summing over the distinct top subgroups is justified by uniqueness.

## 3. Fourier consequence

I checked the reduction in the frozen source rather than assuming that an abstract group optimum automatically implied a Fourier optimum.

For p prime and beta_(x-1)=zeta^x-zeta^(-x), expansion of a prefix polynomial gives an integer coefficient vector on 1,zeta,...,zeta^(p-1). Such a vector evaluates to zero exactly when all its entries coincide. Its coefficient sum is zero, so in this case vanishing means the vector itself is zero. This is equivalent to equality of the two exponent multisets (t+1)T and (t-1)T. The excluded colors t=+/-1 cannot vanish, and the stated fractional-linear map bijects the remaining colors with the nonidentity multiplicative-group elements. Therefore each prefix contributes exactly |Stab(T)|-1 grid zeros.

The multiplicative group of F_p is cyclic of order m=p-1, so the new theorem applies to every permutation of the fixed coefficient family. All beta values are nonzero, and a chain permutation leaves the endpoint support 2p-2 unchanged. The isolated row has no zeros. Consequently the exact optimum in this family is

    max Z = (p-1) W(p-1),
    min nnz(R) = p^2 - (p-1) W(p-1),
    min E = 2p^2 + 2p - 2 - (p-1) W(p-1).

Independent Fourier controls expand beta_(x-1) zeta^((x-1)t) directly into integer coefficient vectors. They use no floating-point zero test and no imported cyclotomic implementation. They check every permutation at p=5 and p=7, and one hierarchy ordering plus fixed-seed controls at p=11,13,17,19,31: **1,244 orders in total**. Every prefix's actual zero count agrees with its directly computed multiplicative stabilizer, and the hierarchy reaches the predicted optimum in every case.

The independently recovered minimum E values are respectively 54, 103, 249, 342, 578, 725, and 1,923.

The proof certifies the optimum only among permutations of this fixed nonzero beta family in the stated single-chain construction. It does not optimize other coefficients, partitions into several chains, arbitrary endpoints, or arithmetic operation counts. The dense input endpoint and quadratic-size coefficient table remain. The frozen predecessor's growing-savings corollary is compatible with this optimum but is not strengthened into a subquadratic claim.

## 4. Useful next directions and an exact defect identity

The ordering-only search within the frozen family is closed. A genuinely different Fourier improvement must alter another degree of freedom, such as the coefficient family, chain partition, or endpoint design. Those are separate problems and are not silently covered by this review.

A precise refinement already follows from the audited proof. For any maximal K containing all proper-prefix stabilizers, with |K|=k and the source's local quantities, there is the exact identity

    m W(m) - Z
      = sum_C [k W(k) - Z_C]
        + sum_(j<m) [|L_j| - |H_j|].

Every term on the right is a nonnegative integer. This decomposes any failure of optimality into internal-order losses and interleaving losses. It supplies a concrete starting point for a near-optimality or stability question without assuming nested stabilizers.

There is also a straightforward monotone-weight extension worth recording separately if broader combinatorial scope is desired. For any nondecreasing real function f on positive integers, define

    M_f(G) = max_orderings sum_j f(|H_j|).

The same upper-bound and attainment arguments prove

    M_f({0}) = f(1),
    M_f(G) = max_(K maximal in G)
        ( [G:K] M_f(K) + f(|G|) - f(|K|) ).

For strictly increasing f, equality again forces consecutive coset blocks and unique top K; the top subgroup must attain this recurrence's maximum and each internal order must be optimal for f. Without strict increase, extra equality cases may occur, so that characterization must not be asserted unchanged. This extension is a consequence of the proof, not a claim of historical novelty or a new Fourier algorithm.
