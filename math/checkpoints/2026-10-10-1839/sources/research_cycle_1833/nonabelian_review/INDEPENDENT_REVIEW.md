## 1. Noncommutative conventions

The convention in Section 1 is correct and is used consistently throughout the proof.

- For left multiplication, K-orbits are right cosets Kx. The identification hx with h intertwines the left K-action exactly, without a conjugation.
- Right translation sends an ordering starting at x to the unique ordering starting at the identity by multiplying every entry by x inverse. It preserves the left stabilizer as a subgroup, not just its cardinality.
- Left translation by y conjugates the left stabilizer by y. The note does not incorrectly use left translation as identity-preserving normalization.
- Any subgroup acts freely on any invariant subset of the regular G-set. Thus its order divides the subset size, as well as |G|. Complementation preserves the stabilizer.

When K is normal, left multiplication permutes the K-cosets. A stabilizer of a prefix with one partially occupied K-coset must fix that coset, since all other cosets are either empty or full. Fixing Kx means belonging to K. Inside K, the global stabilizer then equals the partial block's local stabilizer. This is the precise use of normality in the attainment proof; it is not assumed for arbitrary subgroups.

At a proper whole-block boundary, the occupied cosets form a nonempty proper subset of the prime-order quotient. A nonidentity quotient element acts as one cycle, so the subset has trivial quotient stabilizer. Its full stabilizer in G is therefore exactly K.

The independent controls also check the exact right-translation invariance and left-translation conjugacy identities for every subset and every translating element in S3, D8, and Q8, a total of 4,480 subset/translation pairs.

## 2. Pairwise permutable subgroups: Theorem A

### Normal maximal subgroups and prime index

Section 2.1 is self-contained and valid. If a Sylow p-subgroup P permutes with a conjugate P', their product is a subgroup of p-power order containing P. The Sylow bound forces PP'=P, so P'=P. Thus every Sylow subgroup is normal.

Different normal Sylow subgroups commute elementwise: each commutator lies in their trivial intersection. Consequently the group is their direct product. A subgroup also splits into its p-components, since each component of one of its elements is a power of that element by the Chinese remainder theorem. A maximal proper subgroup can therefore differ from the full group in exactly one Sylow factor, where its intersection must be maximal.

The p-group normalizer argument is sound. If the center is not contained in a proper subgroup L, a central element outside L enlarges its normalizer. Otherwise L/Z is proper in P/Z, and the induction hypothesis lifts to a strict normalizer enlargement in P. For maximal L, the normalizer is all of P. Then P/L has no nontrivial proper subgroup and is a nontrivial p-group, hence has order p. Neither this argument nor the class-equation proof of a nontrivial center assumes pairwise permutability.

This establishes the normality and prime index needed for Theorem A, and subgroup inheritance permits induction. No classification theorem about quasihamiltonian groups is silently required.

### Accumulated stabilizers and the upper bound

Let B_j be the subgroup generated by the first j stabilizers. Pairwise permutability gives B_j=H_j B_(j-1). If B_(j-1)x_1 lies in T_j, applying H_j gives B_j x_1 inside T_j. Hence B_(m-1) is proper. The proof correctly requires permutability with the accumulated subgroup, rather than merely asserting that earlier stabilizers still stabilize later prefixes.

A maximal subgroup K containing B_(m-1) contains every proper-prefix stabilizer. At an insertion into Kx, the global stabilizer preserves the active local prefix and is contained in its K-stabilizer L_j. Summing the local weights counts every nonempty induced prefix exactly once. The last insertion contributes the exact difference

    (m-1) - (|K|-1) = m-|K|.

There is no omitted or duplicated boundary term. Thus the upper bound is q M(K)+m-|K|. Since q is prime, its inductive value is m W(m). The normal-coset argument in Section 1 of this review establishes attainment.

### Equality and counting

Equality makes all nonnegative inclusion deficits |L_j|-|H_j| zero and makes every induced coset order optimal. At the first coset completion, H_j=K. K-invariance forces every other coset to be full or empty; none can already be full at this first completion, so none has been touched. Repeating at the next completion forces successive entire blocks. The final block follows automatically.

Every proper-prefix stabilizer lies in the top K, and the first completed block has stabilizer K. Their generated subgroup is therefore exactly K, proving uniqueness. For each K there are q! choices of coset order and A(K)^q independent local orders. Choosing a coset representative is just a fixed identification and contributes no extra factor. Distinct top choices cannot overlap. The counting recurrence is valid, including the prime-order case K={e}.

## 3. Every finite p-group: Theorem B

### Numerical bound and attainment

The pointwise bound |H_j| ≤ gcd(j,p^a) holds by freeness. Counting indices of each p-adic valuation gives

    sum from j=1 to p^a of (gcd(j,p^a)-1)
      = a(p-1)p^(a-1).

The recursive normal index-p construction attains this sum, by the same partial-block and prime-quotient calculation audited above. It does not rely on the accumulated-stabilizer argument, which is unavailable for general p-groups. Since every pointwise deficit is nonnegative, equality of the total is equivalent to saturation at every prefix.

### Necessity of the hierarchy

For a nontrivial group, put k=p^(a-1). Saturation gives K=H_k of order k and T_k=Kx_1. K is a normal maximal subgroup. At a later proper boundary tk, J=H_(tk) is another normal subgroup of order k. The J-invariant prefix contains Kx_1 and therefore JKx_1. If J differs from K, then JK is a subgroup strictly containing maximal K, so JK=G. This contradicts the proper prefix. Therefore all such boundary stabilizers equal K.

Each boundary prefix is a union of exactly t K-cosets. The successive differences are K-invariant sets of size k and hence individual K-cosets. For p=2 there is only the first proper boundary, and its complement supplies the second block; the argument still works. The partial-block stabilizer identity and exact sum decomposition then force every internal order to be optimal. The converse follows from attainment. The top subgroup and labeled recurrence follow exactly as in Theorem A.

No step requires all subgroups of a p-group to permute. This distinction is essential for D8 and D16 and is handled correctly.

## 4. S3: exact boundary and count

With rightmost-first composition, the given order e,a,b,ba,ab,aba has exact left-stabilizer orders 1,2,1,2,1,6. The second and fourth stabilizers are generated by a and b respectively. They generate S3, so accumulated containment fails at both prefixes 4 and 5. The score is 7.

The upper-bound split is exhaustive. If the third stabilizer has order 3, the first three entries form one A3-coset. The first pair and the two-element complement of the fourth prefix cannot have order-2 stabilizers, giving total 7. Otherwise the third stabilizer is trivial, and the second and fourth contributions are at most one each, again giving at most 7.

The two counted types are disjoint:

- Third stabilizer of order 3: two choices of first A3-coset and 3! orders for each block give 72.
- Third stabilizer trivial: the first and final unordered pairs must each have opposite parity. There are 9 first pairs, 4 disjoint final pairs, and 2! orders in each of the three pairs, giving 288.

Within the second type, fixing the first pair's order-2 stabilizer leaves exactly two possible final cosets with that same stabilizer. This gives 9×2×8=144 hierarchical orders. Together with the 72 order-3-block orders, there are 216 hierarchical optimizers and 144 other optimizers, for 360 in total. The displayed witness is among the latter. All smaller-order groups are abelian, so the stated smallest-order structural boundary is correct.

This refutes the unrestricted equality/counting extension. It does not refute the numerical bound, and the note correctly maintains that distinction.

## 5. Solvable-group lower bound

A nontrivial solvable group has nontrivial abelianization: otherwise its derived series could not reach the identity. A nontrivial finite abelian quotient has a quotient of prime order, supplying a normal prime-index subgroup K. The subgroup K remains solvable.

Inductively constructing orders with score |K|W(|K|) inside consecutive K-cosets therefore gives an order with score |G|W(|G|). This proves the stated lower bound and does not supply the unclaimed upper bound or equality classification.

## 6. Independently authored exact controls

The new program `independent_controls.py` uses only the Python standard library. It does not import or execute either submitted program or any predecessor program. Submitted JSON is read only as static comparison data.

The implementations deliberately differ from the submitted controls:

- Dihedral groups and M16 are generated as concrete permutation groups, not built from the submitted coordinate multiplication routine.
- Q8 is generated by two 2×2 matrices over F3 whose squares are -I and which anticommute, independently of the submitted signed quaternion-unit routine.
- S3 and A4 are generated from permutations. Every table is checked for identity, bijective rows and columns, associativity, and inverses.
- For each left-regular permutation, all unions of its disjoint cycles are enumerated. These give its invariant subsets and hence every subset's exact stabilizer. This differs from the submitted subset-image recurrence.
- Exact dynamic programming uses compressed identity-containing states and counts every maximizing predecessor. Literal set translations recheck its witnesses.
- Every identity-containing subset of each of the ten groups is independently tested for subgroup closure. Relevant normality, pairwise permutability, and recursive counts are then computed.
- All labeled permutations of S3, D8, and Q8 are enumerated, without translation normalization. Every prefix stabilizer is recomputed literally. Every permutation is also tested for recursive right-coset structure. For the two p-groups, optimality, pointwise saturation, hierarchy membership, unique top choice, and recovery of the top by proper-prefix stabilizers agree in every case.
- Every saved stabilizer in every submitted witness is decoded into the independent group model and rechecked literally. The saved containment-failure positions also agree.

## 7. Primary-source verification

I independently inspected the cited primary paper by Anna Giordano Bruno and Flavio Salizzoni, *Additivity of the algebraic entropy for locally finite groups with permutable finite subgroups*, arXiv:2001.02419. The public PDF identifies itself as v1, dated 8 January 2020.

- Page 2 defines a permutable subgroup through HK=KH for every subgroup K and uses “quasihamiltonian” for a group all of whose subgroups are permutable.
- Page 4, Example 2.1(a–b), gives Q8 as a nonabelian quasihamiltonian example and S3 as a finite group that is not quasihamiltonian.

These are precisely the terminology and examples for which the frozen note cites it. The paper's algebraic-entropy theorem and the classifications it references are not premises of the prefix proof. Its inspection does not establish historical priority for the optimization result. [Primary paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.02419), [full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2001.02419).
