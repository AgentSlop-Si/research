# Exact optimality of prefix stabilizers in finite abelian groups

10 October 2026. New continuation of the frozen subgroup-chain construction.

## Result and proof status

**The subgroup-hierarchy value is the global maximum over all orderings.** There is no better ordering of the fixed coefficient family in the predecessor. In fact the statement holds for every finite abelian group, independently of its isomorphism type.

Let G be a finite abelian group of order m, written additively. For an ordering x₁,…,xₘ of its distinct elements, put

    Tⱼ = {x₁,…,xⱼ},
    Hⱼ = {g ∈ G : Tⱼ+g=Tⱼ},
    Z(x₁,…,xₘ) = Σⱼ₌₁ᵐ (|Hⱼ|−1),
    W(m) = Σ_{q prime} v_q(m)(1−1/q),    W(1)=0.

**Theorem 1.** For every finite abelian G,

    max_orderings Z = m W(m).

**Theorem 2.** An ordering is maximizing if and only if it is obtained recursively by choosing a prime-index subgroup K<G, ordering its cosets as consecutive blocks, and using a maximizing ordering of K inside each block. For a singleton group the unique order is maximizing. The subgroup chosen at the top of a maximizing ordering is unique. Internal choices in different cosets may differ, so the full class is broader than orders based on one fixed subgroup chain.

These are proved below without computer search or a literature theorem. Computations are independently authored finite checks, not the reason the theorems hold. A bounded primary-literature search located related necklace-poset chain decomposition results but no equivalent weighted optimization theorem. This is not a claim of historical novelty.

## 1. The accumulated-stabilizer lemma

**Lemma.** Define Kⱼ=H₁+⋯+Hⱼ, a subgroup because G is abelian. Then

    x₁+Kⱼ ⊆ Tⱼ.

Consequently the subgroup Kₘ₋₁ generated by all proper-prefix stabilizers is proper in G whenever m>1.

**Proof.** At j=1, H₁={0}, so the assertion holds. If it holds for j−1, then x₁+Kⱼ₋₁⊆Tⱼ₋₁⊆Tⱼ. Since Tⱼ is Hⱼ-invariant,

    x₁+Kⱼ = (x₁+Kⱼ₋₁)+Hⱼ ⊆ Tⱼ+Hⱼ = Tⱼ.

The claim follows by induction. At j=m−1, the right side omits xₘ, so Kₘ₋₁ cannot equal G. ∎

This is the step that prevents incompatible periods from jointly reaching all of G before the last element. It does not assume that the individual stabilizers Hⱼ are nested, and it does not assert that every Tⱼ is invariant under all earlier stabilizers. Only containment of the accumulated subgroup coset is used. Commutativity is essential to this proof.

## 2. A rigorous coset-restriction upper bound

Write M(G) for the maximum in Theorem 1. Let m>1 and take any ordering. By the lemma, all its proper-prefix stabilizers lie in a common proper subgroup. Enlarge that subgroup to a maximal subgroup K<G. Since G is finite abelian, the quotient G/K is a finite simple abelian group, hence has prime order q. Put k=|K|=m/q.

Restrict the ordering to each of the q cosets C of K. After translating C to K, this is an ordering of K; denote its objective by Z_C.

For each global insertion j, let Cⱼ be the K-coset containing xⱼ, let

    Uⱼ = Tⱼ ∩ Cⱼ,
    Lⱼ = {h ∈ K : Uⱼ+h=Uⱼ}.

The sequence of nonempty Uⱼ encountered while Cⱼ=C consists of all the prefixes of that coset's induced ordering, each exactly once. Therefore

    Σⱼ₌₁ᵐ (|Lⱼ|−1) = Σ_C Z_C ≤ q M(K).

For j<m, Hⱼ⊆K. Its translations preserve both Tⱼ and Cⱼ, so they preserve Uⱼ; hence Hⱼ⊆Lⱼ. At the final insertion j=m, Uₘ=Cₘ and Lₘ=K, while Hₘ=G. It follows that

    Z ≤ Σⱼ₌₁ᵐ (|Lⱼ|−1) + (m−k)
      = Σ_C Z_C + m−k
      ≤ q M(K) + m−k.                         (1)

Inducting on |G|, with M({0})=0, gives

    Z ≤ q[k W(k)] + m−k
      = m[W(k)+1−1/q]
      = m W(m).

This proves the universal upper bound. It is stronger than independently bounding |Hⱼ| by gcd(j,m), which fails to account for incompatibility between different prime periods.

## 3. Attainment without assuming the predecessor

Choose any prime-index subgroup K<G, and list its cosets as consecutive blocks. Within each block, use an optimal ordering of K, available by induction.

Consider a nonempty proper global prefix. If it stops inside a block, there is exactly one partially filled K-coset. A translation stabilizing the whole prefix must map that unique partial coset to itself, so lies in K. Once in K, it fixes every completed or empty coset and stabilizes the prefix exactly when it stabilizes the partial local prefix. Thus Hⱼ=Lⱼ.

If the prefix stops at a block boundary, its occupied cosets form a nonempty proper subset of G/K. Since |G/K|=q is prime, that subset has trivial translation stabilizer in the quotient. The global stabilizer is therefore precisely K, again equal to the active local stabilizer.

At j=m, the global weight is m−1 and the local weight is k−1. Hence equality holds in (1), and

    Z = q M(K)+m−k = m W(m).

The theorem follows. For cyclic G, choosing the same prime-factor subgroup hierarchy consistently in all blocks recovers the predecessor's construction.

## 4. All maximizing orders and their exact count

Suppose Z=mW(m), and choose K as in Section 2. Every inequality in (1) must be equality, because its upper endpoint is mW(m). Therefore:

1. Every induced coset ordering is maximizing for K.
2. Hⱼ=Lⱼ for every j<m.

Consider the moment at which the first K-coset becomes complete. This occurs before j=m because q≥2. Its active local stabilizer is K, so Hⱼ=K. Consequently every K-coset at that moment is empty or full. By definition no other coset is full yet, so no other coset has been touched. Thus the first k entries comprise exactly one coset.

Repeat after removing that completed block. At the next coset completion, all other cosets must again be empty or full, so the next k entries comprise one coset. This proves the consecutive-block condition for all but the final block, and the final block follows automatically. The internal optimality was already established. Conversely, Section 3 proves that every recursively constructed order is maximizing.

The top subgroup is unique. Every proper-prefix stabilizer is contained in K for a block order, while the prefix consisting of its first full block has stabilizer exactly K (also true when k=1). Thus

    K = H₁+⋯+Hₘ₋₁.

Different top choices cannot represent the same maximizing order.

Let A(G) count all maximizing permutations of the labeled elements of G, with A({0})=1. The unique top subgroup gives the exact recurrence

    A(G) = Σ_{K maximal in G} [G:K]! · A(K)^[G:K].       (2)

The factorial orders the distinct cosets. Translation identifies each coset's orders with the orders of K. Their internal choices are independent. No quotient by translation or automorphism is taken in (2).

For cyclic groups there is one maximal subgroup for each prime q|m. Writing A(m)=A(C_m),

    A(1)=1,
    A(m)=Σ_{q prime, q|m} q! · A(m/q)^q.                 (3)

The maximum depends only on |G|; the number of maximizing orders generally depends on the group structure.

A useful warning against an unjustified laminarity assumption is the maximizing C₁₂ order

    0,6,2,8,4,10, 1,5,9,3,7,11.

Its stabilizer orders are

    1,2,1,2,1,6, 1,1,3,1,1,12,

which sum to Z=20 after subtracting 1 at each position. The order-2 stabilizer at j=2 and the order-3 stabilizer at j=9 are not nested. Its top subgroup is the even residues (order 6); the two cosets use different internal prime-factor hierarchies. The directly checked full stabilizers are saved in `nonnested_optimal_witness.json`. Thus the theorem does not say that a maximizing order has a single nested list of all its prefix stabilizers.

## 5. Exact finite reduction and independently authored controls

### 5.1 Dynamic programming over subsets

Translation preserves every prefix stabilizer. Every ordering can therefore be uniquely translated to one with x₁=0. For a nonempty S containing 0, define D(S) as the best objective among orders of S starting with 0, evaluating all stabilizers in the original full group G. Then

    D({0})=0,
    D(S)=|Stab_G(S)|−1 + max_{x∈S\{0}} D(S\{x}).       (4)

Every admissible ordering has one possible final element and one smaller admissible ordering, so (4) is exact and considers all possibilities, not only hierarchy orders. There are 2^(m−1) states. The answer is D(G).

For counting, sum the maximizing-predecessor counts rather than choosing one, start with count({0})=1, and multiply the final count by m to undo the unique first-element translation normalization.

For C_m, a subset is encoded by a binary word of length m. Its stabilizer order equals m divided by its least cyclic period, which must divide m. The C++ control tests every proper divisor shift and reconstructs a maximizing ordering. It then recomputes every witness stabilizer independently by testing all m translations and all m membership bits. The Python control instead builds each product-group addition table and tests translations directly; it also generates all subgroups and checks (2).

    max_orderings Z = (p−1)W(p−1),
