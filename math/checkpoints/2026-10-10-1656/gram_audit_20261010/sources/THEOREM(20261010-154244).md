# Connected-Gram Phase Lock and Sharp Fourier Realification

**Research date:** 10 October 2026  
**Status:** Exact mathematical proofs and independent finite-size numerical checks.  
**Originality:** New combination relative to the inspected project checkpoints. Worldwide novelty **unverified**; ingredients are classical.  
**FFT complexity:** Does **not** prove a new FFT upper bound or an unrestricted lower bound.

## Abstract

We strengthen the earlier one-complex-mask Green/Gram obstruction by allowing arbitrary complex **outer monomial gauges**, *all* mask phase angles, and *all* lengths `N >= 3`. If two invertible real mixing stages have a connected weighted column-Gram support graph, unitarity forces their intervening complex diagonal phases to be collinear modulo `pi`. In particular, if either real mixing stage is entrywise strictly positive, every exactly unitary matrix in this architecture is diagonally equivalent to a real matrix. Since the discrete Fourier matrix has a nonreal gauge-invariant `2x2` cross-ratio for `N >= 3`, no exact Fourier factorization is possible in this family.

We additionally solve an all-length *sharp* Fourier realification problem in Frobenius norm, using quadratic CAZAC/Chu sequences. This yields a conditioning-independent spectral distance from the normalized DFT for any exactly unitary member of the above architecture, converging to `1/sqrt(2)` as `N -> infinity`. The result holds for non-affine, independently permuted **positive** Green sums, even though their inverse is generally dense.

## Definitions and conventions

Let `U_N` denote the normalized complex Fourier matrix,

\[
(U_N)_{jk}=\frac{e^{-2\pi i jk/N}}{\sqrt{N}},\quad 0\le j,k<N.
\]

An invertible *monomial* matrix is a permutation times an invertible diagonal. `A>0` always means **entrywise strictly positive real**; it does not mean positive-definite. A matrix is *diagonally real-equivalent* when it equals `L X R` with `X` real and `L,R` invertible complex diagonal; allowing external permutations yields monomial real-equivalence.

## Theorem 1: connected-Gram phase locking

Let `A,B in GL_N(R)`, `L,R,D in GL_N(C)` invertible diagonal, and suppose

\[
M=L A D B R,\qquad M^*M=I.
\]

Let

\[
K=A^T\operatorname{diag}(|L_{11}|^2,\ldots,|L_{NN}|^2)A,
\]

and form the undirected graph on `{1,...,N}` with edge `i--j` whenever `i!=j` and `K_{ij}!=0`.

**Claim.** Along every edge, the ratio `d_i/d_j` is real and nonzero. On each connected component, all phases of `d_i` agree modulo `pi`. If the graph is connected, `D=e^{i\theta}D_0` for some invertible **real** diagonal `D_0`, and `M` is diagonally real-equivalent.

**Proof.** From `M^*M=I`:

\[
B^T D^*KDB = \operatorname{diag}(|R_{11}|^{-2},\ldots,|R_{NN}|^{-2}).
\]

Since `B` is real and invertible,

\[
D^*KD=B^{-T}\operatorname{diag}(|R_{jj}|^{-2})B^{-1}
\]

is **real symmetric**. In entry `(i,j)`, this says `conj(d_i) K_{ij} d_j` is real. If `K_{ij}!=0`, `K_{ij}` is real and `d_i,d_j` are nonzero, hence `conj(d_i)d_j` is real. Thus `d_j/d_i` is real. Propagating along graph paths proves the component and connectivity statements. Finally, substitute `D=e^{i\theta}D_0` into `M=LADB R`: its core `A D_0 B` is real. QED.

**Dual statement:** applying the same argument to `MM^*=I` with `B\operatorname{diag}(|R_{jj}|^2)B^T` instead gives phase locking whenever the corresponding **row-Gram** support graph of `B` is connected.

**Positive-stage corollary.** If `A` is entrywise strictly positive and invertible, `K_{ij}=\sum_u |L_{uu}|^2 A_{ui}A_{uj}>0` for all `i,j`, so its Gram graph is complete. Similarly if `B>0`, its row-Gram graph is complete. Therefore `A>0` OR `B>0` (with the other stage real invertible) is sufficient.

Outer permutations cause no difficulty: remove them by unitary row/column permutations first; their effect on the target is just a permutation of indices.

**Disconnected caveat.** Connectedness is essential for *phase locking*. Example `A=B=I_3`, `D=diag(1,i,1)`, `L=R=I` is exactly unitary but has noncollinear middle phases. The result is not asserted without a connected Gram graph.

## Theorem 2: no one-mask positive-Green Fourier representation

For every `N >= 3`, no factorization

\[
\boxed{U_N=L A D B R}
\]

exists with invertible complex monomial `L,R`, arbitrary invertible complex diagonal `D`, real invertible `A,B`, and at least one of `A,B` entrywise strictly positive.

**Proof.** If such equality holds, its left side is unitary. Theorem 1 or its dual makes the middle diagonal collinear modulo `pi`; therefore `U_N` would be monomially real-equivalent. But for any real matrix with all entries nonzero, the gauge-invariant cross-ratio

\[
\chi_{ij;k\ell}(X)=\frac{X_{ik}X_{j\ell}}{X_{i\ell}X_{jk}}
\]

is real, and this property is unchanged by nonzero row/column scalings or permutations. For the Fourier matrix, the minor using rows `(0,1)` and columns `(0,1)` has cross-ratio

\[
\chi_{01;01}(U_N)=e^{-2\pi i/N}\notin\mathbb R\quad(N\ge3).
\]

Contradiction. QED.

**N=2 is exceptional and the condition `N>=3` is necessary.** An exact positive-stage factorization of `U_2` is

\[
A=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&2\end{pmatrix},\ B=\begin{pmatrix}3&5/3\\1&1\end{pmatrix},\ D=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1),
\]

\[
L=\operatorname{diag}(1/(2\sqrt2),1/\sqrt2),\quad R=\operatorname{diag}(1,3),\quad LADB R=U_2.
\]

**Specialization to Green networks.** For a Hamiltonian-cycle permutation `P`, `0<rho<1`,

\[
G_\rho=(I-\rho P)^{-1}=\frac{I+\rho P+\cdots+\rho^{N-1}P^{N-1}}{1-\rho^N}
\]

has all entries strictly positive and is invertible. Sums `A=G_1+G_2` and `B=G_3+G_4`, with independently permuted inputs/outputs and positive real endpoint weights, are strictly positive; invertibility of each *sum* must be separately required (though generically holds). Thus **no** four-Green factorization `U_N=L A D B R` exists in this positive-aggregate subclass for `N>=3`, for **any complex phase mask D**, however wide its angular spread. This strengthens the narrower Phase-22 one-mask theorem and the later `W<pi` phase-cone screen of this project. It does not exclude genuinely complex, phase-frustrated aggregate stages, nor a network with >=2 internal masks.

## Theorem 3: sharp all-length Fourier realification in Frobenius norm

Put `g=gcd(N,2)`. Define

\[
\mathcal R_N=\min_{\substack{|u_j|=|v_k|=1\\X\in\mathbb R^{N\times N}}}
\|\operatorname{diag}(u)U_N\operatorname{diag}(v)-X\|_F^2.
\]

Then **for every N>=2**,

\[
\boxed{\mathcal R_N=\frac{N-\sqrt{gN}}2.}\tag{3.1}
\]

Moreover, an explicit pair of row/column unitary gauges constructed from a quadratic CAZAC/Chu chirp achieves equality.

**Proof of the lower bound.** For fixed row and column phases, the best real `X` is the real part of `H=diag(u)U_N diag(v)`. Since `Im(z)^2=(|z|^2-Re(z^2))/2`, summing over `jk` yields

\[
\|\Im H\|_F^2=\frac N2-\frac1{2N}\Re\left(\sum_{j,k}u_j^2v_k^2\omega^{2jk}\right),\qquad \omega=e^{-2\pi i/N}.
\]

Let `W[j,k]=omega^(2jk)`, and `a_j=u_j^2`, `b_k=v_k^2`. All entries of `a,b` have absolute value one. The matrix `W W^*` has `g`-by-`g` all-ones blocks multiplied by `N`, since `N | 2(j-j')` holds precisely within a congruence class modulo `N/g`. Hence

\[
\|W\|_2=\sqrt{gN},\quad
|a^T Wb|\le\|a\|_2\|W\|_2\|b\|_2=N\sqrt{gN}.
\]

The result is `||Im H||_F^2 >= (N-sqrt(gN))/2`.

**Proof of sharpness.** Set `m=N/g` and define for `k=0,...,m-1` the quadratic chirp

\[
c_k=\exp\left(\frac{\pi i}{m}\,k(k+\delta)\right),\quad \delta=\begin{cases}0,&m\text{ even},\\1,&m\text{ odd}.\end{cases}
\]

This is periodic modulo `m`. For every nonzero cyclic shift `t mod m`, its autocorrelation vanishes because

\[
\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}c_{k+t}\overline{c_k}
=e^{\pi i t(t+\delta)/m}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}e^{2\pi i kt/m}=0.
\]

By the discrete Fourier transform of the autocorrelation, its unnormalized Fourier transform has constant modulus `sqrt(m)`. Repeat `c` exactly `g` times to form `b in C^N`. Then every entry of `Wb` has modulus `g sqrt(m)=sqrt(gN)`. Define

\[
a_j=\frac{\overline{(Wb)_j}}{|(Wb)_j|}
\]

and choose any unit-modulus square roots `u_j^2=a_j`, `v_k^2=b_k`. Now `a^T Wb=N sqrt(gN)` is positive real, attaining equality. QED.

**Classical ingredient:** quadratic Chu/Zadoff-Chu sequences have constant amplitude and perfect cyclic autocorrelation. The use of that ingredient is cited, not asserted as newly discovered mathematics.

## Corollary 4: a universal spectral obstruction for exact-unitary positive-Green networks

For arbitrary invertible complex monomial `L,R` and any **real** `X`,

\[
\boxed{\|LXR-U_N\|_2\ge c_N
:=\sqrt{\frac{1-\sqrt{g/N}}2},\qquad g=\gcd(2,N).}\tag{4.1}
\]

**Proof.** Strip phases and permutations from `L,R` by unitary transformations. The transformed candidate is real, and the transformed Fourier matrix is a row/column-rephased and permuted `U_N`. By Theorem 3, the imaginary part of that Fourier matrix has squared Frobenius norm at least `(N-sqrt(gN))/2`. The spectral norm of the complex error is at least the spectral norm of its entrywise imaginary part, itself at least the Frobenius norm divided by `sqrt(N)`.

For **any exactly unitary** one-mask network `M=LADB R` satisfying the connected-Gram assumption of Theorem 1, phase locking makes `M` monomially real-equivalent. Therefore

\[
\boxed{\|M-U_N\|_2\ge c_N.}\tag{4.2}
\]

This bound is independent of condition numbers, Green weights, non-affine cycle permutations, and arbitrary complex phases in the middle mask. It tends to `1/sqrt(2) = 0.70710678...` as `N->infinity`. It is a **valid bound, not a claim that the spectral constant is sharp**. The *Frobenius identity* (3.1), by contrast, is sharp.

**Critical scope:** The stability corollary requires the candidate `M` itself to be **exactly unitary**. A general *approximately unitary* `LADB R` is not automatically phase-locked, and no conditioning-independent extension of (4.2) to all such approximants is claimed. For **exact** FFT representation, exact unitarity is automatic, so Theorem 2 is fully decisive.

## Corollary 5: projective-positive Green stage gate

An entrywise nonzero complex matrix `A` is diagonally equivalent to an entrywise strictly positive real matrix if and only if **every anchored cross-ratio**

\[
\frac{A_{ij}A_{00}}{A_{i0}A_{0j}}\in\mathbb R_{>0}
\quad\text{for all }i,j.
\]

Necessity is immediate. For sufficiency, multiply row `i` by `A_{00}/A_{i0}` and column `j` by `1/A_{0j}`; the resulting entries are exactly these positive ratios.

Thus if **both** aggregate Green stages in `U_N=L A D B R` are individually projectively positive (possibly after independent endpoint permutations), their positive real representatives can be absorbed into the three diagonal gauges, and Theorem 2 rules out exact Fourier equality for all `N>=3`. This is a small exact gauge test for fixed stages, not an `O(N)` test for arbitrary dense black-box matrices.

A successful all-length four-Green construction with a single middle mask must therefore have **essential complex projective frustration in at least one aggregate Green sum**. Passing this necessary condition is not sufficient for a Fourier factorization.

## Scope/limitations and future work

* This eliminates the **positive, or projectively positive, one-internal-mask** Green network, including broad phase spans previously left open by the phase-cone theorem.
* It does not eliminate two or more independent internal complex masks, nor genuinely phase-frustrated complex Green sums, nor general fast circuits.
* It does not prove `O(N)` or `o(N log N)` for arbitrary exact Fourier transforms.
* Although (3.1) is proved here and not present verbatim in the inspected project checkpoints, its classical CAZAC and operator-norm ingredients mean **global publication-level novelty is not established**.
* A next constructive target is a **phase-frustrated** four-Green aggregate stage satisfying exact Weyl covariance and quadratic/Segre compatibility, with an all-length parameter-generation proof and complete charged sequential work analysis.

## Theorem 6: exact realification for every finite abelian Fourier matrix

The cyclic formula has a clean extension that is potentially useful beyond power-of-two FFTs.

Let `G` be **any finite abelian group** of cardinality `N`, let `U_G` be its normalized character-table (Fourier) matrix, and let

\[
g=|G[2]|=|\{x\in G:2x=0\}|.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{\min_{|u_\chi|=|v_x|=1}\;\min_{X\in\mathbb R^{N\times N}}
\|\operatorname{diag}(u)U_G\operatorname{diag}(v)-X\|_F^2
=\frac{N-\sqrt{gN}}{2}.}\tag{6.1}
\]

**Proof.** By the structure theorem, `G` is a product `Z_{n_1} x ... x Z_{n_t}`. Its Fourier character table is (up to row/column permutations) the tensor product of the cyclic `U_{n_i}`. Squaring each entry produces the Kronecker product `W=\bigotimes_i W_{n_i}`, where `W_{n_i}[j,k]=exp(-4 pi i jk/n_i)`. Thus

\[
\|W\|_2=\prod_i\sqrt{n_i\gcd(2,n_i)}=\sqrt{Ng},
\]

since `g=\prod_i gcd(2,n_i)`. The lower-bound argument of Theorem 3 applies verbatim. For equality, tensor the `b_{n_i}` chirp vectors from Theorem 3 to obtain a unimodular `b` for which `|W b|=sqrt(Ng)` entrywise, then take the conjugate phases of `Wb` as the row coefficient vector `a` and choose unit-modulus square roots. This achieves `a^T W b=N sqrt(Ng)` and therefore equality. QED.

**Consequences.** The normalized Fourier table is row/column-phase equivalent to a real matrix **exactly when** `g=N`, equivalently every group element has order at most two. This includes Walsh-Hadamard transforms on `G=(Z_2)^t`, whose Fourier matrices are already real. For cyclic `G=Z_N`, `g=gcd(2,N)` and (6.1) reduces to Theorem 3. The corresponding conditioning-independent spectral distance from any monomially real-equivalent matrix is at least

\[
\boxed{\sqrt{\frac{1-\sqrt{g/N}}2}.}\tag{6.2}
\]

The identity is checked by the independent `verify_abelian.py` program for ten finite abelian groups with differing `2`-torsion sizes. This generalization is not a universal FFT operation-count result.
