# Version 1 supplement: a common quadratic-chirp cycle does not repair the source

10 October 2026. Supplement to `CONSTRUCTIVE_ATTEMPT_AND_BARRIER.md`; the original candidate proof remains unchanged. This broadens the proved specialization to a phase-bearing shared cycle, still within the actual Necklace four-Green grammar. No unrelated-cycle exclusion is claimed.

## 1. Explicit phase-bearing candidate

Keep the field, supplied primitive N-th root omega, symmetric short-orbit Hessian H, and scalar four-Green branch structure from the main note. Choose integers h and ell, and form

    C_ii=omega^(h i^2+ell i),    W=C S C^(-1).

These are genuine input-independent, generally nonconstant cycle-edge gains: W e_j=(C_(j+1)/C_j)e_(j+1), with indices modulo N. For integer h,ell the formula for C is periodic modulo N and uses only the supplied N-th root. It is not a half-integer chirp needing an unstated primitive 2N-th root.

Replace every G_a in the main candidate by

    G_a(W)=(I-aW)^(-1)=C (I-aS)^(-1) C^(-1).

The same a^N!=1 guards suffice because W^N=I. The gains can again be (2,3,5,7) in characteristic zero, or any other legal generated values. For fixed h, the C entries are generated by two multiplicative recurrences: C_0=1; the ratio C_(i+1)/C_i starts at omega^(h+ell) and is multiplied by omega^(2h) each step. Thus the whole phase/gain preparation, applications and transposes remain O(N); ell may be reduced modulo N. No Fourier application is hidden in this construction.

Center Phi as in the main note. It remains a polynomial in x and an origin-regular, uniformly charged O(N) scalar family for k=n+2. Its Hessian is P^T H P.

## 2. Exact chirp-cleared density lemma

Let q(t)=sum_(a=0)^r q_a t^a, r<N, with Q=q(W) invertible. Let P=R Q^(-1), where H has row support at most Delta, and R has column/row support at most s,d. Put

    w=(2|h|+1)r.

If w<N and P^T H P=F_N, then

    N-w <= s d Delta.                                (1)

When w>=N, the corresponding nonpositive lower bound is vacuous; no exclusion is asserted from it. Fixed integer h means its absolute value is bounded independently of N when taking the asymptotic consequence. A growing h is allowed in the exact displayed inequality but may defeat this test.

### Proof with complete exponent convention

Q=C q(S) C^(-1). With S e_j=e_(j+1),

    (Q^T F_N Q)_(i,j)
       = omega^(ij) sum_(a,b=0)^r q_a q_b
           omega^[h(a^2+b^2)+ell(a+b)+ab]
           (omega^i)^(2h a+b) (omega^j)^(a+2h b).     (2)

Wraparound does not alter this identity because all exponents are taken through omega of order N and h,ell are integers. No complex conjugates occur.

For fixed j, the sum is a Laurent polynomial L_j(z) in z=omega^i. Its exponents 2h a+b lie in [0,(2h+1)r] if h>=0, and in [2hr,r] if h<0. In both cases its exponent width is at most w. Multiplying by the appropriate monomial gives an ordinary polynomial of degree at most w and does not change its zeros at nonzero roots.

A separate nonvanishing argument is necessary. Different (a,b) pairs may collide and cancel at the same exponent, so the old constant-term shortcut must not be reused. Since Q and F_N are invertible, Q^T F_N Q is invertible and its j-th column is not zero. Equation (2) therefore makes L_j nonzero at at least one N-th root; in particular it is not the zero Laurent polynomial. The root bound gives at least N-w nonzero entries in every column.

Fourier equality again implies Q^T F_N Q=R^T H R. The latter has at most NsdDelta nonzero entries by the same row-support argument. The former has at least N(N-w). This proves (1). QED.

The q_0!=0 guard is not needed for this supplementary lemma: nonvanishing is supplied by matrix invertibility. It remains present in the unchanged main theorem.

## 3. Consequence for the four-Green construction

Using the same four scalar denominator factors and pair numerators, all polynomials are now evaluated at W. Powers of W are weighted shifts, so the support counts remain r<=4, s<=8, d<=4. With canonical horizon k=n+2,

    N-4(2|h|+1) <= 64(n+2)                          (3)

is necessary, whenever its left-side density bound is positive. For each fixed integer h, it fails at sufficiently large dyadic N. For h=0,+1,-1,+2,-2 it already fails for every dyadic N>=1024: the weakest of these five lower bounds is N-20, exceeding 768 at N=1024, and the difference increases thereafter.

The carrier coefficients and baseline may be retuned without escaping this particular bound, because the short-orbit support Delta is unchanged. Scalar branch cancellations and repeated/zero gains also remain covered.

A fixed-curvature common chirp thus does not turn the four-Green regular source into Fourier. The real open transport problem is still to leave this bounded common-cycle denominator class while establishing the full Fourier identity and uniform costs. Arbitrary varying gains, a nonquadratic diagonal gauge, genuinely distinct cycles, and growing-curvature choices outside the numerical bound are not ruled out.
