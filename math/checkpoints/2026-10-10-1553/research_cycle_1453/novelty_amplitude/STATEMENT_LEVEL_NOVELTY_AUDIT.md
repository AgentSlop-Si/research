# Statement-level novelty audit: amplitude fields and approximation

Date: 10 October 2026. Read-only audit of accepted source artifacts; no source programs were executed or imported and no external records were changed.

## Bottom line

**These two artifacts do not currently establish a mathematically novel breakthrough.** Their strongest result is a correct, sharp coefficient-field characterization in a tightly restricted positive-phase model. Its precise combined formulation may be original, but the main existence, minimum-palette, and coverage conclusions are short consequences of classical results. There is now a direct derivation from Chebotarev and conic Carathéodory that avoids Gaussian periods altogether. Consequently, calling this a breakthrough, or counting it as an independently priority-cleared new theorem, is not justified.

This does **not** prove that nobody previously omitted this exact statement from the literature, or that an original short note is impossible. Novel formulation, useful model clarification, proof correctness, historical priority, and research significance are different questions. The evidence here supports correctness and practical clarification, not a strong novelty claim.

## Exact primary-source overlaps

1. **Galois base change.** Milne, *Fields and Galois Theory*, Proposition 3.19, p. 40, identifies Gal(EK/K) with Gal(E/E∩K) when E/Q is Galois. This directly supplies the minimal-polynomial degree h and the field-intersection reduction. No new Galois theorem is present here. https://www.jmilne.org/math/CourseNotes/FT.pdf

2. **Full-spark independence.** Tao, *An uncertainty principle for cyclic groups of prime order*, Lemma 1.3, p. 3, states that every square minor of the prime Fourier matrix is nonzero, explicitly crediting Chebotarev (1926). Applying the h automorphisms fixing L to a putative L-relation gives exactly the artifact's independence lemma. This is a direct specialization of the established theorem. https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0308286

3. **Conic support reduction.** Bertsekas, *Convex Analysis and Optimization*, MIT 6.253 lecture slides (Spring 2014), PDF pp. 37–38, states and proves that a nonzero point in a generated cone has a positive representation using linearly independent generators. The proof eliminates coefficients along a linear dependence until one vanishes. This is the classical conic Carathéodory theorem. https://www.mit.edu/~dimitrib/Convex_Slides_2014.pdf

4. **Minimum positive-basis cardinality.** Hare–Song, *On the Cardinality of Positively Linearly Independent Sets*, Proposition 1, p. 2, explicitly attributes to Davis Theorem 3.7 that deleting any one member of a positive spanning set leaves a linearly spanning set. Hence at least n+1 vectors are required in dimension n. This supplies the minimum-palette lower bound after passing to L-coordinate space. https://arxiv.org/pdf/1509.07496

5. **The exact Gaussian-period second moment is already published.** Garcia–Lorenz–Todd, *Moments of Gaussian Periods and Modified Fermat Curves*, arXiv version, Lemma 15, p. 7, states V₂(p) = ((d−1)p+1)/d = p−(p−1)/d. Their periods have subgroup size k=(p−1)/d. Substituting k=h gives p−h exactly; when −1 belongs to the subgroup, the periods are real, so absolute squares equal squares. Their introduction also describes the first and second power moments as elementary. Thus the artifact's second-moment identity is not new. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.13886 ; published version: https://doi.org/10.1007/s11139-025-01090-x

6. **Cyclotomic matroid formulation predates this work.** Martin–Reiner, *Cyclotomic and simplicial matroids*, §2, p. 4, writes the exact sequence Q → Q^p → Q(ζ_p), with kernel generated by the all-ones vector. This directly overlaps the rational relation-space/coverage case. The paper studies Q, not the artifact's general real intermediate coefficient fields, so it should not be represented as stating the complete new formulation. https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0402206

Davis's original 1954 publication metadata was verified at https://www.jstor.org/stable/2372648. Its full text was not obtained in this pass. The Davis theorem-number attribution above is verified through Hare–Song's primary paper, not claimed from a full reading of Davis. McKinney's 1962 *Positive Bases for Linear Spaces* was located, but inaccessible full text is not counted as reviewed.

## A short classical derivation of the main exact results

The following is an audit derivation, not a claimed new theorem.

Choose an L-basis of E and let v_r ∈ L^h ⊂ R^h be the coordinate column of ζ_p^r. Chebotarev implies that every h distinct columns are independent. Their sum is zero.

Start from

    −v_0 = Σ_{r=1}^{p−1} v_r.

Apply conic Carathéodory in R^h. It gives a representation of −v_0 with positive coefficients on at most h independent columns v_r, r≠0. The coefficients lie in L: restrict to this independent support and solve the corresponding linear equations, whose coefficients and right-hand side lie in L. Chebotarev rules out fewer than h terms, since together with v_0 those would produce a dependence on at most h columns. Therefore there are exactly h terms, all positive in L.

Adjoin v_0. These h+1 columns have a strictly positive dependence and any h form a basis. They positively span the whole coordinate space: express a target in a basis and add a suitable multiple of the positive dependence to make every coordinate nonnegative, with at least one coordinate zero. The same operation stays inside L. Thus h+1 phases containing phase 0 suffice, and each omitted target uses exactly h of them.

Conversely, a palette that represents every root positively must have at least h+1 members. A palette of h or fewer is independent; summing representations of all roots would produce a nonzero nonnegative dependence, impossible for an independent set.

If L=Q, the relation space is the line generated by (1,...,1), so a positive representation of ζ^k has coefficients c_r=t+1[r=k] with t≥0. The target phase must occur. If L≠Q, then h<p−1 and the h+1 palette above omits a root. Its representation lacks its own phase. Multiplication by a root of unity carries this witness to any prescribed target, preserving positive L coefficients. Coverage therefore fails for every target. This proves the coverage iff.

This route also proves sharp h-term missing-target representations. It uses no period sign argument and no new number-theoretic estimate. The field-constrained issue is real, but is handled by coordinate-space convexity plus linear algebra, rather than by treating E as the ordinary complex plane.

### Coefficient-field descent is equally standard linear feasibility

For A,b over L, if Ax=b has a nonnegative solution over an extension K⊂R, view it as a real feasible solution. Conic Carathéodory reduces to independent support. The unique coefficients on that support solve equations over L and hence belong to L. Therefore existence over K implies existence over L. This is the familiar basic-feasible-solution principle; the source's density argument is another valid elementary proof. No special new descent phenomenon needs to be postulated.

## Statement-by-statement classification

### Exact amplitude theorem

- **Intersection controls relations/minimal polynomial:** classical Galois base-change result, immediate application.
- **At most h roots independent:** direct Chebotarev corollary.
- **Coverage iff K∩Q(ζ_p)=Q:** useful sharp formulation, but a short corollary of the preceding classical facts and positive-cone support reduction. The precise wording has not been priority-cleared.
- **Minimum global palette h+1:** exact application of classical full-spark positive-basis geometry. The proof above is a direct derivation; the artifact's more explicit period construction adds a structured witness.
- **Gaussian periods with a positive and a negative value:** the period object and second moment are established; the needed sign conclusion follows immediately from their sum −1 and squared sum p−h. The application to this palette is potentially an original presentation, not a new Gaussian-period theorem.
- **Optimized depth-one edge count:** once included targets cost one edge and excluded targets cost h, this is weighted counting. Phase 0 occurs 2p−1 times and every other phase p−1 times. A minimum palette containing 0 attains `(2p−1)+h(p−1)(p−h)`. This is a sharp model-specific corollary, with no new combinatorial optimization mechanism.
- **Three phases iff E+⊂K:** substitute h=2 into the palette theorem; the sine-ratio realization is planar linear algebra. An explicit all-prime example is useful for rebutting an incorrectly generalized rational-amplitude obstruction.
- **Fixed number fields escape only at discriminant primes:** immediate consequence of total ramification of p in Q(ζ_p), ramification in towers, and the discriminant criterion. Classical number theory, not an independent new result.
- **Deeper-DAG path-alphabet bound:** composition of the palette bound with elementary multiset counting. It remains necessary-only and is not an unrestricted circuit lower bound.

### Approximation supplement

- **Three rational-amplitude phases approximate arbitrarily well:** classical planar positive spanning plus density of Q in R.
- **Uniform coefficient bound and B-bit error:** a well-conditioned triangle, sine-rule coordinates, and elementary quantization; useful explicit constants, not a new approximation method.
- **Relative operator guarantee from entrywise error:** standard matrix-norm inequalities.
- **Sharp two-ray expression, 1 or cos(π/(2p)) by p mod 4:** a neat elementary discrete-geometry calculation. No exact primary-source match was found in the limited search, but this is not evidence of novelty. Its derivation uses only nearest-ray projection and regular-polygon angle ordering; indeed the root-to-cone formula does not use primality and extends to every odd root count. This is a plausible minor original observation pending priority review, not a breakthrough candidate.
- **Conservative relative spectral lower bound ≥1/3:** standard half-plane projection, orthogonality, Cauchy–Schwarz, and Frobenius-to-spectral comparison. The specific constant/presentation may be distinct, but it is non-sharp and supplies no new lower-bound method.
- **One parameter can hide degree (p−1)/2:** classical maximal-real cyclotomic degree and Chebyshev recurrence; a valuable accounting warning rather than novel mathematics.

## Value and standalone-paper assessment

The value is strongest as a **scope-correcting lemma or compact technical note**: it precisely explains when an exact rational positive-path obstruction survives field extension, and why it does not survive approximation. That can materially improve the surrounding research even without historical novelty.

A standalone research-paper claim is presently weak. A short expository/application note may be reasonable if it (a) presents the minimal classical derivation, (b) clearly attributes the period moments and positive-basis facts, and (c) establishes an audience-relevant consequence beyond relabeling edge counts. A serious original research claim would benefit from genuinely new restrictions or results, such as composite orders, bounded-height/bit-cost field constraints, classification of all optimal palettes, quantitative approximation barriers with constrained amplitudes, or substantive deeper-circuit consequences. These are possible directions, not results established by the two audited artifacts.

## Missing comparison that matters most

The core draft should directly compare its theorem with **conic Carathéodory on the full-spark L-coordinate representation**, not only cite Davis in general terms. That comparison removes the impression that the h+1 theorem requires a new Gaussian-period construction. Further historical review should inspect positive circuits of uniform realizable oriented matroids and cyclotomic matroids over intermediate ordered fields. No located source was verified to state the entire exact field/palette/edge formula verbatim.

The two-ray formula still needs a targeted geometry literature comparison if it is to be claimed as new. Merely locating no exact formula in a keyword search is insufficient.
