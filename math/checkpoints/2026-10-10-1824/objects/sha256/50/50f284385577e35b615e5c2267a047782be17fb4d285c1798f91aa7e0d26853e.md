## Exact overlap and the failed direct substitution

This paragraph is a mathematical comparison derived here, rather than an additional assertion attributed to the source. For an ordering of the finite abelian group G of order m, retain the proof's notation Tⱼ and Hⱼ, and put hⱼ=|Hⱼ|. Consider the poset consisting of all translates of all prefixes, including ∅. Its rank-j layer Wⱼ is the orbit of Tⱼ, so orbit-stabilizer gives

    |Wⱼ| = m/hⱼ.

Consequently its antichain inequality, in the paper's empty-minimum specialization, becomes

    Σⱼ fⱼ hⱼ/m ≤ 1,

where fⱼ counts members of an admissible antichain in the rank-j orbit. Meanwhile our objective is

    Z/m = Σⱼ₌₁ᵐ hⱼ/m − 1.

Thus the same reciprocal orbit sizes occur. The distinction is the admissible family: T₁,…,Tₘ are mutually comparable. Using all of them with fⱼ=1 is not a legal substitution into an antichain theorem.

For a concrete check, take G=C₄ and the order 0,2,1,3. Its stabilizer sizes are 1,2,1,4; Z=4. Substituting the full chain into the displayed antichain expression would give 2≤1. The contradiction diagnoses the invalid substitution, not a defect in either theorem. One rank at a time is legal, but yields no control on incompatibility between stabilizers at different ranks.

## What the singleton observation really establishes

This is a conditional deduction from the singleton regularity property recorded in Section 5 of the earlier comparison. It is not a claim to have reread the missing definition page.

Let A contain x in a regular translation action, and suppose every translate of A containing x must equal A. There are |A| translations g with x∈A+g. Each distinct translate has exactly |Stab(A)| translation representatives. Hence exactly |A|/|Stab(A)| distinct translates contain x, and the supposed property forces |A|=|Stab(A)|. Since A is a union of stabilizer cosets, A is one coset.

Applied to every nonempty prefix, this would require every prefix size to be a subgroup order. For m>2 it already fails at size m−1, because m−1 does not divide m. It therefore excludes even maximizing saturated nonempty-prefix chains in that singleton formulation. Adding ∅ changes the formulation: the empty set is fixed by every group element, and the paper explicitly makes that choice in Section 3. The earlier conditional argument cannot be extended across that change.
