The most useful new lead is **connectivity sets / global ascents / Coxeter support**. These give an exact reformulation of the objective using standard literature terminology.

## Exact reformulation derived in this comparison

For w in S_m, let C(w) be its connectivity set, consisting of j<m for which w({1,...,j})={1,...,j}. Let supp_A(w) be the simple reflections appearing in a reduced word in the standard Coxeter generators (12),(23),...,(m-1,m). This is not the set of moved points. Then

    c(w) = 1 + |C(w)| = m - |supp_A(w)|.

For a labeling of a regular abelian group G of order m, write Gamma for the resulting subgroup of S_m. Double counting gives

    Z = sum_{g in Gamma} |C(g)|
      = m(m-1) - sum_{g in Gamma} |supp_A(g)|.

Indeed, the first sum is sum_{j=1}^{m-1}|Stab(T_j)|; adding the final-prefix contribution m-1 and subtracting 1 at each proper prefix gives exactly Z. Consequently the theorem is equivalent to

    max over conjugates Gamma of G: average_{g in Gamma} |C(g)| = W(m),
    min over conjugates Gamma of G: average_{g in Gamma} |supp_A(g)| = m-1-W(m).

For an m-cycle tau this becomes

    min_tau sum_{r=1}^{m-1}|supp_A(tau^r)| = m(m-1)-mW(m),
    max_tau sum_{r=1}^{m-1}|C(tau^r)| = mW(m)-(m-1).

The identity has empty Coxeter support and connectivity set [m-1], explaining the offsets. These displayed extremal statements are deductions from the supplied theorem, not results attributed to the following papers.

## Source-by-source comparison

### 1. Richard P. Stanley, The Descent Set and Connectivity Set of a Permutation (2005)

Primary full text: https://math.mit.edu/~rstan/papers/decset.pdf

Verified: equation (1) defines the exact proper invariant-prefix statistic C(w). Proposition 1.1 counts permutations whose connectivity set contains a prescribed set. Theorems 1.2 and 1.4 concern joint connectivity/descent enumeration and matrix inversion; Section 2 treats multisets and an inversion-number q-analogue. The paper's references identify earlier equivalent definitions in Comtet and Callan.

Overlap: exact single-permutation statistic and standard terminology. Boundary: the paper does not state the extremum over the jointly constrained powers of one cycle or conjugates of a regular abelian group, nor the prime-index coset optimizer count. Its distribution over all of S_m does not impose those joint subgroup constraints. The inspected searchable full text has no match for “power”.

### 2. N. Bergeron, C. Hohlweg, M. Zabrocki, Posets related to the connectivity set of Coxeter groups

Primary preprint: https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0509271

Full text: https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0509271

Verified: equation (3) defines reduced-word support S(w); Definition 1 gives C(w)=S\\S(w). The introduction also identifies connectivity sets as global ascent sets. The main results construct and analyze a graded poset, its shellable lattice intervals, Galois connections, and Möbius functions. Section 2 gives connectivity-count enumeration in types A, B, and D.

Overlap: exact Coxeter-support and parabolic-subgroup language for the proposed equivalent optimization. Boundary: this is a structural/enumerative poset result, not an average-support extremum over conjugate regular subgroups. No such extremum or recursive optimizer count was found in the inspected paper. Searches for “abelian” and “cyclic” in its accessible full text returned no match.

### 3. Pentti Haukkanen, Jorma K. Merikoski, Timo Tossavainen, The arithmetic derivative and Leibniz-additive functions (2018)

Primary preprint/full text: https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.06849 and https://arxiv.org/pdf/1803.06849

Verified: the introduction gives n'=n sum_p v_p(n)/p. Theorems 2.1–2.4 describe functions f with f(ab)=a f(b)+b f(a), their completely additive quotient f(n)/n, and determination by prime values.

Overlap: M(n)=nW(n)=n Omega(n)-n' is exactly the Leibniz-additive function with M(p)=p-1. This algebraic identification follows directly from the paper's general theorem. Boundary: an existing expression/class of arithmetic functions does not identify its realization as an ordering maximum. The source contains no prefix-stabilizer optimization or equality/count classification. No separate priority claim should be made for the arithmetic-derivative rewrite itself.

### 4. María Isabel González Vasco, Martin Rötteler, Rainer Steinwandt, On Minimal Length Factorizations of Finite Groups (2003)

Primary author-hosted full text: https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/On-Minimal-Length-Factorizations-of-Finite-Groups.pdf

Verified: Definition 2.1 defines logarithmic signatures; the following construction uses ordered coset transversals along a subgroup chain. Remark 2.2 gives the lower bound B(G)=sum_p v_p(|G|)p on signature length. Proposition 3.1 obtains equality for solvable groups from prime-index composition series. Remark 3.2 gives abelian-group constructions.

Overlap: established subgroup-chain/transversal construction and prime-factor structure. Boundary: the optimized quantity is the sum of factor-set cardinalities, not a sum of stabilizer cardinalities along every element prefix. A signature based on one fixed subgroup chain only recovers a restricted hierarchy construction. The supplied equality theorem permits different internal hierarchies in different cosets, and adds a global optimality assertion absent here. No matching optimizer count was found.

### 5. Péter L. Erdős, Ulrich Faigle, Walter Kern, A Group-Theoretic Setting for Some Intersecting Sperner Families (1992)

Primary institutional full text: https://real.mtak.hu/9932/1/ElpFaigleKern-CPC92.pdf

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1017/S0963548300000377

Verification limitation: the web index exposed the abstract and Section 2 definitions, but the opened PDF supplied no usable line text. This was not a full-paper inspection.

Verified overlap: chains of subsets, their orbits/setwise stabilizers, and orbit-stabilizer weights are used for intersecting Sperner-family inequalities. Section 2 imposes a regularity hypothesis (R) relative to the smallest set A_0.

Mathematical distinction: if A_0 is a singleton and the group action is regular, (R) requires every translate of A_i containing A_0 to equal A_i. There are |A_i|/|Stab(A_i)| such translates, so A_i must be a single stabilizer coset. Arbitrary saturated prefix chains do not satisfy this condition. Thus the visible setup does not directly apply to the supplied theorem. Full-text examination is still needed before excluding any separately stated specialization elsewhere in the article.
