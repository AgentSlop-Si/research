# Statement-level priority comparison: the 9/5 Fourier bank theorem

10 October 2026. Bounded primary-source audit. This is a positioning assessment, not a new correctness review or a historical-priority certificate. No external publication or upload was made.

## Finding

The strongest defensible new manuscript claim is a **uniform target-specific upper bound in the stated finite-node dictionary**:

For odd N >= 27 and K = Q(q), the negative Fourier matrix admits at most

    2N - 2 floor((N+13)/10) + 2 = 9N/5 + O(1)

individual permitted banks over a splitting field L/K, with orientations (m,1,1,m), m = N - floor((N+13)/10), and [L:K] <= m!.

The independent step-one receipt now accepts this statement. The author text's “pending independent review” sentence is historical; the receipt controls the present proof-review status. This audit found no inspected theorem that directly supplies the displayed restricted Fourier bound. Priority remains unresolved.

The common-node moment model, simultaneous binary-form language, shared annihilator, and shift-operator technique have strong classical or modern precedents. The defensible contribution to isolate is the **specific Fourier row-space envelope and its safe-interval exclusions, followed by simultaneous legal-root and axis compatibility**, yielding this bank count for every eligible odd N. Do not claim a new general Prony/Padé method, a new rank notion, or optimality of the coefficient 9/5.

## Exact grammar and the classical reduction

The counted object is one entire bank

    B_(epsilon,delta)(alpha,beta;w)
      = (1/N) sum_(j=0)^(N-1) w_j
        (I-alpha S^epsilon-beta q^(epsilon delta j) T^delta)^(-1),

with epsilon,delta in {+1,-1}, the fixed Weyl pair TS=qST, and guard 1-alpha^N-beta^N != 0. Products, free changes of Weyl generators, and uncounted conjugations are not allowed. In the new construction beta=1 and alpha is either 1 or one of m distinct nonzero roots of g. A conjugate root orbit is not one bank: both same-sign orientations each contain m individual banks.

Within one orientation, dividing the permitted triangular coefficients by their Gaussian factors gives t_(r,ell)=eta_ell alpha^r. Homogenizing each sequence identifies the bank with simultaneous powers eta_ell(X+alpha Y)^(N-1-ell). This is a finite-direction portion of the classical scroll S(0,1,...,N-1), not a new geometric dictionary. The four different weighted embeddings and their overlaps remain part of the problem.

### Exact simultaneous-Padé formulation (derived in this audit)

For a positive physical row let L_b=N-b-1 and

    S_b(z)=sum_(j=0)^(L_b-1) v_b(j+1) z^j,
    g(t)=sum_(k=0)^m c_k t^k,   c_m=1,
    lambda(z)=z^m g(1/z).

The coefficient of z^(m+a-1) in lambda S_b is sum_k c_k v_b(a+k). Therefore all full-left recurrence equations are exactly

    deg(lambda S_b mod z^L_b) < m

for rows with L_b>m. Rows with L_b<=m add no recurrence constraint. This is an unequal-precision simultaneous Padé problem with a common denominator and lambda(0)=1. Its degree-m/nonzero-leading requirement corresponds to c_0 != 0; distinct nonzero roots are a further condition, not part of the linear Padé equations. The axis determinant is an additional coupled requirement.

This reduction preserves the common node count m in one orientation. The original bank construction then uses 2m+2 banks; there is no inference that a generic Padé denominator already satisfies its additional guards.

## Source-by-source comparisons

### 1. Comon–Qi–Usevich: the one-orientation dictionary is established

*Identifiability of an X-rank decomposition of polynomial maps*, arXiv:1603.01566, Sections 4.1–4.3, equations (16)–(18), in the served 26-page version. [Primary PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1603.01566)

Equation (16) allows shared vectors with different coefficients across forms of different degrees; the binary case after (18) is a rational normal scroll. Degrees may include zero. This directly supplies the established language for one bank orientation. Its generic identifiability and defining-equation results do not select the Fourier point, glue four differently weighted triangular arrays, exclude the infinite direction, or supply the new full-axis guard. Credit this framework prominently.

### 2. Ciliberto–Russo: the original source is now inspected

*Varieties with minimal secant degree and linear systems of maximal dimension on surfaces*, arXiv:math/0406494v1, Proposition 1.14 (printed p.12), Example 5.1 and Claim 5.3 (printed pp.24–26). [Primary PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0406494)

The proposition gives the secant dimension for S(a_1,...,a_n), including zero-degree cone summands; Claim 5.3 gives generic identifiability in its stated perfect, nondefective case. This closes the original-source gap recorded in the previous audit.

Immediate specialization of Proposition 1.14 to S(0,1,...,N-1), using r summands, gives secant codimension

    max(0, (N-r)(N-r+1)/2-r).

Thus the generic one-orientation rank is the least r satisfying (N-r)(N-r+1)/2 <= r, namely N-sqrt(2N)+O(1). This is the generic threshold in the ambient space of one orientation. It is not directly comparable with a total 9N/5 bank count across four overlapping orientations, and no claim that 9N/5 beats this smaller number r is intended. It does not give a four-overlap decomposition or exact finite-node attainment for the special Fourier target. Any application must also preserve the Gaussian weights and physical row/axis reconstruction. The formula is an explicit deduction here, not a quotation from their paper.

### 3. Rosenkilde–Storjohann: the entire common-denominator linear problem is classical

*Algorithms for Simultaneous Padé Approximations* (ISSAC 2016), Problem 1 and Problem 3, pp.1–2 of the 8-page author version. [Primary PDF](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~astorjoh/issac16.pdf); [arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.00836)

Problem 1 permits separate moduli and degree bounds; Problem 3 seeks a basis generating all bounded-degree solutions. The reduction above fits that formulation with moduli z^L_b. Consequently “put all rows into one common-annihilator kernel” is not itself a new method. The source gives algorithms for solving the input system, not the Fourier-specific rank bound rank A <= 4d-11, avoidance of repeated/zero roots throughout the required range, or an axis-compatible choice. Its algorithmic complexity does not become an FFT complexity bound for the present construction.

### 4. Plonka–Stampfer–Keller: chirp demodulation and adapted shifts predate this proof

*Reconstruction of stationary and non-stationary signals by the generalized Prony method*, Analysis and Applications 17(2) (2019), 179–210; author version Section 4, equation (4.1), and Theorem 4.1. [Primary PDF](https://num.math.uni-goettingen.de/~gplonka/pdfs/general-Prony-revised-sw.pdf)

The source treats H(x) times exponential sums using the conjugated shift H(x)/H(x+h), and applies this to Gaussian chirps. Thus factoring out a known quadratic chirp and using shift annihilation has a clear precedent. Its theorem recovers a signal already assumed to have a sparse expansion. The bank argument instead proves that a constrained polynomial space contains a legal common-node interpolant by excluding derivative functionals on a finite interval. The mixed envelope C_k P(rho^k)+R(q^k), the exact interval/degree inequality N>=10d-13, and the axis construction are not furnished by that recovery theorem.

### 5. Stampfer–Plonka: operator-based annihilation is also a general framework

*The Generalized Operator Based Prony Method*, arXiv:1901.08778, Theorems 3.1, 3.7 and 3.10 in the served 33-page version. [Primary PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1901.08778)

These results recover sparse eigenfunction expansions through admissible functionals, shifted operators, annihilator kernels, and Vandermonde factorizations. They reinforce attribution of the general machinery. Admissibility and sparse representability are hypotheses there; they do not prove the particular Fourier kernel avoids all derivative functionals or that legal roots and the axis determinant coexist. No count-preserving specialization to the four signed bank families was established in this audit.

### 6. Batenkov–Yomdin: Hankel solvability need not mean distinct ordinary nodes

*Local and global geometry of Prony systems and Fourier reconstruction of piecewise-smooth functions*, arXiv:1301.1187, Proposition 1 and Theorem 1 (printed pp.5–6), Section 4.1. [Primary PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1301.1187)

The paper relates moment systems to rational functions and gives Hankel solvability conditions for confluent Prony systems, explicitly allowing multiplicities and studying collisions. This is relevant precedent for why the discriminant guard matters: derivative atoms are allowed in the confluent model, whereas coincident bank nodes simply combine amplitudes. A confluent or border decomposition cannot be charged as the same number of permitted ordinary banks. The source does not replace the new theorem's squarefree existence proof or the coupling between rows and axes.

### 7. Buczyński–Landsberg: fixed generators and subspace rank are established, but the resource differs

*Ranks of tensors and a generalization of secant varieties*, arXiv:0909.4262v5, Definition 1.1 and Theorem 2.5. [Primary PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0909.4262v5)

Subspace X-rank asks for fixed points whose span contains a target linear system, and Theorem 2.5 relates it to Segre-product rank. This supports the older universal-architecture interpretation. A bank supplies a whole ruling subspace, with independent row amplitudes, rather than one scalar-weighted fixed point. The 9/5 result instead adapts nodes to one target. Neither taking the target subspace to be all matrices nor substituting a generic rank into their theorems yields the new bound. The fixed universal 2N-2 theorem and adaptive Fourier upper bound must retain different quantifiers.

### 8. Recent rational-Prony extension: a terminology near-match, not the needed theorem

Dózsa–Voigt–Szabó–Bokor–Kovács, *Generalized rational Prony and Bernoulli methods*, arXiv:2510.03510v1 (2025 preprint), Theorem 4.1. [Primary PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.03510)

This theorem converts an existing generalized-Prony signal model to a pole-finding problem by a linear map and an evaluation scheme. Its rational atoms are scalar kernels 1/(1-lambda z). Those atoms are not the fixed finite-Weyl phase-weighted matrix resolvent banks. The result assumes the model's sparse representation; it does not prove that a Fourier matrix has the required bank representation. The inspected statement is a recent framework-level precedent, not an equivalent count theorem.

## What the 9/5 result changes in a manuscript

Compared with the accepted fixed universal bound 2N-2, the new Fourier construction saves 2 floor((N+13)/10)-4 banks. Its leading coefficient is therefore materially better for this target, although it is not proved optimal. This supports leading the bank paper with:

1. The precise dictionary and the sharp universal count 2N-2.
2. The uniform target-adaptive Fourier upper bound 9N/5+O(1), including field scope.
3. Small-order exact minima and special constructions as evidence of more detailed target-dependent behavior.

Suggested theorem-summary wording:

“We determine the sharp number of fixed signed Weyl-resolvent configurations required for universal synthesis and construct Fourier-specific representations with at most 2N-2 floor((N+13)/10)+2 banks for every odd N>=27. The Fourier construction uses a common-node interpolation space whose special quadratic-phase structure permits simultaneous control of ordinary roots and overlapping row/axis constraints.”

The words “new,” “first,” “optimal Fourier bound,” and “faster FFT” should not appear without additional evidence. No source inspected here establishes historical priority of the specific bound. Each bank contains N inverse branches and N raw phase weights; approximately 1.8N banks still declare approximately 1.8N^2 branch weights, before pricing field construction or matrix application.
