Retirement is an effort-allocation decision, not a finding that the theorem is valueless, false or non-novel.

**Preserved results.**

- The accepted sharp theorem E_min(N)=N*sopfr(N), for every integer N>=2, in the all-entry-unique-path scalar-edge DAG model; sopfr sums prime factors with multiplicity. The earlier accepted minor and Weyl results are retained as attributed.
- In the theorem model, finite scalar-weighted DAGs over the complex numbers have disjoint input sources and output sinks, the full Fourier transfer F_N[i,j]=zeta^(ij), and exactly one nonzero path for every input-output pair. Every active scalar edge is charged, including unit edges and each occurrence of a reused constant. Zero and boundary-invisible edges may be pruned; there are no fan-in, layering, magnitude, root-of-unity-weight or auxiliary-vertex restrictions. N=1 has a separate terminal convention.
- The accepted proof uses rank-one reachability rectangles, cyclic-subgroup labels and a telescoping sopfr potential. Classical mixed-radix Cooley–Tukey supplies the upper construction, not a new fast algorithm.

**Scope restrictions.**

- All-entry path uniqueness is not selected-minor uniqueness. This restricted theorem does not rule out the original multipath/Green conjecture.
- The bounded review did not recover Papadimitriou's 1979 primary theorem body. Later authors' descriptions are evidence of their accounts, not direct verification of Papadimitriou's hypotheses. A difference in stated weights does not establish a strict extension without a cost-preserving model comparison.
- The review did not establish exact rediscovery, a restricted extension or novelty. It did not independently reproduce access failures or conduct a new mathematical proof audit.

**Unresolved archive.** Historical equivalence and novelty remain unresolved. The broader fast multipath/Green compiler remains unconstructed and unresolved, not disproved. No continuing compiler construction or repeated attribution search is scheduled.

**Reopening condition.** Reopen attribution only on a readable primary body or a precise competing theorem; this does not automatically reopen construction.
