## 3. Larger k-disk tail estimates, independently checked

Write M=p^k, N=p^r, m=cM, K=W(N), G=W((m-1)/2), C=P_(2N), B=P_(2N-1), b0=1-q^(2N). The exact transfer is P_n=fC-b0gB, with shifted associated tails f=f_m^(2N), g=g_m^(2N). All recurrence factors used have indices below p^s and are nonzero; v(b0)=N. Put sigma=rho_k and rho=rho_r.

The accepted cap R_k(j)=W(j)-sum_(u<=j) min(V(u),sigma)<=W(j)/2 holds for every j<pM, with equality precisely at multiples of M. The Newton formula then bounds P_j(q) and P_j(q^-1) coefficientwise by W(j)/2. A weight-minimal positive Newton index is a positive multiple of M, and its initial has a positive power of the local coordinate Y. At Y=0 it vanishes. For even j the index-zero term is exactly the constant P_j(0): under H/P transfer its z^(j/2)/v0^j factor cancels identically. For odd j no index-zero term exists. This reasoning applies on every disk, including inverse-q orientation; the center systems agree modulo the appropriate deeper congruence.

The original finite connection for g_m^0 has endpoint ell with W(m-ell)+W(ell-1)=W(m-1)=2G. Its weight is at least G+M-V(ell)>=G, with equality only at ell=bM. At Y=0 the even-b terms have two odd-index polynomial factors and vanish. For odd b both factors have carry-free half-indices, so their constant terms attain the half-W weights. Therefore the whole weight-G initial at Y=0 is the normalized exact constant g_m^0(0)=(-1)^((m-1)/2) product_(j<=(m-1)/2)(1-q^(2j)), whose value is G. No unproved sum of units is declared nonzero: the exact constant evaluates that sum. This proves g_m^0 is a unit of value G throughout strict k-interiors and has Gauss weight at least G on the full k-disks.

For the shifted connections, eta is a scalar unit with v(eta-1)>=N-M. Half-W and superadditivity give the coefficientwise errors

Delta_f >= G+N-M/2,  Delta_f' >= G+N-M/2-sigma;

These follow termwise from the actual shift ratios of values N-V(d) and N-2V(ell), respectively; no endpoint is omitted. ## 4. Outside-core ratio: centers, ties, interval bounds and coordinates

At a strict k-interior point outside the closed old r-cover, define tau=max_(0<=b<2N) v(z+q^b). Then sigma<tau<rho. Representatives modulo beta_r suffice because their omitted shifts have distance value at least beta_r>rho. A maximizing center a gives v(z+q^b)=min(tau,V(b-a)); at a tied level V(b-a)=tau, ultrametricity supplies the lower bound and maximality prevents a larger value. Thus exact p-power tau and multiple closest centers are not exceptional cases.

For any consecutive interval of length 2h+1, choose a maximizer within that interval, of value tau0<=tau. The left and right offsets of lengths h1,h2 have total distances at most T_r(h1)+T_r(h2), where h1+h2=2h. The cap has positive floor coefficients:

T_r(j)=j+sum_(ell>=1) [min(p^ell,rho)-min(p^(ell-1),rho)] floor(j/p^ell).

Consequently it is superadditive, so the interval sum is at most tau+T_r(2h). This is independent of alignment, ties, and whether the best center is an endpoint.

The monic C/H_(2N) root cover is sufficient to show v(H_(2N))=v(N_(2N)) outside the cover: each old disk contains exactly 2L_r roots of H_(2N) and exactly 2L_r center factors of N_(2N), and an outside point has the same distance to every root or center in one such disk. For a numerator Newton term j=2N-1-2h, endpoint reflection and the interval bound give its ratio to N_(2N) value at least R_r(2h)-W(h)-tau. Subtracting yields W(h)-R_r(2h)<=N/2-rho, and therefore every numerator term has ratio value at least

L0=-N/2+rho-tau.
